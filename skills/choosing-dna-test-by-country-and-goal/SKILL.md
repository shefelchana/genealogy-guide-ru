# Choosing a DNA test: by country, by goal, by order

Since 2025 the old plan "test once, then upload the file everywhere for free" mostly does not work (MyHeritage closed uploads, companies changed formats, Russia restricted sending samples). In 2026 experts and forum regulars give one rule: **test with the company whose database you want to search.** So the choice of the first test matters more than before.

**Facts below are as of November 2026.** Country lists, prices and laws change. Always tell the user the date of these facts and ask them to check the company's current "who can order" page before paying. Details and sources: `references/country-availability.md`.

The user buys, registers and pays. You help decide.

## Step 1. Ask three questions

1. **Where does the person live now, and where will the kit be shipped?** (Country of residence decides what can be bought legally.)
2. **What exactly do they want to find?** Pick one main goal:
   - relatives in Russia, Ukraine, Belarus;
   - the Jewish (Ashkenazi) line, relatives in Israel and Europe;
   - emigrant relatives in the USA, Canada, UK;
   - the paternal surname line (only through a man of that line);
   - the direct maternal line;
   - checking an ethnic family legend ("great-grandmother was German").
3. **Budget and patience:** one test now, or a plan for a year?

## Step 2. Match goal to database

| Goal | First choice | Why / caveat |
|---|---|---|
| Relatives in Russia and the former USSR | **Genotek** (Russian database); MyHeritage if it can be bought legally | MyHeritage has many Russian speakers but is not sold in Russia; forums report Genotek quality complaints since late 2024 (imputed data, unstable ethnicity) |
| Jewish line, Israel, continental Europe | **MyHeritage** where it is sold; **FamilyTreeDNA** Family Finder from Israel | MyHeritage is "best for continental Europe" but accepts no uploads now; under endogamy see `dna-matches-endogamy` |
| Emigrant cousins in English-speaking countries | **AncestryDNA** (~30 million kits) | not sold in Russia, Israel, Ukraine, Belarus; no chromosome browser; its 2026 file transfers badly |
| Paternal surname line | **Y-DNA** at FamilyTreeDNA (largest Y database, surname projects) or whole genome 30x | see `y-and-mtdna-lineage-testing` |
| Maternal line | **mtFull** (FamilyTreeDNA) | matches are often very old; useful less often |
| Ethnic legend | any autosomal test | percentages are a model, not a measurement; see `checking-dna-against-tree` |
| Close relationship "for ourselves" | two tests at the same company | no legal force |

Free extra databases where a file can still be added: **GEDmatch** (autosomal files; settings for police access must be chosen), FamilyTreeDNA (free matches for accepted files). Check `dna-raw-data-backup-and-transfer` before promising that a file will be accepted.

## Step 3. Country rules (November 2026)

| Country of residence | Can buy (per company lists) | Main caution |
|---|---|---|
| **Russia** | Genotek, Atlas, Genopoisk (Y/mt/WGS) | **Federal law 43-FZ (from 1 Sep 2026)**: genetic research data may go abroad or to foreigners only by decision of an inter-agency commission [verify against the law text]. Moderators of the main Russian genealogy forum delete advice on sending samples abroad. Sources do not say clearly whether it covers a private person with their own sample. **Do not give instructions for sending samples out of Russia.** Offer Russian labs, or testing while legally staying in another country. |
| **Israel** | FamilyTreeDNA; 23andMe — sources disagree | Genetic Information Law 2000: tests by doctor's referral or court order in approved labs; sources disagree whether consumer tests are "banned" or "strictly regulated". MyHeritage (an Israeli company) and Ancestry do not sell to Israel. |
| **Ukraine** | none of the four big companies ships (early 2026) | test while abroad, or a local lab |
| **Belarus** | MyHeritage only (early 2026) | — |
| **Kazakhstan** | FamilyTreeDNA and MyHeritage ship directly (forum reports) | — |
| **EU, USA, UK** | all major companies | EU accounts are excluded from FamilyTreeDNA police matching automatically |

Never suggest buying through a friend's address abroad or other workarounds of a country restriction. If the user is legally in another country for a while, a test bought and taken there is fine.

## Step 4. Who to test first

1. **The oldest living generation first** — a grandparent carries twice as much of the ancestors' DNA as a grandchild, and samples from the deceased are almost impossible to get later.
2. For a **surname line** — a man who carries the surname in the direct male line (father, brother, paternal uncle, male cousin).
3. For **sorting matches by side** — relatives connected through **only one** grandparent (a half-sibling's child, a cousin through one grandmother). See `dna-matches-across-platforms`.
4. **Consent:** the person tests only with informed consent: who will see the data, what surprises are possible (unknown siblings, different fathers). The kit may be managed by a relative, but only with explicit consent; FamilyTreeDNA lets you hand a kit over by changing the e-mail.

| FamilyTreeDNA Family Finder (NGS since March 2026) | — | ~$40 | 11–16 weeks (mid-2026) |

Say clearly: prices are reference points from forums and blogs with dates, not offers.

## Step 6. Give a plan, not a list

End with a short plan for this family, for example:
- "Now: Genotek for the grandmother (relatives in Russia). In half a year: Y-37 for the uncle while visiting [EU country]. Free: upload the grandmother's original VCF/file where it is accepted."
- One line on what **not** to expect: DNA suggests and tests hypotheses; it does not replace documents, and many matches never answer messages.

## Common beginner mistakes to warn about
- Expecting the test to "find the family by itself" without a tree with places for every person.
- Trusting ethnicity to a tenth of a percent, or expecting a child to show "the father's Ukrainian percent".
- Buying a test that is not sold in their country through workarounds, or sending a sample from Russia abroad after 1 September 2026.
- Deleting an old kit to "start fresh" instead of keeping all results.
- Asking AI to "interpret my G25/K13 calculator" and taking the answer as fact about ancestors.
