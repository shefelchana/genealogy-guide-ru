# Requesting copies from Ukrainian archives

A request is the **last** step for a file, not the first. Most metrical books, revisions and many court files are already online; a letter costs money and months. When the person decides to write, the request should be **addressed** (exact references), **cheap to fulfil** (copies, not a search) and **easy to price item by item**. The decision to write, the text that goes out and the sending are the person's: the AI drafts only when asked and never sends.

Related skills: `ukrainian-archives-on-commons` (is it online?), `searching-familysearch-films` (Images), `searching-archival-name` (references from inventories), `tracing-family-moves-and-registration` and `tracing-revision-records` (which files decide the question), `citizenship-by-descent-dossier` (certified certificates are a different request).

## 1. When a request makes sense

All of these must be true and written in the search log:
1. **The reference is exact**: archive, fond, opys, sprava (and sheets), taken from an inventory, a cover or a citation you can show; the title is copied as in the inventory.
2. **It is not online**, checked on the date of the letter:
   - **Duck Inspector** (inspector.duckarchive.com) by archive-fond-opys-sprava — where the scan is published, if anywhere (an empty answer is not proof);
   - **Wikimedia Commons**: file names with and without the archive prefix and full-text search of descriptions for "F-O-D" (some files carry the reference only in the description);
   - **FamilySearch Images** by place and `keyword=<file number>`;
   - **searcharchives.net.ua** with `Digitized=1`, and the archive's own catalogue;
   - **the archive's list of already digitised files** (the "use fund", фонд користування): copies from it are much cheaper and faster — ask for those by reference too.
3. **It answers a question** that no online document answers (write the question next to each item).
4. Or the person needs an **official certificate or certified copy** (citizenship, inheritance) — then the request type is different (архівна довідка), see `citizenship-by-descent-dossier`.

## 2. What the rules say (doctrine, checked November 2026 on the texts of the orders)

- **Archive Rules** (order of the Ministry of Justice of 08.04.2013 No. 656/5, section VI, item 4.2): a genealogical request is a kind of personal request and may concern other people; thematic, personal and property requests are **paid services**; e-mail requests are considered (not anonymous ones); the archive confirms acceptance or gives reasons for refusal; the term is **up to 30 days from registration**, extendable, **45 days at most**. Forums report about two months in practice [from search results].
- **Procedure for requests** (order of 02.03.2015 No. 295/5): the request must contain the applicant's name, place of residence, the topic and **the chronological frame**; copies are issued with a note that they may not be reproduced or put into networks or databases **without the archive's permission** — ask separately before uploading a copy anywhere.
- Archives also ask for: full name, registration address (as in the passport), postal address, e-mail and phone; a signed consent to the processing of personal data. A request without these may not be registered.
- Kinship does not need to be proven for pre-revolutionary court, notarial or revision files [doctrine; check the archive's own page]. Soviet-period personal files can be restricted (`soviet-era-records`).
- Practical points seen on archive sites and in reports (2022–2026): some archives do not accept mail from Russian e-mail domains (mail.ru, yandex, rambler and similar); since 2022 Ukrainian archives have said they do not serve citizens of Russia and Belarus [press report]. Write from an ordinary international mailbox and give the real address.

## 3. The price list logic (as of November 2026 — check the archive's current price list)

Every state archive publishes its own order on fees. One oblast archive (order of 30.04.2025, read November 2026), in UAH:
| Service | Unit | UAH |
|---|---|---|
| search on a **thematic** request | request | 1,077.57 |
| search on a **genealogical** request | request | 1,187.17 |
| search on a biographical request | request | 228.37 |
| **digital copy of an archival document** | copy | **4.44** |
| copy from the use fund (already digitised) | file | 0.89 |
| digital copy from microfilm | copy | 3.20 |
| archival certificate (copy, extract) | certificate | 32.20 |
| letter that no information was found | letter | 20.49 |
| transcription of palaeographic text | page | 169.20 |
| urgent execution | — | up to +50 % |

The logic: **copies by exact reference are cheap; a "search" is charged per request and costs more than dozens of copies.** If the letter reads like "find everything about my family", it will be priced as a genealogical search. So: exact references, the words "no search needed", one numbered item per file, and "tell me the cost per item before starting". Whether a "copy" is one side of a sheet or a spread is not always stated: ask.

## 4. Payment from abroad (the weak point)

Archives take payment into a **state treasury account in hryvnias**; some offer payment by QR code in a Ukrainian banking app. Paying with a foreign card or by SWIFT to a treasury account is often impossible or not described. In the first letter ask: (1) the invoice and details (IBAN, EDRPOU code, payment purpose) **before** work starts; (2) whether a foreign card can be used; (3) whether a third person in Ukraine may pay, quoting the request number. Choosing a payer is the person's decision. Never send card details by e-mail.

## 5. How to write it

1. **In Ukrainian.** Text in the body of the e-mail **and** as a signed PDF (scan or photo of the signed sheet).
2. **Subject line with the references**: "Запит на виготовлення цифрових копій справ: ф. X оп. Y спр. Z; …".
3. **One letter — one or two fonds**, numbered items, each with: full reference, fond name as in the archive's catalogue, the title as in the inventory, years, number of sheets if known, what is needed (whole file / sheets / a yes-no question), and **which question it answers** (one line; this also helps the archivist find the right part).
4. **Priority order**: first a question that can save money (does the fond survive? see 6), then cheap exact copies, then partial copies of big files, then items "if possible, price separately".
5. **The copy spec**: all sheets including the cover, versos and the certifying sheet (аркуш-засвідчувач), uncropped margins, JPG or PDF, at least 300 dpi, sent by e-mail.
6. **For an existing scan with a defect** (a corner covered, out of focus): say that the digital copy exists and what is wrong with it, ask for a **new copy from the original**, otherwise you will get the same file from the use fund; as a fallback, a transcription of the hidden lines.
7. Ask for the **registration number**. If there is no confirmation after about ten working days, the person may phone the reference desk.

## 6. Ask about the fond's condition first

Fires, evacuation and war losses destroyed whole fonds (in one archive a 2003 fire almost destroyed the fonds of the governor's chancellery, the treasury chamber and others; printed post-fire guides list what survived). Before paying for a search in such a fond, ask whether the files are **available** ("чи є справи в наявності"). A reply "not found" may mean a lost file, a wrong reference or a renumbered opys: ask which.

## 8. Case-list table (keep it in the project, send only the chosen rows)

| No. | Reference (archive, fond-opys-sprava, sheets) | Title verbatim from the inventory | Years | What is needed (whole / sheets / question) | Which question it answers | Priority 1–3 | Where the reference comes from | Checked online (where, date) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Priority: 1 decides kinship; 2 refines; 3 background. Estimate the size (sheets × 2 sides) and the cost from the price list before the person decides. Mark items removed because they turned out to be online.

## 9. Worked example (invented family)

The Levitan family: a district court file of 1879 on an inheritance (9 sheets) and a notary's file of 1886 on a mortgage of the family's house, both from published inventories of an oblast archive; a revision of 1858 that may hold the family.
1. Inspector, Commons (names and full text), FamilySearch Images by place and file number, the archive's digitised list → none of the three online (dates written).
2. Case list: the court file — priority 1 (names the heirs), whole file, about 18 sides × 4.44 ≈ 80 UAH; the notary's file — priority 2, whole file, size unknown; the 1858 revision — priority 1, but the treasury chamber fond burned: item 0 asks whether the file survives before any search.
3. The letter: item 0 (condition of the revision fond), item 1, item 2 "price separately"; "no search needed"; copies spec; cost and payment details before work; registration number.
4. The person reads the draft, decides and sends it. The AI records "draft prepared, not sent" in the log.

## Answer format

1. Why each file needs a request (online checks with dates).
2. The case-list table and an estimate of cost and size, with the price-list date.
3. The draft in Ukrainian with placeholders for the person's details (never fill in personal data the person has not given).
4. Questions to settle before sending: payment route, which items to include, whether to ask permission to publish.
