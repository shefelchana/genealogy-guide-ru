# Borders and administrative reforms by region

Read this for every place where the event (birth, marriage, revision, census, arrest) fell in years of wars or reforms. The rule: documents are sought in the archive of the authority and the division that existed **in the year of the event**.

This is a short cheat sheet for orientation. For a specific place, confirm the jurisdiction for the relevant year with a directory of the era (the Lists of Populated Places, a guberniya memorial book, a Soviet directory of administrative-territorial divisions) or with JewishGen Communities. Where a date is approximate, this is stated.

## 1. Right-bank Ukraine: Podolia, the Kyiv region, Volhynia

| Years | What there was |
|---|---|
| until 1793-1795 | Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth (voivodeships); Jewish communities were kahals |
| 1793-1797 | passes to Russia; from 1797 the guberniyas **Podolia, Kyiv, Volhynia** |
| 1797-1923/25 | guberniya → uezd → volost; Jewish vital records kept by crown rabbis (from 1835) |
| 1921 | Peace of Riga: **western Volhynia** (Rivne, Lutsk, Kovel, Kremenets) belongs to Poland until 1939 |
| 1923-1930 | in the Ukrainian SSR guberniyas and uezds are replaced by **okrugs** and districts (a transition period, the division changed several times) |
| from 1932 | **oblasts**: Vinnytsia, Kyiv and others |
| 1937 | on 22.09.1937 the **Kamianets-Podilskyi** oblast was separated from Vinnytsia; by decree of 12.05.1941 its centre was moved to Proskurov (the oblast kept its name until 1954) |
| 1941-1944 | German occupation (Reichskommissariat "Ukraine"); the south-west of Podolia between the Dniester and the Bug was under Romania ("Transnistria") |
| 1954 | Proskurov → **Khmelnytskyi**, the oblast → Khmelnytskyi oblast |
| 2016 | decommunization: mass renaming of villages and towns |
| 2020 | enlargement of districts: the old districts were merged, "district X" today may differ from that of 2019 |

Consequence for archives: pre-revolutionary Podolia is divided between the archives of the Khmelnytskyi and Vinnytsia oblasts; some documents of the guberniya institutions are in TsDIAK (Central State Historical Archive of Ukraine, Kyiv).

## 2. Bessarabia and Transnistria

| Years | What there was |
|---|---|
| until 1812 | Principality of Moldavia under Ottoman suzerainty; the fortresses Khotin, Bender, Akkerman, Izmail, Kiliya were Ottoman |
| 1812 | Treaty of Bucharest: to Russia; the Bessarabia oblast, then guberniya |
| **1856-1878** | Treaty of Paris: **southern Bessarabia** (Izmail, Kiliya, Bolgrad, Cahul, Reni) to the Principality of Moldavia, then Romania. **Akkerman remains Russian** |
| 1878 | Congress of Berlin: the south returned to Russia |
| **1918-1940** | all of Bessarabia is Romania; Romanian civil registration |
| 1924-1940 | the left bank of the Dniester (Tiraspol, Balta): **Moldavian ASSR** within the Ukrainian SSR |
| 1940 | to the USSR: the Moldavian SSR; the south (Izmail, Akkerman), the Khotin uezd and northern Bukovina go to the Ukrainian SSR |
| 1941-1944 | Romania again; between the Dniester and the Bug the Romanian "Transnistria" |
| 1944 | USSR; the Izmail oblast was merged into the Odesa oblast in 1954 |

Consequences: a person born in Izmail in 1856-1878 **has no Russian metrical record**: there was none. Romanian records of those years for southern Bessarabia have almost all been lost or are not digitized: the absence is a property of the source. Metrical books of the Russian period are in ANRM (Chisinau; National Archives of the Republic of Moldova), and much is on FamilySearch films; for the south also the Izmail archive: from 1954 a branch of the Odesa oblast archive, and since 12.11.2003 a communal institution "Izmail Archive" of the Izmail city council, outside the Odesa oblast archive system.

## 3. Galicia and Bukovina

| Years | Galicia | Bukovina |
|---|---|---|
| 1772 / 1775 | Austria (Kingdom of Galicia and Lodomeria) | Austria |
| until 1918 | Austrian metrical books; Jewish metrical **districts**: the record of a small shtetl is in the book of a neighbouring large one | the same |
| 1918-1919 | West Ukrainian People's Republic, Polish-Ukrainian war | Romania (1918) |
| 1919-1939 | **Poland** (Lwow, Stanislawow, Tarnopol voivodeships) | Romania until 1940 |
| 1939-1941 | USSR | the north: USSR from 1940 (Chernivtsi oblast) |
| 1941-1944 | District "Galicia" (General Government) | Romania |
| from 1944 | USSR | the north: USSR |

Sources: AGAD (Warsaw), TsDIAL (Lviv), oblast archives, JRI-Poland, Gesher Galicia.

## 4. Transcarpathia

Hungary until 1919/1920; Czechoslovakia 1919-1938; Hungary 1938/39-1944; USSR from 1945. Metrical records: State Archive of the Transcarpathian Oblast; the languages of the documents are Hungarian, Czech and Latin.

## 5. Belarus and the Vilnius region

| Years | What there was |
|---|---|
| 1772-1795 | partitions of the Commonwealth; guberniyas Minsk, Mogilev, Vitebsk, Grodno, Vilna |
| 1921-1939 | **western Belarus is Poland** (Vilnius, Nowogrodek, Polesie, Bialystok voivodeships) |
| 1920-1939 | Vilnius: "Central Lithuania", then Poland; passed to Lithuania in 1939 |
| 1939 | western Belarus joins the Belarusian SSR; Bialystok returned to Poland in 1945 |

Sources: NIAB (Minsk, branch in Grodno), Radzima.org, JewishGen Belarus; for 1921-1939 the Polish archives and Geneteka.

## 6. Kingdom of Poland

1815-1915 under Russia, but with its own system of **civil-religious records** (the metrical books were kept as civil status acts; in Russian from 1868). It is not included in the "Lists of Populated Places of the Russian Empire": search the Słownik geograficzny. Poland from 1918. Sources: JRI-Poland, Geneteka, Polish state archives (szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl has blocked some foreign addresses; do not bypass).

## 7. The Baltic

- **Lithuania**: Kovno and Vilna guberniyas; Suwalki was in the Kingdom of Poland. Independent Lithuania in 1918-1940 (Vilnius belonged to Poland until 1939). Sources: LVIA, LitvakSIG.
- **Latvia**: Courland and Livonia; ⚠️ **Latgale** (Dvinsk/Daugavpils, Rezhitsa) belonged until 1918 to the **Vitebsk guberniya**; its documents are sought in the Belarusian and Latvian systems. Sources: Raduraksti.
- **Estonia**: the Estonia guberniya and northern Livonia. Sources: Rahvusarhiiv (Saaga, AIS).

## 8. Russia

| Years | What there was |
|---|---|
| until 1923-1929 | guberniya → uezd → volost |
| 1923-1929 | enlargement of volosts, abolition of uezds; transition to oblasts, krais, okrugs and districts (in different years by region) |
| from 1929-1930 | oblasts / krais → districts → village soviets |
| 1930s-1950s | frequent changes of district centres and district borders |

Church metrical books are kept by parish (church), and a parish could cover several villages of different volosts. Cross-fond indexes of oblast archives ("village → church → fond") solve this problem fastest.

## 9. Soviet renamings

- Towns and villages were renamed in the 1920s-1960s (after public figures, after new collective farms) and back after the 1990s; in Ukraine massively in 2016.
- The old and new name are found through JewishGen Communities (variants), directories of administrative-territorial divisions of the Soviet republics, and Wikipedia village entries (these usually list former names; check against a directory of the era).
- Documents of the 1920s-1930s contain **transitional** names that did not last long; they occur only in ZAGS records and newspapers of those years.
