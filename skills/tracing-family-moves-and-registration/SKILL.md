# Tracing a family's move: registration vs residence

In the Russian Empire every townsman belonged to a **society** (мещанское or купеческое общество) of a particular town and stayed in it until a formal transfer, wherever he lived. So a family has two geographies: **where it was registered** and **where it lived**. They change at different times, and different documents record each of them. Most "contradictions" between documents disappear once you sort each mention into one of the two columns.

Related skills: `tracing-revision-records` (revisions and family numbers), `identifying-places` (uezd and gubernia for the year), `ukrainian-archives-on-commons` and `searching-familysearch-films` (where the files are), `searching-archival-name` (file titles in inventories), `reconstructing-family-clusters` (the whole family), `verifying-genealogy-findings`.

## 1. The rule: «мещанин города X» is registration

"Meshchanin of town X" (in Jewish records "мещанин м. X", "X-скій мещ.") states the society the man belonged to, **not where he lived** (Y. Kogan, "Estate and other categories of Jews in Bessarabia", IAJGS 2012, re-posted 2022 — [handout](https://www.jewishgen.org/bessarabia/files/conferences/2022/2012EstatePresentation.pdf)). A small shtetl could have no society of its own, and its people were registered to the nearest town. Revisions, family lists, conscription lists and many tax records were made **by registration**; censuses, police and notarial records, and metrical records of the place of the event show **residence**.

Consequence: a son can be born in town B to a "meshchanin of town A" for decades. That is normal and is not evidence of a second family.

## 2. Documents and what each one dates

| Document | What it shows | Column |
|---|---|---|
| Revision lists (1795–1858), supplementary revision lists (between revisions, e.g. 1859–1870s) | membership of the society; arrivals "перечислен из…", departures "перечислен в…", with year | registration |
| Family lists (посемейные списки, 1870s) | the society, with reference to the 10th revision | registration |
| **1875 Jewish military census** | the family by registration, **and members living elsewhere — where and on what document** | both |
| Recruit / conscription lists and files | the society's family numbers, sometimes the revision number of the family; files on evaders list the man's actual whereabouts | registration (+ residence) |
| **Passport** issued by the home society or town duma | that the holder lived outside his society; its number and date; the police registration in the new town ("прописан", "проживает по паспорту … выданному … думою") is often noted in court, notarial or police files | residence |
| Transfer files: «о перечислении», «о причислении», «об увольнении/исключении из общества», «о приёме в общество» (treasury chamber, town duma, magistrate, gubernia board) | the formal change of registration, with the society's consent (приговор) and the receiving society's acceptance | registration — the decisive date |
| Merchant guild files (admission to a guild, «о записке в купцы») | moving into the merchant estate of another town | registration |
| Metrical records | place of the event; father's society | residence (place) + registration (father's status) |
| 1897 census | place of birth, registration and residence in separate fields | all three |
| Notarial, court, police files; directories; house-owner lists | where the person owned, rented, traded, was summoned | residence |
| Student and service files | certificate of registration (свидетельство о приписке), birth certificate | registration + birth |

Series of transfer files can be large and missing from name indexes (one archive holds 1,312 files "on registration in tax-paying estates" for 1846–1851 alone): search them in inventories **by title words**, not by surname.

## 3. Method: build the timeline and bracket both windows

1. **Collect every mention** of every family member, with the place it gives and the column it belongs to (birth / registration / residence). Include siblings, children and in-laws: a child's birthplace dates the parents' residence.
2. **Sort by year** in one table: year · document (archive reference) · person · place · column · quote.
3. **Bracket the residence move:** last document that puts the family *living* in A → first that puts it *living* in B. Births of children are the densest evidence here.
4. **Bracket the registration move:** last "meshchanin of A" → first "meshchanin of B". Expect it years or decades after the residence move, or never.
5. **Name the decisive document** for each window: the transfer file for registration; for residence, a metrical record of a child, a passport registration, a house purchase.
6. **Look for push and pull factors**, as context only (doctrine, not evidence for a specific family):
   - a **marriage** to a woman from B (see 4);
   - **trade**: leases, a mill, a shop in B (notarial and court files);
   - a **railway** opening near B;
   - the **1843 order to remove Jews from the 50-verst zone** along the Prussian and Austrian borders. Before 1843 the ban concerned new settlement in villages of the zone; in 1843 it was extended to towns and shtetls (Senate decree of January 1844). Enforcement was uneven and it was later relaxed [date of the relaxation — check]. Tested November 2026: a gubernia board file of 1843–1848 contains the surveyor's list of shtetls inside the zone per uezd, but no lists of evicted people were found in the parts read. Use it to know whether a town was in the zone, not as proof that a family was expelled.
7. **Write the result as two windows** with levels: "residence in B: between 18xx and 18yy 🟢; registration in B: between 18zz and 18ww 🟡; why: hypothesis 💭".

## 4. Naming as a clue to the bride's family (doctrine, use with care)

Ashkenazi Jews named children after **deceased** relatives. If a son carries a name absent from the father's line, he may be named after the **mother's father, already dead** at the birth. That points to the bride's family, and her family's town may be the reason for the move. This is a hypothesis generator: it tells you whom to look for (a man of that name with a daughter of the mother's name, dying before the birth, in town B), never a fact by itself.

## 5. Where the files are online

- **Wikimedia Commons** (Ukrainian archives): revisions, supplementary lists, family lists, 1875 census files, gubernia board files; search by fond-opys-sprava (`ukrainian-archives-on-commons`).
- **FamilySearch Images** (filming since 2022): for at least one Ukrainian gubernia it includes **town magistrates** (files 1796–1862), **town dumas** (e.g. a file "on the registration of Jews into…", 1852–1854; residents' books), courts, notaries, consistory — but not the treasury chamber fond with revisions (checked November 2026). Search by FamilySearch place ID with `keyword=<file number>`; rerun every few months.
- **Inter-archive portal** (searcharchives.net.ua) and **archival.name** inventories: search **by place**, then filter titles for «перечисл», «причисл», «исключ», «паспорт», «жительств».
- What is only in the archive (passport books of town dumas, many supplementary lists): list it with exact references and leave the decision to request to the person (`requesting-records-from-ukrainian-archives`).

## 6. Worked example (invented family)

The Rozental family, meshchane of Nemyriv.
| Year | Document | Place | Column |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1850 | revision, family No. 112 | Nemyriv | registration |
| 1858 | revision: Gershko 24, wife Tsirlia | Nemyriv | registration |
| 1864 | birth of son Itsko, father "Nemyriv meshchanin" | Bratslav | residence (+ registration) |
| 1874 | family list | Nemyriv | registration |
| 1876 | court file: passport of the Nemyriv duma No. 312, registered by Bratslav police | Bratslav | residence |
| 1897 | census: born Nemyriv, registered Bratslav, living Bratslav | — | all |

Residence in Bratslav: **between 1858 and 1864** 🟢 (the 1864 birth). Registration moved **between 1876 and 1897** 🟡 (the census row; the transfer file not yet found). Itsko is not a name in Gershko's line; Tsirlia's father, a Bratslav man named Itsko who died before 1864, would explain both the name and the move 💭 → search Bratslav deaths before 1864 and the magistrate files of 1858–1864.

## Answer format

1. The timeline table (year · document with archive reference · person · place · column · verbatim quote).
2. The two windows (residence, registration) with levels and the document that closes each.
3. Context factors marked as doctrine or hypothesis, never as facts about the family.
4. Next documents: exact references where known, where they are online, and what is archive-only.
