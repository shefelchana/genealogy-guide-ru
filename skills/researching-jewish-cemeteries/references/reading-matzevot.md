## 1. Structure of the inscription

| Line | Example | What it gives |
|---|---|---|
| Opening formula | פ״נ / פ״ט | "here lies buried / hidden" |
| Epithet, title | האשה החשובה, הבחור, מו״ה | marital status, learning, role in the community |
| Name | יהודה ליב | full Jewish name, often a double name |
| Father's name | בן / בת / ב״ר + name | a step up one generation |
| Descent | הכהן / הלוי | Kohen or Levite, passed down through the father |
| Surname, town | sometimes | link to documents |
| Date of death | נפ׳ י״ב כסלו תרס״ה לפ״ק | date in the Jewish calendar |
| Age | בן / בת … שנים | year of birth ± 1 |
| Verses, acrostic | the first letters of the lines spell the name | a check on the reading of the name |
| Closing | ת.נ.צ.ב.ה. | standard formula |

## 2. Abbreviations

| Abbreviation | Expansion | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| פ״נ | פה נקבר / נקברה | here lies buried |
| פ״ט | פה טמון / טמונה | here lies hidden |
| ב״ר | בן / בת רבי | son (daughter) of reb ... |
| ר׳ | רבי (reb) | polite form of address, "Mr."; **not** a rabbi. JewishGen: "The word reb is a simple honorific" [opened] |
| מו״ה | מורנו הרב | "our teacher, Mr.": a respected, learned man |
| הר״ר | הרב רבי | honorary title of a scholar |
| אב״ד | אב בית דין | head of the rabbinic court: the town rabbi |
| נפ׳ | נפטר / נפטרה | died |
| נלב״ע | נלקח לבית עולמו | "taken to his eternal home": died |
| ז״ל | זכרונו לברכה | "of blessed memory": said of the deceased |
| ע״ה | עליו / עליה השלום | "peace be upon him (her)": said of the deceased |
| לפ״ק | לפרט קטן | year "by the small count": without the thousands |
| ת.נ.צ.ב.ה. | תהא נשמתו צרורה בצרור החיים | "may the soul be bound up in the bond of life" (1 Samuel 25:29) [opened: JewishGen] |

## 3. Epithets and titles: what they tell you

| Word | Meaning | What it means for the search |
|---|---|---|
| הבתולה | maiden | never married: do not look for a marriage, look in the parents' family |
| הבחור | young man, unmarried | the same for a man |
| הילד / הילדה | child | birth record a few years before the death |
| האשה, מרת | woman, "Mrs." | married; look for the husband among the neighbouring stones |
| האשה החשובה, הצנועה | "the esteemed", "the modest" | standard praise of a married woman |
| אבינו / אמנו, אבי / אמי | our father / mother, my father / mother | the stone was put up by the children |
| בעלי / אשתי | my husband / my wife | the stone was put up by the spouse |
| הרב | rabbi | look in rabbi lists and the press |
| הנגיד, הגביר | wealthy benefactor | merchant documents, donations in the press |
| החסיד | Hasid or pious man | not necessarily a Hasid in the narrow sense |

## 4. Father alive or dead

After the father's name:
- **ז״ל** or **ע״ה**: the father had **died** by this time; his death is earlier than the date on the stone.
- **נ״י** (נרו יאיר) or **שיחי׳** (שיחיה): the father was **alive**.
This gives a free bound for the date of the father's death: write it down.

## 5. Date: gematria, months, conversion

**Numbers as letters:** א1 ב2 ג3 ד4 ה5 ו6 ז7 ח8 ט9 · י10 כ20 ל30 מ40 נ50 ס60 ע70 פ80 צ90 · ק100 ר200 ש300 ת400. 15 and 16 are written **ט״ו** and **ט״ז** (not י״ה, י״ו).

**Year:** תרס״ה = 400+200+60+5 = 665 → 5665 (thousands omitted, often with לפ״ק). Gregorian year = abbreviated year + 1240 for the months from Shevat to Elul; **+1239** for Tishrei, Cheshvan, Kislev and the start of Tevet (the year begins in autumn) [+1240 from JewishGen; the −1 correction for the autumn months was checked with the Hebcal converter, 08.10.2026].

**Months:** תשרי, חשון (מרחשון), כסלו, טבת, שבט, אדר (in a leap year אדר א׳ and אדר ב׳, or ואדר), ניסן, אייר, סיון, תמוז, אב (מנחם אב), אלול.

**Checked with the Hebcal converter** [opened]:
- 12 Kislev 5665 → 20 November 1904; 12 Nisan 5665 → 17 April 1905.
- 7 Adar I 5673 → 14 February 1913; 7 Adar II 5673 → 16 March 1913.
- The Jewish day begins in the evening: a death in the evening is recorded under the next day.
- To compare with Russian documents, convert to the Julian calendar: −12 days in the 19th century, −13 days from 1 March 1900 until February 1918.

An animal that matches the name on the stone confirms the reading of the name; one that does not match is a reason to reread it.

## 7. Soviet and bilingual monuments

- Russian (Ukrainian) text: full name, dates in the Gregorian calendar, "from the children", "from the wife".
- **An enamel or ceramic photo** is also a source for the family photo archive: compare it with family pictures (`interpreting-jewish-family-photos`).
- A line in Hebrew (often one or two): the Jewish name and the father's name. The Russian patronymic is often "translated" by sound: Berko → Borisovich, Mordechai → Markovich, Shmuel → Semyonovich. The two sides of the stone are **two independent pieces of evidence**: record both.
- Often there is only Russian text and a Star of David, with no Jewish name; look for it in the metrical record or on the neighbouring stones of the parents.

## 8. Common reading mistakes

- Confusion of letters: ד/ר, ה/ח/ת, ו/ז/י, ב/כ, ס/ם, ע/צ on a weathered stone. Compare with the same letter in another word of the inscription.
- The father's name is read as a surname; there is almost never a surname on old stones.
- "ר׳" before a name is taken for "rabbi".
- A year without thousands is converted wrongly: check with the converter.
- The stone could have been put up a year or two after the death, and the inscription restored later, with errors.
- A cenotaph or memorial plaque (for victims of the Holocaust, or killed at the front) is not a burial place; such stones say "in memory", often without a date.
