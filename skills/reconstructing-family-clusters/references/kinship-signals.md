# Signals of kinship between families of one surname

The strength of a signal is a guide, not a formula. A link gets its level from the totality of **independent** signals: two signals from one document or one index count as one.

## Signals

| Signal | How to check | Strength | Caveats |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Direct naming of the kinship** in a document (son, brother, widow, "family member living separately") | verbatim from the scan | 🟢 on its own | check whom the word refers to: fields in metrical records and indexes get mixed up |
| **Age bridge between revisions** + matching household composition | age "by the last revision" = age in the last revision ± 1-2 years; the children's names match | 🟢 | the family number is not carried over; compare people, not numbers |
| **Clerk's reference to the number in the last revision** (family lists, 1875 census) | read the number and open that number in 1858 | 🟢 if the composition matches | the number is valid only within its own society and its own source |
| **Shared place of registration** with a surname that is neither a place name nor an occupation | revisions of one society | 🟡 contribution | in a large society, unrelated namesakes are possible |
| **Transfer between societies** (a transfer file) | file on transfer/assignment, those who left in the revisions | 🟢 for the one who moved, 🟡 for a link with those who stayed | "transferred" in the column for departures: look in the receiving society |
| **Set of names across 2-3 generations** (family names repeat every other generation) | table of names by family | 🟡 | one matching name means nothing; the set matters |
| **Name in memory of the dead** (a grandson bears the name of the dead grandfather; a child is named for a relative who has just died) | date of the ancestor's death ≤ date of birth of the namesake | 🟡 contribution; 🔴 against if the "grandfather" is alive at the birth of the namesake grandson | Ashkenazim do not name after the living, but there are exceptions and second names |
| **Father's double name → different patronymics among the children** (sons by one half, daughters by the other, plus Russian forms) | the fathers' names in different documents reduce to one double name | 🟡 | different patronymics among the children do not disprove kinship |
| **Change of name during life** (illness, misfortune, a daughter's marriage to a namesake of the father) | family number, names of the closest relatives, age, registration | 🟡 | at least two matches in the circle are needed |
| **A clerk's unique error** repeated in two documents (a distorted form of the surname, a rare spelling) | the same "wrong" form in two families | 🟢-🟡 | only if the error is really rare and the documents are independent |
| **Witnesses, godparent-equivalents (sandaks), guarantors** | metrical records, marriages, court and notarial files | 🟡 contribution | witnesses were often synagogue attendants and neighbours: look for repetition |
| **Shared business** (partners, a shared lease, lawsuits against each other, inheritance) | courts, notaries, press | 🟡, 🟢 if the kinship is named | partners could also be unrelated |
| **Shared patronymic** in two men of one generation (probable brothers) found in different fonds or documents | compare age, place of registration, occupation, children's names | ⚪ on its own; 🟡 only with a second independent signal | a patronymic from a common name (Abram, Moshko, Leiba) tells almost nothing |
| **Proximity** (one yard, neighbouring numbers, one address in a directory) | revision, directories, census | ⚪-🟡 | not kinship in itself |
| **One's name = another's patronymic + neighbouring shtetls + same occupation** | summary table | 🟡 (a hypothesis for searching) | too little for legal purposes |
| **DNA match** with descendants of another family | `dna-matches-endogamy` | contribution; be careful with endogamy | a shared segment does not say through which line |

## Signals that do not prove kinship

- One surname + one region, if the surname is a **place name** ("-sky" from the name of a place) or an **occupation**: such surnames arose independently. The bearer of a place-name surname often lived **not** in the place of that name.
- A match of the surname's form with the local form: families deliberately changed one letter to set themselves apart, or merged with the local spelling.
- The area of a surname from documents of the early 20th century. Look at the earliest lists with surnames (1805-1816; Jews usually have no surnames in the 1795 lists).
- A family legend of kinship with a famous namesake.
- A match in someone else's online tree (one tree copied into several is one piece of evidence).

## Contradictions: what to do

- **Age differs by 2-5 years**: usual; compare the intervals between the children and the birth order.
- **Age differs by 10+ years** or the order of children is different: more likely different people; look for a third document.
- **A person is listed in two places at once**: registration and residence; check whether there is a transfer file.
- **Namesakes "uncle and nephew"**: compare the fathers and the years; do not merge by name and patronymic.

## How to write down the rationale for a link

For each link at level 🟡 write a short text: which signals, from which documents, why they are independent, what speaks against it, which alternatives were considered and why they were dropped, what is missing for 🟢. Without such a text the link stays ⚪.
