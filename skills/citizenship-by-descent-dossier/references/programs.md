# Citizenship-by-descent programmes: genealogical requirements

**Currency: checked against official pages and law texts on 07.10.2026.**

> ⚠️ **Not legal advice.** This lists which *genealogical* evidence is usually needed. Laws change. Before filing, the user checks the conditions on the official site of the authority or consulate. The note **"not verified against the page"** means that the primary source did not open and the information was taken from search results — tell the user this.

## By country — what to prove

### Germany
- **Documents for every generation** (BVA leaflet, *Merkblatt*): birth certificates or Abstammungsurkunden, marriage certificates, family books along the ascending line up to the persecuted person; the form Anlage_AV for each generation older than the parents; documents on change of name, divorce, adoption.
- **Status:** proofs of citizenship or residence (Meldebestätigungen, address books, Meldekarten of cities) and of persecution (an entry of religion in the metrical record, documents of internment, expulsion, compensation files).
- **Form:** originals or officially/notarially certified copies on both sides; apostille or legalization (exceptions — the EU, Switzerland, multilingual extracts of a number of countries, including Moldova); translation only by a sworn translator.
- **There are no fees**; under German law it is not necessary to renounce the previous citizenship. If a relative has already gone through the procedure, the BVA (Federal Office of Administration) can pull his file by number.
- General transmission under § 4 StAG: now — from either citizen parent; in earlier versions (before 1975 mainly through the father) — **not verified against the page**.
- Source: BVA, Merkblatt § 15 StAG (as of March 2025); BVA, "Anspruchseinbürgerung Art. 116 Abs. 2 GG"; gesetze-im-internet.de.

### Austria
- **Applicant:** passport, birth certificate, documents on marriage and change of name, certificate of no criminal record not older than 8 months.
- **Line:** birth and marriage certificates of all links, establishment of paternity, adoption, all changes of name.
- **Persecution:** according to the Foreign Ministry FAQ an entry of religion in the metrical record is often enough; not needed if the ancestor is already recognized as a victim (Nationalfonds payments and the like). If there are no documents, the authorities search the archives themselves.
- German or English, otherwise a sworn court translation; apostille if the country is not exempt. Filing through the embassy; no federal fees.
- According to the Austrian Foreign Ministry FAQ: **Ukraine** may deprive of its own citizenship on acquiring an Austrian one.
- Source: oesterreich.gv.at (updated 29.01.2026); FAQ bmeia.gv.at; the text of § 58c checked at jusline.at. ris.bka.gv.at did not open.

### Poland
- This is a **confirmation**, not a new citizenship; the voivode decides, the consul forwards.
- Law of 20.01.1920: Art. 2 — those "settled" ("osiedlone") on the territory of Poland became citizens; Art. 5 — legitimate children received the citizenship of the **father**, illegitimate ones that of the mother; Art. 7 — a foreign woman who married a Pole became a citizen; Art. 11 — citizenship was lost on taking another citizenship or entering foreign service without permission. **One who left before 1920 and naturalized abroad most likely did not become a citizen.**
- Law of 31.03.1938: the minister could **deprive** of citizenship a person living abroad (including 5 years of stay and "loss of connection"); the decision is separate, not automatic; it extended to the wife and children under 18. Its application to a specific chain is for a lawyer.
- Documents: a form in Polish; records of the applicant, parents, grandparents; **Polish documents of the ancestors** (passport, identity card); changes of name; the full birth certificate. Copies are certified by the consul; translation — sworn or by the consul; outside the EU — apostille. Fee at the US consulate — 118 USD.
- **The Pole's Card (Karta Polaka) is not citizenship**: it requires Polish nationality of a parent, grandparent or two great-grandparents, basic Polish, a declaration.
- Source: gov.pl/web/usa; api.sejm.gov.pl (texts of 1920 and 1938); powroty.gov.pl.

### Israel
- The right belongs to a child and a **grandchild** of a Jew (including through the paternal line) and to their spouses, except those who converted to another religion; it does not matter whether the ancestor is alive.
- For people from the USSR the consul of "Nativ" looks at Soviet documents with the **"nationality"** column: birth certificates of parents and children, passports, military booklets, work books, archival certificates, pre-revolutionary rabbis' metrical records. When ordering a repeat certificate, ask to have the nationality of the parents stated.
- Apostille on documents issued after 31.12.1997 (since 01.08.2019), originals, certificate of no criminal record not older than 6 months — **not verified against the page** (the Nativ sites returned 403).
- Outside the former USSR (the Jewish Agency): legalized originals, birth certificate, marital status, no criminal record, a letter from a rabbi of a recognized community.
- Source: text of the 1970 amendment; jewishagency.org/aliyah.

### Romania
- Art. 11: those who lost citizenship "din motive neimputabile lor" [“for reasons not attributable to them”] and their descendants up to the 3rd degree; loyalty, age 18, reputation; application in Romanian, may be submitted through a consulate.
- Documents: records of all ancestors up to the former citizen **born on the territory of Romania before 28.06.1940** (birth, marriage, change of name, death, divorce) — originals and certified copies, apostille, translation into Romanian; passport; no criminal record — **not verified against the page** (cetatenie.just.ro, mae.ro did not open).
- **Legea 14/2025**: descendants prove Romanian at level **B1** (certificate of an accredited university or 3 years of study in Romanian); former citizens themselves and applicants aged 65 and over are exempt. The deadline for submitting the certificate was extended by OUG 16/2026 to **15.03.2027** (according to secondary publications of the text).
- The 1938 decree on revision of the citizenship of Jews — if the ancestor fell under it, the link "was a citizen" must be proved separately (**not verified against the page**).
- Metrical records of 1918–1940 from Bessarabia are Romanian civil-status records; in Moldova — Arhiva Națională or Agenția Servicii Publice (**not verified against the page**).

### Lithuania and Latvia
- **Lithuania:** citizenship before 15.06.1940 and descendants; dual — for those who left or were deported before 11.03.1990 and their descendants. Evidence: a pre-war Lithuanian passport, service in the army or civil service, metrical records with a reference to citizenship; indirect — study, work. Metrical records — LVIA (Lithuanian State Historical Archives). Filing — MIGRIS. The list of evidence — from search results (mfa.lt, migracija.lrv.lt — 403); the text of the law in the 2010 version.
- **Latvia:** a citizen on 17.06.1940. Evidence: metrical records, the 1935 census, tax and conscription files, a pre-war passport. Application in Latvian, translation and apostille (except EU/EEA, the United Kingdom, Switzerland). Dual citizenship — with the EU, NATO, EFTA, Australia, Brazil, New Zealand; for others (including citizens of Russia) renunciation may be required. Source: pmlp.gov.lv (updated 05.06.2025).

### Czechia, Hungary, Slovakia
- **Czechia § 31:** children and grandchildren of former citizens of Czechia or Czechoslovakia, if they are not citizens of Slovakia. Documents of birth and marriage of the applicant, parents, grandparents; a document on how the ancestor acquired and lost Czechoslovak citizenship. Fee 500 CZK, decision within 60 days. Source: archiv.mv.gov.cz (page of 18.05.2026).
- **Hungary § 4 (3):** spoken Hungarian; an unbroken chain; accepted are state and church metrical records, archival certificates with the exact reference, military and work books, school certificates; certified translation into Hungarian, legalization. Source: Kormányhivatal information note, 05.07.2023.
- **Slovakia:** since 01.04.2022 — children, grandchildren, great-grandchildren of Czechoslovak citizens born on the territory of Slovakia; from 15.07.2026 the residence condition is abolished, filing at the embassy, by post or electronically (according to the Slovak Interior Ministry as reported in a secondary publication). The list of documents — **not verified against the page**.

### Closed programmes
- **Spain 12/2015** — filing until 01.10.2019, supplementing documents until 01.09.2021. Ashkenazi origin gave no grounds under it.
- **Spain 20/2022, DA 8ª** — registration was accepted until 22.10.2025.
- **Portugal** — Lei Orgânica 1/2026 of 18.05.2026 abolished the special regime for Sephardim from 19.05.2026; the service page justica.gov.pt may still describe the old route — trust the report on the law. The same law extended the possibility to **great-grandchildren** of Portuguese citizens — this is not about Sephardim.
