# Territory → state by date: whose citizen was the ancestor?

**Currency: sources checked 2026-11-18.** The source keys [S1]… are listed at the end. ⚠️ = taken from a secondary source or not confirmed against the primary text — say so to the user.

> **Not legal advice.** A border change does not settle a person's citizenship by itself. What counted was the rule of the new state: domicile, "Heimatrecht", registration in a community, birth, the date of an option. Use this file to choose which law to check, not as the answer.

## How to use

1. For each link in the chain, take **three dates**: birth, the key date of the programme (for example 15.06.1940 for Lithuania, 28.06.1940 for Romania), and the date of leaving or naturalising abroad.
2. Find the place in the table below and note **which state held it on each date**.
3. Then check in `citizenship-changes.md` **by which rule** a resident of that place became (or did not become) a citizen. Residence alone is not citizenship: in many cases the link was **domicile or registration on a fixed date**.
4. Look for records of each period where the last column points. An empty answer from an archive of the wrong period is a false zero.

## Bessarabia

| Period | Northern and central Bessarabia (Khotyn, Soroca, Bălți, Chișinău, Orhei, Bender, Akkerman) | Southern Bessarabia (Cahul, Bolgrad, Izmail, Kiliya, Reni) |
|---|---|---|
| 1812–1856 | Russian Empire (Treaty of Bucharest 1812) [S24 ⚠️] | Russian Empire |
| 1856–1878 | Russian Empire | **Moldavia, from 1859/1862 the United Principalities, i.e. Romania** — ceded after the Crimean War, returned by the Treaty of Berlin 1878 [S24, 1911 Britannica "Bessarabia" ⚠️] |
| 1878–1918 | Russian Empire | Russian Empire again |
| 27.03/09.04.1918–28.06.1940 | Romania (date of union used by the Romanian 1924 law, Art. 56 p. 2) [S6]. International recognition: Treaty of Paris 28.10.1920 [S5], which never entered into force ⚠️ | Romania |
| 28.06.1940–VII.1941 | USSR (Moldavian SSR from 02.08.1940; Khotyn, Akkerman, Izmail counties to the Ukrainian SSR ⚠️) [S7] | USSR, Ukrainian SSR (Izmail oblast) ⚠️ |
| 1941–1944 | Romania (Governorate of Bessarabia) ⚠️ | Romania |
| 1944–1991 | USSR; border confirmed by the Paris peace treaty with Romania 10.02.1947 ⚠️ | USSR |
| since 1991 | Moldova; the north (Khotyn area) — Ukraine | Ukraine (Odesa oblast) |

**Became a citizen of…**
- 1918: Romanian citizens "without any formality" were those who on 27.03/09.04.1918 had **administrative domicile** in Bessarabia under the laws then in force and had not opted for another nationality [S6, Art. 56 p. 2]. One who had already left (for example to America in 1905) was not covered by this point.
- 1940: Soviet citizens retroactively from 28.06.1940 — those who on 07.11.1917 were subjects of the Russian Empire and lived in Bessarabia on 28.06.1940, and their children; persons who had acquired another foreign citizenship before 28.06.1940 were excluded [S7, decree 08.03.1941, Art. 1, 3].
- ⚠️ Southern Bessarabia 1856–1878: a person born there in those years was born a subject of Moldavia/Romania. How the 1878 transfer treated them (automatic Russian subjecthood, option) — **not verified**; ask a lawyer before building a Romanian claim on these years.

**Records:** Russian period — metrical books and revisions: National Archives of the Republic of Moldova (Chișinău); for the Ukrainian part — State Archive of Odesa oblast and its Izmail branch, State Archive of Chernivtsi oblast (Khotyn) ⚠️ check the catalogue. Romanian period 1918–1940 — Romanian civil-status registers (stare civilă), see `ordering-and-apostille.md`. Southern Bessarabia 1856–1878 — Romanian/Moldavian civil-status records ⚠️ location not verified.

## Northern Bukovina and the Hertsa region

| Period | Northern Bukovina (Chernivtsi, Storozhynets, Vyzhnytsia, Kitsman, Zastavna) | Hertsa region |
|---|---|---|
| to 1774/1775 | Moldavia (Ottoman vassal) | Moldavia |
| 1775–1918 | **Austria** (Duchy of Bukovina) [S24 ⚠️] | **Moldavia → Romania** (Dorohoi county) [S24 ⚠️] |
| XI.1918–28.06.1940 | Romania; Austria renounced Bukovina in favour of Romania (Saint-Germain Art. 59) [S2]; Romanian law dates local membership (indigenat) at 18.11/01.12.1918 [S6, Art. 56 p. 1] | Romania |
| 28.06.1940–1941 | USSR, Ukrainian SSR [S7] | USSR (not named in the Soviet ultimatum) [S24 ⚠️] |
| 1941–1944 | Romania | Romania |
| since 1944 | USSR → Ukraine (Chernivtsi oblast) | USSR → Ukraine |

**Became a citizen of…**
- 1918–1920: residents of Bukovina with local membership (indigenat = Austrian *Heimatrecht*) on 18.11/01.12.1918 — Romanian [S6, Art. 56 p. 1]; under Saint-Germain the *pertinenza* (Heimatrecht) decided, not mere residence [S2, Art. 70]. A Galician Jew who lived in Chernivtsi but kept his Heimatrecht in a Galician commune obtained, by the treaty rule, the citizenship of the state that got **that** commune — Poland ⚠️ check the case.
- 1940: Soviet citizens from 28.06.1940 — all who lived in Northern Bukovina on that date, except foreigners and those evacuated to Romania after 28.06.1940 [S7, decree 08.03.1941, Art. 4].
- Hertsa: residents were Romanian citizens long before 1918 (Old Kingdom). Whether the 1941 decree's words "Northern Bukovina" covered Hertsa — ⚠️ not verified.

**Records:** Austrian period — State Archive of Chernivtsi oblast (Jewish and Christian metrical books), Romanian period — Romanian civil-status registers (partly in the same archive) ⚠️; Hertsa — Romanian civil-status records of Dorohoi county ⚠️.

## Brief notes

- **Crimea:** Russian Empire from 1783; RSFSR; transferred to the Ukrainian SSR in 1954; Ukraine from 1991; occupied and annexed by Russia in 2014, not internationally recognised ⚠️ [S24]. There is no interwar foreign citizenship here: European citizenship-based programmes do not apply; only ethnic routes (for example German *Spätaussiedler*) — see `ethnic-programs.md`. Archive access in Crimea is difficult.
- **Finland:** Swedish to 1809, then an autonomous Grand Duchy of the Russian Empire **with its own Finnish citizenship**; independent 06.12.1917 ⚠️ [S24]. A Russian subject living in Helsinki was not automatically a Finnish citizen.
- **Transcaucasia:** Russian Empire (Georgia from 1801, Erivan 1828) ⚠️; independent Georgia, Armenia, Azerbaijan from V.1918; Sovietised 1920–1921; Kars and Ardahan to Turkey by the Treaty of Kars 13.10.1921 ⚠️ [S24]. No EU programme is based on these states; check Armenian and Georgian rules for descendants separately.

## Traps

- **Same name, different place.** Two villages with the same name in different counties; "Podolia" in Russia and in Austrian Galicia. Check uyezd/county and volost, not the name.
- **Residence ≠ registration.** A Jew could live for decades in one town and be registered (*pripisan*) in another. Polish, Romanian and treaty rules asked about **registration, domicile or Heimatrecht**, not where the person lived.
- **Date of transfer ≠ date of citizenship.** Treaties came into force later than the de facto change (Versailles 10.01.1920, Saint-Germain 16.07.1920 ⚠️, Riga — exchange of ratifications 45 days after signing [S1, Art. XXVI]).
- **Occupation years 1941–1944** did not create a new citizenship for most residents and do not count for programmes; records of these years may be in German or Romanian archives.
- **Laws change.** These are historical rules; the programme rules of today — `programs.md`, checked on the official page on the day of filing.
