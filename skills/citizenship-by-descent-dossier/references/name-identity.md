# One person — different names: how to reconcile them and with what to confirm

## Why names diverge

- **Double Jewish names** (Khaim-Iosl, Meishe-Leiba) — one name; the scribe could have written down half.
- **Everyday and full forms:** Berko = Dov-Ber; Srul = Israel; Moshko, Movsha = Moshe; Leizer, Leizor = Eliezer.
- **Patronymic in a pre-revolutionary record:** «Мошковичъ», «Мошковъ сынъ», «Мошков» [Moshkovich, Moshko's son, Moshkov] — one and the same.
- **Soviet change of names** (Amosova and Nikolaeva, Podolia and Bukovina, 2010): often through a diminutive form — Moishe → Misha → Mikhail. Stable pairs:

| Traditional | Soviet |
|---|---|
| Moishe, Movsha | Mikhail |
| Khaim | Efim |
| Borukh, Berko | Boris |
| Abram | Arkady |
| Srul | Alexander, Israel |
| Gersh Shlyumovich | Grigory Solomonovich |
| Rakhil | Raisa |
| Khana | Anna |
| Khaya | Klara |

- ⚠️ **Children's patronymics were changed separately**: the father is "Berko", the children are «Борисовна» [Borisovna]. Registrars did this both by mistake and of their own will.
- ⚠️ **A father's double name — different patronymics for full brothers and sisters**: some after one half of the name, others after the other («Абрамович» and «Александрович», «Гершевна» and «Григорьевна» [Abramovich and Aleksandrovich, Gershevna and Grigorievna] among children of one father). In the correspondence sheet keep both halves of the father's name.
- **ZAGS around 1928–1933** did not require documents from the parents when registering a child: the mother's name in the record is sometimes new, "Russian". The discrepancy was later closed by a "same person" certificate (*spravka «он же»*) or by a court (often for the sake of an inheritance) — look for such documents as a bridge.
- **Women:** the surname changes on marriage — a marriage record is needed at each such link.
- **Transliteration:** one Cyrillic surname gives many Latin ones (Sz-/Sch-/Sh-, -berg/-berh), and the Polish, Romanian, German record of an ancestor has its own form.
- **Official change of name** after the 1924 decree — notices in local official newspapers (for example the oblast executive committees' "Vestniks"); look in the newspaper of the city where the person lived at the time of the change.

## How to compile a correspondence sheet

For each person in the chain:

| Document (archival ref.) | Given name | Patronymic | Surname | Date of birth / age | Place | Parents / spouse |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| metrical record 1898 | Movsha | Shlemov | … | III.1898 | shtetl … | Shlema and Reizya |
| marriage cert. 1925 | Mikhail | Solomonovich | … | 1898 | … | — |
| passport 1950s | Mikhail | Solomonovich | … | 1898 | … | — |

Rows match when **at least two points besides the name** coincide (year and place of birth, names of parents, spouse, address). Note in the table which document closes the transition "Movsha → Mikhail". If there is no such document, it is an open item.

## How to close a discrepancy (from simple to complex)

1. **A document where both forms stand side by side:** a marriage record, a child's birth certificate, a questionnaire, an archival "same person" certificate. The Soviet "List of Medical Doctors of the USSR as of 1 January 1924" (1925; a continuation of the "Russian Medical List") and similar directories often give both forms — this is a hint, but the authority needs a certificate.
2. **An archival certificate of identity** — the archive writes that in the documents the person is recorded in this way and that way. Ask the archive to state both forms directly.
3. **Correction or supplement of the civil record** in ZAGS/DRATS, if the error is in the record itself.
4. **Court establishment of the fact** of kinship or of the ownership of a document — when there are no other ways. The decision on such a step is made by a lawyer.
5. **An explanatory note to the package** with the correspondence sheet — it does not replace a document but makes checking easier.

## Translations

- The applicant's name — as in the foreign passport, the same in all translations.
- For ancestors ask the translator to give the original spelling (in Cyrillic, in Hebrew) next to the transliteration.
- Check that the translator has not "normalized" the name (Movsha → Moses) without a note: this creates a new discrepancy.
