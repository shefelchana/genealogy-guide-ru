# When the rules changed: acquisition, option, loss

**Currency: sources checked 2026-11-18.** Source keys [S…] — at the end (same numbering as `territory-timeline.md`). ⚠️ = secondary or not confirmed against the primary text.

> **Not legal advice.** Below are historical rules that decide whether the ancestor **was** a citizen at the key date and whether he **passed** the citizenship on. How today's authority applies them to a particular family is for the authority, the consulate or a lawyer. Laws and their interpretation change.

## The question for every link

For each ancestor in the chain ask four things, in this order:
1. **Did he acquire** the citizenship (automatically, by option, by naturalisation) — on what date?
2. **Did he lose it** before the birth of the next link (naturalisation abroad, foreign service, years abroad, deprivation, marriage for women)?
3. **Did it pass to the child** (through the father only? the mother? only legitimate children?) under the law **in force at the child's birth**?
4. Does today's programme **repair** that loss (Germany § 15 StAG, § 5 StAG, Art. 116(2) GG; Poland restoration) — or does it not?

## 1. Automatic citizenship and option on border changes

| Instrument | Rule | Wife and children | For a descendant today |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Versailles 28.06.1919**, Poland | German nationals habitually resident in territory that became Polish → Polish ipso facto; settled after 01.01.1908 → only with a Polish authorisation; option for Germany within 2 years; Poles who were German nationals in Germany or abroad could opt for Poland [S4, Art. 91] | the husband's option covers the wife, the parents' — children under 18 [S4, Art. 91] | Polish confirmation: find whether the ancestor **opted for Germany** (then not Polish) |
| **Versailles**, Danzig | German nationals ordinarily resident → Danzig nationals; option for Germany within 2 years, then move within 12 months [S4, Art. 105–106] | as above | Danzig nationality is not Polish citizenship; Polish citizenship after 1945 — decree 22.10.1947 [S13] ⚠️ |
| **Saint-Germain 10.09.1919** (Austria) and **Trianon 04.06.1920** (Hungary) | Whoever had **pertinenza / Heimatrecht** in a commune of the former Monarchy obtained the citizenship of the state that received that commune, ipso facto [S2, Art. 70; S3, Art. 61]. Heimatrecht acquired after 01.01.1910 in territory going to Czechoslovakia or the Kingdom SHS — only with that state's permit [S2, Art. 76; S3, Art. 62]. Option for the state of the earlier Heimatrecht within 1 year [S2, Art. 78; S3, Art. 63]; option by "race and language" within 6 months [S2, Art. 80; S3, Art. 64] | the married woman follows the husband, children under 18 — the parents [S2, Art. 82; S3, Art. 66] | Look for the **Heimatschein** or the commune's register: residence in Vienna or Chernivtsi says nothing if Heimatrecht was in Galicia. The successor states also settled Heimatrecht disputes in the Rome convention 06.04.1922 [S13] ⚠️ content not read |
| **Riga 18.03.1921** | Persons ≥18 in Poland at ratification who on 01.08.1914 were Russian subjects and were registered in the population books of the former Kingdom of Poland, or in a commune or estate organisation of the lands that became Polish, could opt for **Russia or Ukraine**. Such persons who were in Russia/Ukraine became Polish **only by option**; also those descending from participants of the 1830–1865 uprisings or from residents of the old Commonwealth (within 3 generations) who showed attachment to Polish nationality. Option also open to those living in third countries if not citizens there. Deadline — 1 year from ratification (15 months in the Caucasus and Asian Russia); the option did not itself give citizenship until the consul's decision [S1, Art. VI.1–6] | the husband's choice covers the wife and children under 18 unless the spouses agree otherwise; if they disagree the wife chooses for herself and the children she raises [S1, Art. VI.4] | A person from Podolia or Kyiv with Polish registration who did **not** opt stayed Soviet. Polish option files — Polish consular records ⚠️ location not verified; Polish implementing regulation 11.06.1921 (Dz.U. 1921 nr 59 poz. 375) [S13] |
| **Polish law 20.01.1920** | Citizen: anyone "settled" — in the population books of the former Kingdom of Poland, Heimatrecht in a former Austrian/Hungarian commune, Prussian-area residents since before 01.01.1908, registered in a commune or estate organisation of former Russian lands now Polish; or born in Poland if not a citizen of another state; or by treaty [S9, Art. 2]. Persons of Polish origin with another citizenship — on return and declaration [S9, Art. 3] | legitimate children — the father's citizenship, illegitimate — the mother's [S9, Art. 5] | The key document is **registration**, not birth: a book of permanent population (*księga ludności stałej*), a commune list |
| **Bessarabia treaty 28.10.1920** | Russian nationals settled in Bessarabia → Romanian ipso facto; born there to domiciled parents → Romanian, could renounce within 2 years; option for another nationality within 2 years [S5, Art. 4–6] | the husband's option/declaration covers the wife, the parents' — children under 18 [S5, Art. 5–6] | ⚠️ The treaty **never entered into force**; Romania applied its own 1924 law |
| **Romanian law 724/1924**, Art. 56 | Romanian "without any formality": residents of Bukovina, Transylvania, Banat etc. with indigenat on 18.11/01.12.1918; residents of Bessarabia with **administrative domicile** on 27.03/09.04.1918; persons born there to domiciled parents; ethnic Romanians from the former Russian Empire who opted for Romania — unless they opted for another nationality before 24.02.1924 [S6] | ⚠️ wife-follows-husband rule of 1924 not read in the text | Thousands of Bessarabian Jews were left without recognised citizenship by the 1924 commissions ⚠️ [S23]; for Romania Art. 11 of Law 21/1991 you must prove the ancestor **was** a citizen |

## 2. Soviet citizenship after the annexations of 1939–1940

| Territory | Act | Who became Soviet | For a descendant today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Western Ukraine, western Belarus | decree 29.11.1939 with the law on citizenship 19.08.1938 | citizens of those regions present there at their incorporation (1–2.11.1939) [S8 ⚠️] | Polish confirmation: Soviet citizenship imposed in 1939 is **not** a voluntary acquisition of a foreign citizenship under Art. 11 of the 1920 law — but how the voivode treats it, and later Soviet passports, is for a lawyer ⚠️ |
| Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia | decree 07.09.1940 | citizens of the three SSRs from the day of their admission to the USSR; those abroad had to register by 01.11.1940 [S7] | Lithuania and Latvia treat the occupation as illegal; for them the pre-war status counts [S20, S21] |
| Bessarabia | decree 08.03.1941 | former Russian subjects (on 07.11.1917) living in Bessarabia on 28.06.1940, and their children, regardless of Romanian citizenship — "restored" from 28.06.1940; excluded those with another foreign citizenship before 28.06.1940 [S7, Art. 1, 3] | Romania Art. 11: loss of Romanian citizenship "for reasons not attributable to them" — the 1940 annexation is the typical case [see `programs.md`] |
| Northern Bukovina | the same decree | all residents on 28.06.1940, except foreigners and those evacuated to Romania [S7, Art. 4] | as above |
| Poles and Jews who were Polish citizens before 17.09.1939 | Polish–Soviet agreements 1944–1945 and 1957 on repatriation and "change of Soviet citizenship" ⚠️ [S13, 1957 agreement title] | those who left for Poland under them | Karta Polaka excludes those who repatriated (or whose ascendants repatriated) **from** Poland to the USSR under the 1944–1957 agreements [S12, Art. 2.1(4)] |

The Soviet citizenship law of 19.08.1938: citizens are all who were Russian subjects on 07.11.1917 and did not lose Soviet citizenship; marriage does not change citizenship; children under 14 follow the parents' change, from 14 to 18 — only with their consent [S7, law 19.08.1938, Art. 2, 5, 6].

## 3. Loss and deprivation

| Rule | Text | For a descendant today |
|---|---|---|
| **Poland, law 20.01.1920, Art. 11** | loss by acquiring a foreign citizenship, or by taking public office or military service abroad without consent; those liable to active service needed the Minister's permission to naturalise abroad, otherwise Poland kept treating them as citizens [S9, Art. 11]; loss extends to the wife and children under 18 unless the decision says otherwise [S9, Art. 13] | The commonest break: the ancestor **naturalised in the USA or elsewhere before the next link was born**. Check the date of naturalisation against the child's birth. Restoration under Art. 38 of the 2009 Act is for the person who lost citizenship (on application) [S11, Art. 38] |
| **Poland, law 31.03.1938** | a Polish citizen abroad **may be deprived** if he acted against the state, or stayed abroad continuously at least 5 years after the state arose and "lost connection" with it, or did not return when summoned; decision of the Interior Minister, appealable; extends to the wife and children under 18 abroad unless excluded [S10, Art. 1–3] | Not automatic: there must be an **individual decision**. Absence of one in the archive is not proof; ask the voivode or a lawyer how it is checked. Repealed 19.01.1951 [S13 record] |
| **Romania, decree-law 169/1938** (Goga government, 21 or 22.01.1938 — sources differ ⚠️) | revision of the citizenship of Jews; about a third of those revised were struck off the registers ⚠️ [S23] | If the ancestor was struck off in 1938–1940, the link "was a Romanian citizen until 28.06.1940" needs separate proof; the decree was later repealed ⚠️ [S23] |
| **Germany, 11th Decree to the Reich Citizenship Law, 25.11.1941** | a Jew lost German citizenship if his habitual residence was abroad when the decree came into force, or on moving abroad later [S17, quoting § 2] | The Federal Constitutional Court (14.02.1968, BVerfGE 23, 98) held the decree **void from the start**; persecuted persons did not lose citizenship unless they showed they did not want it [S17]. Descendants apply under Art. 116(2) GG [S14] |
| **Germany, law 01.06.1870, Art. 13, 21** | loss after **10 years' uninterrupted stay abroad**, interrupted by registration (*Matrikel*) at a consulate; extended to the wife and minor children with him [S16] | An emigrant who left before 1904 and did not register may have lost German citizenship before the 1913 law ⚠️ — check the dates of departure and of the child's birth |
| **Germany, RuStAG 22.07.1913 (from 01.01.1914), § 17, § 25** | loss by release, by acquiring a foreign citizenship on application while having no residence in Germany (unless a retention permit was granted), by not fulfilling military duty, for a German woman — by marriage to a foreigner [S15] | § 15 StAG and § 5 StAG close some of these losses for persecution or for marriage; plain naturalisation abroad before 1933 is not closed by them [S14] |
| **Hungary, Act L of 1879, §§ 31–32** | loss after **10 years' uninterrupted absence** without a government mandate and without registering at a consulate; extended to the wife and minor children with him [S19]; Act XIII of 1939: loss by naturalisation abroad, 10-year rule restated [S19] | For Hungary § 4(3) it is enough that an ancestor **was** a Hungarian citizen or that Hungarian origin is made probable [S19, LV/1993 § 4(3)] — loss later does not bar this route |
| **USSR, decree 15.12.1921 and resolution 27.05.1933** | deprivation of certain émigrés; former Russian subjects who left before 25.10.1917 and took a foreign citizenship were not Soviet [S7, titles listed in the 1939 and 1941 decrees] | matters for the Israeli "nationality" chain only indirectly; for EU programmes the question is the pre-war state's citizenship |

## 4. The wife followed the husband

| State | Rule | Until |
|---|---|---|
| Germany | the foreign wife of a German became German [S15, § 6]; a German woman lost citizenship by marrying a foreigner [S15, § 17; S16, Art. 13 p. 5] | losses before **01.04.1953**; children of such mothers and their descendants may acquire German citizenship **by declaration** (§ 5 StAG) — window of 10 years from 20.08.2021 [S14; S18] |
| Hungary | the foreign wife of a Hungarian became Hungarian; a Hungarian woman marrying a non-Hungarian lost citizenship [S19, 1879 §§ 5, 34] | ⚠️ change date not checked |
| Poland | the foreign wife of a Pole became Polish [S9, Art. 7]; a Polish woman marrying a foreigner lost it and could regain it after the marriage ended [S9, Art. 10]; acquisition and loss by the husband extend to the wife [S9, Art. 13; S10, Art. 3] | 1920 law in force until 19.01.1951 [S13] |
| Latvia | a woman lost Latvian nationality under s. 7 of the law of 23.08.1919; today she and her descendants can register [S20, s. 2(1)4] | — |
| USSR | marriage does not change citizenship [S7, law 19.08.1938, Art. 5] | — |
| USA | Expatriation Act 1907: an American woman lost US citizenship by marrying a foreigner; Cable Act 22.09.1922 ended this for most cases ⚠️ [S25] | relevant when an emigrant line marries back |
| Treaties | the wife's status followed the husband's in the option clauses [S2, Art. 82; S3, Art. 66; S4, Art. 91, 106; S5, Art. 5–6]; Riga let the wife choose separately if the spouses disagreed [S1, Art. VI.4] | — |

**What this means for the chain:** on the maternal line the woman's citizenship at the child's birth is usually **her husband's**, not her father's. An invented example: a woman born Polish who married a foreign citizen in 1925 normally lost Polish citizenship (Art. 10 of the 1920 law presupposes this loss), and her legitimate children took the father's citizenship [S9, Art. 5, 10]. Germany repairs this only through § 5 StAG and only within the window [S14; S18].

## 5. Emigration before the state existed

- A person who left the Russian Empire **before 1918–1920** and settled abroad was, as a rule, **never** a citizen of Poland, Lithuania or Latvia: these states defined their first citizens by registration, domicile or residence on dates after their foundation [S9, Art. 2; S6, Art. 56; S20].
- Exceptions to check: **option** for those registered in Poland but living in Russia or a third country [S1, Art. VI.2–3]; Poles who were German nationals abroad [S4, Art. 91]; Polish origin + return + declaration [S9, Art. 3].
- Romania: Bessarabians count only with administrative domicile on 27.03/09.04.1918 [S6, Art. 56 p. 2]; the unratified 1920 treaty's rule for those born there of domiciled parents [S5, Art. 6] ⚠️ did not become law.
- Today's descent routes (Hungary, Lithuania for persons of Lithuanian descent, Latvia for Latvians/Livs, Karta Polaka) do **not** require the ancestor to have held the interwar citizenship — see `ethnic-programs.md`.

## 6. Naturalisation abroad and foreign military service

- **Poland 1920:** foreign naturalisation or foreign public/military service without consent → loss; extended to the wife and children under 18 [S9, Art. 11, 13].
- **Germany 1913:** naturalisation abroad on application **while having no residence in Germany** → loss, unless a retention permit was granted [S15, § 25].
- **Germany 1870:** 10 years abroad without consular registration → loss [S16, Art. 21].
- **Hungary 1879/1939:** 10 years abroad without registration; from 1939 also naturalisation abroad [S19].
- **Lithuania 2010:** "service of another state" includes military service [S21, Art. 2(5)] ⚠️ consequences in the current text not checked.
- **Check in documents:** US naturalisation papers (Declaration of Intention, Petition, Certificate) give the exact date; compare it with the birth date of the next link. In the US before 22.09.1922 a wife usually acquired citizenship with her husband, and minor children derived it ⚠️ [S25] — so one naturalisation could cut off the whole family.
