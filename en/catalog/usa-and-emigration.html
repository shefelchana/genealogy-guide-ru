USA and emigration: sources

What this section covers: where to look for the documents of emigrants — American databases, ports of arrival, departure lists of European ports, sources for Canada, Argentina, Palestine, South Africa and Australia. The step-by-step algorithm "from American documents to the shtetl" and the traps are in Emigration.

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified] and others) are explained in the Glossary. Interface-language notes are in README.md. Prices and tariffs change.

Missouri death certificates (s1.sos.mo.gov/records/archives/archivesmvc/deathcertificates)

  • Certificates with scans; free, no registration. Search: surname "Starts with" + year.
  • The scan is a PDF in an old format; it may look blank on screen — open it with another program. For immigrants the parents' boxes often say "Unknown".

American FamilySearch collections and related databases

  • Passenger lists of New York and Philadelphia, naturalization index cards, "Pennsylvania Civil Marriages 1677–1950" (license applications with the parents' names), censuses of 1930/1940/1950, draft cards of two wars, SSDI. In addition — the USA database on JewishGen and the Ellis Island Foundation index.
  • Techniques: 20 or more variants of a surname in "exact" and "fuzzy" modes; search by the mother's maiden name and place of birth; manifests have "going to" — a direct lead; verify identity from several documents.
  • Traps: a look-alike surname in the index; a misread year. Read the frame, not the index.
  • An unused lead: the books of immigrant banks in Philadelphia (Philadelphia Jewish Archives, Temple University) give, by the ticket order number, a form with the sender's address and the recipient's shtetl.

2. Departure lists and ports of Europe

  • Hamburg — 1850–1934 (except 1915–1919). About 90% of emigrants from Eastern Europe went through Hamburg. The lists are direct and indirect (with a change of ship). They contain each emigrant's home town and relatives on the same ship. In German. Ancestry — paid, full index; FamilySearch — images in FamilySearch centers, partial index 1877–1914 [checked: FS Wiki]. Morse has Hamburg forms.
  • Bremen — 738,102 records, mostly 1920–1939, plus 1907/08 and 1913/14. Old lists were destroyed from 1875, the rest burned in World War II. Free (passagierlisten.de) [checked].
  • Antwerp — the archive of the Red Star Line, including passenger lists, is lost [checked]. A substitute — police files on foreigners: FelixArchief (1840–1930, all digitized [from search snippet]; the index on FamilySearch has errors — check against the image [checked]) and the national files in the State Archives in Brussels (1839 — September 1944; open after 100 years; after World War I usually with a photo) [checked].
  • Rotterdam — Holland-America Line lists in the city archive; the 1900–1920 index is free [from search snippet].
  • Libau (Liepāja) — Russian American Line 1906–1914, more than 500,000 passengers in all; most of the records are lost [checked]. Search by the American arrival.
  • Liverpool — departure registers 1890–1960 in the UK National Archives, without an index [checked].
