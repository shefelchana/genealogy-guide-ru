# University and foreign archives

What this section covers: archives where a person left a trace "on another occasion" — university study, life abroad, immigration to Palestine. Such forms often name the father directly, with his occupation and place of residence. Markers are in [README.md](README.md).

Advice: think about where the person **studied, served, emigrated** — that is where the forms with parents are.

---

## Universities

### Tartu (Dorpat, Yuryev): Album Academicum, dspace.ut.ee, ais.ra.ee
- **What is there:** students of the Yuryev (Dorpat) University, where people from Jewish families of the southern guberniyas also studied. The printed edition *Album Academicum Universitatis Tartuensis 1889–1918* (3 volumes, Tartu, 1986–1988) with an index, and the earlier Hasselblatt–Otto edition of 1889 — for those admitted **before** 1889 (up to matriculation no. 14331).
- **The "wrong volume" trap:** in one study Yuryev was at first "ruled out" after the person was not found in the main part of the 1889–1918 volume; he was found in the appendix of volume III and in the 1889 edition — he enrolled at the turn of 1888/89. **Choose the volume and appendix by the year of admission.**
- **The "spelling" trap:** the surname is set as *Wainberg*, while the recognized text gives "Weinberg"; in the index the blocks "Wai-" and "Wei-" are on different pages; in the Estonian catalog "Vainbarg" occurs.
- **What the entry gives:** matriculation number, year and place of birth, the father's estate (at the time of admission), the gymnasium, the faculty, the reference of the personal file.
- **The chain:** matriculation number → file reference `EAA.402.<inventory>.<number>` → check in ais.ra.ee (the archival system of Estonia) → the mark "Digiteeritud → SAAGA" means the file is digitized and available free of charge. The student file holds a metrical certificate (the father's name) and a registration certificate (*pripiska*).
- **Note:** files of those admitted before 1889 (inventory 2) were, by a check in one study, **not digitized**. The OCR of the first volume is on dspace.ut.ee.
- **A related source:** "Lichnyi sostav Imperatorskogo Yuryevskogo universiteta" (Personnel of the Imperial Yuryev University) of 1893 (Google Books) — student number, name, patronymic. See [directories-and-books.md](directories-and-books.md).

### University of Vienna, PHAIDRA (phaidra.univie.ac.at)
- **What is there:** scans of student forms ("Nationale der Studierenden") by faculty and semester, in alphabetical order: name, age, **place of birth, father and his occupation**. Free, no registration; works from abroad too.
- **Experience:** the form of a medical student of 1889 named the father directly: "Vater: Berco, Kaufmann, Ismail" — the merchant Berko from Izmail.
- **Techniques:** the collections for regular students and for auditors are different — check both; look for the name in its German spelling; there is one form per page, and it is convenient to crop the name line as a narrow strip and enlarge it. Not all semesters are posted (as of 06.10.2026 the forms of regular students for the winter semester 1888/89 were not yet there).
- **Limitation:** the list of documents in the collection is served only to an ordinary browser.
- **For advanced users:** every form has an IIIF manifest; images are requested by cropping the needed region.

### Moscow University
- Inventories of personal files of students 1872–1917 — on archive.org ([directories-and-books.md](directories-and-books.md)). The universities of Kyiv, Kazan and Warsaw are not in that collection.

## Belgium and France: the experience of searching for an emigrant

- **Antwerp.** In the Antwerp police immigration file (a film on FamilySearch, 1893–1894) a man was found who was born in Izmail around 1828, with a wife and daughter; the daughter's birth record gives his place of birth. The link to the family being sought remained a hypothesis.
- The "own bulletin" with the parents' names is kept in the archive of the security service (Sûreté publique) in Brussels — it is not online. A search in the Belgian archival system AGATHA gave 0.
- **Paris.** Marriage records of 1883–1892 across the 20 arrondissements were reviewed without result: the family note "marriage 1889 in Paris" turned out to be false.
- Sources for Belgium, France and other countries — [europe-by-country.md](europe-by-country.md).

## Israel

- **Israel State Archives** (www.archives.gov.il — the address without "www" does not open, checked 2026-10-07): immigration and naturalization under the British Mandate — over 60,000 files of 1937–1947, about a dozen pages each: place and date of birth, former citizenship, photo, family, date of arrival. Everything is scanned; the index on the site is partial, the full one is in MyHeritage (paid) [page: MyHeritage Wiki]. The catalog is mostly in Hebrew [from search snippet]. Rabbinical courts 1919–2006 (marriages and divorces).
- **Central Zionist Archives** — a paid genealogical service.
- **CAHJP** (Central Archives for the History of the Jewish People, now at the National Library of Israel): archives of hundreds of communities; since the early 1990s it surveyed and microfilmed the holdings of archives of the former USSR; a database of inventories of archives of Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Moldova, the Baltics; JCA records (including censuses of the colonies in Argentina). Some of the copied metrical books are indexed in JewishGen databases. The site did not open (403) [from search results].
- **International Institute for Jewish Genealogy** (IIJG, Paul Jacobi Center, Jerusalem): founded in 2004 [from search results; the site did not open].
- **Douglas E. Goldman Jewish Genealogy Center** (ANU Museum, Tel Aviv): accepts family trees (GEDCOM); the database holds the trees of over 5 million people [from search results].
- **IGRA**: All Israel Database, name changes from the Palestine Gazette — [indexed-databases.md](indexed-databases.md), [usa-and-emigration.md](usa-and-emigration.md).
- **Yad Vashem** — [indexed-databases.md](indexed-databases.md).
