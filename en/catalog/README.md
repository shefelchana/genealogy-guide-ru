# Site catalog: what to look for where, and how

What this section covers: a reference to sites for tracing ancestors from the Russian Empire and the USSR (above all Jewish families) — what is on each site, whether registration is needed, where the CAPTCHAs are and what pace is safe, working techniques and traps. Below is a "question → where to go" cheat sheet and the general rules.

**Interface language.** Where a site entry states or makes obvious the language of the interface, it is shown in parentheses — "(English interface)", "(in Russian)", "(Ukrainian only)"; where there is no such note, the language was not recorded, so check on the site. Don't read Russian? See [For English speakers](../guide/0-start/08-for-english-speakers.md).

**Where the information comes from.** From the experience of one real research project (20 September – 8 October 2026) and from reading outside guides and site pages. Sites change fast: they close, introduce CAPTCHAs, move their databases. Where it matters, a check date is given: "from experience on 03.10.2026" means that is how it was at that time.

**Reliability markers** in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], (LRF)) are explained in the [Glossary](../guide/0-start/06-glossary.md).

Labels in the cards: **Free / Paid** · **Registration: yes / no** · **Pace** — how often you can hit the site. The **"For advanced users"** line holds technical tricks (request addresses and the like); a beginner can skip it.

## Rules for working with sites

The general search rules (an index is not a document; "not found" only with stated coverage; a second independent identifier; write it down at once) are in the [Quick start](../guide/0-start/01-quick-start.md) and [Standard of proof](../guide/3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md). Here are the rules that concern sites specifically:

1. **Old text is misread by the software.** In scanned books, search **without the first letter**: the recognition program often drops or replaces it (Вайнбергъ → "Байнбергъ", "айнбергь"). Try all spellings — [Names, surnames, spellings](../guide/2-reference/03-names-spellings.md).
2. **Test the search on a control record** — a word that is certainly in this volume. A zero together with site errors (CAPTCHA, 403/429) is a technical zero, not a result.
3. **A CAPTCHA is passed by a human, and only by a human.** At the first "I am not a robot" check, stop, pass it yourself and continue more slowly. Do not get around it.
4. **Pace matters more than speed.** Almost everywhere what stops you is not a ban but too-frequent requests. One request every few seconds, a pause at the first failure. The figures are in [Access, CAPTCHAs, pace](../guide/2-reference/19-access-captchas-pace.md).
5. **A VPN and a foreign address can break access.** From abroad some Russian government sites do not open; the opposite happens too. Do not get around the blocks.
6. **Passwords, cookies and keys — to no one and nowhere.** For a helper (a person or a program) it is enough that you yourself have logged in to the account in your own browser.
7. **Don't pay for what is free.** Paid "genealogical searches" often only name the archive and the file. Almost all Ukrainian metrical books, revisions and inventories are openly available.
8. **Access to documents:** in Russia and Ukraine documents with personal data are closed for 75 years (in Ukraine the files of the repressive organs are open under the 2015 law); pre-revolutionary metrical books, revision lists, censuses and family lists are available to anyone without proof of kinship.
