# Site catalog: what to look for where, and how

What this section covers: a reference to sites for tracing ancestors from the Russian Empire and the USSR (above all Jewish families) — what is on each site, whether registration is needed, where the CAPTCHAs are and what pace is safe, working techniques and traps. Below is a "question → where to go" cheat sheet and the general rules.

**Where the information comes from.** From the experience of one real research project (20 September – 7 October 2026) and from reading outside guides and site pages. Sites change fast: they close, introduce CAPTCHAs, move their databases. Where it matters, a check date is given: "from experience on 03.10.2026" means that is how it was at that time.

**Reliability markers:**
- **[checked]** or **[checked]** — the page was read;
- **[from search snippet]** or **[from search snippet]** — information from search results or someone else's overview, the page was not opened; verify before any important decision;
- "not verified on the page" — means exactly that;
- **(LRF)** — a figure from the Lost Russian Family consolidated list, not verified on the site itself.

Labels in the cards: **Free / Paid** · **Registration: yes / no** · **Pace** — how often you can hit the site. The **"For advanced users"** line holds technical tricks (request addresses and the like); a beginner can skip it.

## Ten rules for all sites

1. **A surname is spelled in different ways, and old text is misrecognized.** Try every spelling. In scanned books search **without the first letter**: the recognition software often "eats" or substitutes it (Вайнбергъ → "Байнбергъ", "айнбергь"). Details — [Names, surnames, spellings](../guide/2-reference/03-names-spellings.md).
2. **An index is a pointer, not a document.** Check every database entry against the scan. Indexes make mistakes in surnames, mix up fields ("daughter of Duvid" turned out to be the groom's father), and take a place for a surname ("Рашковскій мещ." — "meshchanin from the shtetl of Rashkov"). 
3. **"Not found" means nothing yet.** A zero is valid only if you state where you searched, which spellings, and whether it was the index or the document itself. Test the search on a **control record** — a word that is certainly in that volume. Details — [Standard of proof](../guide/3-results/02-standard-of-proof.md).
4. **A CAPTCHA is passed by a human, and only by a human.** At the first "are you a robot" check, stop, pass it yourself and continue more slowly. Do not bypass it.
5. **Pace matters more than speed.** Almost everywhere what stops you is not a ban but requests that are too frequent. One request every few seconds, a pause at the first failure. Figures are in [Access, CAPTCHAs, pace](../guide/2-reference/19-access-captchas-pace.md).
6. **A VPN and a foreign address can break access.** From abroad some Russian government sites do not open; the reverse also happens. Do not circumvent blocks.
7. **Passwords, cookies and keys — to no one and nowhere.** For a helper (a person or a program) it is enough that you logged in to the account yourself in your own browser.
8. **Do not pay for what is free.** Paid "genealogical searches" often merely point to the archive and the file. Almost all Ukrainian metrical books, revision lists and inventories are openly available.
9. **Record a find immediately:** the archival reference (fond–inventory–file–folio), the page address, an exact quotation, the date. Save the frame itself.
10. **Access to documents:** in Russia and Ukraine documents with personal data are closed for 75 years (in Ukraine the files of the repressive organs are open under a 2015 law); pre-revolutionary metrical books, revision lists, censuses and family lists are available to anyone without proof of kinship.

## How to work through a shtetl: the order developed in practice

1. **Routes to Roots** or M. Weiner's book — what should exist for the shtetl.
2. **"Zvedenyi katalog metrychnykh knyh"** (consolidated catalog of metrical books; Ukrainian-language) — what survives and in which archive.
3. **Register of inventories / inventory** on the archive's site or on Commons — which files exist, what has been digitized.
4. **JewishGen and FamilySearch** — whether there is an index; the index first, then continuous reading.
5. **Commons / FamilySearch films** — the scan itself. Check every index match against the scan.
6. **Directories and the press** (archive.org, Yandex archives, NLI, Genealogy Indexer): business, addresses, doctors, advertisements.
7. If the file is not digitized — bring the reference to an **exact archival reference** and check that it has survived; then a request ([Requests and letters](../guide/2-reference/20-requests-letters.md)).

A consolidated list of over 50 free databases for Russia and the USSR (repression, war, emigration, vanished villages, emigré newspapers) — **Lost Russian Family**: lostrussianfamily.wpcomstaging.com/free-databases/ [checked].
