# What to read and where to learn: by level

What this section covers: books, free courses, webinars, journals and training programs — from the first steps to professional certification. Divided into three levels: beginner, experienced, specialist. Authors, institutes and dictionaries already described in the guide are only named here, with a link.

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified]) are explained in the [Glossary](../guide/0-start/06-glossary.md). Checked 08.10.2026. "Where to get it": *free* / *buy* / *library*. Prices and enrollment dates change fast — check on the site before paying. English-language items come first within each level wherever the order does not matter; Russian-language ones are marked "(in Russian)".

**Already in the guide:**
- N. Lipes, Beider, Weiner, Feldblum, Mokotoff and Sack, Steve Morse, Kleinwaks, Lara Diamond, Pickholtz, the historians Avrutin, Freeze, Petrovsky-Shtern; method — Mills, BCG, Elder — [Genealogists and authors](genealogists-and-authors.md);
- dictionaries, encyclopedias, memorial books, pre-reform spelling — [Dictionaries and encyclopedias](dictionaries-and-encyclopedias.md);
- the courses of JewishGen, JewishGen Talks, IGRA, Eilam, IAJGS, Avotaynu, Am HaZikaron, first steps from Ukraine RD, the FamilySearch Research Wiki — [Institutes and projects](institutes-and-projects.md);
- AI in genealogy — [AI in genealogy](ai-in-genealogy.md).

### Free reading online

- **S. Dubnov, *History of the Jews in Russia and Poland*** (English translation, JPS, 1916–1920) — on archive.org and in Project Gutenberg [from search snippet]. In Russian — his "Brief History of the Jews" (Краткая история евреев) [from search snippet].
- **The "Library" of the Jewish Roots portal** (Еврейские корни, j-roots; forum.j-roots.info/biblio.php) (in Russian) — public-domain books: laws on Jews, directories and statistics, periodicals ("Voskhod", "Evreiskaya starina", "Perezhitoe"), geography by region. Opens without login; on 08.10.2026 the site's certificate had expired [checked].

### History for context

It explains the gaps in records and the movements of families. Avrutin, Freeze and Petrovsky-Shtern are in [Genealogists and authors](genealogists-and-authors.md). More:

| Author, title, year | What it is for | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| John Klier, *Russia Gathers Her Jews* (1986); *Imperial Russia's Jewish Question, 1855–1881* (1995) | policy toward Jews before and after the Great Reforms | [from search snippet] |
| Michael Stanislawski, *Tsar Nicholas I and the Jews*, 1983 | 1825–1855: conscription, the abolition of the kahals — the time of the revisions | [from search snippet] |
| Benjamin Nathans, *Beyond the Pale*, 2002 | if the ancestors lived outside the Pale (St Petersburg, Moscow) | [from search snippet] |
| Gershon Hundert, *The Jews in a Polish Private Town* (1992); *Jews in Poland-Lithuania in the Eighteenth Century* (2004) | private towns of the Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth before 1795 — before the Russian revisions (Podolia, Volhynia) | [from search snippet] |

All four are in English; translations into Russian were not looked for.
