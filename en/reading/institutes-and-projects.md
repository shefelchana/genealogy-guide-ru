# Institutes and projects

What this section covers: Israeli and international organizations engaged in Jewish genealogy — what they do, what you can learn from them and what of it is free.

## JewishGen (jewishgen.org)
The main free resource of Jewish genealogy; a free account is needed for searching [checked].
- **Courses** (jewishgen.org/education): paid, $150 each. In the 2026 schedule — "Advanced AI: Beyond the Prompt", "Organizing Genealogy with Digital Tools", "Brick Wall or Dead End" (with an individual mentor), "Map It! Using Historical Maps" (Eastern Europe), "Research in Belarus" [checked].
- **JewishGen Talks**: free webinars of 2021–2024, recordings on the YouTube channel @JewishGen613 [checked].
- **Ukraine Research Division**: "Tips To Begin Your Research" — 10 steps for beginners (jewishgen.org/Ukraine/TPL_Base.asp?id=5) [checked]. It covers the guberniyas of Chernigov, Kharkov, Kherson, Kyiv, Podolia, Poltava, Taurida, Volhynia, Ekaterinoslav.
- **Bessarabia SIG** (jewishgen.org/bessarabia): its own database, cemetery projects, a mailing list; free [checked].
- **InfoFiles** and the FAQ (jewishgen.org/infofiles) — short guides by topic.
- Databases, JGFF, ViewMate — [../catalog/indexed-databases.md](../catalog/indexed-databases.md), [../catalog/forums-and-communities.md](../catalog/forums-and-communities.md).

## Am haZikaron (amhazikaron.org)
- A nonprofit organization, founded in Tel Aviv in 1998 by Yonatan Vidgop. Research and educational programs on the history of Jewish families, help in finding documents for repatriation. According to the "About us" page, over 60,000 people have attended seminars since 2008 [checked]. The site is in 6 languages, Russian included.
- **What to read:** the article "Basic methods of research on the history of Jewish clans" (amhazikaron.org/en/research/basic-methods-of-research-on-the-history/) — analysis of a surname and its geography, "dynastic" names, historical context, migrations, archives, DNA [checked].
- **JewAge** (jewage.org) — the institute's portal since 2008: a general tree of Jewish families and articles on the origin of surnames [checked].
- **Pogrom memorial database** (pogrom.amhazikaron.org) — 7,929 victims, 118 pages on shtetls, each record has a source [checked, October 2026; the earlier figure "more than 1,500 localities" was not confirmed on the site]. How to search — [Pogroms 1918–1922](../guide/2-reference/11-pogroms-1918-1922.md).
- **The institute's techniques** that entered the guide: a "dynastic" set of names as a sign of a common family; prefaces and approbations of rabbinical books as a genealogical source; interviews with elders as the first step; documents with a "nationality" box as a substitute for metrical books ([Names](../guide/2-reference/03-names-spellings.md), [Jewish genealogy](../guide/2-reference/02-jewish-genealogy.md), [Citizenship](../guide/2-reference/14-citizenship-by-descent.md)).
- **Paid services:** a family study is packaged as a "Book of Generations"; there are no prices on the open pages. The press (Jerusalem Post, 12.04.2026) retells the institute's statements about "inherited traits" that supposedly persist in a family for 950 years. These are the institute's own claims, not a standard genealogical method — treat with caution.

### The program "Rod'N'ya" (Russian-language)
- A year-long program of the Am haZikaron institute for restoring the history of a Jewish family, supported by the Genesis foundation, recruitment through Hillel (amhazikaron.org/project/rodnya/) [checked].
- For young people aged 18–35, Taglit alumni; it teaches three methods: interviews with relatives, internet resources, archives; experts accompany the participants.
- A spin-off: "Istoriya semei Rossii — svyaz pokolenii" (History of Russian families — the link of generations) by the Hillel Center foundation (2019–2020, ages 18–27, hillel.ru/programs/semeinaya-istoria/) [checked].
- This is a selective program, not an open database: not suitable for adults without Taglit, and there are no open materials for self-study.

## IGRA — Israel Genealogy Research Association (genealogy.org.il)
- All Israel Database — over 4 million records (Israel and Mandatory Palestine); free registration opens the main part; there is a paid membership [checked].
- Webinars in English and Hebrew (genealogy.org.il/videos): for example, "Using Russian and Ukrainian Language Internet Resources for Jewish Genealogy" (05.02.2026), "Polish Jewish Refugees in the USSR During WWII" (2022), "From Liepaja to Latgale" (2021). In-person lectures in Givatayim and Ra'anana [checked].

## Eilam — the Israeli Genealogical Society (isragen.org.il)
- Founded in 1983; branches in Haifa, Hadera, the Tel Aviv area; courses ("Midrasha"), consultations, lectures (including on Zoom); the journal "Sharsheret ha-dorot". The site is in Hebrew [checked].

## IAJGS — International Association of Jewish Genealogical Societies (iajgs.org)
- Unites over 90 Jewish genealogical societies (list — iajgs.org/membership/member-societies/). Free: the IAJGS Cemetery Project and the blog (including articles on AI). Conferences: the virtual one has been moved to 4–6 April 2027, the in-person one to 2028 (per a blog post of March 2026) [checked].

## Avotaynu (avotaynu.com)
- The main publisher on Jewish genealogy: over 75 books since 1991, a journal (over 2,900 articles for 1985–2011, digitized), the Consolidated Jewish Surname Index, the free mailing "Nu? What's New?". The books are paid [checked]. ⚠️ The Avotaynu CSI service gave a server error in September 2026.

## Routes to Roots Foundation (Miriam Weiner)
- The free "Archive Database": by the name of a shtetl — which documents survive and in which archive (Belarus, Lithuania, Moldova, Poland, Ukraine; selectively Russia, Latvia, Romania) [checked: FamilySearch Wiki]. The site's main page returned an empty response in October 2026. The book "Jewish Roots in Ukraine and Moldova" is on archive.org.

## CAHJP — Central Archives for the History of the Jewish People (Jerusalem)
- Founded in 1939; since the early 1990s it microfilmed the holdings of archives of the former USSR; a database of inventories of archives of Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Uzbekistan, Moldova, the Baltics. Some of the metrical books are indexed in JewishGen databases [from search snippet]. The "CAHJP Documents" collection in Ukraine RD: jewishgen.org/ukraine/RES_Collection.asp?id=714.

## Yad Vashem
- The Central Database of Shoah Victims' Names (collections.yadvashem.org/en/names) — its basis is the Pages of Testimony. A page can be submitted online or by mail [from search snippet].
- **"The Untold Stories"** — the sites of mass murder of Jews in the occupied territory of the USSR, over 2,700 sites [from search snippet].
- Search techniques — [../catalog/indexed-databases.md](../catalog/indexed-databases.md).

## International Institute for Jewish Genealogy (IIJG, Paul Jacobi Center, Jerusalem)
- Founded in 2004, opened at the National Library of Israel in 2006 [from search snippet; the site did not open].

## Douglas E. Goldman Jewish Genealogy Center (ANU Museum, Tel Aviv)
- A department of the former Beit Hatfutsot; accepts family trees (GEDCOM); the trees of over 5 million people [from search snippet].

## Center for Jewish History (New York)
- Guides "Basic Resources", "Finding Your Ancestral Town", guides to Ukraine, Lithuania, Israel — free PDFs (cjh.org/pdfs/BasicResources.pdf, cjh.org/pdfs/Ukraine.pdf) [from search snippet]; guides to American sources (libguides.cjh.org) [checked].

## Standards and methodology (not specific to Jewish genealogy)
- **BCG — Board for Certification of Genealogists** (bcgcertification.org): the Genealogical Proof Standard, the code of ethics, the articles "Ten-Minute Methodology", "Skillbuilding" [checked].
- **NGS — National Genealogical Society (USA)**: standards and recommendations, an AI program.
- **FamilySearch Research Wiki** (familysearch.org/en/wiki): "Research Process", "Research Logs", "Cite Your Sources", pages on Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Russia, Jewish records, guides to letters to archives [checked].
