# Institutes and projects

What this section covers: Israeli and international organizations that deal with Jewish genealogy — what they do, what you can learn from them, and what of it is free.

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified] and others) are explained in the [Glossary](../guide/0-start/06-glossary.md).

---

## JewishGen (jewishgen.org) (English interface)
The main free resource of Jewish genealogy; a free account is needed for searching [checked].
- **Courses** (jewishgen.org/education): paid, $150 each. The 2026 schedule includes "Advanced AI: Beyond the Prompt", "Organizing Genealogy with Digital Tools", "Brick Wall or Dead End" (with an individual mentor), "Map It! Using Historical Maps" (Eastern Europe), "Research in Belarus" [checked].
- **JewishGen Talks**: free webinars of 2021–2024, recordings on the YouTube channel @JewishGen613 [checked].
- **Ukraine Research Division**: "Tips To Begin Your Research" — 10 steps for beginners (jewishgen.org/Ukraine/TPL_Base.asp?id=5) [checked]. It covers the guberniyas of Chernigov, Kharkov, Kherson, Kyiv, Podolia, Poltava, Taurida, Volhynia, Ekaterinoslav.
- **Bessarabia SIG** (jewishgen.org/bessarabia): its own database, cemetery projects, a mailing list; free [checked].
- **InfoFiles** and the FAQ (jewishgen.org/infofiles) — short guides by topic.
- Databases, JGFF, ViewMate — [indexed-databases.md](../catalog/indexed-databases.md), [forums-and-communities.md](../catalog/forums-and-communities.md).

## Am HaZikaron (amhazikaron.org)
- A non-profit organization, founded in Tel Aviv in 1998 by Yonatan Vidgop. Research and educational programs on the history of Jewish families, help in finding documents for repatriation. According to the "About us" page, more than 60,000 people have attended its seminars since 2008 [checked]. The site is in 6 languages, Russian among them.
- **What to read:** the article "Basic methods of research on the history of Jewish clans" (amhazikaron.org/en/research/basic-methods-of-research-on-the-history/) — analysis of a surname and its geography, "dynastic" names, historical context, migrations, archives, DNA [checked].
- **JewAge** (jewage.org) — the institute's portal since 2008: a common tree of Jewish clans and articles on the origin of surnames [checked].
- **The pogrom memorial database** (pogrom.amhazikaron.org) — 7,929 victims, 118 pages about shtetls, a source for every record [checked, October 2026; the earlier figure "more than 1,500 localities" was not confirmed on the site]. How to search — [Pogroms 1918–1922](../guide/2-reference/11-pogroms-1918-1922.md).
- **The institute's techniques** that entered the guide: a "dynastic" set of names as a sign of a common clan; the prefaces and approbations of rabbinical books as a pedigree source; an interview with the elders as the first step; documents with a "nationality" column as a substitute for metrical books ([Names](../guide/2-reference/03-names-spellings.md), [Jewish genealogy](../guide/2-reference/02-jewish-genealogy.md), [Citizenship](../guide/2-reference/14-citizenship-by-descent.md)).
- **Paid services:** a clan study is delivered as a "Book of Generations"; there are no prices on the open pages. The press (Jerusalem Post, 12.04.2026) retells the institute's claims about "heritable traits" that supposedly persist in a clan for 950 years. These are the institute's own statements, not a standard genealogical method — treat them with caution.

### The program "Rod'N'ya" (Род'N'я)
- A year-long program of the institute Am HaZikaron for restoring the history of a Jewish family, supported by the Genesis fund, enrollment through Hillel (amhazikaron.org/project/rodnya/) [checked].
- For young people aged 18–35, Taglit alumni; they are taught three methods: interviews with relatives, internet resources, archives; experts accompany the participants.
- A spin-off: "Family history of Russia — a link of generations" (История семей России — связь поколений) by the Hillel Center fund (2019–2020, ages 18–27, hillel.ru/programs/semeinaya-istoria/) [checked].
- This is a selective program, not an open database: it does not suit adults who are not Taglit alumni, and there are no open materials for self-study.

## IGRA — Israel Genealogy Research Association (genealogy.org.il)
- The All Israel Database — more than 4 million records (Israel and Mandatory Palestine); free registration opens the main part; there is a paid membership [checked].
- Webinars in English and Hebrew (genealogy.org.il/videos): for example, "Using Russian and Ukrainian Language Internet Resources for Jewish Genealogy" (05.02.2026), "Polish Jewish Refugees in the USSR During WWII" (2022), "From Liepaja to Latgale" (2021). In-person lectures in Givatayim and Ra'anana [checked].

## Eilam — the Israel Genealogical Society (isragen.org.il)
- Founded in 1983; branches in Haifa, Hadera, the Tel Aviv area; courses (the "Midrasha"), consultations, lectures (including on Zoom); the journal "Sharsheret ha-Dorot". The site is in Hebrew [checked].

## IAJGS — International Association of Jewish Genealogical Societies (iajgs.org)
- It unites more than 90 Jewish genealogical societies (the list is at iajgs.org/membership/member-societies/). Free: the IAJGS Cemetery Project and the blog (including articles on AI). Conferences: the virtual one moved to 4–6 April 2027, the in-person one to 2028 (from a blog entry of March 2026) [checked].

## Avotaynu (avotaynu.com)
- The main publisher on Jewish genealogy: more than 75 books since 1991, a journal (more than 2,900 articles for 1985–2011, digitized), the Consolidated Jewish Surname Index, the free mailing list "Nu? What's New?". Books are paid [checked]. ⚠️ In September 2026 the Avotaynu CSI service returned a server error.

## Routes to Roots Foundation (Miriam Weiner)
- The free "Archive Database": by the name of a shtetl — which documents have survived and in which archive (Belarus, Lithuania, Moldova, Poland, Ukraine; selectively Russia, Latvia, Romania) [checked: FamilySearch Wiki]. In October 2026 the site's home page returned an empty answer. The book "Jewish Roots in Ukraine and Moldova" is on archive.org.

## CAHJP — Central Archives for the History of the Jewish People (Jerusalem)
- Founded in 1939; since the early 1990s it microfilmed holdings of archives of the former USSR; a database of inventories of archives of Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Uzbekistan, Moldova, the Baltics. Some of the metrical books are indexed in JewishGen databases [from search snippet]. The collection "CAHJP Documents" in Ukraine RD: jewishgen.org/ukraine/RES_Collection.asp?id=714.

## Yad Vashem
- The Central Database of Shoah Victims' Names (collections.yadvashem.org/en/names) — its basis is the pages of testimony. A page can be submitted online or by post [from search snippet].
- **"The Untold Stories"** — the sites of mass murders of Jews in the occupied territory of the USSR, more than 2,700 sites [from search snippet].
- Search techniques — [indexed-databases.md](../catalog/indexed-databases.md).

## International Institute for Jewish Genealogy (IIJG, Paul Jacobi Center, Jerusalem)
- Founded in 2004, opened in the National Library of Israel in 2006 [from search snippet].

## Douglas E. Goldman Jewish Genealogy Center (ANU Museum, Tel Aviv)
- A department of the former Beit Hatfutsot; accepts family trees (GEDCOM); trees of more than 5 million people [from search snippet].

## Center for Jewish History (New York)
- Guides "Basic Resources", "Finding Your Ancestral Town", guides to Ukraine, Lithuania, Israel — free PDFs (cjh.org/pdfs/BasicResources.pdf, cjh.org/pdfs/Ukraine.pdf) [from search snippet]; guides to American sources (libguides.cjh.org) [checked].

## Standards and method (not Jewish-specific)
- **BCG — Board for Certification of Genealogists** (bcgcertification.org): the Genealogical Proof Standard, a code of ethics, the articles "Ten-Minute Methodology", "Skillbuilding" [checked].
- **NGS — National Genealogical Society (USA)**: standards and recommendations, an AI program.
- **FamilySearch Research Wiki** (familysearch.org/en/wiki): "Research Process", "Research Logs", "Cite Your Sources", pages on Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Russia, Jewish records, guides to letters to archives [checked].
