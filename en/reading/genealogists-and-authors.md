# Genealogists and authors: whom to read

What this section covers: researchers and popularizers of Jewish genealogy and of the method of proof — their books, sites, lectures and advice. Specialists in AI in genealogy are in [ai-in-genealogy.md](ai-in-genealogy.md).

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified] and others) are explained in the [Glossary](../guide/0-start/06-glossary.md).

---

## Nadia (Sara-Beila) Lipes

A Ukrainian historian, genealogist and writer, a citizen of Israel and Ukraine. She has indexed more than a million archival records of the Russian Empire; since 2007 she has worked on Jewish cemeteries, since 2008 she has run guide courses, since 2009 she has organized tours (Wikipedia, "Nadia Lipes") [checked]. In search results the name is spelled "Липес" (Lipes).

**Sites:**
- **lipesdatabase.com** (English and Russian) — an index database of documents of Ukrainian archives: the 1897 census, the military censuses of 1875; there are incomplete indexes for Kyiv guberniya and Odesa. The database does not contain the documents themselves — it shows in which archive a document lies. Paid services: archive search, consultations, checking documents for repatriation. Her book on pogroms can be downloaded free. Languages — English and Russian [checked].
- **nadialipes.info** — a blog: "Archive stories", "Personalities", articles on pogroms (Odesa 1917–1919, Tetiev), on DNA; the latest articles on the home page are from 2020 [checked].
- **jewishpogroms.info** — a database of pogrom victims (a link from the lipesdatabase page).
- **YouTube channel** "Jewish Genealogy" (Еврейская Генеалогия; in Russian): youtube.com/c/SarahNadiaLipesaboutjewishgenealogy.

**Books** (in Russian; Odesa: Astroprint), the series "Genealogy for Dummies":
- "And what is your nationality?" (А вы по национальности?) (2017);
- **"Soviet documents. What, where, why" (Советские документы. Что, где, зачем) (2018)** — all kinds of Soviet documents from ZAGS (civil registry) to the files of the repressed, with photocopies of real documents. The main book for the section [War, repression, siege](../guide/2-reference/12-war-repression-siege.md);
- "Pre-Soviet documents. What, where, why, 1786–1917" (Досоветские документы…) (2019);
- "Pogroms: the inconvenient truth, 1917–1921" (Погромы: неудобная правда…) (2021);
- "An atypical Holocaust. Part 1: The German zone of occupation" (Нетипичный Холокост…) (2025) — a documentary narrative about the occupied Ukrainian SSR.

**Her advice** (from an article by V. Pletinsky, isrageo.com/2019/04/10/lipes301/) [checked]:
- formulate the aim of the search clearly in advance, so as not to be disappointed later;
- Soviet documents are organized the same way in different countries, only the difficulty of getting them differs;
- a DNA test is only an auxiliary tool, "you can't move to Israel on one analysis";
- with pre-revolutionary documents it is better to work through local genealogists; it can take months.

**Interview** "I have a mission" (У меня есть миссия) ("Evreiskii obozrevatel", June 2020, jew-observer.com): she writes books and gives lectures "so that people can find information themselves" [checked].

**Her techniques that entered the guide** (from the blog nadialipes.info and the interview on isrageo.com) [checked]: look at all surviving years, not only the "right" one; a double name of the father gives the children different patronymics; a long list of Russifications of names; a scribe's unique mistake as an argument for kinship; the range of a surname — by the revisions of 1805–1816, not by the lists of 1906–1907; a chain of substitutes for metrical books (censuses, police files); metrical books in the fond of the zemsky court of the neighboring uezd; ZAGS 1928–1933 without documents; the military census of 1875; the pogrom database jewishpogroms.info with archival references. See [Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](../guide/2-reference/01-records-empire-ussr.md), [Names](../guide/2-reference/03-names-spellings.md), [Pogroms 1918–1922](../guide/2-reference/11-pogroms-1918-1922.md).

**Lectures:** a joint talk by Nadia and Oleksii Lipes, "Resources in Ukrainian Archives…" for the JGS of Greater Boston (23.07.2014): metrical books, rabbinical records, revisions, the censuses of 1875 and 1897, police files, recruit lists, kahal and pogrom files, lists of refugees of World War I [checked: jgsgb.org]. Articles in the journal Avotaynu (2014–2017).

## Alexander Beider
Dictionaries of Jewish surnames: of the Russian Empire (2nd ed. 2008), of the Kingdom of Poland, of Galicia, of Prague. "Handbook of Ashkenazic Given Names and Their Variants" (2009): 15,000 variants from 735 root names. The books are paid (Avotaynu); a **free index** of surnames across all the dictionaries — stevemorse.org/phonetics/beider.php. See [dictionaries-and-encyclopedias.md](dictionaries-and-encyclopedias.md).

## Miriam Weiner
The books "Jewish Roots in Ukraine and Moldova" (1999; on archive.org) and "Jewish Roots in Poland"; the Routes to Roots Foundation and its archive database (what has survived for a shtetl and where). See [institutes-and-projects.md](institutes-and-projects.md).

## Boris Feldblyum
"Russian-Jewish Given Names: Their Origins and Variants" (Avotaynu, 1998): a translation of Kulisher's work of 1911, more than 6,000 names. Paid.

## Yefim Kogan
The founder of Bessarabia SIG (2011): the revision lists of Bessarabia (more than 300 thousand records), a cemetery project (more than 90 cemeteries, about 75 thousand burials). The book "Jewish Families in Shtetl Kaushany" (JewishGen Press, 2025). The presentation "History and Genealogy of Jews in Bessarabia" (May 2026) is on the Bessarabia SIG site [from search snippet].

## Sammy Lerner
The IAJGS 2024 talk "Russian Language Internet Resources for Jewish Genealogy" with a list of Russian-language resources: jewishgen.org/bessarabia/files/conferences/2024/IAJGSPresentation2024SammyLerner.pdf [checked].

## Josif and Vitaly Charny
A translation of the name index of the "Russian Jewish Encyclopedia" (8,500 persons) on the Belarus SIG site; a work on the distribution of surnames in Minsk [from search snippet].

## Vladimir Paley
A historian-genealogist, head of the agency "Arkhivarius"; in 1992 one of the founders of the Jewish Genealogical Society of the former USSR. He ran the online courses "Jewish genealogy. How to look for information about ancestors" (2022), "The genealogy of a St Petersburg Jew" (2020) and a cycle at the Jewish Museum (Moscow) (in Russian) [from search snippet].

## Gary Mokotoff and Sallyann Sack
"Where Once We Walked" — a directory of about 23,500 shtetls with a Jewish population (Avotaynu).

## Shelley Talalay Dardashti
A journalist and genealogist, founder of the blog Tracing the Tribe (tracingthetribe.blogspot.com, since 2006) and the Facebook group of the same name — one of the largest groups on Jewish genealogy (almost 82 thousand members in 2025). She died on 16 August 2025 [checked: IAJGS obituary]. The blog and the group remain a useful archive of advice and discussions.

## Mikhail Tyaglyi
A historian of the Holocaust (the Ukrainian Center for Holocaust Studies), "The Holocaust in Crimea". Not a genealogist, but useful for the context of the occupation [from search snippet].

## Researchers of the world: what to take from whom

Briefly — who is useful for what in searching in Eastern Europe. The techniques are described in more detail in the guide's sections, by the links.

### The USA and Canada: documents, emigration, search

- **Stephen P. Morse** — stevemorse.org: search forms on top of other people's databases, transliteration, calendars; the tool "printed Cyrillic → cursive" (russian/cyrprintcurs.html), a converter of dates from gravestones (jcal/tombstone.html), search forms for Ellis Island, including by town without a surname [checked]. → [Handwriting](../guide/2-reference/05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md), [Emigration](../guide/2-reference/13-emigration.md).
- **Warren Blatt** — a long-time editor of JewishGen, author of the FAQ: naturalization as the best source of an emigrant's shtetl; about 40% of Eastern European Jews left through Hamburg (the FAQ's estimate); the American name was chosen by the first sound of the Jewish one (jewishgen.org/infofiles/faq.html) [checked].
- **Logan Kleinwaks** — the creator of Genealogy Indexer (genealogyindexer.org): full-text search of directories, memorial books, military lists; a "proximity" search `"a b"~4`, a year filter `{1903-1923}`; the mailing list on new sources is a reason to repeat old negative searches [checked].
- **Lara Diamond** — the blog "Lara's Jewnealogy" (larasgenealogy.blogspot.com): the Ashkenazic Shared DNA Survey with Ashkenazi norms of shared cM; advice on the FamilySearch catalog (look for the shtetl in all spellings) [checked]. → [DNA](../guide/2-reference/15-dna.md).
- **Gary Pokrassa and Joel Spector** — the JewishGen Ukraine Research Division: transliteration of Cyrillic indexes from the files scanned by A. Krakovsky; copies of scans on JewishGen servers; a Town Leader for every shtetl [checked: announcements of talks; volumes — from search results].
- **Phyllis Kramer** — the JewishGen Learning Center: a shtetl page (KehilaLinks) as a "magnet" for relatives (Avotaynu Online, 2009) [checked].
- **Jennifer Mendelsohn** — #resistancegenealogy, the Holocaust Reunion Project: tracing living relatives of survivors (DNA + forums + documents); each public find with a scan of the document [checked].
- **Marian L. Smith** — a historian of the US Immigration and Naturalization Service: the manifest was compiled before sailing, from the words of the emigrant or an agent; the arrival certificate in a naturalization file names the ship and the date [from search snippet].
- **Brooke Schreier Ganz** — Reclaim The Records: free indexes and scans of American records, including New York marriage applications 1908–1929 (archive.org/details/nycmarriageindex) [checked].
- **Valery Bazarov** — the HIAS relatives-search service: HIAS arrival cards 1909–1978 at YIVO (Center for Jewish History) [from search snippet].
- **Judy G. Russell** — "The Legal Genealogist": ask of each source under what law it was created [from search snippet].
- **Ron Arons** — "Mind Maps for Genealogy" (2014): a map of contradictions instead of a list [from search snippet].
- **Historians of the Jews of the Russian Empire** — Eugene Avrutin ("Jews and the Imperial State", 2010: the registration was incomplete), Chaeran Freeze ("Jewish Marriage and Divorce in Imperial Russia", 2002: frequent divorces in the first half of the 19th century), Yohanan Petrovsky-Shtern ("Jews in the Russian Army, 1827–1917", 2009) [from search snippet]. Useful for context and for explaining the gaps.

### DNA under endogamy

- **Louis Kessler** — beholdgenealogy.com: a test of the WATO calculator on an endogamous family — the right answer after discarding segments under 7 cM; experiments in reading manuscripts with AI [checked]. **Jonny Perl** — the author of DNA Painter.
- **Israel Pickholtz** — "Endogamy: One Family, One People" (2015): whom to test for a specific hypothesis [from search snippet].
- **Adam Brown** — the Avotaynu DNA Project: a catalog of Jewish Y-lines; a Y-haplogroup confirms a line, not an ancestor [checked].
- **Shai Carmi and Doron Behar** — population geneticists: the Ashkenazi "bottleneck", four maternal "founder" lines [from search snippet].
- **Kevin Alan Brook** — "The Maternal Genetic Lineages of Ashkenazic Jews" (2022): a catalog of maternal haplogroups [checked].
- **Jeffrey Mark Paull** — Y-DNA of rabbinical dynasties: a protocol for verifying a paternal line [checked].

### Israel: rabbinical lines and data

- **Chaim Freedman** — "Eliyahu's Branches", "Beit Rabbanan"; linking generations before 1804 by patronymics and the birth order of children; lists of subscribers to Jewish books; checking a claimed *yichus* (pedigree) against gravestones (chfreedman.blogspot.com) [checked].
- **Neil Rosenstein and Paul Jacobi** — compilations of rabbinical pedigrees ("The Unbroken Chain", "The Jacobi Papers"); the prefaces and approbations of rabbinical books as a source [from search snippet; the CJH guide "Rabbinical Genealogy" — checked].
- **IGRA: Rose Feldman, Garri Regev** — the All Israel Database (Israeli databases of the Ottoman, British and Israeli periods) [from search snippet]. Alongside — an open dataset of name changes in the "Palestine Gazette" 1921–1947 (mandatenamechanges.org: 19,043 records, each with a link to the scan page) [checked; the project's author was not verified from search results].

### Ukraine, Bessarabia, Poland, Germany

- **Alex Krakovsky (Oleksandr Krakovskyi)** — since 2017 he has been scanning metrical books, revisions and censuses of Ukrainian archives and posting them on Wikisource and Commons; his files include alphabetical indexes and name lists [checked].
- **Alex Dunai (Oleksandr Dunai)** — a historian and researcher from Lviv: Soviet lists by name of Holocaust victims (acts of the Extraordinary State Commission) [from search snippet].
- **Historians of the Holocaust in Ukraine** — Alexander Kruglov, Ilya Altman (ed., "The Holocaust on the Territory of the USSR. Encyclopedia", 2009; in Russian), Boris Zabarko, Faina Vinokurova: dates and places of death by locality [from search snippet].
- **Veniamin Lukin and Boris Khaimovich** — "100 Jewish Shtetls of Ukraine. A Historical Guide" (in Russian) [from search snippet].
- **Inna Vayner** — Bessarabia SIG: databases of the revisions of Bessarabia and Tiraspol uezd; lists from before the introduction of surnames, indicating where the family came from [checked: introductions to the databases].
- **Alla Chastina** — a talk at IAJGS 2012 on the archival documents of Moldova: what exists and what does not (there is no 1897 census by name for Bessarabia) [the talk is confirmed: iajgs.org, jewishgen.org/bessarabia; the contents — from a retelling, not verified].
- **Stanley Diamond and Mark Halpern** — JRI-Poland: a full extract for a shtetl and sorting by parents' names; records of one shtetl in the books of another (Avotaynu, 2018) [checked].
- **Jürgen Sielemann** — an archivist of the Hamburg State Archives: the Hamburg passenger lists do not give religion before 1920 [checked].

## Method of proof (general genealogy)

- **Elizabeth Shown Mills**, "Evidence Explained" (the current one is the 4th edition, 2024) and the short QuickLessons on evidenceexplained.com: three axes of evaluation (source, information, evidence), independence of confirmations, negative evidence, the FAN principle (QuickLesson 11 and 17) [checked]. The basis of the section [Standard of proof](../guide/3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md).
- **The Board for Certification of Genealogists (BCG)**: the Genealogical Proof Standard, the articles "Ten-Minute Methodology", "Planning Effective Research", "Focused versus Diffuse Research", the code of ethics (bcgcertification.org) [checked].
- **Diana Elder and Nicole Elder Dyer** (FamilyLocket): "Research Like a Pro" and "Research Like a Pro with AI"; articles on endogamy in DNA (familylocket.com).
- **Kitty Cooper** (blog.kittycooper.com) and **Dana Leeds** (danaleeds.com) — genetic genealogy, endogamy.
- **The VGD forum, "Advice to beginners"** (docs.vgd.ru/kak-najti-predkov) — in Russian, for the very first steps [checked].
