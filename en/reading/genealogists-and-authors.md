# Genealogists and authors: whom to read

What this section covers: researchers and popularizers of Jewish genealogy and of the methodology of proof — their books, sites, lectures and advice. Specialists in AI in genealogy are in [ai-in-genealogy.md](ai-in-genealogy.md).

Markers: **[checked]** — the page was read (October 2026); **[from search snippet]** — information from search results, to be verified.

## Nadia (Sarah-Beila) Lipes

A Ukrainian historian, genealogist and writer, a citizen of Israel and Ukraine. She has indexed over a million archival records of the Russian Empire; since 2007 she has worked on Jewish cemeteries, since 2008 she has run guide courses, since 2009 she has organized tours (Wikipedia, "Nadia Lipes") [checked]. In search results the name is spelled "Липес".

**Sites:**
- **lipesdatabase.com** — an index database of the documents of Ukrainian archives: the 1897 censuses, the military censuses of 1875; there are incomplete indexes for Kyiv guberniya and Odesa. The database does not contain the documents themselves — it indicates in which archive a document lies. Paid services: archival search, consultations, checking documents for repatriation. Her book on pogroms can be downloaded free. Languages — English and Russian [checked].
- **nadialipes.info** — a blog: "Архивные истории" (Archive stories), "Персоналии" (Personalities), articles on pogroms (Odesa 1917–1919, Tetiev), on DNA; the latest articles on the main page are from 2020 [checked]. (Russian-language)
- **jewishpogroms.info** — a database of pogrom victims (linked from the lipesdatabase page).
- **YouTube channel** "Еврейская Генеалогия" (Jewish Genealogy; in Russian): youtube.com/c/SarahNadiaLipesaboutjewishgenealogy.

**Books** (Odesa: Astroprint), series "Genealogy for Dummies" ("Генеалогия для чайников"; Russian-language):
- "А вы по национальности?" ("And what is your nationality?"; 2017);
- **"Советские документы. Что, где, зачем" ("Soviet documents: what, where, why"; 2018)** — all kinds of Soviet documents from ZAGS (civil registry) to the files of the repressed, with photocopies of real documents. The main book for the section [War, repression, siege](../guide/2-reference/12-war-repression-siege.md);
- "Досоветские документы. Что, где, зачем, 1786–1917" ("Pre-Soviet documents: what, where, why, 1786–1917"; 2019);
- "Погромы: неудобная правда, 1917–1921" ("Pogroms: the inconvenient truth, 1917–1921"; 2021);
- "Нетипичный Холокост. Часть 1: Немецкая зона оккупации" ("The atypical Holocaust. Part 1: The German zone of occupation"; 2025) — a documentary narrative about the occupied Ukrainian SSR.

**Her advice** (from an article by V. Pletinsky, isrageo.com/2019/04/10/lipes301/) [checked]:
- formulate the goal of the search clearly in advance, so as not to be disappointed later;
- Soviet documents are organized the same way in different countries, only the difficulty of obtaining them differs;
- a DNA test is only an auxiliary tool, «на одном анализе в Израиль не уедешь» ["you won't get to Israel on one test"];
- with pre-revolutionary documents it is better to work through local genealogists; it can take months.

**Interview** "У меня есть миссия" ("I have a mission"; "Evreiskii obozrevatel", June 2020, jew-observer.com): she writes books and gives lectures «чтобы люди самостоятельно могли находить информацию» ["so that people could find information on their own"] [checked].

**Her techniques that entered the guide** (from the blog nadialipes.info and an interview on isrageo.com) [checked]: look at all surviving years, not only the "right" one; a double name of the father gives the children different patronymics; a long list of Russifications of names; a scribe's unique error as an argument for kinship; the range of a surname — from the revisions of 1805–1816, not from the lists of 1906–1907; a chain of substitutes for metrical books (censuses, police files); metrical books in the fond of the zemstvo court of a neighboring uezd; ZAGS 1928–1933 without documents; the 1875 military census; the pogrom database jewishpogroms.info with archival references. See [Documents of the Russian Empire and the USSR](../guide/2-reference/01-records-empire-ussr.md), [Names](../guide/2-reference/03-names-spellings.md), [Pogroms 1918–1922](../guide/2-reference/11-pogroms-1918-1922.md).

**Lectures:** a joint presentation by Nadia and Oleksiy Lipes, "Resources in Ukrainian Archives…" for the JGS of Greater Boston (23.07.2014): metrical books, rabbinical records, revisions, the censuses of 1875 and 1897, police files, recruit lists, kahal and pogrom files, lists of refugees of the First World War [checked: jgsgb.org]. Articles in the journal Avotaynu (2014–2017).

## Alexander Beider
Dictionaries of Jewish surnames: of the Russian Empire (2nd ed. 2008), the Kingdom of Poland, Galicia, Prague. "Handbook of Ashkenazic Given Names and Their Variants" (2009): 15,000 variants from 735 root names. The books are paid (Avotaynu); **a free index** of surnames across all the dictionaries — stevemorse.org/phonetics/beider.php. See [dictionaries-and-encyclopedias.md](dictionaries-and-encyclopedias.md).

## Miriam Weiner
The books "Jewish Roots in Ukraine and Moldova" (1999; on archive.org) and "Jewish Roots in Poland"; the Routes to Roots foundation and its archive database (what survives for a shtetl and where). See [institutes-and-projects.md](institutes-and-projects.md).

## Boris Feldblyum
"Russian-Jewish Given Names: Their Origins and Variants" (Avotaynu, 1998): a translation of Kulisher's work of 1911, over 6,000 names. Paid.

## Yefim Kogan
Founder of Bessarabia SIG (2011): revision lists of Bessarabia (over 300 thousand records), a cemetery project (over 90 cemeteries, about 75 thousand burials). The book "Jewish Families in Shtetl Kaushany" (JewishGen Press, 2025). The presentation "History and Genealogy of Jews in Bessarabia" (May 2026) — on the Bessarabia SIG site [from search snippet].

## Sammy Lerner
The IAJGS 2024 presentation "Russian Language Internet Resources for Jewish Genealogy" with a list of Russian-language resources: jewishgen.org/bessarabia/files/conferences/2024/IAJGSPresentation2024SammyLerner.pdf [checked].

## Josif and Vitaly Charny
A translation of the name index of the "Rossiiskaya evreiskaya entsiklopediya" (Russian Jewish Encyclopedia; 8,500 persons) on the Belarus SIG site; a study of the distribution of surnames in Minsk [from search snippet].

## Vladimir Paley
A historian-genealogist, head of the "Arkhivarius" agency; in 1992 one of the founders of the Jewish Genealogical Society of the former USSR. He ran online courses "Evreiskaya genealogiya. Kak iskat svedeniya o predkakh" (Jewish genealogy: how to look for information about ancestors; 2022), "Genealogiya peterburgskogo evreya" (Genealogy of a St. Petersburg Jew; 2020) and a series at the Jewish Museum (Moscow) [from search snippet]. (Russian-language)

## Gary Mokotoff and Sallyann Sack
"Where Once We Walked" — a directory of about 23,500 shtetls with a Jewish population (Avotaynu).

## Schelly Talalay Dardashti
The blog Tracing the Tribe (tracingthetribe.blogspot.com) and moderator of the Facebook group of the same name.

## Mikhail Tyagly
A historian of the Holocaust (Ukrainian Center for Holocaust Studies), "Holocaust in Crimea" ("Холокост в Крыму"). Not a genealogist, but useful for the context of the occupation [from search snippet]. (Russian-language)

## Researchers of the world: what to take from whom

In short — who is useful for what in searching in Eastern Europe. Markers: **[checked]** — the page or publication was read; **[from search snippet]** — information from search results. Techniques in more detail are in the sections of the guide, by the links.

### USA and Canada: documents, emigration, search

- **Stephen P. Morse** — stevemorse.org: search forms on top of other people's databases, transliteration, calendars; the tool "printed Cyrillic → cursive" (russian/cyrprintcurs.html), a converter of dates from gravestones (jcal/tombstone.html), Ellis Island search forms, including by town without a surname [checked]. → [Handwriting](../guide/2-reference/05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md), [Emigration](../guide/2-reference/13-emigration.md).
- **Warren Blatt** — for many years an editor of JewishGen, author of the FAQ: naturalization as the best source for an emigrant's shtetl; about 40% of Eastern European Jews left through Hamburg (the FAQ's estimate); an American name was chosen by the first sound of the Jewish one (jewishgen.org/infofiles/faq.html) [checked].
- **Logan Kleinwaks** — creator of Genealogy Indexer (genealogyindexer.org): full-text search of directories, memorial books, military lists; "proximity" search `"a b"~4`, a year filter `{1903-1923}`; a mailing list about new sources — a reason to repeat old negative searches [checked].
- **Lara Diamond** — the blog "Lara's Jewnealogy" (larasgenealogy.blogspot.com): the Ashkenazic Shared DNA Survey with Ashkenazi norms of shared cM; advice on the FamilySearch catalog (look for a shtetl under all spellings) [checked]. → [DNA](../guide/2-reference/15-dna.md).
- **Gary Pokrassa and Joel Spector** — JewishGen Ukraine Research Division: transliteration of the Cyrillic indexes from the files scanned by A. Krakovsky; copies of the scans on JewishGen servers; a Town Leader for each shtetl [checked: announcements of talks; volumes — from search snippet].
- **Phyllis Kramer** — JewishGen Learning Center: a shtetl page (KehilaLinks) as a "magnet" for relatives (Avotaynu Online, 2009) [checked].
- **Jennifer Mendelsohn** — #resistancegenealogy, the Holocaust Reunion Project: searching for the living relatives of survivors (DNA + forums + documents); every public find with a scan of the document [checked].
- **Marian L. Smith** — a historian of the US Immigration and Naturalization Service: the manifest was compiled before sailing, from the words of the emigrant or an agent; the certificate of arrival in a naturalization file names the ship and the date [from search snippet].
- **Brooke Schreier Ganz** — Reclaim The Records: free indexes and scans of American records, including New York marriage applications 1908–1929 (archive.org/details/nycmarriageindex) [checked].
- **Valery Bazarov** — the HIAS relatives search service: HIAS arrival cards 1909–1978 at YIVO (Center for Jewish History) [from search snippet].
- **Judy G. Russell** — "The Legal Genealogist": ask of every source under what law it was created [from search snippet].
- **Ron Arons** — "Mind Maps for Genealogy" (2014): a map of contradictions instead of a list [from search snippet].
- **Historians of the Jews of the Russian Empire** — Eugene Avrutin ("Jews and the Imperial State", 2010: the registration was incomplete), Chaeran Freeze ("Jewish Marriage and Divorce in Imperial Russia", 2002: frequent divorces in the first half of the 19th century), Yohanan Petrovsky-Shtern ("Jews in the Russian Army, 1827–1917", 2009) [from search snippet]. Useful for context and for explaining gaps.

### DNA under endogamy

- **Louis Kessler** — beholdgenealogy.com: a test of the WATO calculator on an endogamous family — the correct answer after discarding segments smaller than 7 cM; experiments in reading manuscripts with AI [checked]. **Jonny Perl** — author of DNA Painter.
- **Israel Pickholtz** — "Endogamy: One Family, One People" (2015): whom to test for a specific hypothesis [from search snippet].
- **Adam Brown** — the Avotaynu DNA Project: a catalog of Jewish Y-lines; a Y-haplogroup confirms a line, not an ancestor [checked].
- **Shai Carmi, Doron Behar** — population geneticists: the Ashkenazi "bottleneck", four maternal "founder" lines [from search snippet].
- **Kevin Alan Brook** — "The Maternal Genetic Lineages of Ashkenazic Jews" (2022): a catalog of maternal haplogroups [checked].
- **Jeffrey Mark Paull** — Y-DNA of rabbinical dynasties: a protocol for verifying the paternal line [checked].

### Israel: rabbinical lines and data

- **Chaim Freedman** — "Eliyahu's Branches", "Beit Rabbanan"; linking generations before 1804 by patronymics and the birth order of children; subscriber lists of Jewish books; checking "yichus" against gravestones (chfreedman.blogspot.com) [checked].
- **Neil Rosenstein and Paul Jacobi** — compilations of rabbinical pedigrees ("The Unbroken Chain", "The Jacobi Papers"); prefaces and approbations of rabbinical books as a source [from search snippet; the CJH guide "Rabbinical Genealogy" — checked].
- **IGRA: Rose Feldman, Garri Regev** — All Israel Database (Israeli databases of the Ottoman, British and Israeli periods) [from search snippet]. Alongside — an open dataset of name changes in the "Palestine Gazette" 1921–1947 (mandatenamechanges.org: 19,043 records, each with a link to the page of the scan) [checked; the project's author was not verified from search results].

### Ukraine, Bessarabia, Poland, Germany

- **Alex Krakovsky (Oleksandr Krakovsky)** — since 2017 he has been scanning the metrical books, revisions and censuses of Ukrainian archives and posting them to Vikidzherela (Ukrainian Wikisource) and Commons; his files include alphabetical indexes and name lists [checked].
- **Alex Dunai (Oleksandr Dunai)** — a historian and researcher from Lviv: Soviet name lists of Holocaust victims (acts of the Extraordinary State Commission, ChGK) [from search snippet].
- **Historians of the Holocaust in Ukraine** — Alexander Kruglov, Ilya Altman (ed. "Kholokost na territorii SSSR. Entsiklopediya" [The Holocaust in the Territory of the USSR. Encyclopedia], 2009), Boris Zabarko, Faina Vinokurova: dates and places of death by locality [from search snippet].
- **Veniamin Lukin and Boris Khaimovich** — "100 evreiskikh mestechek Ukrainy. Istoricheskii putevoditel" (100 Jewish shtetls of Ukraine: a historical guide) [from search snippet].
- **Inna Vayner** — Bessarabia SIG: databases of the revisions of Bessarabia and of Tiraspol uezd; lists from before the introduction of surnames with an indication of where the family came from [checked: introductions to the databases].
- **Alla Chastina** — an IAJGS 2012 presentation on the archival documents of Moldova: what exists and what does not (there is no name-by-name census of 1897 for Bessarabia) [the presentation is confirmed: iajgs.org, jewishgen.org/bessarabia; the content — from a retelling, not verified].
- **Stanley Diamond and Mark Halpern** — JRI-Poland: a full extract of a shtetl and sorting by parents' names; records of one shtetl in the books of another (Avotaynu, 2018) [checked].
- **Jürgen Sielemann** — an archivist of the Hamburg State Archives: the Hamburg passenger lists do not give religion before 1920 [checked].

## Methodology of proof (general genealogy)

- **Elizabeth Shown Mills**, "Evidence Explained" and the short QuickLessons on evidenceexplained.com: three axes of evaluation (source, information, evidence), independence of confirmations, negative evidence, the FAN principle (QuickLessons 11 and 17) [checked]. The basis of the section [Standard of proof](../guide/3-results/02-standard-of-proof.md).
- **Board for Certification of Genealogists (BCG)**: the Genealogical Proof Standard, the articles "Ten-Minute Methodology", "Planning Effective Research", "Focused versus Diffuse Research", the code of ethics (bcgcertification.org) [checked].
- **Diana Elder and Nicole Elder Dyer** (FamilyLocket): "Research Like a Pro" and "Research Like a Pro with AI"; articles on endogamy in DNA (familylocket.com).
- **Kitty Cooper** (blog.kittycooper.com) and **Dana Leeds** (danaleeds.com) — genetic genealogy, endogamy.
- **VGD forum, "Sovety nachinayushchim"** (Advice for beginners; docs.vgd.ru/kak-najti-predkov) — in Russian, for the very first steps [checked]. (Russian-language)
