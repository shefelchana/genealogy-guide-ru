# About the compiler

Hi! I'm Anna Shefel. I first got to know archives when I was gathering documents for repatriation to Israel. I got the documents, but my interest in the family's story stayed — and I've been working on it, bit by bit, for many years.

**How I got here.** For about ten years I searched in bursts: lectures on Jewish genealogy, requests to archives, civil registry offices and the Red Cross, looking for people with my surname on social networks, talking to relatives. In September 2026 I took up the search seriously, now with AI agents, and within a month I turned what I had collected into this guide. People in the vibe-coding community often asked me about research, and almost everything needed was already openly available — it just had to be gathered in one place and checked.

**What it gives me.** I understand the family's habits and characters better: I found, for example, several generations of traders. It's clear why relatives moved, changed surnames and kept quiet about the past. I have addresses I can travel to. And it's simply interesting: instead of computer games, I get lost in archives.

I'm not a professional genealogist, just an enthusiast. I rely on my own experience and on open sources.

**What I use**
- **Claude (Claude Code)** — an AI assistant and agents that read scans, try out surname spellings, search databases and keep the research log. **Gemini** — for a second opinion.
- **Scans and databases:** FamilySearch, JewishGen, Wikimedia Commons (files from Ukrainian archives), Yad Vashem, the newspapers of the National Library of Israel, the "Jewish Roots" forum (Еврейские корни, j-roots; in Russian).
- **Tree** — MyHeritage. **DNA** — 23andMe, FamilyTreeDNA, GEDmatch.

This guide is my way of working plus the experience of the genealogists and communities I refer to in the sections.

Examples from my own research are anonymized: names, surnames and archival references are changed; the techniques and traps are real.

**Where I'm looking from.** The guide is written with an emphasis on Jewish genealogy: the Russian Empire, the USSR, Europe, the USA. It is strongest where I searched myself — Jewish families of Podolia, the Kyiv region and Bessarabia. Much of it will suit other searches, but I've surely missed something. What is covered only a little — Orthodox and peasant lines, the exiled and the shot, emigration to South America and China, other communities — is listed in [What this guide does not cover](guide/0-start/07-not-covered.md).

**Sharing what I learn.** I talk about the search — the finds and the dead ends I walked into myself. In the autumn I spoke in a small live stream: how I work with archives and AI agents, where I started, where I went wrong. The more people look for their own families, the more shared sources and stories turn up.

**Where I'd look first** (more in the [Catalog](catalog/README.md)):
- **JewishGen** — indexes of Jewish records, researchers of the same surnames (JGFF), volunteer help with translation (ViewMate) — [Indexed databases](catalog/indexed-databases.md), [Forums and communities](catalog/forums-and-communities.md).
- **The "Jewish Roots" forum (j-roots, in Russian)** — topics by shtetl and by archive fond, links to digitised files — [Forums and communities](catalog/forums-and-communities.md).
- **Scans of Ukrainian archives on Wikimedia Commons and FamilySearch** — whole metrical books, revision lists, censuses — [Archive file scans](catalog/archive-file-scans.md).
- **archival.name** — file titles from archive inventories and card indexes: you can search a surname across many archives at once — [Indexed databases](catalog/indexed-databases.md).
- **Yandex Archive Search** — recognised Russian archives, newspapers and directories — [Press](catalog/press.md), [Directories and books](catalog/directories-and-books.md).
- **Yad Vashem, Pamyat Naroda, OBD Memorial** — the Holocaust, the war, evacuation — [Indexed databases](catalog/indexed-databases.md).
- **DNA tests** (23andMe, MyHeritage, FamilyTreeDNA, GEDmatch, Genotek) — [DNA services](catalog/dna-services.md). Tip: let the oldest living relatives test first — they get more matches.
- **Familio Telegram chats** and regional groups — [Groups and communities](reading/groups-and-communities.md).

**Let's search together.** If you have sources, techniques or experience for your region — send them. We'll build shared collections and search together: one person can't cover everything.
