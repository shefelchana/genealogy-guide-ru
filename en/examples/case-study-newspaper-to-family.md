# Case study: from a newspaper advertisement to a family

What this section covers: a short connected example from a real research project (October 2026) — how one find leads to the next, where a check against the scan was needed and where the conclusion stayed a hypothesis. The chain: an advertisement of 1887 → a correspondence of the same year → a marriage record of 1895 ("also Repetur") → the signature under an article of 1884 → a death certificate in Los Angeles.

All the people in the example died long ago. The archival references and dates are from the research records.

---

## The starting point

A doctor was known, Moisei Berkovich Weinberg, born in 1868 in Izmail (southern Bessarabia). From 1856 to 1878 Izmail belonged to Romania, so there is no Russian metrical record of his birth and there cannot be. The question of the round: **who was his father Berko, and who was he connected with in Izmail?**

The method: look for the family where it could have left a trace "on another occasion" — in Jewish newspapers. A search in the Historical Jewish Press of the National Library of Israel: the surname in Yiddish and Hebrew in several spellings **plus** the name of the town in several spellings (איזמאיל, איזמאהיל). Don't read Russian? See [For English speakers](../guide/0-start/08-for-english-speakers.md).

## Step 1. The advertisement of 1887

**Found:** "Yidishes folksblat" no. 30, 4.VIII.1887, and "Ha-Tsfira", 9.VIII.1887 (the same request in Hebrew). The committee of the synagogue at the Izmail Talmud Torah asks to print an advertisement of a "poor *agunah*": her husband Motl Repitor took the money, jewelry and dresses and fled, leaving her pregnant. Description: short, fair-haired, big blue eyes; rumor has it he left along the line Kaushany — Bender — Akkerman. Telegrams are asked to be sent to "my *shvoger* Beril Weinberg"; in a footnote: "The cost of the telegrams is on me, Beril Weinberg"; the signature — "On behalf of the unfortunate woman — B. Weinberg".

**Checked against the scan:** the recognized text of the newspaper found the article, but the quotation and the translation were made **from the image**. The Yiddish version gave a detail that is not in the Hebrew one: the *agunah* is pregnant.

**What this gave:** Beril Weinberg in 1887 is a settled resident of Izmail, with money, connected with community institutions. Beril and Berko are everyday forms of one Jewish name (Dov-Ber), so he is a candidate for Moisei's father; but a match of name and town is not yet proof. For the first time a person from the documents came alive.

**What stayed open:** a *shvoger* (brother-in-law) — is he her sister's husband or her husband's brother? The word does not settle it.

## Step 2. A correspondence of the same year

If the newspaper has an advertisement, there might be a note too. A search for the runaway's surname (Repitor, in all spellings in Yiddish) over all years gave exactly three articles — all about this case.

**Found:** "Yidishes folksblat", 18.VIII.1887, the rubric "Correspondence", "Izmail (Bessarabia), 16 July 1887", signed "A guter bruder" ("A good brother"). The correspondent mocks the fashion of marrying **without a matchmaker**: "the cavalier of today's fashion" presented himself to the bride's family as a decent man, got the money, took his young wife to where **his parents** lived, drove her out pregnant, and after the court (he was ordered to pay 500 rubles a year) persuaded her to sell her things and ran away with the furniture, clothes and jewelry. The name is given: Motl Repitor, 26 years old.

**Checked against the scan:** the correspondence was read in full from the image and translated completely; one faded word is marked as uncertain.

**What this changed:** Motl is an outsider, his parents live not in Izmail; the bride is "of a respectable family" in Izmail. This tilts toward the reading "Beril is her sister's husband", not "her husband's brother". **The second document changed the interpretation of the first** — which is why the first guess should not have been entered into the tree at once.

## Step 3. A marriage record of 1895: "also Repetur"

The surname Repitor is rare. The next step is the indexes of Bessarabian metrical books on JewishGen. "Repitor" — zero. But a similar entry turned up: a marriage in Chișinău on 23.V.1895, the groom from Izmail — "VAYNBERG / REPETUR Khuna".

**Checked against the scan — and a correction of the index.** From the index it was first read as "Khuna, son of Abram". The scan of the marriage book (ANRM — National Archives of the Republic of Moldova — 211-11-238, record no. 149, FamilySearch, frame 841) showed: "Unmarried resident of Izmail **Khuna Mikhelev Weinberg /also Repetur/**" (Холостъ Измаильскій житель Хуна Михелевъ Вайнбергъ /онъ же Репетуръ/), the bride — Dvosya, daughter of the Chișinău townsman (*meshchanin*) **Abram** Rosenberg. Abram is the bride's father, not the groom's. The index mixed up the fields.

**Confirmation from an independent source:** the directory "All Russia" of 1900 — "Repetur (Weinberg) Khuna Mikhel." (Репетур (Вайнберг) Хуна Михел.).

**What this gave:** it is proved that Repetur is a second name of one of the Izmail Weinberg families (the line of Mikhel). This is a strong sign: such a pairing is rare.

**What stayed a hypothesis:** whether "Repitor" from the newspaper and "Repetur" from the metrical record are one name. There is no document that would link them. So only the fact of the 1895 marriage goes into the tree; the link with the runaway of 1887 goes into the hypotheses file.

⚠️ **The trap of expectation.** In another record of the same circle a scribe's "Mikhel Kr…tor" (Михель Кр…торъ) was very tempting to read as "Repitor". On enlargement it turned out to be "Kroitor" (Кройторъ). When you are looking for a specific name, the eye "completes" it — enlarge and compare the letters with the neighboring records by the same scribe.

## Step 4. The signature under an article of 1884

Further searching in the same newspaper (surname + town, neighboring years) found the article "Down with the mask!" ("Yidishes folksblat", 26.III.1884) with the signature **"Eliezer Moshkov Weinberg. Izmail, March 1884"**. In the annual index of correspondents for 1883 the same author ("Weinberg, E. Moshkov, from Izmail") is listed with one more note.

**Checked against the scan:** the signature was read from the image. "Moshkov" is a patronymic in Yiddish: son of Moshe.

**What this gave:** in the Izmail of the 1880s there lived a literate author Eliezer (Leizer), son of Moshe Weinberg. A new person — and a new thread.

## Step 5. A death certificate in Los Angeles

Among natives of Izmail in American documents one was already known, Leizer Weinberg, who died in Los Angeles in 1940. According to American documents, above all the death certificate: born in Izmail around 1858; father — Morris (the American form of the name Moshe); occupation — teacher of languages (25 years).

**Checked against the scan:** the father's name and place of birth in the death certificate must be read from the image of the form, not from the index: indexes of American documents also make mistakes (see [Emigration](../guide/2-reference/13-emigration.md)).

**Comparison:**

| Feature | Newspaper 1884 | Certificate 1940 |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Eliezer | Leizer (Leizer is an everyday form of Eliezer) |
| Father | Moshkov — son of Moshe | Morris = Moshe |
| Town | Izmail | born in Izmail |
| Period | 1883–1884, an adult author | born about 1858 — in 1884 he is about 26 |
| Occupation | writes articles | teacher of languages |

**Conclusion:** a strong link — five features match, and the sources were created independently (a 19th-century Jewish newspaper and a 20th-century American form). But there is no direct document saying "this is one person"; the data of the death certificate were supplied by an informant who was a relative (secondary information). So the honest status is **a hypothesis with a strong basis**, not "proved"; such a conclusion is worth setting out in a written argument (see [Standard of proof](../guide/3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md)). How Leizer is related to Berko has not yet been established.

## What this example teaches

1. **Place matters more than surname.** A search for "Weinberg" without Izmail would give thousands of matches; the pairing "surname + town" — a few dozen.
2. **One find suggests the next:** the runaway's name → a search on it → the metrical index → a double surname; neighboring issues of the newspaper → a new author → an American document.
3. **An index is a hint.** The index mixed up the bride's father with the groom's father; only the scan caught the error.
4. **A second document can overturn the interpretation of the first** (the correspondence changed the meaning of the word *shvoger*).
5. **Not everything is proved.** Honest statuses — "proved", "strong hypothesis", "open" — matter more than a beautiful story.
6. **Every step is recorded at once:** the archival reference, frame, quotation, translation, confidence of the reading ([finding.md](../templates/finding.md)), empty results go into the research log (for example, "Repitor" in JewishGen — 0).
