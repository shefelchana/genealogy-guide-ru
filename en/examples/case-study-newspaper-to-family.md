# Case study: from a newspaper advertisement to a family

What this section covers: a short connected example from a real study (October 2026) — how one find leads to the next, where a check against the scan was needed and where the conclusion remained a hypothesis. The chain: an advertisement of 1887 → a correspondence of the same year → a marriage record of 1895 ("also known as Repetur") → a signature under an article of 1884 → a death certificate in Los Angeles.

All the people in the example died long ago. The archival references and dates are from the study's records.

---

## The starting point

A doctor, Moisei Berkovich Weinberg, born in 1868 in Izmail (southern Bessarabia), was known. In 1856–1878 Izmail belonged to Romania, so there is no Russian metrical record of his birth and there cannot be one. The question of the round: **who was his father Berko and with whom was he connected in Izmail?**

The method: look for the family where it could have left a trace "on another occasion" — in the Jewish newspapers. A search in the Historical Jewish Press of the National Library of Israel: the surname in Yiddish and Hebrew in several spellings **plus** the name of the town in several spellings (איזמאיל, איזמאהיל).

## Step 1. The advertisement of 1887

**What was found:** "Yidishes folksblat" no. 30, 4.VIII.1887, and "Ha-Tsfira", 9.VIII.1887 (the same request in Hebrew). The committee of the synagogue at the Izmail Talmud Torah asks to print an advertisement of a "poor *agunah*": her husband Motl Repitor took the money, jewelry, dresses and ran away, leaving her pregnant. His description: short, fair-haired, big blue eyes; rumor has it he left "along the line" Kaushany — Bendery — Akkerman. Telegrams are to be sent "to my *shvoger* Beril Weinberg"; in a footnote: "The cost of the telegrams is on me, Beril Weinberg"; the signature — "On behalf of the unfortunate woman — B. Weinberg".

**Check against the scan:** the recognized text of the newspaper found the article, but the quotation and translation were made **from the image**. The Yiddish version gave a detail that is not in the Hebrew one: the agunah is pregnant.

**What this gave:** Beril Weinberg in 1887 is a settled resident of Izmail, with means, connected with communal institutions. Beril and Berko are everyday forms of one Jewish name (Dov-Ber), so he is a candidate for Moisei's father; but a match of name and town is not yet proof. For the first time a person from the documents came alive.

**What remained open:** is the *shvoger* (brother-in-law, *svoyak*) her sister's husband or her husband's brother? The word does not settle this.

## Step 2. A correspondence of the same year

If the newspaper has an advertisement, there could also have been a note. A search by the runaway's surname (Repitor, in all its Yiddish spellings) over all years gave exactly three articles — all about this case.

**What was found:** "Yidishes folksblat", 18.VIII.1887, the heading "Correspondence", "Izmail (Bessarabia), 16 July 1887", signed "A guter bruder" ("A good brother"). The correspondent mocks the fashion of marrying **without a matchmaker**: "the cavalier of the current fashion" introduced himself to the bride's family as a decent man, received money, took the young wife away to where **his parents** lived, threw her out pregnant, and after the trial (he was ordered to pay 500 rubles a year) persuaded her to sell her things and ran away with the furniture, clothes and jewelry. The name is given: Motl Repitor, 26 years old.

**Check against the scan:** the correspondence was read from the image in full and fully translated; one faded word was marked as uncertain.

**What this changed:** Motl is an outsider, his parents live not in Izmail; the bride is "from a decent family" in Izmail. This favors the reading "Beril is her sister's husband" rather than "her husband's brother". **The second document changed the interpretation of the first** — which is why the first guess should not have been written into the tree at once.

## Step 3. The marriage record of 1895: "also known as Repetur"

The surname Repitor is rare. The next step is the indexes of the metrical books of Bessarabia on JewishGen. "Repitor" — zero. But a similar record turned up: a marriage in Chisinau on 23.V.1895, the groom from Izmail — "VAYNBERG / REPETUR Khuna".

**Check against the scan — and a correction of the index.** From the index it was first read as "Khuna, son of Abram". The scan of the marriage book (NARM [National Archives of the Republic of Moldova] 211-11-238, record no. 149, FamilySearch, frame 841) showed: «Холостъ Измаильскій житель **Хуна Михелевъ Вайнбергъ /онъ же Репетуръ/**» ["Bachelor, resident of Izmail, Khuna Mikhelev Weinberg /also known as Repetur/"], the bride — Dvosya, daughter of the Chisinau meshchanin (townsman) **Abram** Rosenberg. Abram is the bride's father, not the groom's. The index mixed up the fields.

**Confirmation from an independent source:** the directory "Vsya Rossiya" of 1900 — «Репетур (Вайнберг) Хуна Михел.» ["Repetur (Weinberg) Khuna Mikhel."].

**What this gave:** it is proven that Repetur is the second name of one of the Izmail Weinberg families (the line of Mikhel). This is a strong sign: such a pairing is rare.

**What remained a hypothesis:** whether "Repitor" from the newspaper and "Repetur" from the metrical record are one name. There is no document that would link them. Therefore only the fact of the marriage of 1895 goes into the tree; the link to the runaway of 1887 goes into the hypotheses file.

⚠️ **The trap of expectation.** In another record of the same circle, the scribe's «Михель Кр…торъ» was very tempting to read as «Репиторъ». Under magnification it turned out to be «Кройторъ». When you are looking for a specific name, the eye "completes" it — enlarge and compare the letters with neighboring records by the same scribe.

## Step 4. The signature under the article of 1884

Further searching in the same newspaper (surname + town, neighboring years) found the article "Down with the mask!" ("Yidishes folksblat", 26.III.1884) with the signature **"Eliezer Moshkov Weinberg. Izmail, March 1884"**. In the annual index of correspondents for 1883 the same author ("Weinberg, E. Moshkov, from Izmail") is listed with one more note.

**Check against the scan:** the signature was read from the image. "Moshkov" is a patronymic in Yiddish: son of Moshe.

**What this gave:** in Izmail of the 1880s there lived a literate author Eliezer (Leizer), son of Moshe Weinberg. A new person — and a new thread.

## Step 5. The death certificate in Los Angeles

Among natives of Izmail in American documents, Leizer Weinberg, who died in Los Angeles in 1940, was already known. According to the American documents, above all the death certificate: born in Izmail around 1858; father — Morris (the American form of the name Moshe); occupation — teacher of languages (25 years).

**Check against the scan:** the father's name and place of birth in the death certificate must be read from the image of the form, not from the index: indexes of American documents also make mistakes (see [Emigration](../guide/2-reference/13-emigration.md)).

**Comparison:**

| Feature | Newspaper 1884 | Certificate 1940 |
|---|---|---|
| Name | Eliezer | Leizer (Leizer is an everyday form of Eliezer) |
| Father | Moshkov — son of Moshe | Morris = Moshe |
| Town | Izmail | born in Izmail |
| Era | 1883–1884, an adult author | born around 1858 — in 1884 he was about 26 |
| Occupation | writes articles | teacher of languages |

**Conclusion:** a strong link — five features match, and the sources were created independently (a 19th-century Jewish newspaper and a 20th-century American form). But there is no direct document saying "this is one person"; the data on the death certificate was supplied by an informant who was a relative (secondary information). Therefore the honest status is **a hypothesis with a strong basis**, not "proven"; such a conclusion is worth formalizing as a written argument (see [Standard of proof](../guide/3-results/02-standard-of-proof.md)). How Leizer is connected with Berko has not yet been established.

## What this example teaches

1. **Place matters more than the surname.** A search for "Weinberg" without Izmail would have given thousands of matches; the pairing "surname + town" — a few dozen.
2. **One find suggests the next:** the runaway's name → a search by it → the index of metrical books → a double surname; neighboring issues of the newspaper → a new author → an American document.
3. **An index is a hint.** The index mixed up the bride's father with the groom's father; only the scan caught the error.
4. **A second document can overturn the interpretation of the first** (the correspondence changed the meaning of the word *shvoger*).
5. **Not everything is proven.** Honest statuses — "proven", "strong hypothesis", "open" — matter more than a pretty story.
6. **Every step is recorded at once:** archival reference, frame, quotation, translation, confidence of reading ([../templates/finding.md](../templates/finding.md)); empty results go into the research log (for example, "Repitor" in JewishGen — 0).
