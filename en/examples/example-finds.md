# Example finds: what you can actually find and where

What this section covers: what a find looks like on each type of site, how much it gives and where the trap is. All the examples are from one real research project on Jewish families of Podolia and Bessarabia and one Russian family (September–October 2026). The names are historical, of people who died long ago.

---

## The Jewish press — the National Library of Israel (NLI)
- **An advertisement of 1887** (in "Ha-Tsfira" in Hebrew and "Yidishes folksblat" in Yiddish): an abandoned wife is looking for her runaway husband (she is an *agunah* — without a divorce she cannot marry again); on behalf of the family it is signed by her *shvoger* (brother-in-law) Beril Weinberg of Izmail. → For the first time the great-grandfather appeared as a living person — with relatives and a community.
- **A correspondence "Izmail, 16 July 1887"** in Yiddish — a whole story of a marriage without a matchmaker, a court case and a flight; fully translated.
- **The signature under an article of 1884:** "Eliezer Moshkov Weinberg. Izmail, March 1884" — linked the American emigrant Leizer Weinberg (died in Los Angeles) with Izmail and gave the name of his father.
- **How to find your way:** search by a word in Hebrew and Yiddish (the town in several spellings: איזמאיל, איזמאהיל), annual indexes of correspondents, the rubrics "Correspondence" and "Advertisements". Recognition of the old type makes mistakes — check the key word on the image.
- **Trap:** Cloudflare blocks the site at frequent requests — work slowly.

How these finds link to one another — [case-study-newspaper-to-family.md](case-study-newspaper-to-family.md).

## FamilySearch — films of archival books
- **Revision lists of Akkerman of 1835 and 1859** — a whole family with ages; the link between the revisions is through the column "by the last revision".
- **The book of birth records of the Jews of Izmail, 1883** — detailed records with witnesses and their signatures in Hebrew: this is how "Shmil Weinberg, uncle of the newborn" was found.
- **Birth books of Chișinău 1855–1869** — with control records from the JewishGen index.
- **The marriage index of Chișinău 1855–1869** — it shows that "Berko-Duvid" is a double name, not "son of Duvid" (for other grooms the patronymics are written with "-ov").
- **How to find your way:** catalog → the film you need → frame number; books are often hidden inside "foreign" collections (the Jewish book of Izmail of 1883 is listed in the collection of Orthodox metrical books of the uezd).
- **Traps:** an index ≠ a document; some films are available only in FamilySearch centers; a CAPTCHA after about 45–150 opened frames (details — [Access, CAPTCHAs, pace](../guide/2-reference/19-access-captchas-pace.md)).

## JewishGen — index databases
- **A marriage of 1895 in Chișinău:** "Huna Mikhelev Weinberg /also Repetur/" (Хуна Михелевъ Вайнбергъ /онъ же Репетуръ/) — a double surname that echoes the mysterious surname from the newspaper of 1887.
- **Jewish evacuees in Tashkent** (cards of 1942) — a family from Proskurov with patronymics, occupations, an address.
- **How to find your way:** first the index, then the scan at the indicated frame; search by phonetics and by town.
- **Traps:** only the first 50 rows are visible — narrow the query; fields in the index can be mixed up ("daughter of Duvid" turned out to be the groom's father; "son of Abram" — the bride's father).

## University and foreign archives
- **Vienna, the university archive (PHAIDRA):** the form of a medical student of 1889 — "Vater: Berco, Kaufmann, Ismail" — the name, occupation and town of the father.
- **Tartu (Dorpat / Yuryev), Album Academicum:** date of birth, gymnasium, the student's estate ("of the sons of merchants").
- **Antwerp, police files of immigrants:** a native of Izmail born about 1828, his wife and daughter (the link with the family is a hypothesis).
- **USA:** a death certificate (Los Angeles, 1940) — place of birth and the father's name; border-crossing cards (Nogales, 1926).
- **How to find your way:** look for where the person studied, served, emigrated — that is where the forms with parents are.

## Directories of the era (archive.org, Google Books, NEB, Yandex "Archive Search", Genealogy Indexer)
- **"All Russia" 1912:** a timber dealer in a village of Podolia guberniya — the great-great-grandfather's partner in a sawmill.
- **"The Personnel of the Yuryev University" 1893:** the student's number and patronymic — the key to searching other databases. It was found only by searching a phrase in pre-reform spelling.
- **The "Russian Medical List"** (annual): where a doctor served year by year.
- **Trap:** recognition "eats" the first letter and confuses letters — search without the first letter too; a zero in a full-text search proves nothing.

## Scans of archival files on Wikimedia Commons (Ukrainian archives)
- **The revision of 1850 for Ushitsa uezd** (DAKhmO — State Archive of Khmelnytskyi Oblast — 226-79-5224): family No. 237 — the first documentary step of the line.
- **The census of 1811 for Dunaivtsi:** three brothers are entered under three different nicknames (Shefel, Gruz, Krotnik) — a lesson: before the 1820s search by patronymic, not by surname.
- **ZAGS (Soviet civil registry) of the 1920s–1930s** (Proskurov, Kyiv, rural districts): dates and addresses.
- **How to find your way:** the file's inventory (Wikisource or the archive's site) → the exact file name → download it whole, not the preview.

## War, evacuation, siege
- **"People's Memory" / OBD "Memorial":** one person in five documents (called up at 16, regiment, captivity and liberation, wound) — identified by year and place of birth despite different spellings of the surname and patronymic.
- **Yad Vashem, evacuation cards:** proved the patronymics of two family members where there are no metrical books.
- **The Memorial Book "Siege"** (RNB): the grandmother's parents, both died in 1942, one address; the mother's surname is distorted beyond recognition and stands in a different volume.
- **An oblast archive (EAIS), the register "Movement of children":** the evacuation of a child from Leningrad.
- **How to find your way:** search by year and place of birth, try the spellings and patronymics; distorted surnames in the siege books are common.

## Cemeteries
- **JOWBR (JewishGen):** a grave in Bender (1873–1927) confirmed the years of life of a person known from metrical books.
- **Mitzvat Emet, Toldot:** photos of monuments; in Hebrew — the father's name ("ben …").

## What these examples teach
1. The strongest finds are **not where you search for the surname**, but where the person left a trace on another occasion: study, a newspaper, a court, emigration, the war.
2. **Each database gives a piece**, and only comparing several turns it into proof.
3. **Errors of reading and of indexes are the norm**; checking against the image is obligatory.
