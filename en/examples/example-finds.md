# Example finds: what you can actually find and where

What this section covers: what a find looks like on each type of site, how much it gives and where the trap is. All the examples come from one real study of Jewish families of Podolia and Bessarabia and one Russian family (September–October 2026). The names are historical, of people who died long ago.

---

## The Jewish press — National Library of Israel (NLI)
- **An advertisement of 1887** (in "Ha-Tsfira" in Hebrew and "Yidishes folksblat" in Yiddish): an abandoned wife is looking for her husband who ran away (she is an *agunah* — without a divorce she cannot remarry); the signature on behalf of the family is her *shvoger* (brother-in-law, *svoyak*) Beril Weinberg of Izmail. → For the first time the great-grandfather appeared as a living person — with relatives and a community.
- **A correspondence "Izmail, 16 July 1887"** in Yiddish — a whole story of a marriage without a matchmaker, a court and an escape; fully translated.
- **A signature under an article of 1884:** "Eliezer Moshkov Weinberg. Izmail, March 1884" — linked the American emigrant Leizer Weinberg (died in Los Angeles) with Izmail and gave the name of his father.
- **How to find your way:** search by a word in Hebrew and Yiddish (the town in several spellings: איזמאיל, איזמאהיל), annual indexes of correspondents, the headings "Correspondence" and "Advertisements". The recognition of the old typeface makes mistakes — check the key word on the image.
- **Trap:** Cloudflare blocks the site at frequent requests — work slowly.

How these finds hook onto one another — [case-study-newspaper-to-family.md](case-study-newspaper-to-family.md).

## FamilySearch — films of archival books
- **Revision lists of Akkerman of 1835 and 1859** — a whole family with ages; the link between revisions is through the column "per the last revision".
- **The book of birth records of the Jews of Izmail of 1883** — detailed records with witnesses and their signatures in Hebrew: this is how "Shmil Weinberg, uncle of the newborn" was found.
- **Birth books of Chisinau 1855–1869** — with control records from the JewishGen index.
- **The marriage index of Chisinau 1855–1869** — it shows that "Berko-Duvid" is a double name and not "son of Duvid" (for other grooms the patronymics are written with "-ov").
- **How to find your way:** catalog → the needed film → frame number; books are often hidden inside "other people's" collections (the Jewish book of Izmail of 1883 is listed in the collection of Orthodox metrical books of the uezd).
- **Traps:** an index ≠ a document; some films only at FamilySearch centers; a CAPTCHA after ~45–150 opened frames (more often about 130 in 40 minutes; earlier as time goes on).

## JewishGen — index databases
- **A marriage of 1895 in Chisinau:** "Khuna Mikhelev Weinberg /also known as Repetur/" — a double surname that resonates with the mysterious surname from the newspaper of 1887.
- **Jewish evacuees in Tashkent** (cards of 1942) — a family from Proskurov with patronymics, occupations, an address.
- **How to find your way:** first the index, then the scan at the frame indicated; search by phonetics and by town.
- **Traps:** only the first 50 rows are visible — narrow the query; fields in the index can be mixed up ("daughter of Duvid" turned out to be the groom's father; "son of Abram" — the bride's father).

## University and foreign archives
- **Vienna, university archive (PHAIDRA):** the form of a medical student of 1889 — "Vater: Berco, Kaufmann, Ismail" — the father's name, occupation and town.
- **Tartu (Dorpat / Yuryev), Album Academicum:** date of birth, gymnasium, the student's estate ("из сыновей купцов" ["from the sons of merchants"]).
- **Antwerp, police files on immigrants:** a native of Izmail born around 1828, his wife and daughter (the link to the family is a hypothesis).
- **USA:** a death certificate (Los Angeles, 1940) — place of birth and the father's name; border-crossing cards (Nogales, 1926).
- **How to find your way:** look for where the person studied, served, emigrated — that is where the forms with parents are.

## Directories of the era (archive.org, Google Books, NEB, Yandex "Archive Search", Genealogy Indexer)
- **"Vsya Rossiya" (All Russia) 1912:** a timber dealer in a village of Podolia guberniya — the great-great-grandfather's partner in a sawmill.
- **"Lichnyi sostav Yuryevskogo universiteta" (Personnel of the Yuryev University) 1893:** the student number and patronymic — the key to searching other databases. Found only by a phrase search in pre-reform spelling.
- **"Rossiiskii meditsinskii spisok" (Russian Medical List)** (annual): where a doctor served year after year.
- **Trap:** recognition "eats" the first letter and confuses letters — search without the first letter too; a zero in a full-text search proves nothing.

## Scans of archive files on Wikimedia Commons (Ukrainian archives)
- **The 1850 revision for Ushitsa uezd** (DAKhmO [State Archive of Khmelnytskyi Oblast] 226-79-5224): family no. 237 — the first documentary step of the family line.
- **The 1811 census for Dunaevtsy:** three brothers recorded under three different nicknames (Shefel, Gruz, Krotnik) — a lesson: before the 1820s search by patronymic, not by surname.
- **ZAGS (civil registry) of the 1920s–1930s** (Proskurov, Kyiv, rural districts): dates and addresses.
- **How to find your way:** the file inventory (Vikidzherela [Ukrainian Wikisource] or the archive's site) → the exact name of the file → download it whole, not the preview.

## War, evacuation, siege
- **"Pamyat naroda" (People's Memory) / OBD "Memorial":** one person in five documents (called up at 16, regiment, captivity and liberation, wound) — identified by year and place of birth despite different spellings of the surname and patronymic.
- **Yad Vashem, evacuation cards:** proved the patronymics of two members of the family where there are no metrical books.
- **The memorial book "Blokada" (RNB [National Library of Russia]):** a grandmother's parents, both died in 1942, one address; the mother's surname is distorted beyond recognition and stands in a different volume.
- **Oblast archive (EAIS), the register "Dvizhenie detei" (Movement of children):** the evacuation of a child from Leningrad.
- **How to find your way:** search by year and place of birth, go through spellings and patronymics; distorted surnames in the siege books are commonplace.

## Cemeteries
- **JOWBR (JewishGen):** a grave in Bendery (1873–1927) confirmed the dates of a person's life from the metrical books.
- **"Mitzvat Emet", Toldot:** photos of monuments; in Hebrew — the father's name ("ben …").

## What these examples teach
1. The strongest finds are **not where you look for the surname**, but where the person left a trace on another occasion: study, a newspaper, a court, emigration, war.
2. **Each database gives a piece**, and only comparing several turns it into proof.
3. **Reading errors and index errors are the norm**; checking against the image is mandatory.
