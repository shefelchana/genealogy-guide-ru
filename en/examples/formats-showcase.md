# Formats showcase: 13 ways to show and store a family research project

> **A teaching example.** An invented Shapiro family; all names, dates and archival references are made up (references are marked [invented]). The archives, record types, websites and methods are real.

**[Open the interactive showcase →](https://shefelchana.github.io/genealogy-guide-ru/en/examples/formats-showcase-shapiro.html)** — with filters, a tree, a map and profile cards.

What this section is about: once you have many findings, you need somewhere to keep them and some way to show them — to yourself, to relatives, to other researchers. Below are 13 formats: what each is, why, and where to make it. Each one is shown in the interactive showcase on one invented family.

**Where to start.** You do not need all 13. Two are a must — the source catalogue (2) and the document library (12): without them there is nothing to build on. The rest depends on the task.

---

## 1. How genealogists work: the cycle

Question → version → where the document must be → search → check against the scan → level of evidence → record. The showcase walks through one pass of the cycle ("why is a great-great-grandfather registered in another town's community") and six methods with examples: a cluster of witnesses and neighbours, a one-house study, a one-name study, zero with coverage, comparing sources, DNA against the tree.

More: [Search route](../guide/1-ai-search/01-search-route.md), [False zero](../guide/1-ai-search/09-false-zero.md).

## 2. Source catalogue

A table of every source: reference, year, type, who, what it gave, level, where the scan is, where the conclusion is recorded, date checked. Filters by line and type; a separate "zero with coverage" button — a negative result is a source too.

Where to make it: Google Sheets or Excel; Notion / Airtable if you need "source ↔ person" links; Zotero for citations. Template: [Source register](../templates/source-register.md).

## 3. People catalogue

One card per person: line colour, years, role, and the level of the **link** ("son of…"), not of the person as a whole. This shows who rests on family memory alone.

Where to make it: tree sites (MyHeritage, Geni, FamilySearch), a Notion database in gallery view, notes in Obsidian. Template: [Persons](../templates/persons.md).

## 4. A person profile, like on tree sites

Photo, key facts, timeline, family, sources, open questions. The difference from a usual profile: every timeline line has a reference and a level, and disputed points go to "Open questions".

More: [Tree platforms](../guide/2-reference/16-tree-platforms.md), template [Timeline](../templates/timeline.md).

## 5. The tree

A 4-generation pedigree chart (colour = line) and a "ladder" of one direct line going back, with a level on each step. Other variants: fan chart, descendant chart, hourglass, a tree with centimorgans, a hypothesis tree with dashed links. Rule: leave unknown ancestors as "?" rather than filling them in with a name from a legend.

Where to make it: Gramps, MyHeritage, Family Echo, Topola (GEDCOM viewer).

## 6. Migrations

A diagram at real coordinates: points are places, arcs are family routes, filter by line, hover to see who was there and when. Variants: maps by year ("where they lived in 1897 / 1926 / 1941"), animation, one map per person.

Where to make it: Google My Maps (a layer per line), kepler.gl, Felt. More: [Places and maps](../guide/2-reference/06-places-maps.md).

## 7. Ethnicity estimate, checked against the tree

Test percentages next to the expectation from the tree: one grandparent ≈ 25 % ± 3–4. Shares under 2–5 % are at the noise level; a component's name is the company's reference panel, not the ancestor's people.

More: [DNA](../guide/2-reference/15-dna.md).

## 8. Comparisons: an evidence matrix

When documents disagree (three names for a mother, two death dates, three patronymics), put them in one table: who made it, how long after the event, original or retold — and decide by weight, not by count.

More: [Standard of proof](../guide/3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md), [Names, surnames, spellings](../guide/2-reference/03-names-spellings.md).

## 9. Photos and personal stories

"Chapters of a life": short chapters with a date, each saying how it is known. A family saying goes in as a quote marked 💭 with a plan to check it. Every photo gets a caption: who, when, where, who identified, where the original is.

More: [Old photographs](../guide/2-reference/17-photographs.md), [Family history](../guide/3-results/02-family-history.md).

## 10. A surname origin certificate

The look of a commercial certificate, but honest: the meaning from a dictionary, what does **not** follow from it, spellings, the earliest record in the family, a practical conclusion ("a common surname — you need a second feature"). Sources of meanings: Beider (Jewish surnames), Unbegaun, Nikonov, Fedosyuk (Russian).

## 11. Coat of arms or a family emblem

In the Russian Empire coats of arms belonged to noble families; townspeople, merchants and peasants had none, so a "family coat of arms" from an online shop is invented. To check: the "General Armorial of the Noble Families of the All-Russian Empire". Instead of arms — a family emblem made of what is known: the surname's meaning, an ancestor's trade, a place. Each part is labelled with its level.

Other "family themes": a family palette and font, a bookplate, a map poster, a calendar of memorial dates. More: [Ideas for a family history](../guide/3-results/05-ideas-for-family-history.md).

## 12. A document library

One folder scheme (findings log, persons, hypotheses, source catalogue, archive by line, photos "originals / working", backup), one file-naming rule — `Archive_fond-opis-file_year_who_what.ext`, a metadata card for every document and its path: found in an index → scan downloaded → read → checked against the frame → recorded → level set. The 3-2-1 rule: three copies, two different media, one away from home.

More: [Keeping files](../guide/0-start/02-keeping-files.md), [Ethics and preservation](../guide/3-results/03-ethics-preservation.md).

## 13. What to choose

| Format | Effort | Made with | For whom |
|---|---|---|---|
| Source catalogue | ●○○ | a spreadsheet | everyone, from day one |
| Document library | ●○○ | folders + naming rule | everyone, from day one |
| Tree and line ladder | ●○○ | Gramps, MyHeritage | everyone |
| Evidence matrix | ●○○ | a 3-column table | whenever documents disagree |
| People catalogue and profile | ●●○ | Notion, Obsidian, a tree site | once you have 30+ people |
| Migration map | ●●○ | Google My Maps | to tell relatives |
| DNA against the tree | ●●○ | table + 50/25/12.5 | after a test |
| Chapters of a life and photos | ●●○ | text, Tropy | for the family |
| Surname certificate, emblem | ●●● | Canva, Figma, HTML | a gift |
