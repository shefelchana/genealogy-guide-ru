# A worked example for beginners: from family memory to the first verified find

> **A teaching example.** An invented family, all names are made up; the sources and steps are real. The record found in step 6 is also invented: this is how it would look in a real case. Archival references marked [checked] are real files, opened by the compiler on 08.10.2026.

What this section covers: one short path on an Orthodox peasant family — from a grandmother's story to a record verified against the scan, and a step back one generation. How to do it in a browser (level 1) and what a chat and files add (level 2). The levels are in [Three levels](../guide/0-start/00-three-levels.md); the reference on sources is in [Orthodox, peasants and other non-Jewish lines](../guide/2-reference/21-orthodox-and-peasants.md).

Don't read Russian? The scans in this example are in pre-reform Russian script; see [For English speakers](../guide/0-start/08-for-english-speakers.md).

---

## Step 0. What is known at home

Grandmother Nina says: her grandfather **Stepan Karpovich Melnik** "was born near Rovno, in a village, in a peasant family, around 1905", and lived in Kharkiv after the war. At home there is his death certificate of 1968: "year of birth 1905, place of birth — the village of Zarechye, Rovno oblast".

We enter it in a table, honestly stating where each thing is known from:

| Who | What | From where |
|---|---|---|
| Stepan Karpovich Melnik | b. 1905, village of Zarechye | death certificate 1968 (a document) |
| his father Karp | the name — from the patronymic | the patronymic in the certificate |
| the family — peasants | estate | grandmother Nina's story, 2026 |

A death certificate is a document, but the birth details in it were entered from the relatives' words, 63 years later. It is a hint, not proof ([Standard of proof](../guide/3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md)).

## Step 1. One question

Bad: "find out everything about great-grandfather Stepan". Good:

> **Who were the parents of Stepan Karpovich Melnik, b. about 1905 in the village of Zarechye?** Check the metrical books and confession lists of his parish for 1903–1913 and stop.

## Step 2. The place in the year of the event

"Rovno oblast" is a Soviet name. In 1905 this was the **Russian Empire, Volhynia guberniya, Rovno uezd**. In 1921–1939 the same places belonged to Poland. Two conclusions follow:
- look for pre-revolutionary metrical books **under Volhynia guberniya**, and the copies of the metrical books of the Volhynia diocese lie in the archive of the former guberniya capital: DAZhO (State Archive of Zhytomyr Oblast), fond 1 "Volhynia Spiritual Consistory" (Волынская духовная консистория), 1741–1921 [checked: Wikisource];
- there may be several identical "Zarechyes" in the guberniya: check the volost and uezd in the "Lists of inhabited places" and on old maps ([Places and maps](../guide/2-reference/06-places-maps.md)).

**The parish.** Peasants are entered in the books of a church, not of a village. To learn which church Zarechye belonged to, we open the clergy rolls (*klirovye vedomosti*) of the uezd: Commons has "DAZhO 1-78-504. 1915. Clergy rolls of Orthodox churches of the 1st district of Rovno uezd" (ДАЖО 1-78-504. 1915. Клірові відомості православних церков 1-го округу Ровенського повіту) and, for the same year, the 5th district (1-78-506) [checked]. In each roll, part III "Parish roll" (Ведомость о приходе) lists the villages and hamlets of the parish with the number of households. We page through until we meet Zarechye. In our invented case it is assigned to the church of a neighboring village — let us call it "the Intercession church of the village of Vysokoe".

In part I of the same roll there are lines "copies of the metrical books are kept intact since … year" and "confession registers are kept intact since … year" [checked]. We write them down: this shows what could have survived in the church itself.

## Step 3. Which documents must exist

| Document | What it should show | Where the copy is |
|---|---|---|
| Metrical record of birth, 1905 | date, parents, estate, godparents | the church's book; the copy — in the consistory (DAZhO fond 1) |
| Confession lists 1906–1913 | household, father, mother, children with ages | the consistory fond |
| Clergy roll | the parish, the survival of the books | the consistory fond |
| Revision X (1858) | Stepan's grandfather and great-grandfather | the fond of the Volhynia Treasury Chamber (DAZhO fond 118) [checked: Commons] |

If we find nothing, it will be "not found in such-and-such volumes", not "does not exist" ([False zero](../guide/1-ai-search/09-false-zero.md)).

## Step 4. Where it is online

1. **Wikimedia Commons**, a search by file title: `ДАЖО 1-74 Сповідні Ровенського`, `ДАЖО 1-78 Метричні Ровенського`. On 08.10.2026 such a search found for Rovno uezd metrical books only up to 1866, confession lists of 1912–1913 and clergy rolls of 1915 [checked]. The 1905 metrical books are not among them.
2. **"Kachynyi Inspektor"** (Качиний Інспектор; [inspector.duckarchive.com](https://inspector.duckarchive.com/)): enter the archive DAZhO and fond 1 — it shows where the files of the fond have been posted, including on FamilySearch [checked: site]. An empty answer does not mean there is no scan.
3. **FamilySearch**, the [catalog](https://www.familysearch.org/search/catalog) by place (Volhynia, Rovno, Rivne — in all spellings): is there a film of consistory metrical books for 1905? The order "catalog → film → frame" is in [Archives by country](../guide/2-reference/07-archives-by-country.md).

While the metrical record is not found, we turn to a substitute: **the confession list of 1912** will show seven- or eight-year-old Stepan together with his parents. The 1912 volumes are divided alphabetically by village; "Zarechye" begins with Z (З) — so the file is "DAZhO 1-74-211. 1912. Confession lists of Orthodox churches of Rovno uezd. Villages Z–O" (ДАЖО 1-74-211. 1912. Сповідні відомості…Села З-О): 1,105 pages, about 1.7 MB per page — reads well [checked].

**Level 1 — only a browser.** The file weighs almost 2 GB, and you do not need to download it whole. On the file's page on Commons there is a page switcher: look at the pages one by one. First find where the roll of the church you need begins — each church has a title page with its name. Step by 20–30 pages, then refine.

**Level 2 — a chat and three files.** You save the pages you need one by one as images, give them to the chat to read, and transfer the result to the findings log and the research log. You start a new chat with the "Continue from here" file ([template](../templates/continue-from-here.md)).

## Step 5. A request to the AI

At level 1, with a browser plug-in (for example, Claude in Chrome), when the file on Commons is open:

> "These are the confession lists of the Orthodox churches of Rovno uezd for 1912. Help me find the pages where the roll of the Intercession church of the village of Vysokoe begins: open every 20th page and read the heading. When you find it, write out the page numbers of this church and find the hamlet of Zarechye in it. Write only what is visible on the page; mark anything uncertain with (?)."

When the page with Zarechye is found — a second request, now for reading:

> "First read the printed header of the table: what is in which column. Then write out all the households where there are people named Karp or Stepan: the household number, the names with relationships, the age, the mark about confession — verbatim, in pre-reform orthography."

The AI's answer is a hint. You check the page number, the household and the age with your own eyes.

## Step 6. Checking against the scan

The invented find: page 412, household 41:

> "Мельникъ Карпъ Ивановъ — 34; жена его Евдокія Петрова — 31; сынъ ихъ Стефанъ — 7" (Melnik Karp, son of Ivan — 34; his wife Evdokiya, daughter of Petr — 31; their son Stefan — 7) — he did not attend confession "because of being a minor" (по малолѣтству).

We check rather than rejoice:
- **the header:** "7" stands in the column "years from birth", male — it is an age, not a person's number;
- **the identifiers:** the name Stepan (in the document — Stefan, the church form), the father Karp — matches the patronymic, age 7 in 1912 → b. about 1905 — matches the death certificate, the same village. The identifiers are taken from two independent sources;
- **look-alikes:** we go through the whole of Zarechye — is there a second Stepan Karpovich of a suitable age? No;
- **a spare check:** the roll of 1913 — Stepan should be 8. The 1913 volumes are divided differently ("Villages A–G", "L–R", "S–Ya" and volumes without a mark), so first we look for the volume that holds the letter Z.

The status of the link "Stepan from household 41 is our Stepan, son of Karp and Evdokiya": 🟢 after the whole village has been looked through and there is no look-alike; until then — 🟡 ([status scale](../guide/3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md)).

## Step 7. An entry in the log

The findings log (after the [template](../templates/finding.md)):

> **Question:** who were the parents of Stepan Karpovich Melnik, b. about 1905?
> **Source:** DAZhO 1-74-211, PDF p. 412 (invented), the Intercession church of the village of Vysokoe, hamlet of Zarechye, household 41.
> **Verbatim:** "Мельникъ Карпъ Ивановъ — 34; жена его Евдокія Петрова — 31; сынъ ихъ Стефанъ — 7".
> **Confidence of the reading:** confident. **What this changes:** the parents — Karp Ivanovich (~1878) and Evdokiya Petrovna (~1881). **Checked against the scan:** yes.

The research log — empty lines too:

| Date | Where | What was sought | How | Coverage | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2026-10-08 | Commons, file search DAZhO fond 1 | metrical books of Rovno uezd 1903–1907 | search by title | all files with "Ровенського" in the title | no; metrical books only up to 1866 |
| 2026-10-08 | Commons, DAZhO 1-74-211 | Melnik, Karp, Stepan | page by page | the whole roll of the church of the village of Vysokoe | household 41 (invented) |

## Step 8. What next: a generation back

- **Karp** (~1878). His metrical records of the 1870s cannot be found on Commons by title — we check FamilySearch and "Kachynyi Inspektor". And his father **Ivan** should be in **the revision of 1858**: Commons has "DAZhO 118-14-0251" and "118-14-0256. 1858. Revision lists of landlords' peasants of Rovno uezd" (Ревізькі казки поміщичих селян Рівненського повіту) [checked]. Lists of landlords' peasants are grouped by owner, so first find out whose Zarechye was in 1858 (the charter deed, descriptions of estates, the VGD forum for the uezd).
- Having found Ivan in 1858, go down by the column "by the last revision" to 1850 and 1834 — **strictly step by step** ([Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](../guide/2-reference/01-records-empire-ussr.md), section 3).
- Ivan's marriage, if it was before 1866, look for in the consistory metrical books of the uezd on Commons: they exist for 1841–1866, not for every year [checked]. Remember: one volume — all the churches of the uezd for a year.
- **Level 2** pays off here: the research log shows which volumes have already been looked through, and the hypotheses file shows what is still 🟡.

**If the family is from Russia rather than Ukraine,** the steps are the same: the place in the year of the event → the parish → the consistory fond by [D. Kudinov's catalog](https://metrics.tilda.ws/) → the FamilySearch catalog, the website of the oblast archive or EAIS (the unified archival information system), Yandex "Archive Search". Many Russian sites do not open from abroad ([Access, CAPTCHAs, pace](../guide/2-reference/19-access-captchas-pace.md)).

## What this example teaches

1. **The place in the year of the event** determines the archive: the Volhynian metrical books lie in Zhytomyr, not in Rovno.
2. **First the parish**, then the books: a clergy roll saves hours of paging.
3. **No metrical record — there is a substitute**: a confession list shows the whole family.
4. **A match is checked for look-alikes** across the whole village, not at the first suitable line.
5. **Write everything down**, including "no", with the coverage.
