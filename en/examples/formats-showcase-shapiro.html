A showcase of family research formats

Ways to show and store a family research project, from a source catalogue to a surname certificate. Everything is built on one invented family, the Shapiros. Under each format: where to make it and where the guide covers it in more detail.

A teaching example. An invented family: all names, dates and archival references are made up; references are marked [invented]. The archives, record types, websites and methods are real. The structure is taken from the compiler's own research.

01

How genealogists work

Not "find an ancestor" but a cycle: question → version → where the document must be → search → check against the scan → level of evidence → record. Below is one pass through the cycle.

QuestionWhy is Itsko Shapiro registered as a meshchanin of Zhytomyr in 1897, when the whole family is from Berdychiv?
VersionThe family was transferred to another town community
WhereTransfers went through the provincial Treasury Chamber → its fond in the regional archive
SearchThe fond's inventory (opis) online, by surname and town
CheckFile of 1912 [invented]: "on the transfer of Itsko Leibov Shapiro with family"
Level🟡 — found in the inventory, the file itself not read yet
RecordFindings log, hypothesis H-3, person profile

Cluster: friends, neighbours, witnesses

When the ancestor is "silent", look at the people next to him in documents.

Shmul Roitman is a guarantor in the 1912 file and the Shapiros' neighbour in the 1897 census ⇒ trace his family together with ours.

One-house study

All residents of one address over the years: who moved, who died, who was related.

The Savelyevs' Leningrad house: all residents from the Siege Book of Memory, communal flat 12 — which neighbours could be relatives.

One-name study

Collect everyone with the surname in one place and sort them into families.

There are hundreds of Shapiros in Berdychiv. Sort by patronymic, registration and children's names — or "found" will be someone else's family.

Zero with coverage

"Not found" means something only if you record what was read in full.

All 1905 births of the parish read — no Ivan Savelyev ⇒ the parish from family memory is probably wrong.

Comparing sources

Documents disagree — compare who was closer to the event.

Naum's mother: Khaya, Khana or Anna? → section 08.

DNA against the tree

Work out the expected share from the tree and compare with the test.

One Jewish grandparent ⇒ expected 25 %, test 24.3 % — fits. → section 07.

Where to make it

A cycle diagram in any diagram tool (Miro, Excalidraw) or right in your findings log.

People catalogue

A card index: one person — one card with the line colour, years, role and the level of the key link. You can see at once who rests on family memory alone.

Other kinds of trees

  • Fan chart — ancestors in half-circles; gaps show at once. In MyHeritage, Gramps, Geni.
  • Descendant chart — from one ancestor to all descendants, emigrant branches included.
  • Hourglass — the person in the middle, ancestors up, descendants down.
  • Tree with DNA — shared cM written next to each relative.
  • Hypothesis tree — 💭 links dashed, so they are not mixed with proven ones.

Where to make it

Gramps (free, its own reports and fan chart) · MyHeritage (poster print) · Family Echo (quick, in the browser) · Topola — a GEDCOM viewer.

Rule

Leave unknown ancestors as "?" rather than filling them in with a name from a legend.

Migrations

A diagram without a base map: points at real coordinates, arcs are family routes, colour = line. Click a line in the filter to show only it. Hover a point to see who was there and when.

What you see at once

07

Ethnicity estimate, checked against the tree

Test percentages are a model, not a measurement. It helps most to put the expectation worked out from the tree next to them. The black mark is the expectation, the hatching is the usual spread. The numbers are invented.

An invented company's test, full composition
Conclusion: one Jewish grandparent (Lev Naumovich — both his parents were Jewish) ⇒ expected 25 % ± ~3.3; the test shows 24.3 % — fits. "Finnish and Karelian" 3.1 % is at the noise level; it is compatible with the northern (Vologda) Savelyev line 💭 but is not proof. Another company may call a larger share "Russian" on the same data — that is a different reference panel, not a contradiction.

More formats

  • Chromosome painting (23andMe, GEDmatch) — which segment came from which side
  • A match table: cM, side, ancestral places
  • Two companies side by side

Comparisons: an evidence matrix

When documents disagree, put them in one table: who made it, how long after the event, original or retold. Decide by weight, not by count.

10

A surname origin certificate

Commercial certificates look nice but often say the same for everyone: "the surname comes from a nickname…". An honest version keeps the design but adds versions, levels and sources.

Certificate of surname origin
Shapiro
שפירא

Meaning

From the town of Speyer on the Rhine (in Jewish texts — Shpira). One of the oldest Ashkenazi surnames.

What it means for the family

A place-name surname passed down for centuries and is very widespread: the meaning does not show that the ancestors lived in Speyer or were related to famous Shapiros 💭.

Where bearers lived

Almost all over the Pale of Settlement; for our line — Berdychiv and Zhytomyr (revisions, 1897 census).

Earliest record in the family

Mordko Shapiro, Berdychiv, revision of 1816 🟡.

Spellings

Шапиро · Шапира · Спиро · Shapiro · Szapiro · Schapiro — search them all.

Practical conclusion

A common surname ⇒ "found a Shapiro" means nothing without a second feature: patronymic, place, relatives' names.

Kovalev
From koval — blacksmith (South Russian and Ukrainian). First a nickname or the father's name "Koval", then a surname with "-ev". The meaning tells you about a distant ancestor's nickname, not that the great-grandparents were blacksmiths 💭.

Where to find meanings

Beider (Jewish surnames) · Unbegaun, Nikonov, Fedosyuk (Russian) · Forebears.io (distribution). Avoid "meaning of your surname" generator sites.

Format

A framed A4 print, a PDF gift for relatives, the cover of a family book. More: Names, surnames, spellings.
11

Coat of arms or a family emblem

In the Russian Empire coats of arms belonged to noble families; townspeople, merchants and peasants had none, so a "family coat of arms" from an online shop is invented. You can honestly build a family emblem from what is known instead.

What each quarter means

  • ITown gate — Speyer, the origin of the surname Shapirosurname meaning 💭 · not the ancestors' home
  • IIPharmacy scales — Khaim Goldman's pharmacy in Odesadirectory, 1914 🟢
  • IIIHammer and horseshoe — "koval", the meaning of Kovalevsurname meaning 💭
  • IVWaves — the Neva, the Savelyevs' siege of LeningradBook of Memory, evacuation list 🟢

The motto on the ribbon is the main rule of research. Quarter colours are the line colours.

Other "family themes"

  • A family palette and font for all materials
  • A bookplate for the family book
  • A "where we come from" map poster
  • A calendar of memorial dates (birthdays and yahrzeits)

Where to check a real coat of arms

The "General Armorial of the Noble Families of the All-Russian Empire" — if the surname is not there, the family had no arms. Ideas: Ideas for a family history.

A document library: how to store

To find any scan in 10 seconds a year from now: one folder scheme, one file-naming rule, a metadata card and a document's path from "found in an index" to "recorded in the log".

Folders

Family/
├─ 00-FINDINGS-LOG.md      # all findings in order, §§
├─ 01-PERSONS.md           # one entry = one person
├─ 02-HYPOTHESES.md        # H-1, H-2… with statuses
├─ 03-SOURCES.xlsx         # source catalogue (section 02)
├─ Archive/
│  ├─ Shapiro/
│  │  ├─ DAZhO_118-14-702_1912_transfer-Itsko.pdf
│  │  └─ DAZhO_67-3-220_1899_birth-Naum_crop.png
│  ├─ Goldman/
│  ├─ Savelyev/
│  └─ DNA/               # raw files, exports
├─ Photos/
│  ├─ originals/          # TIFF, never edit
│  └─ working/            # JPG, crops, colourised
└─ Backup/               # + cloud + external drive

File name

Archive_fond-opis-file_year_who_what.ext

Example: TsAMO_loss-report_1943_Kovalev-F_sheet2.jpg. Reference first — easy to search; year next — the era is visible.

A document's path

found in an index→scan downloaded→read→checked against the frame→recorded in the log→level set

The 3-2-1 rule: three copies, on two different media, one away from home. Photo originals and raw DNA files are read-only.
