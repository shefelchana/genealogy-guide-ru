# Scenarios: what to do if…

A set of typical situations and the order of action. Each scenario: **when** — **steps** — **how to finish**.
A general rule for all: first write down what is already known and where you have searched (otherwise you will go round in circles), then one question, then the search.
The scenarios are for everyone who searches, with AI or without it. The same forks in the form of an instruction for AI are in the [`search-logic-navigator`](../../../skills/search-logic-navigator/SKILL.md) skill.

Don't read Russian? See [For English speakers](../0-start/08-for-english-speakers.md).

## A. Starting

### A1. "I know only my grandparents' names, nothing more"
1. Gather what is at home: photographs (inscriptions on the back!), documents, work record books, military IDs, birth/marriage/death certificates, letters, postcards with addresses.
2. Write down the family memory **verbatim**, marked "so says …". Especially: towns, occupations, wars, evacuation, "relatives abroad".
3. Find the **place**: where the grandparents were born (from passports, certificates, forms). For Jewish families — the shtetl and the uezd.
4. Check the Soviet layer (the most accessible): "Memory of the People" (Память народа) / OBD, Yad Vashem, evacuation cards, memorial books, the "Open List" (Открытый список), ZAGS scans (Ukraine on Commons), cemeteries.
5. From the oldest person with a known place, take a step back: the metrical books, censuses and revisions of that place.
**How to finish:** a table "person — dates — place — source" for everyone you know. More — [Where to start](../0-start/03-where-to-start.md). How to question relatives — [Talking to relatives](../0-start/04-talking-to-relatives.md); how to date a photograph without an inscription — [Old photographs](../2-reference/17-photographs.md).

### A2. "I have only a surname and an approximate region"
1. Check the surname in dictionaries (Beider for Jewish ones; regional dictionaries) — where it occurs.
2. Search the indexes: JewishGen (all databases, phonetically), FamilySearch, Geni/MyHeritage — **by region**, not across the whole country.
3. Write out all variants of spelling (Cyrillic, Latin, Yiddish/Hebrew, Polish, Romanian), including distortions.
4. Look for **clusters**: where the surname occurs many times, there is most likely the family nest.
**How to finish:** 1–3 candidates for the "family place" to check against documents. More — [Names, surnames, spellings](../2-reference/03-names-spellings.md), [Databases with indexes](../../catalog/indexed-databases.md).

### A3. "I am starting from the American (emigrant) branch"
See [Emigration](../2-reference/13-emigration.md). In short: death certificate → census → naturalisation → ship manifest → the name of the shtetl → the shtetl's documents. Check the names of fellow travellers and the destination address: they often lead to relatives.

## B. Dead ends

### B0. "I found a metrical record — how do I step back a generation"
1. **Write out everything from the record**, not only the name: father and mother with patronymics, the parents' ages (in a marriage and a death), estate and the father's **place of registration** (*pripiska*) ("meshchanin of shtetl N"), witnesses. The *pripiska* is the main key: revisions followed it.
2. **Look at all the surviving years of the same book**: brothers and sisters, deaths of infants, the parents' marriage. This gives the composition of the family and the approximate year of the marriage.
3. **Find the parents' marriage**: it gives ages and the fathers of the groom and bride — that is already the next generation. No record — check the books of neighbouring rabbinates or parishes and of the district town.
4. **Move on to the revision** at the father's place of registration: 1858 → 1850 → 1834… by the bridge of ages, strictly step by step ([Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](../2-reference/01-records-empire-ussr.md), section 3). Between a metrical record of the 1870s–1910s and the revision of 1858, family lists and the Jewish military census of 1875 help.
5. **Check every step with a second independent feature** (age, children's names, *pripiska*) and record it in the finds log with a status.
**How to finish:** the parents' parents with archival references and quotations — or an exact list of what else to look at. In detail — the [`tracing-revision-records`](../../../skills/tracing-revision-records/SKILL.md) skill.

### B1. "A person vanishes — absent from documents where they should be"
First check whether a step of the route was skipped: is it the right **place** (uezd and guberniya for the year), the right **source** (does the volume survive for these years, has it been filmed, is it in someone else's collection). Then, in turn:
1. **Spelling**: all variants, without the first letter (recognition eats it), with a different vowel; mixed-up letters in the handwriting (Б/В, Ш/Т, "-ель/-еръ").
2. **Name**: a double name recorded by half; the Russian form instead of the Jewish one (Berko → Boris, Srul → Israel); a second name. In Soviet documents — a new name (Khaim → Efim, Moishe → Mikhail) and a "reworked" patronymic of the children: see [Special groups and name changes](../2-reference/04-special-groups-name-changes.md), section 6. If the person was baptised or was a cantonist — the same page, sections 1–2.
3. **Estate and *pripiska***: search where the person was **registered**, not where they lived; meshchane and merchants are in different lists; guild artisans separately.
4. **Border and era**: at that moment the territory may have belonged to another state (southern Bessarabia 1856–1878 — Romania). Check which uezd and guberniya the place belonged to in the year needed, and whether there is a second place with the same name: [Places and maps](../2-reference/06-places-maps.md).
5. **Source**: perhaps the book needed did not survive or was not filmed — record that as a result, not as "not found".
6. **Absence inside a document** means something only if the person was obliged to be there (for example, a son who had transferred to another society is not listed in his father's household — and that is normal; the revisions of 1719, 1744 and 1811 did not record women). See [Standard of proof](../3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md), the section on "not found".
7. **Check the search itself on a control record** — a name or word that is certainly in this volume. If the control is not found, the search is broken, not the person absent. Other causes of a false zero (query address, synonym search, a catalogue that searches "by one word") — [The false zero](09-false-zero.md).
8. **Years**: look not at the year the family named but at **all surviving** years of the book or alphabet; write out all bearers of the surname. Age and year of birth in Soviet documents are sometimes shifted.
9. **There is a reference but no scan to be seen**: for Ukrainian archives check the reference in "Duck Inspector" (Качиний Інспектор) — whether the file is online and where ([Archives by country](../2-reference/07-archives-by-country.md)).
10. **The book may have gone to the neighbours**: the records of a shtetl are bound into the books of a neighbouring rabbinate, a parish or the district town; where there was no crown rabbi, the metrical books were handed to the *zemsky* court, sometimes of another uezd.
11. **No marriage record** is not an argument against a marriage: the couple may have married in another town or not registered. Look at the neighbouring books.
12. **Nothing under the surname, but the place is known**: write out the whole shtetl over a window of years and sort by the parents' names ([Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](../2-reference/01-records-empire-ussr.md), section 3).
**How to finish:** a line in the research log with coverage, spellings and method; if it is a zero — with an explanation of what it means.

### B2. "Many namesakes turned up — which one is mine?"
1. Make a table of candidates: name, patronymic, age, place, occupation, relatives. For each — a timeline: "the impossible" at once cuts out the extras ([Plan, timeline, place guide](../0-start/05-plan-timeline-place.md), section 3).
2. Cut out by **incompatible** features (age, patronymic, place).
3. Look for a **second independent feature** among those left: children's names, witnesses, address, profession.
4. Apply the naming custom: a child was named after a **deceased** relative — the children's names hint at the generation above.
5. Do not choose "the most similar" — if two are equal, keep both as hypotheses.
6. If there are many namesakes in one place, assemble each family whole and look for links between the families: [Assembling the whole family](../2-reference/09-whole-family.md).

### B3. "The document can't be read"
1. Take the **original scan**, not a preview; enlarge the column needed. Where to learn to read pre-reform cursive, Polish and Latin metrical records, Kurrent — [Handwriting, transliteration, dates](../2-reference/05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md).
2. Find the **table header** (which column means what; the age "by the last revision" ≠ the current age).
3. Compare the same hand in neighbouring records (how the scribe writes Р, К, Ш, Т).
4. Check your reading of the surname against dictionaries (for Jewish surnames — A. Beider's dictionaries): if such a form is in none of them, it is most likely a reading error. But the dictionaries do not cover every surname, and non-Jewish surnames need other dictionaries.
5. Look for a **second copy** of the document (metrical books and revisions existed in several copies) or a second filming.
6. Ask the community (forums, groups, ViewMate) or AI for a "second opinion" — and check anyway. How to word a request so that people answer — [Other people's trees, communities, researchers](../2-reference/18-communities-researchers.md), section 2. See [Groups and communities](../../reading/groups-and-communities.md), the [`reading-archive-scans`](../../../skills/reading-archive-scans/SKILL.md) skill.

### B3a. "I don't know in which file to look for the surname"
1. First an index, then page-turning: community surname lists, JewishGen databases, full-text corpora — [Community surname indexes](../2-reference/08-surname-indexes.md).
2. Check by the archival reference whether the file is online and where (for Ukraine — "Duck Inspector").
3. If the file was already read for another surname, finish reading the unread sections: this is the cheapest find.

### B4. "The index says one thing, the document another"
Believe the document. The index is a hint where to look. Record the discrepancy (it will help others) and cite the scan.

### B5. "All online sources have been gone through"
1. **Side lines**: brothers, sisters, brothers-in-law, witnesses at weddings, godparents/*mohelim* — their documents may contain yours.
2. **The "circle of acquaintances" (FAN — friends, associates, neighbours)**: neighbours in the house, business partners, guarantors — people moved in groups.
3. **Another type of source**: newspapers (advertisements, lists of donors), directories (merchants, doctors, lawyers), educational institutions, courts, notaries, the aliens police.
4. **Emigrant relatives**: American/European documents often name the shtetl and the parents.
5. **DNA**: a test and work with the matches (see [DNA](../2-reference/15-dna.md)).
6. **A letter to an archive** — when items 1–5 have been gone through and recorded: addressed to a specific archive, with the exact archival reference; you decide and send, AI can help with the text at your request (see [Requests and letters](../2-reference/20-requests-letters.md)). If you need a paid researcher on the spot — how to choose one and what to demand in the report: [Other people's trees, communities, researchers](../2-reference/18-communities-researchers.md), section 3.
7. **New digitisations**: check whether new inventories and scans have appeared (Commons, archive websites, FamilySearch, JewishGen additions).
8. **Set aside** and write down the open questions — in half a year new digitisations will appear.

### B5a. FamilySearch: "the year is not on the film", "film with a lock", "the book is not in the catalogue"
1. **The year is missing** — check the neighbouring films of the same collection, the second copy of the book (rabbinate or consistory) and record "year not filmed".
2. **A film with a lock**, or only the first frames open — it can be viewed in a FamilySearch centre; look for the same reference on Commons or on the archive's website.
3. **The book is not in the catalogue under the shtetl** — search by uezd and guberniya, in all spellings, and page through films of neighbouring collections: a book is sometimes inside someone else's (for example, among the Orthodox metrical books of the uezd).
In detail — [Archives by country](../2-reference/07-archives-by-country.md), the section "FamilySearch: catalogue → film → frame".

### B6. "The site won't let me in (CAPTCHA, block, geo-restriction)"
1. Stop — do not repeat requests one after another.
2. Pass the CAPTCHA yourself; work more slowly (one frame every 5–10 seconds).
3. Geo-restrictions (Russian state sites from abroad and vice versa): use ordinary access from a suitable country or ask acquaintances; do not circumvent the protection.
4. Check whether the same material is elsewhere (FamilySearch ↔ the archive's site ↔ Commons). More — [Access, CAPTCHAs, pace](../2-reference/19-access-captchas-pace.md).

## C. Checking and results

### C1. "It seems I found it! What next?"
1. The scan and a verbatim quotation → record the find ([template](../../templates/finding.md)).
2. Check: two independent features? Contradictions resolved?
3. Update the status of the hypothesis (🟢 / 🟡 / ⚪ / 🔴 — [the status scale](../3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md)), persons, research log.
4. Only then — into the tree. And a paragraph for the family history.

### C2. "An agent/AI brought a find"
1. Open the frame named yourself.
2. Check the quotation letter for letter.
3. Be especially careful with "expected" names from the task: AI tends to see what it is looking for.
4. No address and quotation — no find. The order of checking — [Checking results](04-checking-results.md); how to accept agents' reports — [Working with agents](03-working-with-agents.md), section 5.

## D. Special tasks

### D1. I need documents for citizenship / repatriation
See [Citizenship by descent](../2-reference/14-citizenship-by-descent.md): what proof is needed for each generation, where to get it, how to reconcile different spellings of names.

### D2. Find out the fate of someone killed in the war / repressed / who died in the siege
See [War, repression, the siege](../2-reference/12-war-repression-siege.md): databases by region, typical fields of records, how to search with distorted surnames.

### D2a. The family lived in a shtetl in 1918–1922 (pogroms, flight, orphans)
Databases of pogrom victims, recipients of aid from landsmanshaftn, orphanage lists, death books of a neighbouring large town — [Pogroms of 1918–1922](../2-reference/11-pogroms-1918-1922.md).

### D2b. "Where is my great-grandfather buried?" / read a gravestone
First whether the cemetery survives, then burial databases, then reading the stone and converting the date — [Cemeteries](../2-reference/10-cemeteries.md).

### D3. Find living relatives (distant)
DNA tests, other users' trees (only as a hint, see [Other people's trees, communities, researchers](../2-reference/18-communities-researchers.md)), emigrant branches, letters to namesakes — politely and briefly (see [Requests and letters](../2-reference/20-requests-letters.md)). If DNA showed something unexpected — [Ethics and preservation](../3-results/03-ethics-preservation.md).
