# For English speakers: Russian, Ukrainian and other sources when you don't read them

What this section covers: how to use the Russian and Ukrainian archives and databases in this guide if you don't read Cyrillic, Hebrew or Yiddish — with an AI assistant, browser translation and a few tables. Written for descendants of emigrants in the USA, UK, Canada, Australia, Israel and South America. This page exists only in the English version.

Markers: **[checked]** — the page was opened and read (October 2026); **[from search snippet]** — seen in search results only; **[unverified]** — not checked. More in the [Glossary](06-glossary.md).

If you have never worked with AI before, start with [Three levels](00-three-levels.md), level 1: one check, one relative, one evening.

## 1. What you can do without reading Russian

Honestly: a lot.

| Task | Can you do it with AI + browser translation? | What still needs care |
|---|---|---|
| Find which archive and file holds your town's records | yes | archive names and file titles are mistranslated (section 6) |
| Search a database (war dead, Holocaust victims, repressed) | yes, if you type the name in Cyrillic (section 4) | one spelling is never enough (section 3) |
| Read a **printed** directory, newspaper or typed list | yes, AI reads print well | check names letter by letter against the image |
| Read a **handwritten** 19th-century metrical book or revision list | partly: AI reads the printed form and headings well, handwritten names and places worse | always a second check — by you on a zoomed fragment, or by a human reader |
| Read the Hebrew side of a Jewish metrical record, a gravestone, a Yiddish letter | partly | ask a human reader for anything you will build on |

The rule for everything below: **a translation or an AI reading is a lead, not a fact.** Before a name goes into your tree, compare it with the scan (see [Checking results](../1-ai-search/04-checking-results.md)).

## 2. The Cyrillic alphabet in one table

You don't need to learn it, but recognising the letters lets you check what the AI read.

### Modern Russian letters

Transliteration as in BGN/PCGN, the system JewishGen recommends for Cyrillic (see [Handwriting, transliteration, dates](../2-reference/05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md), section 4).

| Letter | Latin | Letter | Latin | Letter | Latin |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| А а | a | К к | k | Х х | kh |
| Б б | b | Л л | l | Ц ц | ts |
| В в | v | М м | m | Ч ч | ch |
| Г г | g | Н н | n | Ш ш | sh |
| Д д | d | О о | o | Щ щ | shch |
| Е е | e (ye at the start of a word) | П п | p | Ъ ъ | " (hard sign; usually dropped) |
| Ё ё | ë / yo | Р р | r | Ы ы | y |
| Ж ж | zh | С с | s | Ь ь | ' (soft sign; often dropped) |
| З з | z | Т т | t | Э э | e |
| И и | i | У у | u | Ю ю | yu |
| Й й | y | Ф ф | f | Я я | ya |

Traps for English readers: Cyrillic **В** is "v", **Н** is "n", **Р** is "r", **С** is "s", **У** is "u", **Х** is "kh". So "РОСС" is *Ross*, not *Pocc*.

### Letters before 1918

Records before the 1918 spelling reform use a few letters that are gone now (FamilySearch Wiki, "Russia Handwriting") [checked via the reference page]:

| Old letter | Became | Example |
|---|---|---|
| ѣ (yat) | е | мѣщанинъ → мещанин (*meshchanin*, townsman) |
| і | и | Іосифъ → Иосиф |
| ѳ (fita) | ф | Ѳеодоръ → Федор |
| ѵ (izhitsa, rare) | и | |
| ъ at the end of a word | dropped | Вайнбергъ → Вайнберг |

Search **both** spellings: a Google Books search for «Вайнбергъ Моисей» with the final ъ found a directory that two weeks of ordinary searches had missed ([Names, surnames, spellings](../2-reference/03-names-spellings.md), section 4).

### Ukrainian differences

| Ukrainian | Sounds like | Russian equivalent | Latin (Ukrainian 2010 system) |
|---|---|---|---|
| г | h | (Russian г is g) | h |
| ґ | g | г | g |
| и | short "y", like Russian ы | ы | y |
| і | ee | и | i |
| ї | yee | — | yi / i |
| є | ye | е | ye / ie |
| е | e | э | e |

This is why one town has two spellings: Russian **Проскуров** (o) — Ukrainian **Проскурів** (i); Russian **Дунаевцы** — Ukrainian **Дунаївці**.

### Handwriting look-alikes

In 19th-century cursive, lowercase **т** often looks like a Latin *m*, **и** like *u*, **д** has a tail like *g*, **п** looks like *n*, **б** like the digit *6*. Alexander Beider lists pairs that scribes and readers confuse: **а/о, г/ч, м/т** [checked via the reference page]; others are Б/В, Ш/Т, Р/К. To see what a surname looks like in cursive, type it into Steve Morse's printed-to-cursive tool: [stevemorse.org/russian/cyrprintcurs.html](https://stevemorse.org/russian/cyrprintcurs.html) [checked]. More — [Handwriting, transliteration, dates](../2-reference/05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md).

## 3. One name, many spellings

One family name passed through Russian, Polish and German clerks, its owners wrote it in Yiddish or Hebrew, and in emigration it was written in English. Example from this guide's own research: the surname **Шефель** and the given name **Шлиома**.

| Script / setting | Surname | Given name |
|---|---|---|
| Russian, pre-1918 | Шефель | Шлиома |
| Russian, Soviet documents | Шефель | often replaced: Соломон (Solomon); the patronymic Шлюмович became Соломонович |
| Ukrainian | Шефель | Шльома [unverified] |
| BGN/PCGN (JewishGen, US/UK maps) | Shefel' / Shefel | Shlioma |
| Library of Congress (ALA-LC) | Shefelʹ | Shlioma |
| ISO 9 | Šefelʹ | Šlioma |
| Ukrainian national system (2010) | Shefel (soft sign not written) | — |
| Polish-style (Kingdom of Poland, Galicia) | Szefel | Szlama [from search snippet] |
| German-style (emigration, German ports) | Schefel, Scheffel | Schloma [unverified] |
| Yiddish / Hebrew | שעפעל (by Yiddish spelling rules; not seen in a record) [unverified] | שלמה — Shloyme (Yiddish), Shlomo (Hebrew) |
| English (manifests, US/UK records) | Shefel, Sheffel, Shefal, Shafel | Solomon, Sam, Shloime [unverified] |

The variants of Шефель in this table were all actually found in records — about 20 in total, including Шафель, Шеффель, Шефаль, Шейфель, Schaffel, Sofiel. In JewishGen indexes the family is almost never written SHEFEL. And one emigrant, Moische Shefel, became **Max Shechter** in the USA — the new name is in the naturalization file ([Names, surnames, spellings](../2-reference/03-names-spellings.md), sections 2 and 9).

### The standards, briefly

| System | Who uses it | Where to see the table |
|---|---|---|
| **BGN/PCGN** | British and US governments; recommended by JewishGen for Cyrillic | [gov.uk: Romanization systems](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/romanization-systems) — Russian, Ukrainian and Hebrew tables [checked] |
| **ALA-LC** (Library of Congress) | library catalogues, book titles | [loc.gov/catdir/cpso/roman.html](https://www.loc.gov/catdir/cpso/roman.html) [checked] |
| **ISO 9** | some catalogues; one Latin letter per Cyrillic letter, with diacritics | rare in genealogy databases |
| **Ukrainian national, 2010** | Ukrainian passports, maps, signs: Kharkiv, Khmelnytskyi, Dunaivtsi; г → h, и → y, і → i, щ → shch, soft sign dropped | Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine Resolution No. 55 of 27 January 2010 (English text in a UN working paper, 2011) [checked] |
| **YIVO** | Yiddish → Latin | |
| "as heard" | ship manifests, censuses, family memory | no rules — expect anything |

### How to search

1. **Search in Cyrillic too.** Russian directories are OCR'd in Cyrillic. On Genealogy Indexer, "Shefel" in Latin letters gave 1 hit in the whole database, «Шефель» 15, «Шеффель» 92 ([Names, surnames, spellings](../2-reference/03-names-spellings.md), section 4).
2. **Drop the first letter for OCR.** Old-book OCR often eats or swaps the first letter: search «ефель», «айнберг».
3. **Exact first, then phonetic.** In databases with sound-alike search, use **Daitch–Mokotoff** or **Beider–Morse**, built for Eastern European and Jewish names. Phonetics also misleads: "Kessel" returned Кишель and Кисель, not Кесель. Steve Morse explains Beider–Morse at [stevemorse.org/phoneticinfo.htm](https://stevemorse.org/phoneticinfo.htm) [checked].
4. **Write the name as it is in the document** in your notes, with the transliteration next to it — not instead of it. Don't turn Movsha into Moses (JewishGen transcription rules).

The full method — [Names, surnames, spellings](../2-reference/03-names-spellings.md) and the skill [`verifying-genealogy-findings`](../../../skills/verifying-genealogy-findings/SKILL.md).

## 5. Searching Russian and Ukrainian sites

**Browser translation** (Chrome's "Translate this page") makes menus and results readable. Its limits:
- **what you type into the form stays Cyrillic** — the site searches its own data in its own language;
- it **mistranslates archival terms**: Ukrainian «справа» (file) becomes "affairs", «фонд» "stock", «опис» "description" ([Handwriting, transliteration, dates](../2-reference/05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md), section 1). Read file titles in the original, or ask the AI;
- a surname that is also a word may be translated as the word [unverified];
- if a form or dropdown behaves oddly on the translated page, switch translation off, run the search, then translate the results.

**An AI browser plugin** (such as Claude in Chrome) can type the Cyrillic query into the form itself, open each result and make a table — see [Three levels](00-three-levels.md), level 1. **CAPTCHAs are yours to pass**: when a "you are not a robot" check appears, solve it yourself and continue more slowly. Never give the plugin your password ([Access, CAPTCHAs, pace](../2-reference/19-access-captchas-pace.md)).

### Reading a citation

**ДАХмО 226-80-1177, арк. 194** means: State Archive of Khmelnytskyi Oblast (DAKhmO), **fond 226, opys 80, sprava 1177, folio 194**. On Commons this file is the 1875 census of Jews of Letychiv uezd, split into four PDFs (Т.1–Т.4) [checked].
- A **folio** is a numbered leaf: the number is stamped on the front, usually top right. "194 зв." / "194 об." is its back. **The folio is not the PDF page number** — there are two sides per leaf, plus covers.
- A fond with **Р-** (Р-6450) is a Soviet-era fond.
- The archive is named after the **modern oblast**, not the old province: Proskurov, Podolia guberniya, is now in Khmelnytskyi Oblast.

## 7. Dates

- Russia used the **Julian calendar** until 1918. Difference: 12 days in the 19th century, 13 from March 1900. Write both: "3/15 March 1875" ([Handwriting, transliteration, dates](../2-reference/05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md), section 5).
- **Hebrew dates**: the day begins at sunset, so one Hebrew date covers two civil days; families remembered birthdays and *yahrzeits* by the Hebrew calendar, so civil dates in emigration papers are often recalculated from memory. Allow ±1 day. Converter: [Hebcal](https://www.hebcal.com/converter) [checked].
- A boy's birth record often gives the date of circumcision; the birth was 7–8 days earlier.

## 8. Place names

| Russian | Ukrainian | Polish | Yiddish | English forms | Today |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Проскуров (Проскуровъ before 1918) | Проскурів | Płoskirów, Chmielnicki | Proskurov | Proskurov, Proskuriv; "Proskaw" in a US manifest | **Khmelnytskyi** (renamed 16 January 1954) [checked: Wikipedia] |
| Дунаевцы | Дунаївці | Dunajowce | דינעוויץ (Dinovitz) | Dunayevtsy, Dunaivtsi | Dunaivtsi [checked: Wikipedia] |

Tips:
- **Spell the place several ways**: on JewishGen "Kamenets-Podolsk" gave 0, "Kamenets" gave 8.
- Use the **JewishGen Gazetteer and Communities Database** ([jewishgen.org/communities](https://www.jewishgen.org/communities/), English) [checked]: sound-alike search for a garbled name; a town's page lists alternative names by language and the district and province around 1900 (for Proskurov: Proskurov uezd, Podolia guberniya) [from search snippet].
- **Check the district, not just the name**: two places with one name are common. Record the uezd and guberniya for each period — they decide which archive holds the records.

## 9. Ready-made prompts

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md))*
>
> - **Spellings.** "My ancestor's surname was written [Sheffel] in a US record; he came from [town] near [city], [Ukraine], about [year]. Give me the likely Cyrillic spellings (Russian, pre-1918 and modern, and Ukrainian), Polish, German and Yiddish forms, variants with another root vowel, and the forms without the first letter for OCR search. Put them in a table I can copy." *Check yourself:* a form you can't find in Beider's dictionaries (index on stevemorse.org) is probably a misreading.
> - **Reading a scan.** "This is a [metrical book / revision list / census] of [place], [year]. First pass: read the column headings, then row [N] word for word, keeping names in the original Cyrillic and adding a transliteration; mark anything unreadable as (?). Don't guess names. Second pass: translate into English." *Check yourself:* compare names and numbers with the zoomed scan; AI tends to see what is expected. Don't tell it the names you hope for in the first pass.
> - **Using a site.** "I'm on [site]. I don't read Russian. Walk me through searching for [name] born about [year]: which fields to fill, what to type in Cyrillic, what the buttons mean." *Check yourself:* run a control search for a record you know exists; some forms silently ignore filters.
> - **Reading a citation.** "Explain this archival reference: [ДАХмО 226-80-1177, арк. 194]. Which archive, what kind of file, how do I find the folio in the scan?" *Check yourself:* the archive's own inventory or the Commons file title.
>
> *Bad → good:* "Translate this." (no document type, place or year — the AI reads blind) → "This is an 1875 Jewish census of [uezd]: read row [N] word for word, names in Cyrillic plus transliteration, unreadable as (?)."
