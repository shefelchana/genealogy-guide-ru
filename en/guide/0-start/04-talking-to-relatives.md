# Talking to relatives

What this section covers: how to question older relatives so as to get more than "I don't remember". How to prepare, which questions to ask, how to talk about the war and the Holocaust, how to record the conversation and then check what you were told against documents.

Why it is urgent: not questioning living relatives while it is still possible is one of the typical beginner mistakes (petergen.com, "Genealogy mistakes"; the VGD forum). What to do with the account afterwards — record it as a hypothesis, not as a fact ([Where to start](03-where-to-start.md), section 2).

---

## 1. Preparation

- **Meet in person if you can.** Oksana Korneva (vseopredkah.ru, okorneva.ru) writes that meeting in person gives far more than a letter or a questionnaire: people are reluctant to write.
- **Send the questions in advance** (American Ancestors, NEHGS, "Conducting a Family History Interview"). The person will have time to remember and find papers.
- **Bring a ready chart of the relatives** and ask them to correct it. People answer more readily than "from scratch" (Korneva).
- **Take photos and objects** as prompts for memory (NEHGS) and a portable scanner, to digitise at once what they show you (the "Life" project (Проект Жизнь), the "History of You" (История тебя) method).
- **Adjust the length of the conversation** to the storyteller's health (NEHGS). Two short meetings are better than one exhausting one.
- **Separate two aims**: a genealogical conversation (dates, marriages, moves, documents, photos) and a biographical one (character, events, for memoirs). So advises the Am HaZikaron institute (amhazikaron.org).
- **Ask distant relatives too.** According to Am HaZikaron's experience, women usually remember more family stories, and the most valuable information is often kept by very distant relatives. Discrepancies between accounts are leads: write them down rather than smoothing them over.

## 2. Which questions to ask

### Time — through events, not through years

The question "in what year?" usually brings "I don't remember". Ask about neighbouring events:
- "Was that before the wedding or after?"
- "Before your father died or after?"
- "Before the war? In evacuation? When did you come back?"
- "How old were you? What grade were you in?"

So advise the guides to oral history, including the Smithsonian Center for Folklife [from search snippet: genealogy.com, nmaahc.si.edu]. Later, from such anchors the year is reconstructed from documents. Put the answers into the timeline at once ([Plan, timeline, place guide](05-plan-timeline-place.md)).

### Open questions

Questions that cannot be answered "yes" or "no" (same place [from search snippet]; VGD, docs.vgd.ru/kak-najti-predkov):
- "Tell me how you lived in the shtetl."
- "What was grandmother like? What did she do?"
- "Which holidays did you keep? What did you cook?"
- "Why did the family move?"

### What you must ask

- **Places**: where they were born, where they came from, what the shtetl was called at home (it may differ from the official name).
- **Names**: what they were called at home, what in documents, what in the synagogue. What the storyteller's parents and brothers and sisters were called (see [Special groups and name changes](../2-reference/04-special-groups-name-changes.md)).
- **Occupations**, education, service.
- **Relatives abroad**: who left, where, when, whether they corresponded.
- **Documents**: what is kept at home or by other relatives, who may know more.
- **Family legends** — write them down too, even if they sound implausible (Korneva).

### During the conversation

- Ask **follow-up questions outside the list** and **keep the pauses** — the person needs time to remember (NEHGS).
- **A photo as a reason to ask**: "who is next to grandmother?", "where was this taken?" ("History of You"). How to date the picture afterwards — [Old photographs](../2-reference/17-photographs.md).
- **Treat difficult subjects with care** (NEHGS). If the person does not want to talk, do not insist.

## 3. Talking about the war, evacuation, the Holocaust

The model is the interview scheme of the USC Shoah Foundation (sfi.usc.edu/content/interview):

1. **A preliminary meeting** about a week before the recording, and a detailed biographical questionnaire. This builds trust.
2. **The account in order**: life **before** the events, **during**, **after**.
3. **At the end** the storyteller shows photos and documents and explains who is in them.
4. **A copy of the recording is given to the storyteller.**

If the finds turn out to be hard — see [Ethics and preservation](../3-results/03-ethics-preservation.md), section 3.

Irina Shcherbakova (Memorial) recorded oral memoirs from the late 1970s and ran the "Man in History" (Человек в истории) competition. Her methodological guides are mentioned in the literature, but are not yet in open access [from search snippet].

## 4. How to record

- **Record on a dictaphone or phone** (with the storyteller's consent). If you cannot meet, record a video or phone conversation too ("History of You").
- **Note at once**: the date of the conversation, who told it, who recorded it (Korneva).
- **Enter into a table** the full names, years, place and the number of the source — right after the conversation. The paternal and maternal lines go in different folders (VGD).
- **Word for word**, with the note "so says …" — especially names and place names as the storyteller pronounces them.
- Give the storyteller a copy of the recording or transcript (USC Shoah).
- Keep the recording file by the rules in [Ethics and preservation](../3-results/03-ethics-preservation.md), section 6.

## 5. How to check what you were told

An account is a hypothesis. Checking:

1. **Write out the checkable statements** one by one: "grandfather was born in Zhytomyr", "great-grandfather was a doctor", "the family left in 1922".
2. **Tie them to the timeline.** Does the account contradict documents already found? The "impossible" is visible at once ([Plan, timeline, place guide](05-plan-timeline-place.md), section 3).
3. **Pick a document** that will confirm or refute the statement (occupation — directories; a move — address books, questionnaires; war — the databases in [War, repression, the siege](../2-reference/12-war-repression-siege.md)).
4. **Compare the accounts of different relatives.** If two people independently say the same, that is stronger. If one retells the other, it is one piece of evidence (see [Standard of proof](../3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md), section 5).
5. **Look for the "grain of truth".** Family memory mixes up generations, years and names, but often keeps a true detail: the place, the occupation, relatives abroad.

Specialists disagree about legends. At the Am HaZikaron institute they consider that the most improbable legend often turns out to be true; Nadia Lipes writes that family legends almost never get confirmed, especially about origins. The practical conclusion in between: a legend is a hypothesis — it is neither discarded nor accepted, but checked against a document.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The mark (browser agent) is for an AI that opens sites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "Here is a transcript of a conversation with [whom] [text]. Write out the checkable statements one by one, noting who says so; for each, which document could confirm or refute it." *Check yourself:* the AI did not add details that were not in the account.
> - "Help me draft questions for a conversation with [whom, age]: through events, not through years; about places, occupations, the war, relatives abroad." *Check yourself:* the questions suit this person and do not touch painful subjects without need.
>
> *Bad → good:* "Retell grandmother's story nicely: [text]" (will mix what was heard with what was made up, will not mark who said what) → "From the account of [whom] [text], write out the checkable statements one by one and mark who says so."
