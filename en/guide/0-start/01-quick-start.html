Quick start: the first hour

Want to just try it first, without files? Make a single check in a browser — Three levels, level 1.

What this section covers: what to do in the first hour if you are just starting to look for your ancestors, and how to bring your AI assistant into the work. One page; the details are behind the links. An unfamiliar word — see the Glossary. Don't read Russian? See For English speakers.

The whole guide follows one route: question → place → source → index → scan → verification → record. Details — Search route.

The first hour: six steps

  1. Gather what you have at home (15 minutes). Documents, photos with captions on the back, letters, work record books, military IDs, evacuation certificates. Photograph the front and the back. Do not restore the originals or write on them, except for a pencil caption on the back of a photo.
  2. Write what you know into a table (15 minutes): person — dates — place — how it is known. If there is no document, honestly write "Mum's account". Family stories are hypotheses, not facts, but they sometimes contain precise leads.
  3. Pick one person for whom a place is known. Best of all, the oldest person about whom there is at least one document; if there are no documents at all, the oldest person for whom a name and a town are known. Place matters more than surname: it determines which archive to search.
  4. Formulate one question and a stopping condition. Bad: "find out more about my great-grandmother". Good: "where and when was great-grandmother Maria born, about 1890, town N: check the town's metrical books for 1885–1895 and stop". Or: "where was grandfather, born 1912, born: check three military databases and the evacuation cards".
  5. Set up a project folder and three files: a finds log, hypotheses, a research log (templates — in templates/, how to keep them — Keeping files). Ordinary Word documents or Notes will do. Write down empty results too, or in a month you will be searching the same place again.
  6. Make one check in an accessible source (15 minutes). Where to go depends on the period:

(a) The 20th century: war, evacuation, repression - "Pamyat naroda" (People's Memory, in Russian) and OBD "Memorial" (in Russian) — those who served, were killed or went missing, awards, hospitals. Free, no registration. ⚠️ From abroad and through a VPN they often do not open or ask you to pass a check (a "captcha") — pass it yourself. - If the Russian sites do not open: the Yad Vashem names database (English interface available; Holocaust victims, Pages of Testimony, evacuation cards) and memorial books by region; the repressed — "Otkrytyi spisok" (Open List, in Russian). Details — War, repression, the siege.

(b) Before 1917: births, marriages, deaths, revisions - JewishGen — indexes of metrical books, revisions and other lists for Jewish communities. Free, registration required. - FamilySearch — indexes and scans of metrical books and revisions (microfilms). Free, registration required; start with the catalog by place. - Wikimedia Commons — thousands of files from Ukrainian archives as PDFs (metrical books, revisions, ZAGS). Free, no registration, but there is no search by surname inside the files: you search by place and archival reference. How — Archives by country. - An index is only a hint: check the row you found against the scan.

(c) The emigrant branch (the USA and other countries) - A death certificate, a census, naturalization, a ship's manifest: American documents often name the shtetl directly. Indexes and many scans are on FamilySearch (free); lists of arrivals in New York are on the Ellis Island site (free, with registration). Details — Emigration.

How to give this guide to your AI assistant

The guide is written for people and for AI agents alike. If you have Claude, Gemini, ChatGPT or another assistant, copy this message to it:

Read the instructions for the AI agent https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shefelchana/genealogy-guide-ru/main/en/AGENTS.md and the map of the guide https://raw.githubusercontent.com/shefelchana/genealogy-guide-ru/main/en/llms.txt (the guide's site is https://shefelchana.github.io/genealogy-guide-ru/). Work along the route "question → place → source → index → scan → verification → record". First ask me questions about my goal and what is already known about the family — three to five at a time. Then propose a first question and a two-week plan. Give every find with an address (archive, file, image or link) and a verbatim quotation. Do not name fond numbers, village names or dates from memory, and if you do, mark them "[from memory, verify]". If you have no access to my files, at the end of every answer give a "Write to files" block: finds, research-log lines, changes to hypotheses, the next step — I will copy it into my own documents. Captchas, logging in to sites, payment and letters I do myself.

If the assistant does not open links, download the file AGENTS.md (on GitHub, the "Download raw file" button) and attach it to your first message. Attach the relevant section of the guide later, when you reach the task: for example, Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR for metrical books and revisions.

If the assistant works in an ordinary chat without access to your folder, you keep the records: copy the "Write to files" block from its answers into your documents, and at the start of a new conversation paste the "Continue from here" file (template). A new chat does not remember the previous one.

If you work in Claude Code or in the Claude app with access to a folder, install the skills from this guide — then the assistant loads the right instructions itself. How to install — Skills for Claude.

Five rules that save months

  1. Search by place (shtetl, uezd, guberniya), not by surname alone.
  2. A database index is not proof: look at every record on the scan.
  3. Call it "found" only when at least two independent features match (not a surname alone).
  4. Record everything, including empty results, with a note of where and how you searched.
  5. Work slowly on sites with protection and pass captchas yourself. An AI assistant must not pass captchas or enter passwords — you do that.
