# Glossary of terms and marks

What this section covers: short explanations of words that turn up at every step of the guide, one or two lines each. Plus what the working marks in square brackets and the coloured status icons mean.

---

## Documents and archives

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Archival reference** (*shifr*) | the address of a document in an archive: **fond — inventory — file — folio**. For example, "DAKhmO 226-79-5224, folio 12" — the State Archive of Khmelnytskyi Oblast (ДАХмО), fond 226, inventory 79, file 5224, folio 12. Details — [Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](../2-reference/01-records-empire-ussr.md), section 1 |
| **Fond** | all the papers of one institution (a guberniya administration, a crown rabbi, an office) |
| **Inventory** (*opis*) | a list of the files of a fond with their titles; it tells you that a file exists, but not what is in it |
| **File** (*delo*) | one folder or book; a **folio** is its page (there is a "folio" and a "verso") |
| **Metrical book** (*metrika*) | records of births, marriages, divorces and deaths. Kept by church parishes; for Jews, by crown rabbis (from 1835); from 1919–1920 they were replaced by ZAGS books |
| **ZAGS** | Soviet (and present-day) civil registry offices: birth, marriage, divorce, death |
| **Revision**, revision list (*revizskaya skazka*) | a census of the tax-paying population of the Russian Empire (1719–1858, ten revisions) for taxes and military recruitment. A *skazka* is a list of one community by household, with the age now and "as of the last revision". Details — [the revision "ladder"](../2-reference/01-records-empire-ussr.md) |
| **Family list** (*posemeinyi spisok*) | a list of the families of a community from the 1870s, referring back to the last (10th) revision of 1858 |
| **1897 census** | the first all-Russian census of the empire; it shows where people lived, not where they were registered. Survived in part |
| **Finding aid** (*ukazatel*) | a list of surnames or names for a file, fond or book: it tells you that the surname **is there** and where to look. It may be inside a file (an alphabet) or compiled by a community |
| **Index** (database) | an extract from a record made by people or a program (JewishGen, FamilySearch and others): name, year, place, sometimes a link to the image. Convenient for searching, but it makes mistakes. **Neither a finding aid nor an index is proof** — look at the scan itself |
| **Scan**, **image** (*kadr*) | a scan is a photograph of a page of a document; an image is one frame on a film or in a PDF (the image number ≠ the folio number) |
| **FamilySearch film** | a microfilm on which one or several books were shot; every film has a number, a digitised one also has a **DGS** number |
| **DGS** | Digital Group Number, the number of a digitised film on FamilySearch. It opens the viewer of the images. How to get from the catalog to the image — [Archives by country](../2-reference/07-archives-by-country.md), the section "FamilySearch: catalog → film → image" |
| **Full-text search** | search by words inside the recognised text of books and newspapers (not through a ready index) |
| **Recognition (OCR)** | a program turns the image of a page into text; in old books it often errs, especially in the first letter of a word |
| **Wikimedia Commons** | an open repository where thousands of files from Ukrainian archives are posted as PDFs; there is no search by surname inside them |

## People, estates, places

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Estate** (*soslovie*) | the legal status of a person in the empire: nobles, clergy, merchants, meshchane, peasants, guild artisans and others. It is not origin: people often moved between meshchane and merchants |
| **Meshchanin** | a town dweller of the tax-paying estate (an artisan, a small trader). Most of the empire's Jews were registered as meshchane of their shtetl |
| **Merchant** (1st, 2nd, 3rd guild) | the estate of those who declared capital and paid the guild fee; it could change from year to year |
| **Registration** (*pripiska*) | to which community (town, shtetl, volost) a person was **registered**. Revisions and conscription went by the place of registration, and the person could live somewhere quite different. "Rashkovskii meshch." means a meshchanin of the shtetl Rashkov, not a surname |
| **Guberniya, uezd, volost** | the administrative division of the empire: a guberniya (a large region, roughly like a present-day oblast or larger), an uezd (part of a guberniya, like a district), a volost (a rural unit within an uezd). Borders and subordination changed — look at the needed year: [Places and maps](../2-reference/06-places-maps.md) |
| **Shtetl** (*mestechko*) | a small town with a large share of Jewish population in the Pale of Settlement |
| **Pale of Settlement** | the western guberniyas of the empire where Jews were allowed to live permanently (from the end of the 18th century to 1917) |
| **Crown rabbi** (*kazyonny ravvin*), rabbinate | a rabbi chosen by the community and approved by the authorities; he kept the metrical books of the Jews of his district (rabbinate) |
| **Cantonist** | a child taken into a military school (1827–1856); many were baptised. Details — [Special groups and name changes](../2-reference/04-special-groups-name-changes.md) |
| **Double name** | Jews often had two names (Mordko-Leib); in documents one of them might be written |

## Method

| Term | What it is |
|---|---|
| **Search route** | the guide's order of work: question → place → source → index → scan → verification → record. Details — [Search route](../1-ai-search/01-search-route.md) |
| **Stopping condition** | written down in advance, when to end the search on a question: "I'll view all the surviving years 1875–1885 and stop" |
| **Coverage** | what exactly was viewed: source, years, images, spellings, method. Without coverage "not found" means nothing |
| **Control record** | a name or word that is certainly in this source. If the search does not find it either, the search is broken, not the person absent |
| **Second independent feature** | besides the surname — a patronymic, age, place, names of relatives or occupation, taken not from the very same record |
| **FAN** | Friends, Associates, Neighbors — searching through the circle: friends, business partners, neighbours, witnesses. Details — [Standard of proof](../3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md), section 9 |
| **GPS** | Genealogical Proof Standard — five conditions under which a conclusion counts as proven. Same place, section 3 |
| **Age bridge** | the column "as of the last revision" in a revision list: the age of the same person at the previous revision. It is used to step from one revision to the one before |
| **cM** (centimorgans) | the unit of length of a shared stretch of DNA between two people; the degree of kinship is estimated from the sum of cM. Details — [DNA](../2-reference/15-dna.md) |
| **Endogamy** | marriages within a closed community over many generations (among Ashkenazi Jews and others); because of it DNA matches look closer than they are |

## Statuses of hypotheses and connections

Throughout the guide, in the templates and in the skills there is one scale: 🟢 confirmed · 🟡 open / strong version · ⚪ unverified / weak · 🔴 rejected. What each status requires — [Standard of proof](../3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md), the section "The status scale".

## Marks in the text of the guide

In square brackets after some pieces of information it says how far the compiler has checked them:

| Mark | What it means |
|---|---|
| **[checked]** or **[opened]** | the information was checked on the source's own page (as of the date given in the section; usually October 2026) |
| **[from search snippet]** | taken only from search-engine results or from someone else's review: the page itself could not be opened. Before an important decision, open the source yourself |
| **[unverified]** | it could not be confirmed against the primary source. Use as a hint, not as a fact |
| **[reader's experience]** | from material sent in by a reader of the guide: the author tested it on live queries in their own project, the compiler did not repeat it. The figures are from one project; check yourself before an important conclusion. Example — [The false zero](../1-ai-search/09-false-zero.md) |
| **(LRF)** | a figure from the consolidated list of Lost Russian Family, not checked on the site itself |
| no mark | general information, the compiler's experience, or information confirmed by a link next to it |

A mark of its own for the AI assistant: **[from memory, verify]** — this is how an AI should mark a fond number, a village name, a date or any other fact that it named without having a source open. Such information is a hypothesis until checked.
