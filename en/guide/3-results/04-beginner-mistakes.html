Beginner mistakes: a consolidated list

What this section covers: sixteen typical mistakes of beginning genealogists — from the advice of well-known authors and communities — and where in the guide it is written how to avoid them.

Sources of the list:

  • Amy Johnson Crow, "Genealogy Mistakes That Everyone Makes (and How to Avoid Them)", amyjohnsoncrow.com;
  • FamilySearch Wiki, "Rookie Mistakes";
  • Crista Cowan (Ancestry) in the interview "How They Do It", organizeyourfamilyhistory.com;
  • V. V. Krasko, an article in the collection "Genealogiya" (Genealogy) of the Russian Geographical Society (RGO), no. 3, 2003 (petergen.com/metod/sovkrasko.shtml, Russian-language);
  • petergen.com, "Genealogy mistakes that can damage your research" (Oshibki genealogii, kotorye mogut nanesti uron vashim issledovaniyam; author not named; Russian-language);
  • the VGD forum, docs.vgd.ru/kak-najti-predkov (Russian-language).

A short list from VGD's observations is in Where to start, section 7. Here is the full one.

No. Mistake Who warns How to avoid
1 Taking on trust family stories, websites and other people's trees Crow; Krasko Standard of proof, Other people's trees, communities, researchers
2 Hurrying and attaching records without analysis Crow Keeping files, section 6
3 Skipping generations instead of "from the known to the unknown"; starting with distant ancestors or famous namesakes Crow; FamilySearch "Rookie Mistakes"; Russian-language advice [from search snippet] Where to start, section 3
4 Studying a person apart from family and neighbours "Rookie Mistakes" Plan, timeline, place guide, section 6; FAN in Standard of proof
5 Darting between families without finishing one "Rookie Mistakes"; Cowan Plan, timeline, place guide, section 6
6 Keeping records without source citations; not recording unsuccessful searches "Rookie Mistakes"; Cowan; petergen Keeping files, research log
7 Assuming a surname has one spelling "Rookie Mistakes"; Krasko Names, surnames, spellings, Handwriting, transliteration, dates
8 Drawing conclusions from one source; skirting contradictions instead of explaining them "Rookie Mistakes" Standard of proof
9 Deciding that a document "does not exist" after the first failure "Rookie Mistakes" Standard of proof, section 7; scenarios, B1
10 Getting stuck in a routine: the same sites, the same queries Crow scenarios, B5
11 Not understanding how a source is built: not reading the "about" pages and FAQs of databases Crow catalog of sites
12 Keeping research to yourself, showing it to no one "Rookie Mistakes" Other people's trees, communities, researchers, section 2
13 Not interviewing living relatives while it is possible petergen; VGD Talking to relatives

A few more mistakes from this guide

They are not in the lists above but grew out of the practice behind this guide:

  • taking a database index for the document — Standard of proof, section 6;
  • searching by surname rather than by place — Where to start, section 4;
  • believing an AI find without an address and quotation, without checking against the scan — Checking results;
  • looking only at the year the family named rather than all surviving years of the book — Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR, section 2;
  • paging through a file without an index when an index exists — Community surname indexes;
  • searching for one ancestor instead of assembling the whole family — Reconstructing the whole family;
  • believing an encyclopedia or Wikipedia in the "place of birth" field — Standard of proof, section 1;
  • paying for what lies in open access — Access, captchas, pace.
