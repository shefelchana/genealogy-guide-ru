# How to turn finds into a family history

What this section covers: how to turn dry records with archival references into a text that relatives will enjoy reading — without losing accuracy or passing a guess off as fact.

---

## 1. The two-layer record

Record every confirmed find **twice**:

1. **A fact for the tree and the finds log** — briefly, with the archival reference and quotation.
2. **A paragraph for relatives** — a coherent text: who, where, when, what it means, what was going on around.

This corresponds to the fifth element of the GPS standard (a written conclusion) and the NGS advice to separate facts from narrative. The paragraph for relatives is a supplement from practice; the standards have no direct analogue.

Example.

**Fact:**
> ANRM (National Archives of the Republic of Moldova) 211-11-238, Kishinev marriages 1895, no. 149 (FamilySearch, image 841; checked against the scan): «Хуна Михелевъ Вайнбергъ /онъ же Репетуръ/» [Khuna, son of Mikhel, Weinberg, "alias Repetur"], resident of Izmail, 23.V.1895.

**A paragraph for relatives** (sample):
> In May 1895, in Kishinev, a resident of Izmail named Khuna, son of Mikhel Weinberg, was married. The clerk added to his surname an unusual note — "alias Repetur" (*он же Репетур*). So one of the Izmail Weinberg families also bore a second name. This note unexpectedly linked the marriage record with a newspaper announcement of 1887 in which a similar surname, "Repitor", appears. Whether it is one family there is so far no document.

Note that in the paragraph the guess is called a guess.

## 2. Rules for the text

- **Write only from verified facts.** Hypotheses — with explicit words: "most likely", "probably", "there is no document".
- **Do not hide contradictions.** If two documents disagree in age or name, say so — relatives find that interesting, and the researcher after you needs it.
- **The source — in a footnote or at the end of the paragraph.** Relatives will skip it, the researcher will find it.
- **Keep the history in a separate file**, not in the working notes (see [Keeping files](../0-start/02-keeping-files.md)).
- **Living people** — only with their consent; sensitive information (DNA, adoption, repression, illness) — with special care (the ethics of BCG and NGS, see [DNA](../2-reference/15-dna.md)).

## 3. Historical context brings the history to life

A document answers the question "who and when"; context answers "why". For example, why the family moved, why a person "vanished" from the lists, why there is no entry in the metrical book.

What to add:
- **events of the era**: pogroms, wars, evacuation, border changes (southern Bessarabia in 1856–1878 was Romanian — and that explains why there is no Russian metrical book);
- **the life of the town or shtetl**: trades, synagogues, the timber trade, fairs;
- **daily life and customs**: for example, what an *agunah* is and why an abandoned wife looked for her husband through a newspaper;
- **occupations**: what it meant to be a doctor, a merchant of the second guild, a crown rabbi;
- **reasons for moves** — not only pogroms and wars but personal ones: a community invited a rabbi from a particular family, there were trade ties between regions (the Am HaZikaron institute) [checked];
- **why a person may be absent from a record**: conscription (about 1–1.5 million Jews passed through the army in 1827–1917 — Y. Petrovsky-Shtern, "Jews in the Russian Army", 2009), frequent divorces in the first half of the 19th century (C. Freeze), incompleteness of registration (E. Avrutin) [from search snippet];
- **guidebooks to shtetls**, for example "100 Jewish Shtetls of Ukraine" by V. Lukin and B. Khaimovich — plans, synagogues, cemeteries, history [from search snippet].

Where to get context:
- encyclopedias: the Brockhaus and Efron Jewish Encyclopedia (1908–1913), the Russian Jewish Encyclopedia, the Electronic Jewish Encyclopedia, the YIVO Encyclopedia;
- memorial (yizkor) books on shtetls, Pinkas HaKehillot;
- the newspapers of the era themselves — correspondence from shtetls;
- diaries ("Prozhito" — diary entries of the 18th–20th centuries, searchable by place and date);
- gazetteers and provincial memorial booklets.

See [Dictionaries, encyclopedias, reference works](../../reading/dictionaries-and-encyclopedias.md).

## 4. How to structure the text

A working structure:
1. **By places** — chapters on each town and shtetl where the family lived (with a map and the history of the place).
2. **By people** — a sketch of each key person: what is known, from where, what is unknown.
3. **Chronologically** — from the earliest document to family memory.

Include **images**: crops from documents (with a caption and archival reference), photographs, maps. A crop captioned "this is what the birth record of my great-great-grandfather looks like" persuades better than any retelling.

As a separate appendix — a **table of sources** and **open questions**: what has not yet been found and where it may be. That way the history remains a working one and the next person can continue it.

Write also about **how the search went**: what was searched for, what was found, what has not survived. For relatives this is often the liveliest part.

**Publication as a way to find relatives.** A shtetl page on JewishGen (KehilaLinks) or a published tree works as a "magnet": in Phyllis Kramer's experience, researchers gathered around a shtetl page, jointly hired a researcher in the archive and obtained more than 1,500 documents (Avotaynu Online, 2009) [checked]. Data on living people — only with their consent ([Ethics and preservation](03-ethics-preservation.md)).

## 5. With an AI assistant

AI writes a coherent text and historical notes well — **from verified facts in your files**. Give it extracts from the finds log rather than asking it to "write a family history" from scratch: otherwise it will fill the gaps with plausible fiction. Check every phrase with a name, date and place against the finds log. See [AI tools](../1-ai-search/05-ai-tools.md).

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The note "(browsing agent)" is for an AI that opens websites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "Here are extracts from the finds log about [person] [text]. Write a paragraph for relatives using only these facts; after each statement put the source from the finds log in brackets; where there are no facts, say so, do not guess." *Check yourself:* every name, date and place against the finds log.
> - "Here is a draft of a chapter [text]. Find phrases that sound like fact but are not backed by a source." *Check yourself:* go through the list yourself — the AI may have missed a phrase that it made up itself last time.
>
> *Bad → good:* "Write the history of our family" (without your extracts it will fill the gaps with plausible fiction) → "Here are extracts from the finds log [text]: write a paragraph for relatives using only them, with a source for each statement."
