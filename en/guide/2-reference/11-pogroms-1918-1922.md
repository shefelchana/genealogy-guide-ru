# Pogroms 1918–1922: victims, the injured, orphans, refugees

What this section covers: how to look for relatives who lived in the shtetls of Ukraine and neighbouring guberniyas during the Civil War — those killed, wounded, robbed, who fled, children in orphanages and those who received help from fellow townspeople in America. Two databases with archival references, the main archival fond, and primary sources if the databases have no one.

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified] and others) are explained in the [Glossary](../0-start/06-glossary.md).

---

## 1. When you need this

The years in the title are the main wave of the pogroms of the Civil War. Some pogroms were already happening at the end of 1917, so the databases and sources below cover 1917–1922.

- The family lived in a shtetl of Kyiv, Podolia, Volhynia, Kherson, Ekaterinoslav or Chernigov guberniya in 1917–1922.
- A person "vanished" or died around 1919–1921; in the family they speak of a pogrom, a gang, flight, an orphanage, money "from America".
- You are assembling documents for the whole family for those years ([Assembling the whole family](09-whole-family.md)): the pogrom databases belong in the mandatory package alongside ZAGS and rabbinate books.

These sources hold **not only the killed**. They include the wounded, the robbed, widows, refugees, **recipients of money aid in 1922 with addresses**, **children in orphanages of the 1920s**, staff of children's institutions. Check the relatives who survived too.

## 2. Prepare an identification set

Before the first query write down:
- the surname in all spellings — in Cyrillic and in **Latin letters**, with a different vowel in the root, without the first letter;
- names and patronymics in Jewish and Russian forms;
- age around 1919;
- the shtetl, **the neighbouring shtetls and the nearest big city**: people fled there, and many died there in the winter of 1919–1920 from typhus and hunger.

## 3. Nadia Lipes's database: jewishpogroms.info

About 77,777 records [checked], each with **fond, inventory, file, folio and archive**. The sources are only archival documents and memorial sheets.

- Search **in Latin letters only**. The "Sounds like" checkbox hardly widens the search; the asterisk works: `Rabin*`, `*binovich`. Try the Latin forms one at a time.
- Search **by shtetl without a surname** too: you can see which lists for the place there are in the database at all, and distorted surnames get caught.
- The record fields: Found / Register / Case / Page = **fond / inventory / file / folio**; Archive: Dako — the State Archive of Kyiv Oblast (DAKO, ДАКО), State archive of Vinnytsia region — DAViO (ДАВіО).
- The data was entered by volunteers: shifted columns and typos in file numbers happen. A line in the database is a hint; the folio of the file decides.

## 4. Am HaZikaron: pogrom.amhazikaron.org

The memorial project of the Am HaZikaron institute (Ам а-Зикарон): 7,929 victims, 118 pages about shtetls [checked, October 2026]. Each record has a source: E. D. Rosenthal's "Svitok rezni" (Свиток резни, the Scroll of Slaughter) in Russian translation, DAKO files R-3050 (with folios and PDF pages), TsDAVO fond 2497 (the Central State Archive of Supreme Bodies of Power of Ukraine, ЦДАВО), the Cherikover archive in YIVO, the Proskurov lists with addresses. About a third of the project's documents are in Yiddish.

- Search **in Cyrillic, by part of a word**.
- One record often describes **the whole family** ("his wife…, their daughter…"): search by the son-in-law's surname and by given name too.
- The database does not cover the "Svitok rezni" entirely: an empty answer does not mean the person is not in Rosenthal.

## 5. Cross-checking the two databases

The databases overlap but do not coincide: ages and names differ between lists. A person matched in both databases with a reference to **the same file** is one piece of evidence, not two.

## 6. From a database line to the file

1. Turn the fields into an archival reference (fond — inventory — file — folio).
2. Find the file in the inventory. **DAKO, fond R-3050** is the Kyiv District Commission of the Jewish Public Committee for Aid to the Victims of Pogroms, 1919–1924: inventory 1 — 754 files, inventory 2 — 40, inventory 3 — 85; the inventory is itemised piece by piece on Vikidzherela (Вікіджерела, Ukrainian Wikisource; `Архів:ДАКО/Р-3050/1`) [checked]. Compare the file title with the database record.
3. Find the scan: about 910 files of the fond are on Wikimedia Commons (the category "Funds of State Archive of Kyiv Oblast - Fund R-3050"), and the inventory PDFs are there too [checked]. How to read — [Handwriting, transliteration, dates](05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md) and the skill [`ukrainian-archives-on-commons`](../../../skills/ukrainian-archives-on-commons/SKILL.md) (in Russian).
4. Read the folio yourself and copy out verbatim: name, age, what happened, the **address**, who gave the information (often a relative), the amount of aid and from which society of townspeople.

**What else is in inventory R-3050**, besides lists of the killed: "questionnaires" (*oprosnye listy*, 1921), "lists of the pogrom-stricken" by shtetl, "lists of children" and orphanage files, meetings of refugees, "money sent from America" with lists of recipients, name-by-name censuses of shtetls of 1920, personal files of staff and aid recipients. Search the inventory page by the name of the shtetl.

**Other fonds:** TsDAVO fond 2497 (the All-Ukrainian Jewish Public Committee; the inventory is on Vikidzherela) [checked]; on Commons, in the category TsDAVO fond 5, there is a file "1921 god. Spiski evreiskikh bezhentsev na rumynskoi granitse" (1921. Lists of Jewish refugees on the Romanian border) — 159 pages, refugees from Podolia [checked]; DAViO fonds 4262, 4954 (orphanages of Tulchyn and others) and DAOO fond R-5275 (the State Archive of Odesa Oblast) — via the links in the Lipes database [unverified].

⚠️ TsDIAK fond 1164, inventory 1, file 171 (the Central State Historical Archive of Ukraine in Kyiv, ЦДІАК), to which Am HaZikaron refers, is the **metrical book of deaths** of the Jewish community of Kyiv (1918), not a list of those killed in the uezd [checked].

## 7. What counts as a find

- **A find** is a record in a database **plus** a folio of the file read from the scan, where the surname, the name and at least one more feature match: patronymic, age, shtetl, names of relatives, address.
- **A recipient of aid in 1922** from a list of a society of townspeople is a strong lead to the American branch: money was sent to specific people through the society of a specific shtetl. Next — [Emigration](13-emigration.md).
- **A child in an orphanage list** with the place of birth and the parents' names is often the only document about a "vanished" child. Next — the Soviet documents of the orphanage and ZAGS.

## 8. If the databases have no one

Absence from the databases **does not prove** that the person was not harmed, and does not prove that he survived. The databases are compiled from part of the fonds.

1. Check whether the control query worked (a surname that is certainly in the database). If not, it is a technical zero; repeat later.
2. Have all the Latin forms, the asterisk, a search by shtetl and by neighbouring shtetls been tried?
3. Primary sources of the same time:
   - **rabbinate death books** of 1918–1920 — the cause of death often has to be read "between the lines": consecutive dates, young men (N. Lipes);
   - **ZAGS of 1918–1922** of the shtetl **and the nearest big city**. Early records usually have no column for nationality (it appeared in the form of 1926); the signs are the name and the Jewish cemetery;
   - metrical books could have ended up in the fond of the *zemskii sud* of a neighbouring uezd if there was no rabbinate.
4. Published sources: "Kniga pogromov" (Книга погромов, The Book of Pogroms; a collection of documents, 2007); "Khurbn Proskurov" (New York, 1924); "Proskurivskyi pohrom 1919 roku v dokumentakh: mify ta realnist" (Проскурівський погром 1919 року в документах: міфи та реальність, The Proskuriv Pogrom of 1919 in Documents: Myths and Reality; Khmelnytskyi, 2019) [from search snippet]; S. Veigman, "Kadish siroty" (Кадиш сироты, Orphan's Kaddish) — a list of the Jews of Kyiv killed in 1917–1919.
5. Remember: survivors are victims too. Many did not live through the winter after a pogrom with broken windows, typhus and hunger, especially those who had fled (N. Lipes in an interview, isrageo.com, 2023). A death in 1920–1922 in the city people fled to is a continuation of the same story.

Record in your research log: every database, every spelling, the search mode, the number of records, the control query. Without this, "not in the pogrom databases" means nothing.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The (browser agent) label is for an AI that opens websites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "Draw up Latin and Cyrillic variants of the surname [name] for searching the pogrom databases of 1918–1922: with a different root vowel, with an asterisk (`Rabin*`), without the first letter. Separately — the neighbouring shtetls and the nearest big city for [shtetl, uezd]." *Check yourself:* place names — against a gazetteer.
> - "Here is a line from the jewishpogroms.info database: [all fields]. Turn it into an archival reference (fond–inventory–file) and suggest where to look for the inventory and the scan; mark any addresses you haven't checked as [from memory, verify]." *Check yourself:* the file title in the inventory matches the database record.
>
> *Bad → good:* "Was [name] killed in a pogrom?" (it will answer from memory; no shtetl or age) → "[Name], born around [year], lived in [shtetl, uezd] in 1919: in which pogrom databases should I look for him, and in which spellings?"
