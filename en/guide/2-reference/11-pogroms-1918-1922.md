# Pogroms 1918–1922: victims, the injured, orphans, refugees

What this section covers: how to look for relatives who lived in the shtetls of Ukraine and neighbouring guberniyas during the Civil War — the dead, the wounded, the robbed, those who fled, children in orphanages, and those who received aid from hometown fellow-countrymen in America. Two databases with archival references, the main archival fond, and primary sources for when the databases have nobody.

Markers: **[checked]** — the page or query was checked (October 2026); **[from search snippet]** — second-hand information; **[unverified]** — exactly that.

---

## 1. When you need this

- The family lived in a shtetl of the Kyiv, Podolia, Volhynia, Kherson, Yekaterinoslav or Chernihiv guberniya in 1917–1922.
- A person "disappeared" or died around 1919–1921; the family talks of a pogrom, a gang, flight, an orphanage, money "from America".
- You are assembling documents for the whole family for those years ([Assembling the whole family](09-whole-family.md)): the pogrom databases are part of the mandatory package, alongside ZAGS (Soviet civil registry) and rabbinate books.

These sources include **not only the killed**. They include the wounded, the robbed, widows, refugees, **recipients of money aid in 1922, with addresses**, **children in orphanages of the 1920s**, and staff of children's institutions. Check relatives who survived, too.

## 2. Prepare an identification set

Before the first query, write down:
- the surname in all spellings — Cyrillic and **Latin**, with different root vowels, without the first letter;
- given names and patronymics in Jewish and Russian forms;
- the age around 1919;
- the shtetl, **neighbouring shtetls and the nearest big city**: people fled there, and many died there in the winter of 1919–1920 of typhus and hunger.

## 3. Nadia Lipes's database: jewishpogroms.info

About 77,777 records [checked], each with **fond, inventory, file, folio and archive**. The sources are only archival documents and memorial leaflets.

- Search **in Latin letters only**. The "Sounds like" checkbox hardly widens the search; the asterisk works: `Rabin*`, `*binovich`. Try the Latin forms one by one.
- Also search **by shtetl without a surname**: you can see which lists for the place exist in the database at all, and distorted surnames get caught.
- Record fields: Found / Register / Case / Page = **fond / inventory / file / folio**; Archive: Dako — State Archive of Kyiv Oblast (DAKO), State archive of Vinnytsia region — DAViO.
- The data were entered by volunteers: shifted columns and typos in file numbers occur. A database row is a pointer; the folio of the file decides.

## 4. "Am ha-Zikaron": pogrom.amhazikaron.org

A memorial project of the Am ha-Zikaron institute: 7,929 victims, 118 pages about shtetls [checked, October 2026]. Each record has a source: E. D. Rosenthal's "Scroll of Slaughter" (*Svitok rezni*) in Russian translation, DAKO files R-3050 (with folios and PDF pages), TsDAVO (Central State Archive of Supreme Bodies of Power, Ukraine) fond 2497, the Cherikover archive at YIVO, the Proskurov lists with addresses. About a third of the project's documents are in Yiddish.

- Search **in Cyrillic, by part of a word**.
- One record often describes **a whole family** ("his wife…, their daughter…"): search by the son-in-law's surname and by the given name, too.
- The database does not cover the whole of the "Scroll of Slaughter": an empty answer does not mean that Rosenthal has nothing on the person.

## 5. Cross-checking the two databases

The databases overlap but do not coincide: ages and names differ between lists. A person's match in both databases with a reference to **the same file** is one piece of evidence, not two.

## 6. From a database row to the file

1. Convert the fields to an archival reference (fond — inventory — file — folio).
2. Find the file in the inventory. **DAKO, fond R-3050** is the Kyiv District Commission of the Jewish Public Committee for Aid to Victims of Pogroms, 1919–1924: inventory 1 — 754 files, inventory 2 — 40, inventory 3 — 85; the inventory is itemised file by file on Wikisource (`Архів:ДАКО/Р-3050/1`; Ukrainian-language site) [checked]. Compare the file title with the database record.
3. Find the scan: about 910 files of the fond have been put on Wikimedia Commons (category "Funds of State Archive of Kyiv Oblast - Fund R-3050"), with the PDFs of the inventories there as well [checked]. How to read them — [Handwriting, transliteration, dates](05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md) and the skill `ukrainian-archives-on-commons`.
4. Read the folio yourself and copy out verbatim: name, age, what happened, **address**, who submitted the information (often a relative), the amount of aid and from which hometown society.

**What else is in inventory R-3050**, besides lists of the killed: "questionnaires" (1921), "lists of the pogrom-stricken" by shtetl, "lists of children" and files of orphanages, refugee gatherings, "money sent from America" with lists of recipients, name-by-name censuses of shtetls of 1920, personal files of staff and recipients of aid. Search the inventory page by the name of the shtetl.

**Other fonds:** TsDAVO fond 2497 (the All-Ukrainian Jewish Public Committee; inventory on Wikisource) [checked]; on Commons, in the category TsDAVO fond 5, there is a file "1921. Lists of Jewish refugees on the Romanian border" — 159 pages, refugees from Podolia [checked]; DAViO (State Archive of Vinnytsia Oblast) fonds 4262, 4954 (orphanages of Tulchyn and others) and DAOO (State Archive of Odesa Oblast) fond R-5275 — via the links in the Lipes database [unverified].

⚠️ TsDIAK (Central State Historical Archive of Ukraine, Kyiv) fond 1164 inventory 1 file 171, to which "Am ha-Zikaron" refers, is the **metrical book of deaths** of the Jewish community of Kyiv (1918), not a list of those killed in the uezd [checked].

## 7. What counts as a find

- **A find** is a database record **plus** a folio of the file read from the scan, where the surname, the given name and at least one more feature match: patronymic, age, shtetl, names of relatives, address.
- **A recipient of aid in 1922** on a hometown society's list is a strong lead to the American branch: money was sent to specific people through the hometown society of a specific shtetl. Next — [Emigration](13-emigration.md).
- **A child on an orphanage list**, with place of birth and the parents' names, is often the only document about a "disappeared" child. Next — the Soviet orphanage documents and ZAGS.

## 8. If the databases have nobody

Absence from the databases **does not prove** that a person was not harmed, and does not prove that he survived. The databases are compiled from part of the fonds.

1. Check whether the control query worked (a surname that is certainly in the database). If not, it is a technical zero; repeat later.
2. Have all the Latin forms, the asterisk, and the search by shtetl and neighbouring shtetls been tried?
3. Primary sources of the same time:
   - **rabbinate books of deaths** of 1918–1920 — the cause of death often has to be read "between the lines": consecutive dates, young men (N. Lipes);
   - **ZAGS of 1918–1922** of the shtetl **and the nearest big city**. Early records usually have no column for nationality (it appeared in the 1926 form); the signs are the name and the Jewish cemetery;
   - metrical books could end up in the fond of the **zemsky court** (rural district court) of a neighbouring uezd if there was no rabbinate.
4. Published sources: "The Book of Pogroms" (*Kniga pogromov*; a collection of documents, 2007); "Khurbn Proskurov" (New York, 1924); "The Proskuriv pogrom of 1919 in documents: myths and reality" (*Proskurivskyi pohrom 1919 roku v dokumentakh: mify ta realnist*; Khmelnytskyi, 2019) [from search snippet]; S. Weigman, "The Orphan's Kaddish" (*Kadish sirot*) — a list of Jews of Kyiv killed in 1917–1919.
5. Remember: survivors are victims too. Many did not live through the winter after a pogrom — broken windows, typhus and hunger — especially those who had fled (N. Lipes in an interview, isrageo.com, 2023). A death in 1920–1922 in the city people fled to is a continuation of the same story.

Write in the research log: each database, each spelling, the search mode, the number of records, the control query. Without this, "not in the pogrom databases" means nothing.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> - "Make Latin and Cyrillic variants of the surname [N] for searching the 1918–1922 pogrom databases: with different root vowels, with an asterisk (`Rabin*`), without the first letter. Separately, list the neighbouring shtetls and the nearest big city for [shtetl, uezd]." *Check yourself:* the place names — in a gazetteer. (The AI may need to read Russian- and Ukrainian-language sources.)
> - "Here is a row from the jewishpogroms.info database: [all fields]. Convert it to an archival reference and tell me where to find the inventory and the scan of this file." *Check yourself:* the file title in the inventory matches the database record.
> - "Read this folio from DAKO R-3050 [scan]: copy out verbatim the names, ages, address, what happened, who submitted the information, the amount of aid. Mark anything illegible with '(?)'." *Check yourself:* names and numbers on an enlarged fragment; about a third of the documents are in Yiddish.
> - "A person is not in the pogrom databases. Which primary sources of 1918–1922 for [shtetl] and the nearest city should I check, and what has survived?" *Check yourself:* survival — in the archive's inventory.
