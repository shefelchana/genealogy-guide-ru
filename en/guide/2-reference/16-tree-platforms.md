# Family Tree Platforms

What this section covers: where to build and keep your tree — online services and desktop programs, how they differ, who owns the data, and which combination is sensible to choose.

Information as of October 2026. The label "from search results" means the information has not been checked against the page; "not checked against the page" means exactly that.

## 2. Who owns the data

- **Shared trees** (FamilySearch, Geni, WikiTree): the profile of a deceased ancestor can be edited by all participants. Your contribution becomes part of the common whole, and it can be changed.
- **MyHeritage and Ancestry**: the tree is yours, but it lives on the company's server and depends on its rules and prices.
- **Desktop programs** (Gramps, RootsMagic, Family Tree Maker): everything is stored with you. Therefore backups are your responsibility (the 3-2-1 rule in [Keeping files](../3-results/01-keeping-files.md)).

## 3. The recommended combination

This is a proposal by the authors of the guide, not advice from outside sources.

1. **The working tree — in a desktop program** (Gramps for free, or RootsMagic). That is where the sources, citations and notes live, including hypotheses, clearly marked.
2. **Confirmed deceased ancestors — in the FamilySearch Family Tree**, with source citations. It is free, living people are hidden automatically, and researchers around the world can see the tree.
3. **To find relatives — a copy of the confirmed part on MyHeritage.** The free 250 people are enough for the core of the tree. There you get hints from matches with other trees, and DNA matches.

**What to avoid:**
- keeping the only copy of the tree in a single online service;
- uploading unverified hypotheses to a shared tree — they will be copied, and the error will multiply;
- publishing living people without their consent (the BCG code of ethics, NGS recommendations).

## 4. Practical details

- **MyHeritage:** "Smart Matches" from Geni, if the tree on Geni is your own, are your own data, not an independent source. In the profile biography editor, text pasted by a program may silently fail to save — you need to type at least one character from the keyboard; it is better to enter a long note in paragraphs (the limit of one note is about 2,300 characters).
- **FamilySearch:** a profile's "deceased" status changes only when someone enters a death date manually.
- **Every event in the tree** needs a source citation. FamilySearch advises adding one every time you add an event.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> - "Compare [platform A] and [platform B] for my case: [number of people, budget, whether DNA is needed, who will use it]. Who owns the data, and can I export a GEDCOM?" *Check yourself:* prices and terms — on the platforms' websites, as of today.
> - "Here is an export of my tree (GEDCOM) [excerpt]. Find duplicates, impossible dates (a child born before a parent, a mother younger than 14) and people without sources." *Check yourself:* do not give a chat the data of living people unless you have to.
