# Old Photographs: Dating, Attribution, the Family's Way of Life

What this section covers: how to work out when and where a photograph was taken and who is in it, when there is no caption. Maureen Taylor's four-layer method, Russian directories of photo studios, and what a Jewish family photograph says about the family's way of life and about the occasion for which they were photographed.

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified] and others) are explained in the [Glossary](../0-start/06-glossary.md).

For how to handle originals (do not restore them yourself, label in pencil), see [Where to start](../0-start/03-where-to-start.md), section 1. For how to store scans, see [Ethics and preservation](../3-results/03-ethics-preservation.md).

## 1. The four-layer method (Maureen Taylor)

Maureen Taylor (Maureen A. Taylor, "Photo Detective", author of *Family Photo Detective*) dates a photograph by four layers of evidence [checked: maureentaylor.com, "How to Date an Old Photograph"]:

1. **Photographic technique.** The type of image itself limits the years:
   - daguerreotype — from 1839;
   - ambrotype — from 1854;
   - ferrotype (tintype) — from 1856;
   - carte de visite — after 1859.
2. **The photographer and the address.** The name of the studio and the address on the back or on the mount. From these you learn the years the studio operated (directories are below).
3. **Clothing, hairstyles, headwear.** Fashion changes quickly, especially women's.
4. **Background, furniture, props.** Studios changed their backdrops and furnishings.

The main rule: **the clues must confirm one another**. A single clue gives a wide range. The intersection of all four narrows it.

## 2. The mount and the format

The cardboard on which the photograph is mounted also dates it. Look at [from search snippet: photorepair.ru, nasledie.digital] (Russian-language sites):
- **the front and the back**: embossing, the studio's name, exhibition medals;
- **the typography** of the printer of the card, and **the grade of the card**;
- **the corners**: rounded or square.

By these clues, according to the same summary, the accuracy is about ±5 years.

Formats [from search snippet, same source]:
- **visit portrait** (*vizit-portret*; carte-de-visite size) — about 65×105 mm;
- **cabinet portrait** (*kabinet-portret*) — about 110×165 mm, in general use from the 1870s.

## 3. Russian directories of photographers and studios

If the photograph has the photographer's surname and a city, the years the studio operated can often be found (don't read Russian? See [For English speakers](../0-start/08-for-english-speakers.md)):

- **FotoTikon** — a directory of photographers of the Russian Empire 1839–1917, about 9,500–10,000 names, alphabetical, with cities and years (fototikon.blogspot.com) [checked] (Russian-language site).
- **"Bolshoi Russkii Albom"** ("The Great Russian Album") — a catalogue of photo studios **by city**, with addresses, sometimes with years of operation ("in 1870", "ca. 1880") (rusalbom.ru/foto-atelie.html) [checked] (Russian-language site). Convenient when the city is known but the photographer's name is hard to read.
- **A. P. Popov, "Rossiiskie fotografy (1839–1930). Slovar-spravochnik"** ("Russian Photographers (1839–1930): A Dictionary-Reference") — about 16,000 names; an electronic version is on the website of the Museum of Russian Photography (via the blog "El Folio, esquire", elfolio.blogspot.com) [checked] (Russian-language sources).
- **V. I. Sreznevsky, "Spravochnaya knizhka fotografa" (1889)** ("A Photographer's Handbook") — mentioned in the same place; it is not available digitally [checked].

How to search: the photographer's surname — in FotoTikon and in Popov; the city — in the "Great Russian Album". If the years coincide in two directories, you have a dating frame.

**The studio also suggests the place.** If the place of birth or residence of the family is unknown, the address of the photo studio on the mount names the city: this is how a participant in the "Rod'N'ya" project of the Am ha-Zikaron institute learned which city his great-grandmother came from (amhazikaron.org). This is a hint, not proof: people were also photographed while travelling, in the nearest district town.

No separate method for **Jewish** photo studios of the Russian Empire (databases, articles in Avotaynu) could be found. In practice, look for the studio of the shtetl or of the nearest district town (*uezdnyi gorod*) in the same directories.

## 4. Who is in the photograph

- **Ask relatives**, showing the photos one at a time: "who is this? where? on what occasion?" ([Talking to relatives](../0-start/04-talking-to-relatives.md)).
- **Compare with captioned photographs** of the same family: the same faces at different ages, the same photographer, the same backdrop.
- **Check against the timeline.** If by its dating the photograph is from around 1885 and shows an elderly couple, they cannot be people born in the 1870s ([Plan, timeline, place guide](../0-start/05-plan-timeline-place.md)).
- **A caption on the back** is evidence, but not proof: it may have been written later and from memory.
- Record an attribution as a hypothesis: "probably …, because …".

## 5. What to record about each photograph

- where the photograph came from (who holds the original);
- size, type, format, studio, city, inscriptions — verbatim, from both sides;
- the dating and **by which clues**;
- who is pictured and **who says so**;
- a scan of both sides.

For rights to old photographs on publication, see [Ethics and preservation](../3-results/03-ethics-preservation.md), section 4.

## 6. Jewish family photography: way of life and occasion

A photograph shows **a way of life and an occasion**, not nationality and not a name. Turn each clue into a chain **clue → hypothesis → document**: "kapota and sidelocks (*peyes*) on the father, a wig on the mother → a traditional family, probably a shtetl or district town → the rabbinate's metrical books for that place, revision lists and family lists". A clue without a document remains a guess.

First date the photograph (sections 1–3), then add this layer.

**Never draw conclusions about origin from a face.** There is no such clue as a "Jewish appearance". Speak only of what is visible: clothing, objects, inscriptions, place, occasion.

### Describe first, interpret afterwards

For each person: headwear, beard and sidelocks, outer clothing; for women — hair (own hair, wig, kerchief), jewellery. Objects in hands and on the table. Background: the studio backdrop, a house, a street, a cemetery. Signs and inscriptions in the frame. Inscriptions on the back — verbatim. Interpretation comes after the description.

### Clothing

- **Kapota** (a long dark frock coat), **shtraymel** (a fur hat; among Hasidim worn on Sabbaths and holidays), the peaked cap (*kartuz*), the **yarmulke** — a traditional milieu. A shtraymel points to Hasidim more likely than a kapota alone.
- **A wig or kerchief** on a woman — a sign of marriage and of observance of custom. Uncovered hair does not prove she was unmarried: in the city many did not cover their heads.
- **City dress, a gymnasium or student uniform** — education, the city. A father in a kapota and a son in a frock coat in one photograph — a generational difference, not different families.
- **Dress laws** in the Russian Empire: a tax on the sewing (1839) and wearing (1844) of Jewish dress, a ban on wearing it from 1 January 1851 (decree of 1 May 1850), a ban on Jewish women shaving their heads (1851), clarifications on sidelocks (1851–1852); after 1865 the ban weakened [checked: Electronic Jewish Encyclopedia (Russian-language), article "Odezhda" ("Clothing")]. In the Kingdom of Poland — its own decree of 1846. They were applied inconsistently. This is **context only, not a basis for dating**: a kapota in a photograph of the 1880s is an ordinary scene in a shtetl.

### Objects

A tallit and tefillin, books, Sabbath candlesticks, a hanukkiah, a kiddush cup — a sign of observance or of emphasised learning. Check **whether it is a studio prop**: the same book or the same candlestick in other people's photographs from this studio is a studio object. A photograph at home with such objects is stronger.

### Occasion → document

| What is in the photograph | Hypothesis | Which document to look for |
|---|---|---|
| a couple in finery, the bride in a veil, a chuppah | a wedding, year ± 1 | marriage record (rabbinate; after 1918 — ZAGS, Soviet civil registry) |
| a group at a fresh grave, a stone | a funeral or an unveiling of a monument | the gravestone ([Cemeteries](10-cemeteries.md)), death record |
| boys with a teacher | a kheder, a Talmud Torah | birth records of boys of that place ± 3 years |
| a boy of about thirteen in a tallit and with tefillin | a bar mitzvah | the birth record 13 years before the photograph |
| a class in uniform, teachers in the centre | a gymnasium, a school, an ORT school | lists of pupils and graduates, commemorative books (*pamyatnye knizhki*) |
| a group with a flag, a placard in Hebrew | a Zionist circle (interwar Poland, Romania, the Baltics) | memorial books of the shtetl, the community's press |
| a person in uniform | service; the uniform and insignia name the branch of service, sometimes the regiment | draft lists, casualty lists, military databases ([War](12-war-repression-siege.md)); the studio's city is the place of service |
| a small photograph with the trace of a corner mount | a photo taken from a document | passport, naturalization (in the US a photograph in the declaration of intention — from 1 July 1929) |
| a postcard portrait inscribed "as a keepsake" | the photograph was sent to relatives, possibly abroad | letters, ship manifests of relatives ([Emigration](13-emigration.md)) |

### Inscriptions

Copy them verbatim, in the original language, putting anything unread in brackets. Common formulas: Yiddish «צום אנדענק» and Hebrew «למזכרת» — "as a keepsake"; a name with a patronymic in the Jewish form; a date by the Jewish calendar. A studio stamp in Yiddish or Polish names the shtetl.

### Comparative material

Compare clues with **dated and captioned** photographs of the same milieu: the photo archives of YIVO, Yad Vashem, Centropa (about 16,800 photographs) [checked], the "Great Russian Album" (Russian-language site), memorial books of the shtetls.

### Pitfalls

- **Studio props and costumes**: studios kept hats, shawls, books, armchairs.
- **Retouching and later enlargement**: Soviet "photo enlargement" workshops painted in neckties and removed headwear. Compare with the original small photograph.
- **Montage**: in emigration and in the 1920s a portrait of an absent or dead person was pasted into a family group. The people in one photograph may never have met.
- **The absence of clues means nothing**: a Soviet photograph without a yarmulke does not mean the family did not observe the customs.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The note "(browsing agent)" is for an AI that opens websites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "Here is a photograph [both sides]. First describe what is visible, person by person and object by object, without interpreting; inscriptions verbatim. Then date it by technique, mount, studio and clothing, stating which clues you used." *Check yourself:* the studio's years of operation — in FotoTikon and the "Great Russian Album".
> - "What does this photograph say about the family's way of life and the occasion? Give a table: clue → hypothesis → how reliable → which document to look for. Do not draw conclusions from faces." *Check yourself:* whether it is a studio prop — by other photographs from the same studio.
>
> *Bad → good:* "Who are the people in this photo?" (it will guess from faces; the inscriptions and the studio are not given) → "Here is the photo, both sides: describe the people and objects, give the inscriptions verbatim, then date it and name the clues."
