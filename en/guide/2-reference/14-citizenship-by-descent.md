# Citizenship by descent: which genealogical documents and data you need

What this section covers: which genealogical documents are usually needed to restore or obtain citizenship by descent (Germany, Austria, Poland, Israel, Romania, Lithuania, Latvia and others), and how to assemble a chain of documents from yourself to the ancestor.

> **This is not legal advice.** The section describes which *genealogical* evidence the programmes for restoring or obtaining citizenship by descent usually require. Laws change, and the authorities decide each case individually. Before applying, check the official page of the authority or consulate and, if necessary, consult a lawyer.
>
> Checked against the pages on 07.10.2026. Where an official page did not open on the date of checking (some sites of Israel, Romania, Lithuania, Slovakia, Ukraine and Russia), this is stated explicitly. The marker **"not verified against the page"** means that the primary source has not been read. The date up to which each rule is current is given in the "Source" line.

The general logic of all the programmes is the same: you need an **unbroken chain of documents from you to an ancestor** who had the required citizenship or status (residence, persecution, nationality). At every link the same person must be recognisable by name, dates and place of birth, and a change of surname must be confirmed by a document.

## 1. Germany: restoration of citizenship (Art. 116(2) of the Basic Law; § 15 StAG)

- **Art. 116(2) GG (by right).** For those whose German citizenship was taken away on political, racial or religious grounds between 30.01.1933 and 08.05.1945, and for their descendants. You need to prove the ancestor's former German citizenship — a pre-war German passport, a Heimatschein, a certificate of deregistration — and the fact of persecution.
- **§ 15 StAG (in force since 20.08.2021).** For those persecuted in 1933–1945 who lost citizenship *other than* by deprivation (for example, they acquired another one before 26.02.1955), were excluded from acquiring it, or **lost their habitual residence in Germany** established before 30.01.1933. It also extends to descendants, including those adopted before 01.01.1977. **For people from the Russian Empire this is the key provision:** Jews from the Russian Empire who lived in Germany before 1933 without German citizenship may fall under point 4.
- **Documents for each generation** (BVA Merkblatt): birth certificates ("Geburts- oder Abstammungsurkunden"), marriage certificates, family books for all ancestors in the ascending line up to the persecuted person; the form Anlage_AV for each generation older than the parents; documents on change of name, divorce, adoption. Plus proof of citizenship or residence (for example, Meldebestätigungen) and of persecution: a religion entry in metrical records, documents on internment or expulsion, compensation files.
- **Form:** originals or officially (amtlich) or notarially certified copies of both sides. Foreign civil-status documents must be legalised or apostilled (exceptions: the EU, Switzerland, multilingual extracts of a number of countries, including Moldova). Translation only by a sworn translator.
- **There are no fees**, and you do not have to give up your previous citizenship (under German law). The Merkblatt gives no filing deadline; the text of § 15 does not state one either.
- **Typical problems:** the address books and Meldekarten (residents' registration cards) of cities are the main source of proof of residence before 1933. If a relative has already been through the procedure, the BVA (Federal Office of Administration) can pull up his file by its number.
- The general German line of descent: under § 4(1) StAG a child acquires citizenship at birth if at least one of the parents is a German citizen [checked: gesetze-im-internet.de, 2026-10-07]. The current wording does not describe transmission in past decades (before 1975 mainly through the father); for the old links of the chain — **not verified against the page**.
- **Source:** BVA, Merkblatt § 15 StAG (Stand: March 2025, package E15, forms — June 2024/Dec. 2025); BVA, page "Anspruchseinbürgerung Art. 116 Abs. 2 GG"; the text of § 15 StAG on gesetze-im-internet.de.

## 2. Austria: § 58c StbG (those persecuted by the Nazis and their direct descendants)

- **Who is eligible.** The persecuted person must have been an Austrian citizen, **a citizen of a state that succeeded Austria-Hungary** (according to the Foreign Ministry FAQ: Austria, Italy, Yugoslavia, Poland, Romania, Czechoslovakia, Hungary) or a stateless person — in all three cases **with his main residence in Austria**. He left abroad **before 15.05.1955** because he was subjected to persecution by NSDAP or Reich bodies or had grounds to fear it (or did not return out of fear) (the text of § 58c(1) was checked against jusline.at, verified 2026-10-07). Children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren are entitled; so are those adopted as minors.
- **For people from the Russian Empire** the programme is relevant if the ancestor lived in Austria (for example, in Vienna) before the war and by the time of leaving had Polish or Romanian citizenship or was stateless.
- **Documents.** The applicant: passport, birth certificate, documents on marriage and change of name, a certificate of no criminal record (not older than 8 months). On the ancestor's line: birth and marriage certificates of all links, establishment of paternity, adoption, all changes of name. Persecution: according to the FAQ, a note of religion in the metrical record is often enough. No check is needed if the ancestor has already been recognised as a victim (certificate, Nationalfonds payments and the like). If documents are missing, the authorities themselves search the archives.
- **Languages:** German or English, otherwise a sworn court translation. An apostille or legalisation if the issuing country is not exempt.
- **There is no deadline** (applications accepted since 01.09.2020). Filing through an Austrian embassy or consulate; no federal fees. Austria permits dual citizenship, but **Ukraine** (according to the Austrian Foreign Ministry FAQ) may deprive a person of its own citizenship.
- **Source:** oesterreich.gv.at (updated 29.01.2026); Austrian Foreign Ministry FAQ (bmeia.gv.at). The text of § 58c on ris.bka.gv.at did not open on the date of checking.

## 3. Poland: confirmation of citizenship (*potwierdzenie obywatelstwa*) and the Karta Polaka (Pole's Card)

- **The point.** This is not a new citizenship but confirmation that it has passed to you down the chain from an ancestor. The voivode decides; the consul forwards the application. Laws: the Citizenship Act of 2009 and the Consular Law of 2015.
- **The key law for the ancestors** is the law of 20.01.1920 (in force until 19.01.1951). Under Art. 2, those "osiedlone" (settled) in the territory of Poland became citizens; under Art. 11, citizenship was lost on acquiring foreign citizenship or entering foreign military or state service without permission. **An ancestor who left the Russian Empire before 1920 and was naturalized abroad most likely did not acquire Polish citizenship.** For Jews from interwar Poland, check whether the ancestor lost it before the next person in the chain was born.
- **Documents:** the application form (in Polish only); civil-status records of the applicant, parents, grandparents (birth, marriage, baptism); copies of the **Polish documents of the ancestors** (passport, identity card); documents on change of name; a full birth certificate with the parents' names.
- **Form:** copies are certified by the consul; translations by a sworn translator or the consul; documents from outside the EU need an apostille or legalisation. The fee at a US consulate is 118 USD.
- **Transmission through the father and the 1938 law** [checked, 2026-10-07, texts on api.sejm.gov.pl]. Under Art. 5 of the 1920 law, legitimate children received the citizenship of the **father**, illegitimate ones of the mother; a foreign woman who married a Pole received Polish citizenship (Art. 7). The law of 31.03.1938 (Dz.U. 1938 no. 22 item 191) made it possible to **deprive** a person living abroad of citizenship, including for 5 years of continuous stay there with "loss of connection" with the Polish state. This was a separate decision of the Minister of the Interior, not an automatic loss; it extended to the wife and children under 18 abroad. How this applies to your chain — check with a lawyer.
- **The Karta Polaka** is **not citizenship** (it lapses on acquiring citizenship or permanent residence). It requires Polish nationality of a parent or grandparent or of **two great-grandparents**, basic Polish and a declaration before the consul. Evidence: Polish identity documents, documents on service in Polish formations, certificates from Polish organisations. Documents that are not civil-status records but contain an entry of the ancestors' nationality (certificates, household books) — with an apostille.
- **Source:** gov.pl/web/usa (confirmation of citizenship); the text of the 1920 law on api.sejm.gov.pl (Dz.U. 1920 no. 7 item 44); powroty.gov.pl and the voivodeship pages (Karta Polaka).

## 4. Israel: the Law of Return and the "Nativ" check

- **Who is eligible** (Sections 4A and 4B, 1970 amendment; the text was read). The rights of a Jew extend to "a child and a grandchild of a Jew" and to the spouse of a Jew, of his children and grandchildren, except for those who have changed their religion. A Jew is a person born of a Jewish mother or who has converted and does not practise another religion. It does not matter whether this Jew is alive or has moved to Israel. This means that **on the paternal line a Jew's grandchild is also eligible**, although he is not considered a Jew under Section 4B.
- **What to prove.** Kinship with the Jewish ancestor and his Jewishness. For people from the USSR the "Nativ" consul usually looks at **Soviet documents with the "nationality" entry**: birth certificates of parents and children (in the USSR the parents were listed with their nationality), passports, military IDs, work record books, archival certificates, pre-revolutionary rabbinical metrical records. [from search snippet]: when requesting a duplicate certificate, ask that the parents' nationality be stated.
- **Apostille.** According to search results from Israeli embassy pages: since 01.08.2019, documents **issued after 31.12.1997** are submitted to the "Nativ" consul with an apostille; originals are submitted; the certificate of no criminal record must be not older than 6 months, with the apostille of the interior ministry of each country of residence, for all family members from the age of 14 [checked: page of the repatriation department of the Israeli embassy in Riga, embassies.gov.il, 2026-10-07]. The rule "apostille on documents after 31.12.1997 from 01.08.2019" was not found on this page — **not verified against the page** (gov.il did not open on the date of checking).
- **Outside the former USSR** (the Jewish Agency): legalised originals, a birth certificate, documents on marital status, a certificate of no criminal record and a letter from a rabbi of a recognised community as proof of connection with Judaism. The time from opening the case to aliyah is usually 3–6 months.
- **Typical problems:** in Soviet documents the nationality was sometimes changed to "Russian" or "Ukrainian"; if the parents have different nationalities, the deciding document is the one with the Jewish mother or grandfather. Discrepancies in the name (Movsha/Mikhail, Khaya/Klara) are removed by a certificate from an archive or ZAGS that it is the same person. There were rumours of plans to change the "grandchildren clause" — **not verified against the page**.
- **If there are no metrical records.** The Am ha-Zikaron institute lists documents with an indication of nationality that can replace metrical records: military, party, pension, trade-union, workplace certificates, documents on education and evacuation, Red Cross certificates. First reconstruct the ancestor's biography (work, service, study) in order to understand in the archives of which cities to look for these documents (amhazikaron.org) [checked].
- **Two pitfalls**, in N. Lipes's observation: an ancestor who was baptised even before the revolution does not qualify under the Law of Return (Section 4A excludes those who changed religion); a Jewish surname proves only the male line, and a man could have taken his wife's surname [a practitioner's opinion; the decision is the consul's].
- **Source:** the text of the 1970 amendment (Adalah, translation); jewishagency.org/aliyah; the Nativ pages on embassies.gov.il and gov.il/ru/service/confirm_aliyah_rights_for_former_ussr did not open on the date of checking.

## 5. Spain: Sephardim (law 12/2015) and the "grandchildren law" (20/2022)

- **Law 12/2015 is closed to new applications.** The filing deadline expired on **01.10.2019** (three years plus an extension of a year by decision of the Council of Ministers of 09.03.2018). Documents for cases already filed could be sent in until **01.09.2021** (an extension by Resolución 13.05.2020 because of COVID).
- **What was required** (to understand old cases): a certificate of Sephardic origin from the Federation of Jewish Communities of Spain, the DELE A2 and CCSE exams, appearing in person before a notary. Ashkenazi origin, typical for people from the Russian Empire, gave no grounds under this law.
- **Law 20/2022, "Ley de Memoria Democrática", additional provision 8.** For children and grandchildren, born outside Spain, of *originally Spanish* citizens who went into exile (for political, ideological, religious and similar reasons), children of Spanish women who lost their citizenship through marriage to a foreigner before 1978, and so on. **Filing is closed:** appointments were taken until **22.10.2025**, there have been no new applications since 23.10.2025. For people from the Russian Empire/USSR it applies only in rare cases (a Spanish ancestor).
- **Source:** BOE-A-2015-7045; BOE-A-2020-5296; the Spanish embassy in Panama (on the 01.10.2019 deadline); BOE-A-2022-17099 (DA 8ª); the Spanish embassy in Budapest, announcement of 27.10.2025.

## 6. Portugal: the Sephardic programme

- **New applications are no longer accepted.** Lei Orgânica n.º 1/2026 of 18.05.2026 (corrected by Declaração de Retificação n.º 17/2026) **abolished the special regime for descendants of Portuguese Sephardim**. The new rules apply from **19.05.2026**. To cases filed earlier the previous wording continues to apply ("aos processos pendentes continua a aplicar-se a redação anterior"). The Constitutional Court (Acórdão 1133/2025, communiqué of 15.12.2025) found four provisions of the bill (Decreto 17/XVII) unconstitutional — none concerns the Sephardim; on the other provisions it found no unconstitutionality [checked: Constitutional Court communiqué, 2026-10-07]. Whether the court separately assessed the abolition of the Sephardic regime, and on what principle, is not stated in the communiqué; the full text of the decision has **not been read**.
- **Changes before 2026.** Decreto-Lei 26/2022 (18.03.2022): a certificate of the Portuguese Jewish community in the approved form and proof of a connection with Portugal (inheritance, property, regular trips). Lei Orgânica 1/2024: a requirement of **3 years of lawful residence** in Portugal.
- **Discrepancy:** the service page of justica.gov.pt "É descendente de judeus sefarditas portugueses?" on 07.10.2026 still describes this route (fee 250 €, 3 years of residence). It has apparently not been updated; the official announcement of the 2026 law is the one to trust.
- The new law extended the possibility of obtaining citizenship to **great-grandchildren** of Portuguese citizens ("alarga aos bisnetos de portugueses", justica.gov.pt news) [checked, 2026-10-07]. This is not about Sephardim or people from the Russian Empire; the conditions for great-grandchildren are not spelled out in the news item.
- **Source:** justica.gov.pt, news item "Lei da Nacionalidade: novas regras entram em vigor a 19 de maio" (2026); Diário da República, DL 26/2022 and LO 1/2024 ([from search snippet]).

## 7. Romania: restoration of citizenship, Art. 11 of law 21/1991 (Bessarabia, Northern Bukovina, Herța)

- **The law** (consolidated text of 2010, read; the 2025 changes are below, under "Pitfalls"). Art. 11: persons who acquired Romanian citizenship by birth or adoption and lost it "din motive neimputabile lor" (for reasons not attributable to them) or against their will, **and their descendants up to the 3rd degree** (children, grandchildren, great-grandchildren) may restore citizenship while keeping their foreign one and living abroad. Conditions under Art. 8: loyalty, age 18, good reputation. The application is in Romanian; it can be filed at a consulate (Art. 13).
- **For people from the Russian Empire/USSR** this is a route for descendants of residents of Bessarabia, Northern Bukovina and the Herța region who became Romanian citizens after 1918 and lost citizenship when the territories passed to the USSR (28.06.1940).
- **Documents** ([from search snippet] from the mae.ro and cetatenie.just.ro sites; the sites did not open on the date of checking): civil-status records of the applicant and of **all ancestors along the chain up to the former citizen of Romania born in the territory of Romania before 28.06.1940** (birth, marriage, change of name, death, divorce). Submitted as originals and certified copies, with an apostille or super-legalisation and a Romanian translation. Plus a passport and a certificate of no criminal record from abroad. Metrical records of 1918–1940 from Bessarabia are Romanian civil-status records; in Moldova they are searched for in the Arhiva Națională or the Agenția Servicii Publice — **not verified against the page**.
- **Pitfalls for Jewish families** (historical, **not verified against the page**): the 1938 decree on the review of Jews' citizenship (the Goga government) deprived some Jews of citizenship. If the ancestor lost it, the link "was a citizen of Romania" will have to be proved separately. **The language is mandatory:** Legea 14/2025 (Monitorul Oficial no. 218 of 12.03.2025) rewrote Art. 11(1) — descendants must prove knowledge of Romanian at level **B1** (a certificate of an accredited university or 3 years of study in Romanian); former citizens themselves and applicants aged 65 and over are exempt. The deadline for submitting the language certificate was extended by OUG 16/2026 to **15.03.2027** [checked, 2026-10-07: law text — legeaz.net, OUG — juridice.ro].
- **Source:** the text of law 21/1991 (consolidation of 2010, globalcit.eu); the official pages legislatie.just.ro, cetatenie.just.ro, mae.ro did not open on the date of checking (07.10.2026).

## 8. Lithuania and Latvia: descendants of interwar citizens

- **Lithuania.** The right to restore citizenship without a time limit belongs to persons who held citizenship of the Republic of Lithuania **before 15.06.1940**, and their descendants; the place of residence does not matter (Art. 9 of the citizenship law, version of 02.12.2010 no. XI-1196, e-seimas). Dual citizenship is allowed for those who left or were deported before 11.03.1990 and their descendants (Art. 7).
  - Evidence ([from search snippet] from the mfa.lt and migracija.lrv.lt sites; the sites did not open on the date of checking). Direct: a Lithuanian internal or foreign passport from before 15.06.1940, documents on army or civil service, metrical records referring to citizenship. Indirect: study, work, life in Lithuania before 1940.
  - According to the methodology of the Migration Department ([from search snippet]), a person is not considered to have held citizenship if he lost it, acquired a foreign one or **died before 15.06.1940**. **An ancestor who left the Kovno or Vilna guberniya before 1918–1920 was not a Lithuanian citizen.**
  - Metrical records before 1940 are held by the Lithuanian State Historical Archive (LVIA). Filing through MIGRIS.
- **Latvia.** A descendant of a person who was a citizen of Latvia **on 17.06.1940** is registered as a citizen ("the doctrine of state continuity").
  - Evidence of the ancestor's citizenship: metrical records, data of the 1935 census, tax and conscription files, a pre-war Latvian passport. Plus the chain of kinship.
  - The application is in Latvian; foreign documents need a Latvian translation and an apostille (except the EU/EEA, the United Kingdom, Switzerland). A decision is usually made within a month.
  - Dual citizenship is permitted only with a list of countries (the EU, NATO and others). Citizens of other countries, including Russia, may be required to renounce their previous citizenship. According to the PMLP page the list includes the EU, NATO, EFTA, Australia, Brazil, New Zealand [checked, 2026-10-07]; the full list and exceptions (for example, for those who left in 1940–1990) are on linked pages, **not verified against the page**.
- **Source:** e-seimas.lrs.lt (Lithuanian law, 2010 version); pmlp.gov.lv, pages "Descendants of Latvian Citizens" (updated 05.06.2025) and "...citizen of another country".

## 9. Hungary, Czechia, Slovakia (briefly)

- **Hungary: simplified naturalization** (§ 4(3) of the 1993 law LV). For those whose ancestor was a Hungarian citizen or who "valószínűsíti magyarországi származását" (makes his Hungarian origin probable). **Spoken Hungarian is mandatory** at the interview; no exam is needed. The chain must be unbroken: a great-grandfather's metrical record is not enough, the intermediate links are needed as well. On the maternal line marriage certificates are needed. Accepted are state metrical records, church and archival certificates with the exact archival reference (volume, folio, entry), military and work record books, school certificates. Everything — with a certified Hungarian translation and legalisation. Relevant for Transcarpathia (Hungarian until 1920 and in 1938/39–1944). Source: Kormányhivatal leaflet of 05.07.2023.
- **Czechia: declaration under § 31** of law 186/2013. Citizenship is acquired by children and **grandchildren** of former citizens of Czechia or Czechoslovakia, if they are not currently citizens of Slovakia. Documents: birth and marriage certificates of the applicant, parents, grandparents; a document on when and how the ancestor acquired Czechoslovak citizenship and how he lost it. Fee 500 CZK, decision within 60 days. Filing at a consulate or in the districts of Prague. Relevant for **Subcarpathian Rus** (Transcarpathia in 1919–1938). Whether the right extends to Transcarpathians who became Soviet citizens under the 1945 treaty — **not verified against the page**. Source: Ministry of the Interior of the Czech Republic, archiv.mv.gov.cz (page of 18.05.2026).
- **Slovakia.** The amendment in force since 01.04.2022 gives simplified naturalization to children, grandchildren and great-grandchildren of **Czechoslovak citizens born in the territory of Slovakia** ("rodičov, prarodičov alebo praprarodičov"). From **15.07.2026**, under the amendment of law 40/1993, the condition of permitted residence in Slovakia is abolished for them, and the application can be filed at an embassy also by post or electronically (announcement of the Slovak Interior Ministry, republished by reporter24.sk on 14.07.2026) [checked, 2026-10-07]. The official page mzv.sk did not open on the date of checking; the list of documents — **not verified against the page**.

## 10. How to assemble the package: general order and typical mistakes

1. **First determine the "anchor ancestor"** — the person who had the required status: citizenship, residence before a certain date, persecution or nationality. From him down to you a chain "birth → marriage → birth…" is built without gaps. The Hungarian leaflet says directly: one great-grandfather's metrical record is not enough, all the intermediate links are needed.
2. **A document on the ancestor's status ≠ a document on kinship.** Both types are needed: passport, Heimatschein, military ID, census — for status; metrical records — for kinship.
3. **Matching names.** Confirm transliteration and change of name (Khaim → Efim, Cyrillic → Latin, a woman's surname after marriage) with a marriage or name-change document, or an archival certificate of identity. Germany, Austria and Poland directly require documents on all changes of name.
4. **An archival certificate or a copy of a metrical record?** The authorities accept official documents: duplicate ZAGS/RAGS certificates, archival certificates or extracts with the archive's stamp. Photocopies and printouts from databases (JewishGen, Geni, MyHeritage) are only a guide for searching. Germany does not accept uncertified copies or certifications by unauthorised persons.
5. **Where to order.** (Don't read Russian? See [For English speakers](../0-start/08-for-english-speakers.md).)
   - **Ukraine:** duplicate certificates — the DRATs (civil registry) bodies. Metrical records before the 1920s — oblast archives (DAKhmO, DAKO, DAViO and so on). Since 19.09.2023 the apostille on archival documents is affixed by **Ukrderzharkhiv** (State Archival Service), on documents of the justice bodies by the Ministry of Justice (about 610 UAH, usually 2 working days; diia.gov.ua).
   - **Moldova:** the National Archives Agency issues certificates of birth, marriage and death from the metrical records of **1820–1924**, from 58 lei, application online through servicii.gov.md. Records of 1925–1940 — **not verified against the page**.
   - **Russia:** the apostille on ZAGS documents is affixed by the regional ZAGS bodies, on archival certificates by the archives; the state fee is 2,500 RUB for each document (sub-para. 48, para. 1, Art. 333.33 of the Tax Code of the Russian Federation, text on base.garant.ru, verified 2026-10-07); the procedure at a specific ZAGS or archive — **not verified against the page** (the agencies' sites did not open).
   - **Belarus** — **not verified against the page**.
6. **The apostille is affixed by the country that issued the document**, and only on documents of its own bodies. The translation is made after the apostille, usually in the country of filing or by a sworn translator. Example: a certificate from a Ukrainian oblast archive is apostilled in Ukraine and translated into German by a sworn translator.
7. **Typical mistakes:**
   - a missing link — for example, the parents' marriage in a chain through the mother;
   - different spellings of a name without a document of identity;
   - a certificate without a stamp or with corrections;
   - a translation by an unrecognised translator;
   - an old certificate of no criminal record (Austria — 8 months, Israel — 6);
   - filing under a closed programme (Spain 12/2015 and 20/2022, Portugal);
   - an overlooked law on loss of citizenship for the ancestor (Poland 1920, Lithuania before 1940);
   - relying on a MyHeritage tree instead of archival documents.
8. **Soviet documents:** the "nationality" entry (Israel), notes of the place of birth by the old borders (Romania, Poland, Czechia). If a document has been lost, request an "archival certificate of birth" from the metrical book or a "certificate of the absence of a record". The latter is also useful: the authority sees that a search was made.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The note "(browsing agent)" is for an AI that opens websites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "I want to apply for [country] citizenship through the ancestor [who, years, place]. Which documents are needed for each generation, and what are the requirements for copies and the apostille?" *Check yourself:* the requirements — only on the authority's official website on the date of filing; laws change.
> - "Here is how the ancestor's name is written in different documents: [list]. Make a table of correspondences and explain which document is needed to link the forms." *Check yourself:* the consulate decides for itself; the AI's table is not a legal opinion.
>
> *Bad → good:* "Am I entitled to [country] citizenship?" (a legal conclusion from memory, and laws change) → "Which documents for each generation does [authority] require for the ancestor [who, years, place]? Give a link to the official page."
