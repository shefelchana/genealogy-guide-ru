# Other People's Trees, Communities, Paid Researchers

What this section covers: how to use other people's online trees without multiplying their errors; how to ask a question in a group or on a forum so that it gets answered; how to hire a paid researcher and not fall into the hands of a fraudster.

## 1. Other people's online trees

**A tree is not a source but someone else's hypothesis** (see [Standard of proof](../3-results/02-standard-of-proof.md), section 1).

- **Do not attach anything merely because it appears in someone else's tree**: errors multiply. Keep trees separate from documents. Do not hurry to attach records — Amy Johnson Crow ("Genealogy Mistakes That Everyone Makes") [checked].
- **The most vulnerable point is the link between generations.** Check every source of the tree's author. If there are no sources, look for them yourself. Every "child → parents" link needs a document. Beware of namesakes of the same place and age, even with a rare surname — Melanie Mayo (Family History Daily, "Are You Sure They're Your Ancestors?") [checked].

### How to use someone else's tree sensibly

1. Take **hints** from it: place, names, years, references to documents.
2. Open **the documents themselves** that the author cites.
3. If there are no documents, record the information in your hypotheses file, not in your tree ([template](../../templates/hypothesis.md)).
4. Write to the author: they may have family papers that are not in the tree ([Requests and letters](20-requests-letters.md)).

### Geni and shared trees

Israel Pickholtz (Avotaynu Online, "Concerns About Geni and Other Collaborative Genealogy Websites", 2015) [checked]:
- curators edit other people's research;
- automatic merging confuses Hebrew and Yiddish variants of names;
- in a shared tree you cannot see what is proved and what is an assumption.

Advice: keep **your own database** with source citations, and use Geni only as an additional tool. For how to choose a platform, see [Tree platforms](16-tree-platforms.md).

The Geni project "SmartCopy Best Practices" (transferring profiles from MyHeritage to Geni): treat trees without references to documents as unreliable; do not copy many profiles at once; compare in two windows [from search snippet].

### May you take other people's work?

Facts (names, dates, relationships) are not protected by copyright. Notes, reasoning and texts in someone else's tree are protected: you need permission and attribution of the author — Judy G. Russell (The Legal Genealogist, "Using the Work of Others") [from search snippet: the site did not open]. This is American law; for more, see [Ethics and preservation](../3-results/04-ethics-preservation.md), section 4.

## 2. How to ask questions in groups and on forums

### JewishGen Discussion Group

The group's rules ("Discussion Group Guidelines") [checked]:
- **the subject line should be specific**. Not "Help" and not "My grandmother". Bad — "Need census info", good — "Need 1910 Census Info";
- **SURNAMES IN CAPITAL LETTERS** in the subject and in the text;
- **the place name in its modern spelling**, with variants in brackets;
- **a signature** with your name and place of residence;
- **search first** in JewishGen's resources and in the group's archive;
- a reply of interest only to the person who asked belongs in private correspondence.

The rule on capitals and place names is also on JewishGen's "rules" page [from search snippet].

### ViewMate: asking for a document to be read

Answers to ViewMate's frequently asked questions [checked]:
- formats JPEG, GIF, PNG or TIF, **up to 3 MB**; scan handwriting at **200–300 dpi**;
- an image stays up **for 7 days**;
- if you need not a full translation but a single line, **say so**;
- give several images **in one message** in the group;
- say thanks through the reply form.

A native speaker can often decipher a difficult record in minutes (Polish Roots Project) [checked].

### The VGD forum (Russian-language)

A summary of advice from VGD (All-Russian Genealogical Tree) materials [from search snippet]:
- a subject line of the form "Seeking [Surname], [village], [uezd], [guberniya]";
- **everything known goes in the first message**; for each person — spouse, parents and where the information comes from;
- keep adding your finds to your family-surname thread: the more often it comes up in the updates, the better the chance of help.

### General rules

- One question — one message.
- Show **what you have already searched** and where (an extract from your research log).
- Attach **a scan, not a retelling**.
- Check the answer as you would any find ([Standard of proof](../3-results/02-standard-of-proof.md)).

We could not find advice from IAJGS moderators on how to ask questions.

### What else helps get an answer (observations of 2025–2026)

From reading JewishGen, VGD, the Hebrew forum Tapuz and the FamilySearch Community [checked]:
- **State at once where you have already searched.** To a question without this, the first reply on Tapuz began with a reproach, and then the person was sent to databases he had already been through.
- **Give the place with an "address"**: country, guberniya or oblast, modern and historical name. Not everyone knows the names of the shtetls.
- **An archival reference and a link to the image.** Those who can open the same image will answer. Translation requests on the FamilySearch Community with the image and record number get a word-for-word translation in a day or two.
- **Post the scan through ViewMate, not as an attachment**: the JewishGen mailing list does not accept attachments.
- **Check the JewishGen Family Finder before posting** — the answer may already be there.
- **Do not repeat the same question** sooner than three months later, do not post large chunks of your tree, do not ask for extracts from paid databases (JewishGen rules).
- **A screenshot of the archive's reply** helps: it shows at once that the documents, for example, are held not in the archive but in the local administration.
- **Report the outcome and say thank you.** The outcome in the shared thread helps the next person.
- **On VGD** a search thread works like a "message to the universe": a reply can come years later (docs.vgd.ru).

### A shtetl page as a "magnet" for relatives

Phyllis Kramer (JewishGen) advises gathering everyone who has marked the shtetl in the JewishGen Family Finder and combining the finds on the shtetl's page (KehilaLinks): by her account, after the shtetl page was created she and five other researchers of the same shtetl hired a researcher in the archive, and he found more than 1,500 documents (Avotaynu Online, 2009) [checked]. On the shtetl page of the Ukraine Research Division a Town Leader is listed — check his materials before you make your own extract.

Facebook groups are inconvenient for systematic searching: a search within a group shows only the beginnings of posts, and the answers are hidden in the comments. But there are live offers of help on the spot there ("I will be at the archive — I will look at the files"). The arrangements and the correspondence are always made by the person themselves. For a list of groups and forums in Russian, English and Hebrew, see [Groups and communities](../../reading/groups-and-communities.md).

## 3. A paid researcher

### When you need one

When the file is not digitised, the archive answers only on site, or you need someone who reads the handwriting and the language. First check whether the material is online for free ([Requests and letters](20-requests-letters.md), section 1).

For Belarus, before hiring it is worth looking at the shtetl projects of the Belarus SIG (early access to translations for a donation), the district coordinators and the Help Desk (JewishGen Belarus SIG) [checked].

### How to commission

The JewishGen compilations (Peter Haas, Phyllis Kramer, InfoFile "Professional Researchers") [checked] and the Association of Professional Genealogists (APG, "How to Hire a Professional Genealogist") [checked]:

1. **First formulate your own goal** (APG) and the request — as specifically as possible (JewishGen).
2. **Check the researcher's languages**: Cyrillic, Hebrew, Latin, Kurrent — whichever your documents need (JewishGen).
3. **Ask for sample reports and references** (APG). Look for reviews in the JewishGen groups (JewishGen).
4. **A written contract** (APG): goal, scope, hours, fee and expenses, deadline, exactly what is to be delivered, how to keep in touch.
5. **Set a payment ceiling at once** and ask to be notified when it is reached (JewishGen).
6. **Name the file numbers you have already found** from the inventories (*opisi*) and pay for a specific scope of work; it is convenient to pay **archive by archive**: the next archive after the report on the previous one. A genealogist takes money for the work, not for the result, and must report which days and which files were examined (N. Lipes, nadialipes.info).
7. **Warning signs** (same source): promises documents younger than 75–100 years without a power of attorney and proof of kinship; cannot name the fonds available for the place and their volume. Google the researcher's name and compare his answer with what is written on the forums. It is better to hire a resident of the city where the archive is.

### What the report should contain

- **The exact archival reference and location** of each find: archive, fond, inventory, file, folio (Vera Miller, below).
- **Scans** of the documents, not only a retelling.
- Where they searched and **found nothing** — with the coverage (otherwise you will pay for the same search a second time).

### Signs of fraud

Vera Miller (blog "Find Lost Russian & Ukrainian Family", "The Art of Avoiding Scams in Ukrainian and Russian Genealogy", 2021) [checked]:
- **start with a small order**;
- **search for the researcher's name** on Google and Facebook along with words about complaints;
- **demand the exact archival reference** and the storage location of each find;
- **be wary of** a large advance payment and vague prices;
- **pay by a transfer that requires ID**, and check the recipient's name;
- **the main risk is emotion**, the "a family has been found" feeling: that is what scammers play on.

### Questions for a researcher (Russian-speaking)

A checklist from a press release in the newspaper *Sovershenno sekretno* (24.09.2026) [checked]. ⚠️ The commissioner of the publication is not named, it is **promotional text**; the questions are reasonable, but the source is biased.
- Will he name specific oblast archives for your place?
- Do his past reports include scans with fond / inventory / file / folio numbers?
- Will he decipher a page of a metrical book in front of you?
- Where will he start? A good answer is **with the settlement and the parish** (or the rabbinate): archives are organised by fonds, not by surnames.
- Does he know about the **75-year** restriction on access to documents containing personal information ([Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](01-records-empire-ussr.md), section 7)?
- Does he have a plan for a dead end?

Miriam Weiner (Routes to Roots) maintains an inventory of surviving Jewish and civil records by shtetl; no advice on hiring researchers from her was found in the search results [from search snippet].

> **How to ask AI**
>
> - "Help me draft a question for the [name] forum following its rules: a subject line with the SURNAME in capitals and the place, what is known, where I have already searched, the archival reference and a link to the image." *Check yourself:* you post and reply yourself.
> - "Here is someone else's tree with my ancestor [extract]. Which of its statements are backed by documents and which are not? What should I check first?" *Check yourself:* someone else's tree is someone else's hypothesis.
> - "Here is a reply from a researcher or agency [text]. Are there signs of fraud: promised results, documents without a power of attorney, no list of fonds?" *Check yourself:* the decision to hire and to pay is yours.
