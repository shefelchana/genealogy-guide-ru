# Orthodox, peasants and other non-Jewish lines of the Russian Empire

What this section covers: where to look for Orthodox ancestors, peasants, Cossacks, Christian townspeople (*meshchane*), Catholics, Lutherans and Old Believers; what parish metrical books, confession lists and clergy rosters, revision lists by estate and volost, the documents of 1861, resettlement records and Soviet village books give. How this search differs from a Jewish one.

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified] and others) are explained in the [Glossary](../0-start/06-glossary.md). The links were checked on 08.10.2026. The common matters — the archival reference, the revision "ladder", registration (*pripiska*), substitutes for metrical books — are in [Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](01-records-empire-ussr.md); here is only what concerns non-Jewish lines. Most of the archive catalogues and scans below are in Russian or Ukrainian; don't read Russian? See [For English speakers](../0-start/08-for-english-speakers.md).

## 1. How it differs from a Jewish search

The route is the same: question → place → source → index → scan → check → record. But the footholds are different.

- **Surnames appear at different times in different estates.** Among nobles and merchants — early; among the clergy a surname was often given at the seminary, and brothers could bear different ones; among the Cossacks of Little Russia (Malorossiya) — already in the 18th century. Among serf peasants of the central guberniyas a stable surname often appears only in the second half of the 19th or the early 20th century. "Ivan Petrov" in a revision is usually Ivan, son of Pyotr, not the surname Petrov.
- **A person is registered to a parish, not only to a village.** The metrical record is sought in the book of the church to which the village belonged in the year of the event. One church served several villages, and villages moved from parish to parish. First establish the parish (section 4).
- **Serfs were recorded by owner.** Revision lists of landowners' peasants are grouped by landowner and estate. Without knowing the owner, you will have to leaf through the whole uezd.
- **Names were given by the saints' calendar (*svyattsy*).** Often after the saint whose day falls on the birthday or baptism, or on the days nearby. This helps to estimate a date, but the rule is not rigid. The same name could be given to two children if the first died.
- **There is no single "JewishGen".** Indexes are scattered: FamilySearch, regional projects, forums. Reading a book page by page is the usual thing.
- **But there are more sources.** For Orthodox people almost every year has two or three independent documents: a metrical record, a confession list, and once in a few decades a revision.

## 2. Metrical books of Orthodox parishes

- Keeping metrical books was ordered by Peter I in 1722; the book has three parts: births, marriages, deaths (FamilySearch Wiki, "Russia Church Records") [checked].
- **Two copies.** One stayed in the church, the second (a copy, the "duplicate") went to the **spiritual consistory** of the diocese. Consistory copies are usually bound **by uezd for a year**: all the churches of the uezd in one volume, and the needed church is looked for inside (this is how FamilySearch Wiki, "Ukraine Church Records", describes them) [checked]. Example: "ДАЖО 1-78-1143. 1858. Метричні книги православних церков Ровенського повіту" on Commons — 1,512 pages for one uezd for one year [checked]. (ДАЖО = DAZhO, State Archive of Zhytomyr Oblast.)
- **Where the consistory fond is:** in the archive of the former guberniya (diocesan) centre, not where the village is now. Verified examples:

| Consistory | Archive and fond | Marker |
|---|---|---|
| Volhynia | DAZhO (State Archive of Zhytomyr Oblast, Zhytomyr), fond 1, 1741–1921 | [checked: Ukrainian Wikisource (Вікіджерела), Commons] |
| Podolia | DAKhmO (State Archive of Khmelnytskyi Oblast, Khmelnytskyi), fond 315 | [checked: Commons] |
| Kyiv | TsDIAK (Central State Historical Archive of Ukraine, Kyiv), fond 127, 1700–1930 | [checked: Ukrainian Wikisource] |
| Nizhny Novgorod | TsANO (Central Archive of Nizhny Novgorod Oblast, Nizhny Novgorod), fond 570 | [checked: D. Kudinov's catalogue] |

- **What is in a record.** Birth: dates of birth and baptism, the name, the parents with their estate and registration ("peasant of the village of N"), the godparents (*vospriemniki*), the priest. Marriage: ages, which marriage it is for each party, the sponsors. Death: age, cause, where buried. Godparents and sponsors are relatives and neighbours: this is how searching by the surroundings works ([Standard of proof](../3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md), section 9).
- Dates are by the Julian calendar ([Handwriting, transliteration, dates](05-handwriting-transliteration-dates.md)).
- After 1917 the church's copy often ended up in ZAGS (the Soviet civil registry) or a district archive. If there is no consistory copy for a year, look for it there.

## 3. Confession lists (*ispovednye vedomosti*, "rosspisi")

Annual lists of parishioners **by household**: who went to confession and communion in Lent. All estates are recorded; they exist from the 1720s (FamilySearch Wiki) [checked].

**What is in them** (from the 1912 lists of Rivne uezd, DAZhO 1-74-211) [checked]:
- the household number, and a continuous numbering of males and females;
- the head of the household, wife, children, brothers — with the relationship stated;
- "years from birth" ("лета от рождения") — separately for men and women;
- columns "who were at confession", "confessed only and did not take communion", "were not at confession" with the reason: small children are marked "by reason of minority".

**What they are for:**
- **a substitute for a metrical record**: if the birth book for a year is missing, a list several years later will show the child with age and parents;
- **a bridge between revisions**: revisions are rare, the lists are almost annual;
- **the whole family**, including widows, foundlings and hired hands;
- the note "schismatic" ("раскольник") or "did not attend out of obstinacy" points to Old Believers (section 9).

**How to read them:**
- first the printed heading: one column has the household number, the next one the person's number; do not confuse them;
- age "floats" by a year or two from list to list, and for old people it is rounded to 5 and 10 — compare several years;
- household numbers change from year to year: link by names and ages, as in the revisions;
- volumes are sometimes split alphabetically by village: on Commons, for example, Rivne uezd of 1912 is "Villages А–Ж", "З–О", "П–Ч" [checked].

## 4. Clergy rosters (*klirovye vedomosti*): how to find the parish

An annual report of the church. In the rosters of the Volhynia diocese of 1915 there are three parts (DAZhO 1-78-504) [checked]:
1. **About the church:** when it was built, the nearest churches and distances to them, and above all the lines "copies of the metrical books are kept intact since … year" and "confession lists are kept intact since … year". From these you see whether the books survived in the church itself by 1915.
2. **About the clergy:** the priest, deacon and psalmist with service records and children — if the ancestor was from the clergy, this is a ready-made reference.
3. **About the parish:** the villages and hamlets of the parish, the number of households, the number of souls male and female, by estate; separately — people of other faiths, "schismatics", sectarians.

Other ways to find the parish (D. Kudinov's catalogue, the section "How to find the parish?") [checked]:
- **diocesan directories**: they list the villages of each parish;
- **"Lists of populated places"** of some guberniyas give the parish for each village;
- **archival reference books**: in some oblasts alphabets of villages with the church indicated have been compiled.

Record the parish in the [place guide](../../templates/place-guide.md) separately for each year: a village could be assigned to a new church.

## 5. Revisions for peasants

For how to work down through the revisions, see [Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](01-records-empire-ussr.md), section 3, and the skill [`tracing-revision-records`](../../../skills/tracing-revision-records/SKILL.md). For peasants it is important **which category** they belonged to.

| Category | How recorded | Where to look |
|---|---|---|
| **Landowners'** (serfs) and **household serfs** (*dvorovye*) | a list by owner: "the patrimony of the landowner N, village …"; transfers — "bought", "given as dowry", "given as a recruit" | fonds of the treasury chamber (*kazennaya palata*, from the 1780s) in the oblast archive; first find out the owner |
| **State** (crown) peasants | by volost and village community | the same; lists of state peasants are often separate files |
| **Appanage** (before 1797 — palace) | by appanage offices and volosts | fonds of the appanage offices; the treasury chamber |
| **Odnodvortsy** (single-homesteaders) | in separate lists, often already with surnames | the treasury chamber |

- **Revisions I–IV (1719–1782)** concern mainly the Orthodox and peasant population. I and II — men only; from III women are also recorded ([section 01](01-records-empire-ussr.md)). The *landrat* books and revision lists of 1707–1792 in RGADA (Russian State Archive of Ancient Acts, fond 350) are digitised, but can be viewed only in the reading room [from search snippet].
- Examples on Commons [checked]: "ДАЖО 118-14-0256. 1858. Ревізькі казки поміщичих селян Рівненського повіту" (the fond is the Volhynia treasury chamber); "ДАКО 280-2-344. 1816. Ревізькі казки казенних селян Київського повіту" (ДАКО = DAKO, State Archive of Kyiv Oblast).
- **A serf village could change owners** between revisions (sale, division of an inheritance, dowry). If a household "disappeared", look for the same village under a different landowner.

## 6. The year 1861 and the volost boards

- **Charters of allotment** (*ustavnye gramoty*, 1861–1863) fixed the land allotment and the obligations of the former serfs; they were checked by peace mediators; more than 111,000 charters were drawn up; they are kept in oblast archives, with copies in RGIA (Russian State Historical Archive) (Wikipedia, "Уставная грамота помещика и крестьян") [checked]. The main thing in them is the owner, the village and the number of revision souls; according to the State Archive of Lipetsk Oblast, at the end of a charter the heads of families who signed it are sometimes listed [from search snippet]. Look nearby for **redemption deeds** (*vykupnye akty*).
- **Volost boards.** Family lists, books of village-assembly resolutions, conscription lists (from 1874), passport books, volost-court files. Survival varies greatly; volost fonds are in oblast and district archives. Look for the volost in the archive's inventory, not by the name of the village.

## 7. Resettlers

Mass resettlement to Siberia, the Far East and the Steppe region went on from the 1880s, and the peak came with the Stolypin reform (1906–1911).

- **At the place of departure:** petitions for resettlement, family lists of those leaving, release resolutions of village communities — in the fonds of guberniya and uezd institutions.
- **In the centre:** the fond of the Resettlement Administration, RGIA fond 391, 1867–1918; it has, for example, family lists of peasants who petitioned for resettlement (from descriptions on the VGD forum) [from search snippet].
- **At the place of settlement:** lists of settlers by plots; metrical books — in the consistory fond of the **new** diocese (Tomsk, Tobolsk and so on). Compare with D. Kudinov's catalogue (below) for where the metrical books of the needed oblast are kept.
- The metrical records of children of one family may lie in two or three guberniyas.

## 8. Cossacks, odnodvortsy, Christian townspeople

- **Cossacks.** Metrical books of stanitsa churches are in the consistory fonds; service documents (service records, stanitsa boards) are in the archives of the former Cossack hosts. Example: the portal "Donskie arkhivy" ("Don Archives", donarch.ru) with a section "Metrical books"; the site opened, the contents only after logging in to a personal account [checked: only the shell]. The Little Russian Cossacks of Poltava and Chernigov guberniyas were a separate estate with their own revisions.
- **Odnodvortsy** — personally free descendants of service people of the southern uezds; in the revisions they are recorded separately and often with surnames.
- **Christian townspeople (*meshchane*)** — assessment books of townspeople's societies, files of the townspeople's boards and city dumas, metrical books of city parishes. Registration to one town and living in another is the usual thing ([section 01](01-records-empire-ussr.md), section 4).

## 9. Catholics, Lutherans, Old Believers

- **Catholics.** Metrical books under the law of 1826 in three copies; by 1900 there were five dioceses in the empire (without the Kingdom of Poland) — Tiraspol, Zhytomyr, Mogilev, Vilna, Kovno (FamilySearch Wiki) [checked]. The consistory fonds are in the archives of the former diocesan centres. Example: DAZhO fond 178 — the Lutsk-Zhytomyr Roman Catholic Consistory [checked: Ukrainian Wikisource]; Commons has its metrical books, for example "Метричні книги Ровенського деканату" of 1798 (DAZhO 178-49-0004) [checked]. Polish parishes — the Geneteka index and the scans at metryki.genealodzy.pl ([Europe by country](../../catalog/europe-by-country.md), [Regional overview](../../catalog/regional-overview.md)) [checked]. In the same uezd Catholics and the Orthodox have different fonds: do not order the wrong one ([False zero](../1-ai-search/09-false-zero.md)).
- **Lutherans.** Metrical books are obligatory from 1832; until the law of 1891 they were kept in German; copies from all parishes went to St Petersburg, and the copies of 1833–1885 were microfilmed (FamilySearch Wiki) [checked]. FamilySearch has the collection "Russia, Lutheran Church Book Duplicates, 1833–1885" [checked: FamilySearch Wiki]. German colonies of the south — indexes on Odessa3 (Odessa Digital Library) [checked]; Latvia — Raduraksti, Estonia — Saaga ([Europe by country](../../catalog/europe-by-country.md)).
- **Archion** — a paid portal of Evangelical church books of Germany: 1 month — €19.90, a year — €178.80 [checked]. In the list of its archives as of 08.10.2026 there are only German church archives, no Russian ones [checked]. It will be useful when a colonist line goes back to Germany before the resettlement, not for searching in Russia.
- **Old Believers.** The Church did not recognise their marriages; before 1874 they were often not recorded at all, or recorded in confession lists with the note "schismatic". From 1874 the births and marriages of Old Believers were registered by the police (FamilySearch Wiki) [checked]: look in the fonds of city and uezd police administrations. Civil registration covered Baptists from 1879 (same source) [checked].

## 10. The 20th century

- **ZAGS**, military documents, evacuation — [Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](01-records-empire-ussr.md), section 6, and [War, repression, the siege](12-war-repression-siege.md).
- **Household books of village councils (*pokhozyaistvennye knigi*)** — according to archives, kept from 1934 [from search snippet]. They list household members with their relation to the head, year of birth, those who left and arrived, land and livestock. They are kept in village administrations and municipal (district) archives, partly transferred to oblast archives (docs.vgd.ru) [from search snippet]. In catalogues search for the document type as a separate word — see the example in [False zero](../1-ai-search/09-false-zero.md). Alongside are **collective-farm** (*kolkhoz*) membership lists and personal accounts.
- **Dekulakization and special settlements** — in brief: the main databases and what is missing are in [War, repression, the siege](12-war-repression-siege.md) and in the [Regional overview](../../catalog/regional-overview.md), section 2. An example of a regional database: "Раскулаченные. Свердловская область" (раскулаченные.рф) — 79,402 records of residents of the Urals oblast dekulakized in the 1920s–1930s, from the documents of state archives and the Ministry of Internal Affairs [checked]. The "Open List" with search by place of birth shows all the repressed of a village at once.

## 11. Non-Jewish cemeteries (briefly)

- Village cemeteries are hardly described at all. The best source on the place of burial is the death record in the metrical book ("buried in the parish cemetery").
- For nobles, clergy and merchants — necropolis books of the early 20th century, all on archive.org [checked]: "Petersburg Necropolis" (4 volumes, 1912–1913), "Moscow Necropolis" (3 volumes, 1907–1908), "Russian Provincial Necropolis" (vol. 1, 1914).
- Find a Grave and BillionGraves — the emigrant branch and later burials ([Cemeteries](10-cemeteries.md), section 3).

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The note "(browsing agent)" is for an AI that opens websites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "The ancestor was a peasant of the village [N], [volost], [uezd], [guberniya], around [year]. Draw up a plan: how to find the parish for that year (clergy rosters, a diocesan directory, 'Lists of populated places'), in the fond of which consistory to look for the metrical books and confession lists, and **where I can check** what has survived and been digitised (Kudinov's catalogue, FamilySearch, Commons, the archive's site). Mark fonds named from memory [from memory, verify]." *Check yourself:* the parish — against a clergy roster or a diocesan directory; the fond — against the archive's site or the catalogue.
> - "Here is a spread of a confession list [scan]. First read the printed heading. Then write out the household of [name of head]: household number, all family members with relationship and age, the confession notes — verbatim. Mark anything uncertain with (?)." *Check yourself:* the household number is not confused with the person's number; check the age against one more list.
>
> *Bad → good:* "In which fond are the metrical books of the village [N]?" (it will invent a fond from memory; the parish is not named) → "Where can I check which parish the village [N] belonged to in [year], and in which archive are the copies of that parish's metrical books?"
