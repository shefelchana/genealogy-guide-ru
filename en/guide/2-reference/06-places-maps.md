# Places and maps: how to find a shtetl or a vanished village

What this section covers: how to identify a shtetl when all you have in a document or a family story is a distorted name, how to learn its uezd and guberniya, and how to find a village that is no longer on the map. Gazetteers and old maps by region.

## 1. The order: first the features of the place, then the name

The guide of the Ackman & Ziff Genealogy Institute (Center for Jewish History), "Finding Your Ancestral Town" (2011, cjh.org/pdfs/FindingYourAncestralTown07.pdf) [checked]:

1. **Collect everything about the place except its name**: what people did there for a living, what language they spoke, which rivers and forests were nearby, how far the border was, which was the nearest big city. This is best learned from relatives ([Talking to relatives](../0-start/03-talking-to-relatives.md)).
2. **Look for the name in gazetteers that give spelling variants** (below).
3. **Check the candidates against the features** from step 1. There may be several shtetls with similar names, sometimes in one guberniya.

## 2. Gazetteers

### General ones for Jewish genealogy

- **JewishGen Communities Database, Gazetteer and ShtetlSeeker** — search, including by Daitch-Mokotoff Soundex phonetics: it will find a place even if the name is distorted (CJH, same guide) [checked]. More on the database — [Directories and books](../../catalog/directories-and-books.md).
- **Mokotoff, Sack, Sharon, *Where Once We Walked*** — about 23,500 places with coordinates and pre-war Jewish population (CJH) [checked].
- **Routes to Roots** — maps of Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Poland and Lithuania, and an inventory of surviving documents by shtetl (CJH) [checked].

### The Russian Empire

- **"Spiski naselennykh mest Rossiiskoi imperii"** [Lists of Settlements of the Russian Empire] — about 100 volumes; arranged "guberniya → uezd → settlement". The FamilySearch Wiki ("Russian Empire Gazetteers") links to digital copies by guberniya [checked]. Limitations:
  - **you cannot use them without knowing the guberniya** — first determine the guberniya with other gazetteers;
  - **Congress Poland is not included in the "Spiski"**.
- **M. Vasmer, *Russisches Geographisches Namenbuch*** — the fullest gazetteer of names, 11 volumes, in print only (FamilySearch Wiki, same page) [checked].
- **Guberniya "Spiski" and memorial books** (*pamyatnye knizhki*) for individual guberniyas — examples in [Directories and books](../../catalog/directories-and-books.md).

### Poland and the borderlands

- **"Słownik geograficzny Królestwa Polskiego"** (1880–1905) and the **JRI-Poland place index** (guberniya, coordinates) (CJH) [checked].
- **Kartenmeister** — settlements of the former Prussian provinces (East and West Prussia, Posen, Silesia, Pomerania), searchable by German and Polish name (FHGuide, "Poland Goal 6") [checked].

## 3. Old maps

### Poland and Central Europe

A selection by Julie Roberts Szczepankiewicz (blog "From Shepherds and Shoemakers", "Maps for Polish Genealogy") [checked]:
- **Mapster** — maps of the 15th–20th centuries; you can find all the maps on which a given settlement appears;
- **the WIG map archive** (Wojskowy Instytut Geograficzny, the Military Geographical Institute) — scales from 1:10,000 to 1:500,000;
- **Austro-Hungarian maps** at 1:200,000;
- **Arcanum Maps** — an old map is overlaid on a modern one, with adjustable transparency.

Advice from the same source: **search both with and without diacritics** (Łódź and Lodz).

- **Easteurotopo** — maps of Central and Eastern Europe from the late 18th to the early 20th century [from search snippet: easteurotopo.org].
- **Gesher Galicia Map Room** — cadastral and "special" maps of Galicia. The metrical books of small Galician shtetls were kept by Jewish administrative districts: a record may turn out to be in the book of a neighbouring, larger place [from search snippet: maps.geshergalicia.org].

### The Russian Empire: the three-verst map and land-survey maps

For vanished villages (a summary of search results from familio.media — the page did not open, etomesto.ru, the VGD forum) [from search snippet] (Russian-language sites):
- **Schubert's maps (the three-verst map, *trekhverstka*)**, **Mende**, **Strelbitsky** and **land-survey maps** (*mezhevye karty*) — try them in turn;
- **the three-verst map of Bessarabia** is on etomesto.ru;
- helpful are the **databases of vanished settlements** compiled from the "Spiski naselennykh mest".

## 4. If the village is no more

The order (steps 1 and 3 are from the same summary [from search snippet], the rest are ordinary checking):

1. **Determine the "focus"** from documents: the rivers, neighbouring villages, the volost, the parish or rabbinate to which the village belonged.
2. **Find the focus on a modern map** — by the neighbouring villages that have survived.
3. **Overlay the old maps** (three-verst, Mende, Strelbitsky, land-survey; for Poland — Arcanum, Mapster) and find the missing name next to its neighbours.
4. **Check against the "Spiski naselennykh mest"** of the right year: uezd, volost, number of households.
5. Record everything in a **place guide** with the date and a link to each map ([Plan, timeline, place guide](../0-start/04-plan-timeline-place.md), section 5).

⚠️ Two places with the same name are the norm. Check the **uezd and volost**, not just the name.

## 4a. A few more techniques

- **The shtetl's name + "genealogy"** in an ordinary search engine: forums are often found where this place has already been researched and where the survivals are described. Check them before deciding that "there is nothing for the shtetl" (advice from N. Lipes).
- **A guide to the shtetl from books.** For example, V. Lukin and B. Khaimovich, "100 evreiskikh mestechek Ukrainy. Istoricheskii putevoditel" [100 Jewish Shtetls of Ukraine. A Historical Guide] (the issues on Podolia — with plans, synagogues, cemeteries and history) [from search snippet] (in Russian). Such a book suggests where to look and gives context for the family history.
- **The shtetl's page on JewishGen** (KehilaLinks, Ukraine Research Division): what has already been extracted and who is in charge of the shtetl.
- **The address of a photo studio** on a family photograph names the town if the place is unknown ([Old photographs](17-photographs.md)).
- **Who ruled the place in each period** determines the language of the records and the archive where they lie (lecture by H. Bookbinder, "The Changing Borders of Eastern Europe") [from search snippet].

## 5. What to record about a place

- all variants of the name: in the language of the document, in the modern language, in Latin letters, in Yiddish;
- subordination by year: state, guberniya, uezd, volost;
- coordinates and by which map it was found.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> - "Find all settlements named [N] in [guberniya/region]; for each give the uezd, volost, present-day name and country, and whether there was a Jewish population. Some gazetteers are in Russian, Polish or Ukrainian — please use them." *Check yourself:* against the JewishGen Communities Database and the "Spiski naselennykh mest"; check the uezd and volost, not the name alone.
> - "What was the shtetl [N] called and who did it belong to in [years]? In which archive do its documents lie for each period?" *Check yourself:* dates of border changes — against a reference work.
> - "Draw up a guide to the place [N]: subordination by year, rabbinate, cemeteries, pogroms, occupation, what documents survive and where. Every statement with a source." *Check yourself:* open the links; "survives" — against the catalogue.
