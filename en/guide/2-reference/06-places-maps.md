# Places and maps: how to find a shtetl or a vanished village

What this section covers: how to identify a shtetl when all you have in a document or a family story is a distorted name, how to find out its uezd and guberniya, and how to find a village that is no longer on the map. Gazetteers and old maps by region.

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified] and others) are explained in the [Glossary](../0-start/06-glossary.md).

Why place matters more than surname — in [Where to start](../0-start/03-where-to-start.md), section 4. How one name is spelled in different ways — in [Names, surnames, spellings](03-names-spellings.md), section 3. Site cards — in [Directories and books](../../catalog/directories-and-books.md).

## 1. The order: first the features of the place, then the name

The guide of the Ackman & Ziff Genealogy Institute (Center for Jewish History) "Finding Your Ancestral Town" (2011, cjh.org/pdfs/FindingYourAncestralTown07.pdf) [checked]:

1. **Collect everything about the place except its name**: what people did there, what language they spoke, which rivers and forests were nearby, whether the border was far, which was the nearest big city. It is best to find this out from relatives ([Talking to relatives](../0-start/04-talking-to-relatives.md)).
2. **Look for the name in gazetteers that give spelling variants** (below).
3. **Check the candidates against the features** from step 1. There can be several shtetls with similar names, sometimes in one guberniya.

## 2. Gazetteers

### General ones for Jewish genealogy

- **JewishGen Communities Database, Gazetteer and ShtetlSeeker** — search, including by Daitch–Mokotoff Soundex phonetics: it finds a place even if the name is distorted (CJH, same source) [checked]. More on the database — [Directories and books](../../catalog/directories-and-books.md).
- **Mokotoff, Sack, Sharon, *Where Once We Walked*** — about 23,500 places with coordinates and pre-war Jewish population (CJH) [checked].
- **Routes to Roots** — maps of Ukraine, Belarus, Moldova, Poland, Lithuania and an inventory of surviving documents by shtetl (CJH) [checked].

### The Russian Empire

- **"Spiski naselennykh mest Rossiiskoi imperii"** (Lists of populated places of the Russian Empire) — about 100 volumes, arranged "guberniya → uezd → settlement". On the FamilySearch Wiki ("Russian Empire Gazetteers") there are links to digital copies by guberniya [checked]. Limitations:
  - **without knowing the guberniya you cannot use them** — first determine the guberniya from other gazetteers;
  - **the Kingdom of Poland is not included in the "Spiski"**.
- **M. Vasmer, *Russisches Geographisches Namenbuch*** — the most complete gazetteer of names, 11 volumes, on paper only (FamilySearch Wiki, same place) [checked].
- **Guberniya "Spiski" and memorial books** for individual guberniyas — examples in [Directories and books](../../catalog/directories-and-books.md).

### Poland and the borderlands

- **"Słownik geograficzny Królestwa Polskiego"** (Geographical Dictionary of the Kingdom of Poland; 1880–1905) and the **JRI-Poland town index** (guberniya, coordinates) (CJH) [checked].
- **Kartenmeister** — localities of the former Prussian provinces (East and West Prussia, Posen, Silesia, Pomerania), searchable by German and Polish name (FHGuide, "Poland Goal 6") [checked].

## 3. Old maps

### Poland and Central Europe

A selection by Julie Roberts Szczepankiewicz (the blog "From Shepherds and Shoemakers", "Maps for Polish Genealogy") [checked]:
- **Mapster** — maps of the 15th–20th centuries; you can find all the maps that show the locality you need;
- **the WIG map archive** (Military Geographical Institute) — scales from 1:10,000 to 1:500,000;
- **Austro-Hungarian maps** 1:200,000;
- **Arcanum Maps** — an old map is overlaid on a modern one, with adjustable transparency.

Advice from the same source: **search both with diacritics and without** (Łódź and Lodz).

- **Easteurotopo** — maps of Central and Eastern Europe from the late 18th to the early 20th century [from search snippet: easteurotopo.org].
- **Gesher Galicia Map Room** — cadastral and "special" maps of Galicia. The metrical books of small shtetls of Galicia were kept by Jewish administrative districts: a record may turn out to be in the book of a neighbouring, bigger place [from search snippet: maps.geshergalicia.org].

### The Russian Empire: the three-verst map and land survey maps

For vanished villages (familio.media, etomesto.ru, the VGD forum) [from search snippet]:
- **the Schubert maps (the "three-verst map")**, **Mende**, **Strelbitsky** and **land survey maps** — go through them one by one;
- **the three-verst map of Bessarabia** is on etomesto.ru;
- **databases of vanished settlements**, compiled from the "Spiski naselennykh mest", help.

## 4. If the village is no longer there

The order (steps 1 and 3 come from the same summary [from search snippet], the rest is an ordinary check):

1. **Identify the "core area"** from documents: the rivers, neighbouring villages, the volost, parish or rabbinate the village belonged to.
2. **Find the core area on a modern map** — by the neighbouring villages that survive.
3. **Overlay old maps** (three-verst, Mende, Strelbitsky, land survey maps; for Poland — Arcanum, Mapster) and find the missing name next to its neighbours.
4. **Check against the "Spiski naselennykh mest"** of the year you need: uezd, volost, number of households.
5. Record everything in a **place guide** with the date and a link to each map ([Plan, timeline, place guide](../0-start/05-plan-timeline-place.md), section 5).

⚠️ Two places with the same name are the norm. Check the **uezd and volost**, not just the name.

## 4a. A few more tricks

- **The name of the shtetl + "genealogy"** in an ordinary search engine: forums are often found where this place has already been searched and what survives described. Check them before deciding that "there is nothing for this shtetl" (advice from N. Lipes).
- **A shtetl guide from books.** For example, V. Lukin and B. Khaimovich, "100 evreiskikh mestechek Ukrainy. Istoricheskii putevoditel" (100 Jewish Shtetls of Ukraine: A Historical Guide; the issues on Podolia — with plans, synagogues, cemeteries, history) [from search snippet]. Such a book suggests where to look and gives context for the family's history.
- **The shtetl's page on JewishGen** (KehilaLinks, Ukraine Research Division): what has already been transcribed and who looks after the shtetl.
- **The address of the photo studio** on a family photograph names the town if the place is unknown ([Old photographs](17-photographs.md)).
- **Who ruled the place in each period** determines the language of the records and the archive where they lie (a lecture by H. Bookbinder, "The Changing Borders of Eastern Europe") [from search snippet].

## 5. What to record about a place

- all variants of the name: in the language of the document, in the modern language, in Latin letters, in Yiddish;
- subordination by year: state, guberniya, uezd, volost;
- coordinates and by which map it was found.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The (browser agent) label is for an AI that opens websites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "I need to find all the settlements called [name] in [guberniya/region]. Which gazetteers should I check them in (JewishGen Communities, 'Spiski naselennykh mest', old maps), and which fields should I compare: uezd, volost, modern name, country, Jewish population? If you list places yourself, mark each as [from memory, verify]." *Check yourself:* compile the list from gazetteers; compare the uezd and volost, not the name alone.
> - "What was the shtetl [name] called and who owned it in [years]? In which country's archives should I look for its documents for each period, and where can I check this (a guide, the archive's website, Routes to Roots)? Mark archives and fonds from memory as [from memory, verify]." *Check yourself:* dates of border changes — against a gazetteer, the archive — on its website.
>
> *Bad → good:* "Where is [name]?" (there are many places of that name, and no guberniya or year is given) → "Where was [name], [guberniya], in [year]: uezd, volost, modern name — and in which gazetteer can I check this?"
