# Assembling the whole family and linking people with the same surname

What this section covers: what to do when you have found a record with a father and children. How to assemble a package of documents for the whole family instead of going from find to find, and how to tell whether several families with the same surname are related.

A single find does not link branches. A family assembled as a package gives the ages of all the children, the place of registration, the occupation, the witnesses — and these are what show kinship with other families. A document about your ancestor often lies in the file of his brother or in the record of his sister's wedding.

---

## 1. The anchor

Write down what you found verbatim, with the reference and the frame: the head of the family, the wife, the children, ages, the family number, estate, community. This is the anchor; everything else is checked against it.

## 2. Who this family is in the system of the empire

1. **Estate**: *meshchane*, merchants, guild artisans, agricultural colonists, honorary citizens. This is a status, not an origin: a family moved from *meshchane* to merchants and back.
2. **Registration and residence.** Where they were listed (the community under which the revisions and conscription went) and where they lived (where the children were born, where the 1897 census, directories, courts are). These are two different archival addresses — write down both.
3. **Guberniya and uezd for the year of the document**: borders and subordination changed ([Places and maps](06-places-maps.md)).
4. **Occupation**: timber and leases, a mill, trade, a craft, medicine, service at the rabbinate. Directories, courts, notaries and the press depend on the occupation.

## 3. Which documents must exist

Make a list of the types of documents that **should have been** created for such a family, and for each find out whether it survives and where it lies.

| Document | Years | Where to look | What it gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revision lists | 1795–1858 | place of registration | composition of the household, age "by the last revision", those who departed with the year |
| Files on transfer and registration | 19th century | both communities | where the family came from and where it went |
| Recruit lists | until 1874 | registration | men in order, age |
| Family lists; for Jews, the military census of 1875 | 1870s | registration | the family number by the revision of 1858, **family members living apart — where and by what document**; age sometimes "by appearance" (N. Lipes's database, lipesdatabase.com) |
| Metrical books | for Jews mostly from the 1840s–1850s to 1919–1920 | place of residence | dates, parents, witnesses; look at **all** the surviving years |
| Voter lists of the crown rabbi | 19th – early 20th century | community | adult men of the community |
| Census of 1897 | 1897 | place of residence | composition of the family, registration, occupation; local copies survive for only some uezds |
| Police files of the guberniya administration | whole period | place of residence | passports, expulsions, right of residence, addresses |
| Press, directories, courts, notaries | whole period | place of residence | auctions, lawsuits, inheritance, donations, addresses |
| 1914–1922: First World War losses, refugees, pogroms | — | — | see [Pogroms 1918–1922](11-pogroms-1918-1922.md) |
| Soviet ZAGS — all surviving years | from 1919–1920 | — | new names, moves, infants who died |
| Evacuation cards 1941–1942 | — | Yad Vashem and others | who left together: a ready-made list of relatives |
| The Holocaust, emigration | — | — | see [War](12-war-repression-siege.md), [Emigration](13-emigration.md) |

For merchants add guild certificates and lists of capital; for craftsmen — guild lists; for timber dealers and leaseholders — notarial contracts and lawsuits; for doctors — the "Rossiiskii meditsinskii spisok" (Russian Medical Directory); for students — personal files.

## 4. The package for a family

Keep a family card. For each type of document, one line with one of these statuses:
- **found** (reference, frame);
- **not there — searched** (where, in what forms, coverage);
- **did not survive** (what confirms it);
- **not searched — where to look**.

An absence where a record **must** be is negative evidence (died, fled, transferred, registered elsewhere). Record it as a result.

Go through the card **one family at a time**, from the cheap (indexes, full text) to the expensive (reading from start to finish). Before paging through a file, check the indexes ([Community surname indexes](08-surname-indexes.md)).

If there are few traces in the shtetl of registration, look at the records of the **big city** people moved to: they point to the shtetl of origin. The full extracts from the metrical books of Łódź for 1906 (about 5,500 records) name 884 other places of birth or residence (JRI-Poland, an article by S. Diamond in Avotaynu, 2018) [from search snippet].

## 5. How to link people with the same surname into one tree

When two or more families of one surname have been assembled, look for links between them. The signs, from strong to weak:

| Sign | Strength |
|---|---|
| A document directly names the kinship (son, brother, widow, "family member living apart") | strong on its own |
| An age bridge between revisions and a matching composition of the household | strong |
| A clerk's reference to the number in the last revision with a matching composition | strong |
| A **unique clerk's error** repeated in two independent documents | strong, if the error is really rare: "two different census-takers could not have made the same mistake" (N. Lipes, nadialipes.info) |
| A matching **set** of names over two or three generations (family names repeat) | medium; one matching name means nothing (Am HaZikaron, N. Lipes) |
| The order of seniority of the children, derived from ages, matches in two revisions (Kh. Fridman) | medium |
| A double name of the father → different patronymics among the children | medium; different patronymics do not disprove kinship |
| A shared place of registration, where the surname is not from a place name or a trade | medium contribution |
| Witnesses, guarantors, partners, a shared business | medium contribution |
| A shared patronymic in two men of one generation from different fonds | weak on its own — a working hypothesis, needs a second sign |
| Living nearby | weak |

Give each link a status on the guide's single scale ([Standard of proof](../3-results/01-standard-of-proof.md), section 4a):
- 🟢 **confirmed** — a record directly names the kinship (or an age bridge between revisions with a matching household composition) plus a second independent sign;
- 🟡 **open / strong version** — two or more independent indirect signs, no contradictions, the reasoning written down;
- ⚪ **unverified / weak** — one sign;
- 🔴 **rejected** — with the reason.

Into a publicly available tree — only 🟢; into your personal working one — 🟡 marked "version"; ⚪ and 🔴 — only in the hypotheses file.

## 6. What does not prove kinship

- One surname + one region, if the surname is **from a place name or a trade**: such surnames arose independently. The bearer of a surname from a place name often lived **not** in the place of that name (N. Lipes).
- A match of the surname's form with the local form: relatives sometimes deliberately changed one letter to separate themselves from another family, or merged with the local spelling.
- **The range of a surname by documents of the early 20th century.** Look at the earliest lists with surnames — 1805–1816 (in the revision of 1795 Jews usually still have no surnames), not at the voter lists of 1906–1907: by the 1900s families had scattered.
- A family legend of kinship with a famous namesake; a match in someone else's online tree.

## 7. Traps

- **Children of one head of household can be by different wives**: a big gap in ages or a different name of the mother is a reason to look for a second marriage. Divorces among Jews in the first half of the 19th century were frequent (C. Freeze) [from search snippet].
- **Age "floats"** by 2–5 years between documents; brides' ages were "made older", conscripts' ages were corrected. Compare intervals and the order of the children, not the exact year.
- **The family number does not carry over between revisions.** The link is given by the age bridge, the family composition or an explicit clerk's reference. Several separate families are sometimes recorded under one number, and an adult "foster child" with a young head of household is not necessarily adopted.
- **Namesakes "uncle — nephew"** of similar age because of the custom of naming after the dead: it is easy to mix up generations.
- **A name was changed during life** (illness, failures, a daughter's marriage to a man with the father's name): children of one father can have different patronymics in different documents (N. Lipes). Check by the family number and the surroundings.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The (browser agent) label is for an AI that opens websites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "Here is a record of a family: [extract with reference]. Draw up a list of the documents that should have been created for such a family (estate [ ], registration [ ], lived in [ ], occupation [ ], years [ ]) — with years and where they are usually looked for. Mark what is by place of registration and what by place of residence. Give specific archives and fonds only as guesses marked [from memory, verify]." *Check yourself:* the survival and reference of each type — against the inventory and catalogue.
> - "Here are two families with the surname [name]: [extracts]. Draw up a table of signs of possible kinship (set of names, registration, witnesses, occupation, clerk's errors), mark which signs are independent, and give the link a status on the scale 🟢 / 🟡 / ⚪ with an explanation." *Check yourself:* the independence of the signs — whether they were not taken from one document or one index.
>
> *Bad → good:* "Are these my relatives? [two surnames]" (without extracts and references the AI judges by the surname) → "Here are two families [extracts with references]: which signs of kinship are independent and what status does the link have, 🟢 / 🟡 / ⚪?"
