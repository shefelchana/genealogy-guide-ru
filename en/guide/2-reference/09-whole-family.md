# Assembling the whole family and linking people with the same surname

What this section covers: what to do once you have found a record with a father and children. How to assemble a package of documents for the whole family instead of going from find to find, and how to tell whether several families with the same surname are related.

A single find does not link branches. A family assembled as a package gives the ages of all the children, the place of registration, the occupation, the witnesses — and these are what show kinship with other families. A document about your ancestor often lies in his brother's file or in the record of his sister's wedding.

---

## 1. The anchor

Write down what you found verbatim, with the archival reference and frame: head of the family, wife, children, ages, family number, estate (*soslovie*), community. This is the anchor; everything else is checked against it.

## 2. Who this family is in the imperial system

1. **Estate**: meshchane (townspeople), merchants, guild artisans, agricultural colonists, honorary citizens. This is a status, not an origin: a family moved from meshchane to merchants and back.
2. **Registration and residence.** Where they were registered (the community that the revisions and conscription went by) and where they lived (children were born there; the 1897 census, directories and court records are there). These are two different archival addresses — write down both.
3. **Guberniya and uezd in the year of the document**: boundaries and subordination changed ([Places and maps](06-places-maps.md)).
4. **Occupation**: timber and leasing, a mill, trade, a craft, medicine, service at the rabbinate. Directories, courts, notaries and the press depend on the occupation.

## 3. Which documents must exist

Make a list of the types of documents that **should have been** created for such a family, and for each find out whether it survived and where it is held.

| Document | Years | Where to look | What it gives |
|---|---|---|---|
| Revision lists (*revizskie skazki*) | 1795–1858 | place of registration | composition of the household, age "by the previous revision", those who left, with the year |
| Files on transfer and registration to another community | 19th century | both communities | where the family came from and where it went |
| Recruit lists | until 1874 | registration | men in order of turn, age |
| Family lists (*posemeinye spiski*); for Jews, the military census of 1875 | 1870s | registration | the family number from the 1858 revision, **family members living apart — where and by what document**; age sometimes "by appearance" (N. Lipes's database, lipesdatabase.com) |
| Metrical books | for Jews mostly from the 1840s–1850s to 1919–1920 | place of residence | dates, parents, witnesses; look at **all** surviving years |
| Voters' lists of the crown rabbi (*kazyonny ravvin*) | 19th — early 20th century | the community | adult men of the community |
| 1897 All-Russian Census | 1897 | place of residence | composition of the family, registration, occupation; local copies survive only for some uezds |
| Police files of the guberniya administration | the whole period | place of residence | passports, expulsions, right of residence, addresses |
| Press, directories, court records, notarial deeds | the whole period | place of residence | auctions, lawsuits, inheritance, donations, addresses |
| 1914–1922: First World War losses, refugees, pogroms | — | — | see [Pogroms 1918–1922](11-pogroms-1918-1922.md) |
| Soviet ZAGS (civil registry) — all surviving years | from 1919–1920 | — | new names, moves, infants who died |
| Evacuation cards of 1941–1942 | — | Yad Vashem and others | who left together: a ready-made list of relatives |
| The Holocaust, emigration | — | — | see [War](12-war-repression-siege.md), [Emigration](13-emigration.md) |

For merchants add guild certificates and capital lists; for craftsmen, guild lists; for timber merchants and leaseholders, notarial contracts and lawsuits; for physicians, the "Russian Medical List" (*Rossiiskii meditsinskii spisok*); for students, personal files.

## 4. The family package

Start a family card. For each type of document, one line with one of these statuses:
- **found** (archival reference, frame);
- **not found — searched** (where, with which forms, coverage);
- **not preserved** (what confirms it);
- **not searched — where to search**.

Absence where a record **must** exist is negative evidence (died, fled, transferred, registered elsewhere). Record it as a result.

Work through the card **one family at a time**, from cheap (indexes, full text) to expensive (page-by-page reading). Before paging through a file, check the indexes ([Community surname indexes](08-surname-indexes.md)).

If there are few traces in the shtetl of registration, look at the records of the **big city** people moved to: they point to the shtetl of origin. In the full extracts of the 1906 Łódź metrical records (about 5,500 records), 884 other places of birth or residence are named (JRI-Poland, S. Diamond's article in Avotaynu, 2018) [from search snippet].

## 5. How to link people with the same surname into one tree

When two or more families with the same surname have been assembled, look for links between them. The signs, from strong to weak:

| Sign | Strength |
|---|---|
| A document directly names the relationship (son, brother, widow, "family member living apart") | strong on its own |
| An age bridge between revisions and a matching household composition | strong |
| A scribe's reference to the number from the previous revision, with matching composition | strong |
| **A unique scribe's error** repeated in two independent documents | strong if the error is truly rare: "two different census-takers could not have made the same error" (N. Lipes, nadialipes.info) |
| A matching **set** of names across two or three generations (family names repeat) | medium; one matching name means nothing ("Am ha-Zikaron", N. Lipes) |
| The birth order of the children, derived from ages, matches in two revisions (H. Friedman) | medium |
| A father's double name → different patronymics for the children | medium; different patronymics do not disprove kinship |
| A shared place of registration, where the surname is not from the name of a place or from a profession | medium contribution |
| Witnesses, guarantors, partners, a shared business | medium contribution |
| A shared patronymic for two men of one generation from different fonds | weak on its own — a working hypothesis, a second sign is needed |
| Living nearby | weak |

Give each link a level:
- 🟢 **document** — a record directly names the relationship, or there is an age bridge with a matching family;
- 🟡 **strong version** — two or more independent indirect signs, no contradictions, the reasoning written down;
- ⚪ **hypothesis** — one sign.

Only 🟢 goes into the public tree; ⚪ goes only into the hypotheses file.

## 6. What does not prove kinship

- One surname + one region, if the surname is **from a place name or from a profession**: such surnames arose independently. The bearer of a surname from a place name often lived **not** in the place of that name (N. Lipes).
- A match between the form of a surname and the local form: relatives sometimes deliberately changed one letter to set themselves apart from another family, or merged into the local spelling.
- **The distribution of a surname from early 20th-century documents.** Look at the earliest lists with surnames — 1805–1816 (in the 1795 revision Jews usually have no surnames yet) — not at the voters' lists of 1906–1907: by the 1900s families had scattered.
- A family legend of kinship with a famous person of the same surname; a match in someone else's online tree.

## 7. Pitfalls

- **Children of one head of household may be from different wives**: a large gap in ages or a different name for the mother is a reason to look for a second marriage. Divorce among Jews in the first half of the 19th century was frequent (C. Freeze) [from search snippet].
- **Ages "float"** by 2–5 years between documents; brides' ages were "made older", conscripts' ages were corrected. Compare intervals and the order of the children, not the exact year.
- **The family number is not carried over between revisions.** The link is given by an age bridge, the composition of the family or an explicit reference by the scribe. Under one number several separate families may be entered, and an adult "foster-son" with a young head of household is not necessarily adopted.
- **Namesakes, "uncle and nephew"**, close in age because of the custom of naming after the dead: it is easy to mix up the generations.
- **People changed their name during their lifetime** (illness, misfortune, a daughter's marriage to a namesake of the father): children of one father may have different patronymics in different documents (N. Lipes). Check by the family number and the surroundings.

> **How to ask AI**
>
> - "Here is a record of a family: [extract with archival reference]. Make a list of the documents that must have been created for such a family (estate [ ], registered in [ ], lived in [ ], occupation [ ], years [ ]) — with years and archival addresses. Mark what belongs to the place of registration and what to the place of residence." *Check yourself:* whether each type survived — in the inventory and catalogue. (The AI may need to read Russian-language sources.)
> - "Here are two families with the surname [N]: [extracts]. Make a table of signs of possible kinship (set of names, registration, witnesses, occupation, scribe's errors), mark which signs are independent, and give the level of the link: document, strong version or hypothesis." *Check yourself:* the independence of the signs — whether they are not taken from the same document or index.
> - "Here are the children's ages in the revisions of [year] and [year]. Does the order of seniority match? Are there signs of children from different wives?"
> - "Help me write a full justification of the link: the signs, why they are independent, what speaks against it, which alternatives there were and why they were dropped."
