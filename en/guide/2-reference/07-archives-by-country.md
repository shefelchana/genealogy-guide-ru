# Archives by country: where things are held

What this section covers: a short map — which country's archive to search for the documents of ancestors from the Russian Empire and the USSR, and what of it is already available online. Detailed cards for the sites, with techniques, are in [../../catalog/](../../catalog/).

The general rule: pre-revolutionary documents lie in **regional (oblast) archives** at the place where people lived and were registered; documents of central institutions lie in the **central** archives of the capitals. Soviet ZAGS (civil registry) records are in the ZAGS offices or have been transferred to archives.

Markers: **[from search snippet]** — information from search results, the page was not opened; "not checked against the page" means exactly that. Before an important decision, check against the archive's website.

## Ukraine

- **Regional archives** (DAKhmO — State Archive of Khmelnytskyi Oblast; DAViO — State Archive of Vinnytsia Oblast; DAZhO — State Archive of Zhytomyr Oblast; DAKO — State Archive of Kyiv Oblast, and others): metrical books, revisions, censuses, court records, Soviet ZAGS records. Their sites have sections "Elektronnyi arkhiv" [Electronic archive] [from search snippet] and registers of inventories.
- **TsDIAK** (Central State Historical Archive of Ukraine, Kyiv): metrical books of the 18th–20th centuries, police materials, the rabbinates of Kyiv guberniya and much else. Search the finding aids at archium.cdiak.archives.gov.ua; files that are not digitised can be ordered to the reading room through a personal account.
- **Online:**
  - **Wikimedia Commons** — thousands of whole files of Ukrainian archives (metrical books, revisions, censuses, inventories, ZAGS 1919–1944) — the project of Alex Krakovsky;
  - **Vikidzherela (Wikisource)** — lists of fonds with links to scans (⚠️ a selection, not a complete inventory);
  - **"Zvedenyi katalog metrychnykh knyh"** [Consolidated Catalogue of Metrical Books] — 10 volumes in 15 books (2009–2021), with text search; Lviv Oblast is not in volumes 1–10;
  - **Mizharkhivnyi poshukovyi portal** [Inter-Archive Search Portal] (searcharchives.net.ua) — 13 archives, search by titles of fonds and files, annotations and indexes;
  - **FamilySearch** — metrical books of TsDIAK, TsDIAL (Central State Historical Archive of Ukraine, Lviv) and the regional archives [from search snippet].
- **Where a scan lies, by archival reference:** "Kachynyi Inspektor" [Duck Inspector] (inspector.duckarchive.com) — a volunteer combined index of files of Ukrainian archives published anywhere: FamilySearch, Commons, archive websites. Search by archive, fond, inventory and file, or by title; it shows whether the file is online [page read; advice from Lara Diamond's blog, 2025]. An empty answer does not mean there is no scan.
- **First, the indexes inside the files.** Among the files scanned by A. Krakovsky there are alphabetical indexes and name lists, including Cyrillic indexes to the metrical books of large cities. The JewishGen Ukraine Research Division transliterates them and keeps copies of the scans — a fallback if a file on Commons is renamed or deleted [from search snippet]. The order: index in the file → JewishGen index to this file → page-by-page reading.
- **Where to look for revisions and censuses:** revision lists often lie in the fonds of the **treasury chamber** (*kazyonnaya palata*; for example, DAKO f. 280 "Kyiv Treasury Chamber"), the 1897 census in the fond of the guberniya census commission (FamilySearch handout on Krakovsky's files) [checked].
- **Browser auto-translation spoils terms**: "справа" (*sprava*) is a file, not "affairs"; "опис" (*opys*) is an inventory, not a "description". Read titles in the original.
- **Access:** the sites of some archives in the archives.gov.ua zone return a 403 error to automated requests but often open in an ordinary browser. According to reports from 2026, Ukrainian archives do not work with researchers from Russia and Belarus, and some have introduced IP-address blocking; the archives of Kherson were looted before the city's liberation in November 2022; the archives of Crimea and of the occupied parts of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts are inaccessible (generaggs.substack.com, April 2026) [checked].

In detail: [Archive file scans](../../catalog/archive-file-scans.md).

## Moldova (Bessarabia)

- **National Archives of the Republic of Moldova** (NARM, ANRM, Chișinău): the metrical books of the rabbinates of Chișinău, Bălți and Bender survive well [from search snippet]; revisions, censuses. Much has been filmed on **FamilySearch microfilms** (Chișinău, Cahul, Bolgrad, Izmail).
- The reading room is free, but, according to search results, an application with a letter of recommendation is needed; work with digitised metrical books in the reading room is free, copies and certificates are paid [from search snippet].
- Remember the Romanian period of southern Bessarabia in 1856–1878 and of Bessarabia in 1918–1940 (see [Jewish genealogy](02-jewish-genealogy.md)).
- Gazetteers: the Bessarabia Geographical Dictionary (Bessarabia SIG), bessarabia.ru (a description of the types of documents in the archives of Moldova) (Russian-language site).
- **FamilySearch microfilms for Bessarabia are labelled incompletely**: according to Bessarabia SIG, of 292 Bessarabian revision films, Jewish records are definitely on 30, and only the Bender ones are labelled Jewish in the catalogue [from search snippet]. Do not filter the catalogue by the word "Jewish" — browse the films of the uezd.
- **What exists and what does not** (a talk by A. Chastina, IAJGS 2012; the talk is confirmed by the conference programme, the list below is from a retelling and is not checked) [unverified]: there are no name-by-name sheets of the 1897 census for Bessarabia in the archive of Moldova, only summary statistics — their absence is not an argument. But there is the 1875 census of the Jews of Podolia guberniya registered to Bessarabian places (a Podolia ↔ Bessarabia bridge); early metrical books of 1842–1843 and 1851, more detailed than the later ones; the *meshchanin* tax-assessment lists of Chișinău of 1910; merchant lists of the 1840s–1850s with the ages of the whole family; Romanian lists of those who received citizenship (1918–1941); police questionnaires of the 1918–1930s.
- According to E. Kogan (2018): in NARM fond 153 — the house books of Chișinău 1894–1919, fond 403 — conscript lists 1864–1917 [from search snippet]. Check whether they have already been filmed.
- The platform doc.arhiva.gov.md (beta since 28 January 2025): it began with fond R-2848 (Council of Ministers of the Moldavian SSR, 1940–1941 and 1944–1948, about 60,000 pages) with metadata search in Russian; the main page now also has databases on the repressed and repatriated, documents on the Holocaust and prisoners of war [page read: arhiva.gov.md, doc.arhiva.gov.md].

## Russia

- **Regional archives** (GA*O, State Archives of oblasts): metrical books, revisions, Soviet documents. Many run an **EAIS** (unified archival information system) — paid remote viewing of digitised files (for example, in one archive from November 2025 — 60 rubles an hour or 110 rubles for 2 hours). Before ordering files, check whether there are **name and cross-fond indexes** — they are sometimes in open PDF inventories.
- **RGIA** (Russian State Historical Archive, St Petersburg): the inventories are scanned, electronic orders through a personal account [from search snippet]. **RGADA** (Russian State Archive of Early Acts): the Landrat books and revision lists of 1707–1792 (fond 350) are digitised, but can be viewed only in the reading room [from search snippet].
- **Yandex "Search in Archives"** (Russian-language site) — recognised handwritten text of 30 Russian archives, newspapers and directories; there are no Ukrainian archives.
- According to FamilySearch, many Russian archives are not set up for "family history" requests.
- **Access from abroad:** some Russian state sites (archives, libraries, military databases) do not open from foreign addresses. This is a network restriction, not a sign that the site is closed.

In detail: [Russian-language resources](../../catalog/russian-language-resources.md).

## Belarus

- **NIAB** (National Historical Archives of Belarus, Minsk; a branch in Hrodna): metrical books, revisions, synagogue records; 29 fonds of Jewish communities of Minsk and Mogilev guberniyas [from search snippet]. Genealogical search is paid; you can work on your own in the reading room by archival reference [from search snippet].
- Online: Radzima.org (a gazetteer of places), the JewishGen Belarus databases, the "Kniga Pamyati" [Book of Memory] (memory.mil.by).

## Poland

- **JRI-Poland**: 6.4 million records, links to scans, free.
- **Geneteka** (Polish Genealogical Society): 68 million records; includes Ukraine, Belarus, Lithuania.
- **szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl**: more than 55 million scans of state archives; search by settlement name [from search snippet]. ⚠️ The portal has blocked some foreign addresses entirely.
- **PRADZIAD** — where parish or civil registry books are held [from search snippet].
- **AGAD** (Archiwum Główne Akt Dawnych, Central Archives of Historical Records, Warsaw): Jewish metrical books of the eastern territories, including Galicia [from search snippet].

## The Baltic states

- **Lithuania:** LitvakSIG (All Lithuania Database, more than 2.8 million records); metrical books up to 1940 — the Lithuanian State Historical Archives (LVIA); epaveldas.lt [from search snippet].
- **Latvia:** Raduraksti — church books of all confessions (the Jewish ones are under "Mosaic faith"), revisions, the 1897 census; free registration, interface in Latvian.
- **Estonia:** the National Archives (Rahvusarhiiv) — Saaga (digitised) and AIS; Jewish metrical books of the communities of Tallinn, Tartu, Narva and others; Album Academicum of the University of Yuryev (Tartu).

## Romania

- **ROM-SIG** (JewishGen) — the database for Romania, but it **does not cover Bessarabia and Transylvania**: those are handled by Bessarabia SIG and the Hungarian Research Division.
- The National Archives of Romania (ANR) — the pages did not open, not checked.

## Germany and Austria

- **Arolsen Archives** — documents on persecution, camps, forced labour, displaced persons; search is open, a request can be submitted in Russian.
- **Bundesarchiv Gedenkbuch** — victims of the persecution of Jews in Germany 1933–1945.
- **Hamburg and Bremen departure lists** — see [Emigration](13-emigration.md).
- **GenTeam** (Austria) — more than 23 million records, including the Jewish metrical books of Vienna; free after registration.
- **University of Vienna, PHAIDRA** — student questionnaires with the father's name, occupation and place of residence.

## Israel

- **Israel State Archives** — immigration and naturalisation under the British Mandate (more than 60,000 files of 1937–1947), rabbinical courts.
- **Central Zionist Archives** — has a paid genealogical service.
- **CAHJP** (Central Archives for the History of the Jewish People, at the National Library) — community archives, microfilms from the archives of the former USSR (since the early 1990s).
- **Yad Vashem** — the central database of names of Holocaust victims, evacuation cards.
- **IGRA** (Israel Genealogy Research Association) — All Israel Database (more than 4 million records).

## Other countries of Europe

Belgium (police dossiers on foreigners), France (naturalisation files), Great Britain (naturalisation with the parents' names) — see [Europe by country](../../catalog/europe-by-country.md).

## If there is nothing online

First check the cheap routes: indexes and full text, neighbouring years and neighbouring rabbinates, substitutes for metrical books ([Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](01-records-empire-ussr.md), "If there are no metrical books"), new digitisations — they are posted constantly. If that too gives no result, trace the search to an **exact archival reference** (fond–inventory–file). Whether to write a request to an archive is for you to decide — how to do it is in the section [Requests and letters](20-requests-letters.md).

> **How to ask AI**
>
> - "Where are documents of type [type] for [place] for [years] held: which archive, which fond? What of this is digitised and where is it posted (the archive's site, FamilySearch, Commons)? The archives' own sites may be in Ukrainian, Russian, Romanian or Polish." *Check yourself:* the reference codes — against the inventory; for Ukraine, check the code in the "Kachynyi Inspektor".
> - "Here are file titles from an inventory in Ukrainian [text]. Translate them and explain the terms (fond, opys, sprava, revizki kazky, kazenna palata)." *Check yourself:* browser auto-translation spoils terms: "sprava" is a file, not "affairs".
> - "The archive's site [address] will not open. Is the same material available elsewhere (FamilySearch, Commons, JewishGen)?" *Check yourself:* do not circumvent a block; the decision about a VPN is up to the person.
