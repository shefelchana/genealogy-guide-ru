# Archives by country: where things are held

What this section covers: a short map — in which country's archive to look for the documents of ancestors from the Russian Empire and the USSR, and what of it is already available online. Detailed site cards with tricks — in [../../catalog/](../../catalog/).

The general rule: pre-revolutionary documents lie in the **oblast archives** of the place where people lived and were registered; documents of central institutions lie in the **central** archives of the capitals. Soviet ZAGS records are held by the ZAGS offices or have been transferred to archives.

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified] and others) are explained in the [Glossary](../0-start/06-glossary.md). Check the archive's website before an important decision.

## Ukraine

- **Oblast archives** (DAKhmO, ДАХмО — Khmelnytskyi Oblast; DAViO, ДАВіО — Vinnytsia; DAZhO, ДАЖО — Zhytomyr; DAKO, ДАКО — Kyiv, and others): metrical books, revisions, censuses, courts, Soviet ZAGS. Their sites have "Електронний архів" (Electronic archive) sections [from search snippet] and registers of inventories.
- **TsDIAK** (ЦДІАК, the Central State Historical Archive of Ukraine, Kyiv): metrical books of the 18th–20th centuries, police materials, the rabbinates of Kyiv Guberniya and much else. Search through the finding aids at archium.cdiak.archives.gov.ua; files that are not digitised can be ordered to the reading room through a personal account.
- **Online:**
  - **Wikimedia Commons** — thousands of whole files from Ukrainian archives (metrical books, revisions, censuses, inventories, ZAGS 1919–1944) — Alex Krakovsky's project;
  - **Vikidzherela** (Вікіджерела, Ukrainian Wikisource) — listings of fonds with links to scans (⚠️ a selection, not a full inventory);
  - **"Zvedenyi katalog metrychnykh knyh"** (Consolidated Catalogue of Metrical Books) — 10 volumes in 15 books (2009–2021), with text search; Lviv Oblast is not in volumes 1–10;
  - **Mizharkhivnyi poshukovyi portal** (Міжархівний пошуковий портал, the inter-archive search portal; searcharchives.net.ua) — 13 archives, search by titles of fonds and files, annotations and indexes;
  - **FamilySearch** — metrical books of TsDIAK, TsDIAL (the Central State Historical Archive in Lviv) and oblast archives [from search snippet].
- **Where a scan lies by archival reference:** "Kachynyi Inspektor" (Качиний Інспектор, "Duck Inspector"; inspector.duckarchive.com) — a volunteer combined index of files of Ukrainian archives published anywhere: FamilySearch, Commons, archive sites. Search by archive, fond, inventory and file, or by title; it shows whether the file is online [checked; advice from Lara Diamond's blog, 2025]. An empty answer does not mean there is no scan.
- **Indexes inside files first.** Among the files scanned by A. Krakovsky there are alphabetical indexes and name lists, including Cyrillic indexes to the metrical books of big cities. The JewishGen Ukraine Research Division transliterates them and keeps copies of the scans — a fallback if a file on Commons has been renamed or deleted [from search snippet]. The order: the index in the file → the JewishGen index for this file → page-by-page reading.
- **Where to look for revisions and censuses:** revision lists often lie in the fonds of the **treasury chamber** (*kazennaya palata*; for example, DAKO fond 280 "Kyivska kazenna palata" / Kyiv Treasury Chamber), the 1897 census in the fond of the guberniya census commission (a FamilySearch handout on Krakovsky's files) [checked].
- **Browser auto-translation spoils terms**: "справа" (*sprava*) is a file, not "affairs"; "опис" (*opys*) is an inventory, not "description". Read the titles in the original. Don't read Russian or Ukrainian? See [For English speakers](../0-start/08-for-english-speakers.md).
- **Access:** the sites of some archives in the archives.gov.ua zone return a 403 error to automated requests but often open in an ordinary browser. According to reports of 2026, Ukrainian archives do not work with researchers from Russia and Belarus, and some have introduced blocking by IP address; the archives of Kherson were looted before the city's liberation in November 2022; the archives of Crimea and the occupied parts of Donetsk and Luhansk oblasts are unavailable (generaggs.substack.com, April 2026) [checked]. If an archive's site does not open, look for the same file on FamilySearch or Commons, without bypassing the block ([Access, captchas, pace](19-access-captchas-pace.md), section 4).

In detail: [Archive file scans](../../catalog/archive-file-scans.md).

## Moldova (Bessarabia)

- **National Archives of the Republic of Moldova** (NARM, ANRM, НАРМ, Chișinău): the metrical books of the rabbinates of Chișinău, Bălți and Bender survive well [from search snippet]; revisions, censuses. Much was filmed onto **FamilySearch microfilms** (Chișinău, Cahul, Bolhrad, Izmail).
- The reading room is free, but according to search results you need an application with a letter of recommendation; working with digitised metrical books in the room is free, copies and certificates cost money [from search snippet].
- Remember the Romanian period of southern Bessarabia 1856–1878 and Bessarabia in 1918–1940 (see [Jewish genealogy](02-jewish-genealogy.md)).
- Gazetteers: the Bessarabia Geographical Dictionary (Bessarabia SIG), bessarabia.ru (a description of the types of documents in the archives of Moldova).
- **FamilySearch films for Bessarabia are labelled incompletely**: according to Bessarabia SIG, of 292 Bessarabian revision films, Jewish records are definitely on 30, and in the catalogue only the Bender ones are marked as Jewish [from search snippet]. Do not filter the catalogue by the word "Jewish" — page through the films of the uezd.
- **What exists and what does not** (a talk by A. Chastina, IAJGS 2012; the talk is confirmed by the conference programme, the list below is from a retelling and was not checked) [unverified]: there are no name lists of the 1897 census for Bessarabia in the archive of Moldova, only summary statistics — their absence is not an argument. But there is the census of 1875 of the Jews of Podolia Guberniya registered at Bessarabian places (a Podolia ↔ Bessarabia bridge); early metrical books of 1842–1843 and 1851, more detailed than the later ones; the *meshchane* tax rolls of Chișinău of 1910; merchant lists of the 1840s–1850s with the ages of the whole family; Romanian lists of those who received citizenship (1918–1941); police questionnaires of the 1918–1930s.
- According to E. Kogan (2018): in NARM fond 153 — house books of Chișinău 1894–1919, fond 403 — lists of conscripts 1864–1917 [from search snippet]. Check whether they have already been filmed.
- The platform doc.arhiva.gov.md (beta since 28.01.2025): it began with fond R-2848 (the Council of Ministers of the Moldavian SSR, 1940–1941 and 1944–1948, about 60,000 pages) with metadata search in Russian; the home page now also has databases on the repressed and repatriated, documents on the Holocaust and prisoners of war [checked: arhiva.gov.md, doc.arhiva.gov.md].

## Russia

- **Oblast archives** (GA*O, state archives of oblasts): metrical books, revisions, Soviet documents. Many run an **EAIS** (ЕАИС) — a paid remote viewing of digitised files (for example, in one archive since November 2025, 60 rubles an hour or 110 rubles for 2 hours). Before ordering files, check whether there are **name and cross-fond indexes** — they sometimes lie in open PDF inventories.
- **RGIA** (РГИА, the Russian State Historical Archive, St Petersburg): inventories are scanned, electronic orders through a personal account [from search snippet]. **RGADA** (РГАДА, the Russian State Archive of Ancient Acts): the landrat books and revision lists of 1707–1792 (fond 350) are digitised, but can be viewed only in the reading room [from search snippet].
- **Yandex "Search in archives"** (Яндекс «Поиск по архивам») — recognised handwritten text of 30 Russian archives, newspapers and directories; there are no Ukrainian archives.
- According to FamilySearch, many Russian archives are not set up for "family history" requests.
- **Access from abroad:** some Russian state sites (archives, libraries, military databases) do not open from foreign addresses. This is a network restriction, not a sign that the site is closed.

In detail: [Russian-language resources](../../catalog/russian-language-resources.md).

## Belarus

- **NIAB** (НИАБ, the National Historical Archives of Belarus, Minsk; a branch in Hrodna): metrical books, revisions, synagogue records; 29 fonds of the Jewish communities of Minsk and Mogilev guberniyas [from search snippet]. Genealogical search is paid; you can work on your own in the reading room by archival references [from search snippet].
- Online: Radzima.org (a gazetteer of places), JewishGen Belarus databases, the "Book of Memory" (memory.mil.by).

## Poland

- **JRI-Poland**: 6.4 million records, links to scans, free.
- **Geneteka** (Polish Genealogical Society): 68 million records, includes Ukraine, Belarus, Lithuania.
- **szukajwarchiwach.gov.pl**: more than 55 million scans of the state archives; search by the name of the locality [from search snippet]. ⚠️ The portal blocked some foreign addresses entirely.
- **PRADZIAD** — where the books of a parish or a civil registry are held [from search snippet].
- **AGAD** (Warsaw): Jewish metrical books of the eastern territories, including Galicia [from search snippet].

## The Baltic states

- **Lithuania:** LitvakSIG (All Lithuania Database, more than 2.8 million records); metrical books before 1940 — the Lithuanian State Historical Archives (LVIA); epaveldas.lt [from search snippet].
- **Latvia:** Raduraksti — church books of all denominations (Jewish ones are in the section "Mosaic faith"), revisions, the 1897 census; free registration, Latvian interface.
- **Estonia:** the National Archives (Rahvusarhiiv) — Saaga (digitised; the old address does not connect as of 08.10.2026 — see the [catalog](../../catalog/europe-by-country.md)) and AIS; Jewish metrical books of the communities of Tallinn, Tartu, Narva and others; the Album Academicum of the University of Dorpat (Tartu).

## Romania

- **ROM-SIG** (JewishGen) — a database for Romania, but it **does not cover Bessarabia and Transylvania**: those are handled by Bessarabia SIG and the Hungarian Research Division.
- The National Archives of Romania (ANR) — the pages did not open, not checked.

## Germany and Austria

- **Arolsen Archives** — documents on persecution, camps, forced labour, displaced persons; search is open, and an inquiry can be submitted in Russian.
- **Bundesarchiv Gedenkbuch** — victims of the persecution of Jews in Germany 1933–1945.
- **Hamburg and Bremen departure lists** — see [Emigration](13-emigration.md).
- **GenTeam** (Austria) — more than 23 million records, including the Jewish metrical books of Vienna; free after registration.
- **University of Vienna, PHAIDRA** — student questionnaires with the name, occupation and place of residence of the father.

## Israel

- **Israel State Archives** — immigration and naturalisation under the British Mandate (more than 60,000 files of 1937–1947), rabbinical courts.
- **Central Zionist Archives** — has a paid genealogical service.
- **CAHJP** (the Central Archives for the History of the Jewish People, at the National Library) — community archives, microfilms from the archives of the former USSR (since the early 1990s).
- **Yad Vashem** — the central database of names of Holocaust victims, evacuation cards.
- **IGRA** — the All Israel Database (more than 4 million records).

## Other countries of Europe

Belgium (police files on foreigners), France (naturalisation files), Great Britain (naturalisation with the parents' names) — see [Europe by country](../../catalog/europe-by-country.md).

## FamilySearch: catalogue → film → frame

Many metrical books and revisions of Ukraine, Moldova, Belarus and Poland were filmed onto microfilm and put on [FamilySearch](https://www.familysearch.org/). Free, registration required. The path from a place to the page you need is four steps.

1. **The catalogue.** Open the [FamilySearch catalogue](https://www.familysearch.org/search/catalog) and search **by place**: shtetl, uezd, guberniya — in all spellings in Latin letters (Nezhin and Nizhin find different things). In the catalogue record each film has a film number, a digital film number **DGS** and a list of books (items) with archival references and years. Do not filter by the word "Jewish": the label is incomplete, and a Jewish book may be hidden inside someone else's collection (for example, among the Orthodox metrical books of the uezd).
2. **The film.** The camera icon opens viewing by DGS. One film can hold 5–10 different books, sometimes from different places and denominations.
3. **The right book on the film.** If the frame number is known from an index (for example, the "Image" field in JewishGen), go straight to it. If not, switch on thumbnail mode (the "mosaic") and look at every 20th frame: the boundaries of books are visible by the black "END OF ITEM" cards and the title pages with the reference; then refine in steps of 2–3 frames.
4. **The frame.** First read the printed header of the table, then the column you need, enlarged. A spread is sometimes shot twice in a row; with double shooting the father's and the mother's columns lie on different frames. Write down: the DGS number, the frame, the record number, a verbatim quotation.

**If something is wrong:**
- **The year you need is not on the film** — check the neighbouring films of the same collection and the second copy of the book (the rabbinical or the consistory one); write "year not filmed", not "not found".
- **The film is locked** or gives only the first frames — it can be viewed only at a FamilySearch centre or at partners. This is a limitation of the source; look for the same reference on Commons or on the archive's site (for Ukraine, through "Kachynyi Inspektor").
- **Pace.** After about 45–150 opened frames FamilySearch shows an "are you a robot" check (in detail — [Access, captchas, pace](19-access-captchas-pace.md)). A human passes it. Do not turn pages more often than one frame in 5–10 seconds, with breaks; do not download a whole film.

A detailed method, written for AI but understandable for a human too — the skill [`searching-familysearch-films`](../../../skills/searching-familysearch-films/SKILL.md) (in Russian).

## If there is nothing online

First check the cheap routes: indexes and full text, neighbouring years and neighbouring rabbinates, substitutes for metrical books ([Records of the Russian Empire and the USSR](01-records-empire-ussr.md), "If there are no metrical books"), new digitisations — they are put online all the time. If that gives nothing either, bring the search to an **exact archival reference** (fond–inventory–file). Whether to write a request to an archive is for you to decide — how to do it is in [Requests and letters](20-requests-letters.md).

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The (browser agent) label is for an AI that opens websites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "In which archive could documents of type [type] for [place] for [years] be held, and how can I check it: which guides, inventories and catalogues should I open, where to see what is digitised (the archive's website, FamilySearch, Commons)? Give fond numbers only with a link to the source, otherwise mark them [from memory, verify]." *Check yourself:* archival references — against the inventory; for Ukraine, check the reference in "Kachynyi Inspektor".
> - "Here are file titles from an inventory in Ukrainian [text]. Translate and explain the terms (fond, opys, sprava, revizki kazky, kazenna palata)." *Check yourself:* browser auto-translation spoils terms: "sprava" is a file, not "affairs".
>
> *Bad → good:* "Give me the fond number with the metrical books of [shtetl]" (it will invent a number from memory, no years given) → "Where can I check in which archive and fond the metrical books of [shtetl] for [years] are held and whether they are digitised?"
