# War, evacuation, repression, the siege, the Holocaust: Soviet sources

What this section covers: how to find documents about a person who fought, was killed or went missing, was evacuated, was repressed, died in the siege (of Leningrad) or was killed in the Holocaust. Databases by region, which fields they have, and how to search when the surname is distorted.

Markers: **[checked]** — the page was read (October 2026); **[from search snippet]** — information from search results or someone else's overview, verify figures before using them; **(LRF)** — a figure from the Lost Russian Family consolidated list (lostrussianfamily.wpcomstaging.com/free-databases/), not verified on the sites themselves.

⚠️ Many Russian government sites on this list do not open from foreign addresses. This is a network restriction, not a sign that the site is closed (see [Access, captchas, pace](19-access-captchas-pace.md)).

Most of the databases below are Russian- or Ukrainian-language sites; search in Cyrillic.

---

## 1. How to search: general rules

All the guides I have read repeat these rules, and they have been confirmed in practice:

- **Search in Cyrillic.** FamilySearch states that the Russian spelling gives noticeably more than a transliteration [checked: FamilySearch blog on Second World War military records].
- **Try all variants of the full name** — scribes distorted them. In one study, a relative recorded as "Sheffel" (arrested in 1937) was found only on the second attempt: the first time only "Shefel" was searched.
- **Enter the beginning of the surname** where the database allows: on martyrology.org.ua "Shev" finds Shevchenko, Shevchuk and so on.
- **Use the patronymic** (among Jews often Russified) and **the year of birth ±2**.
- **The main filter is the place of birth.** It helps to identify a person across different spellings of the surname.
- **Cross-check a record in several databases.**
- **Printed memorial books reprint distortions**: a surname in a book may be distorted so that "there is no such surname". Check against the primary record in OBD (Unified Database) or "Pamyat naroda" ("People's Memory"; the volunteer project "Imya voina" drew attention to this, 2026).

A real example. On "Pamyat naroda" and in OBD "Memorial" one man turned up in five documents: called up at 16, regiment, captivity and liberation, a wound. The surname and patronymic are spelled differently in them; he was identified by the year and place of birth.

Another example. In the memorial book "Blockade" the grandmother's parents were found — both died in 1942, one address. The mother's surname was distorted beyond recognition and stood in a different volume. Distorted surnames in the siege books are common.

## 2. Nadia Lipes's advice

Nadia (Sara-Beila) Lipes is a Ukrainian historian and genealogist who has indexed more than a million archival records of the Russian Empire (more in [Genealogists and authors](../../reading/genealogists-and-authors.md)). Her advice (from an article by V. Pletinsky, isrageo.com, 10.04.2019) [checked]:
- formulate the goal of the search clearly in advance so as not to be disappointed later;
- **Soviet documents are organised in the same way in different countries**; only the difficulty of getting them differs;
- a DNA test is only an auxiliary tool;
- with pre-revolutionary documents it is better to work through local genealogists; it can take months.

The main book on the topic of this section is **N. Lipes, "Soviet Documents. What, Where, Why"** (*Sovetskie dokumenty. Chto, gde, zachem*; Odessa: Astroprint, 2018, the series "Genealogy for Dummies"). It describes all kinds of Soviet documents — from ZAGS to the files of the repressed — with photocopies of real documents. No separate articles by Lipes specifically on OBD, "Pamyat naroda", evacuation cards and the SBU archives were found on open pages.

## 3. All-Union databases and Russia

### War

| Database | What is there | How to search |
|---|---|---|
| **"Pamyat naroda"** (pamyat-naroda.ru) | awards, **the 1985 jubilee card file (only here)**, loss reports, **hospital registers** (they have a column "address of relatives" — more informative than the loss reports), documents of prisoners of war | the main filter is the place of birth, combined with the given name and patronymic; in the "grouping" mode exact matches come first, then fuzzy ones. A captcha on automated queries — a person completes it |
| **OBD "Memorial"** (obd-memorial.ru) | the killed and missing, documents of TsAMO (Central Archive of the Russian Ministry of Defence). Fields: full name, date or year of birth, date and place of call-up, last place of service, rank, reason for leaving | the search is **exact** by surname — each spelling as a separate query; truncated words are not searched |
| **"Podvig naroda"** (podvignaroda.ru) | awards, scans of award sheets; now part of "Pamyat naroda" | — |
| **"Doroga pamyati"** | photos of front-line soldiers; sometimes photos from personal files not in other databases [from search snippet] | — |
| **"Bessmertny polk"** (Immortal Regiment; moypolk.ru) | stories of front-line soldiers from their relatives | ordinary search by surname |
| **1914.svrt.ru**, the 1914–1918 loss card file (gwar.mil.ru) | the First World War; the loss card file is a source of the guberniya, uezd and volost of a soldier's birth | gwar.mil.ru did not open from abroad |
| **Soviet prisoners of war** — the Dokumentationsstelle Dresden database (en.stsg.de/cms/dokstelle/search-online-archives) | about 900 thousand records [from search snippet]; fields: surname, given name, father's name, date of birth | free, the interface is also in Russian [checked] |
| **Arolsen Archives** (collections.arolsen-archives.org) | concentration camps, Ostarbeiter (forced labourers), displaced persons; more than 40 million documents in the digital archive [checked: arolsen-archives.org, 2026-10-07; an old press release says about 30 million documents and 17.5 million people] | a request can be submitted in Russian, free of charge [checked: press release] |
| Medals "For the Defence of Moscow" (vov.mos.ru), "For the Defence of Leningrad" (medal.spbarchives.ru) | (LRF) | — |

### Evacuation

- **Yad Vashem**: the central database includes data on more than half of the roughly 1.5 million evacuated Jews [from search snippet]. In one study the evacuation cards proved the patronymics of two family members where there were no metrical records.
- **An evacuation card is also a list of relatives.** Whoever was evacuated together, look for them afterwards in the ZAGS books: the births of children of **all** the men of the family. The place of birth named by the evacuee may lead to a shtetl, not a city (N. Lipes, nadialipes.info).
- **Tashkent, JewishGen database** (jewishgen.org/databases/holocaust/0136_uzbek.html) [checked]: 151,966 cards of Jewish evacuees of 1941–1942 from the Central State Archive of Uzbekistan. Fields: surname, given name, **patronymic**, year of birth, sex, where evacuated from, district of evacuation. The **scan of the card** opens by number. The cards themselves also have occupation, place of work, children under 16. Limitation: only those who arrived in Tashkent before February 1942.
- **USHMM survivors and victims database** (ushmm.org/online/hsv): the same evacuation cards, free and without logging in. ⚠️ The pages announce that the site will stop working in autumn 2026 — save what you need beforehand.
- **Regional databases**: Chelyabinsk oblast (archive74.ru), Kirov (the form has reCAPTCHA), Tomsk, the Altai Republic, Komi (LRF).
- **Oblast archives**: in one case a child's evacuation from Leningrad was found in the register "Movement of children" of an oblast archive (via EAIS, the unified archival information system).

| Database | What is there |
|---|---|

Full-text search is switched off. Harsh throttling: after three fast queries, a pause |
| **"Immortal Barrack"** (*Bessmertny barak*; bessmertnybarak.ru) | about 2.6 million names (LRF). | **"Last Address"** (*Posledny adres*; poslednyadres.ru) | more than 340 thousand names with last addresses (LRF) |
| **"Victims of Political Terror in the USSR"** (base.memo.ru) | about 3.1 million names (LRF); ⚠️ not working since spring 2026, archived copies remain |
| Moscow: "Execution lists. Moscow, 1937–1941. 'Kommunarka', Butovo" (2000) | the names are in the "Open List" by category [from search snippet] |

## 4. Leningrad: the siege and repression

- **"Blockade. 1941–1944. Leningrad. Book of Memory"** — 35 volumes, 631,063 records; the electronic version is on the site of the "Returned Names" centre of the Russian National Library (visz.nlr.ru/blockade), searchable by surname and by the text of the entry [from search snippet].
- **The consolidated "Book of Memory of Besieged Leningrad"** (blockade.spb.ru) — combines TsAMO, the siege books, evacuation and regional archives; no captcha. Only the Leningrad circle — it is **not** a universal replacement for "Pamyat naroda". Scans of files lie as separate numbered files: neighbouring images of the same file can be opened by changing the number, and the file can be read in full.
- **"Leningrad Martyrology 1937–1938"** — volumes and a consolidated name index on visz.nlr.ru [from search snippet].
- **Evacuation from Leningrad 1941–1943** (evacuation.spbarchives.ru, the Central State Archive of St Petersburg) — about 620.8 thousand cards; search by full name, year of birth, year of evacuation [from search snippet].
- **Piskaryovskoe Cemetery** (pmemorial.ru) — ⚠️ as of September 2026 the search database is no longer on the site.

## 5. Ukraine

- **martyrology.org.ua** (National Museum of the History of Ukraine in the Second World War) [checked]: 697,836 records for 12 oblasts, including Vinnytsia, Khmelnytskyi, Zhytomyr, Kyiv, Chernivtsi. Fields: full name, year of birth, oblast and district, rank, unit, year and reason of leaving, place of burial. Search by the beginning of the surname.
- **"Archive of Ukrainians' Memory"** (*Arkhiv pamiati ukraintsiv*; archive.memory.org.ua) [checked]: Red Army soldiers from Ukraine in German captivity (so far Vinnytsia oblast); full name in Cyrillic and Latin, date and place of birth, camps, photos.
- **Archive of National Memory (HDA UINP)**, hdauinp.org.ua [checked]: free, accessible from abroad; video lessons on searching for the repressed; free manuals "Practical guide to access to archives. The right to truth" and "KGB archives for the media" (both in Ukrainian).
- **The SBU sectoral state archive** — since 2015 documents of the Soviet period have been open; the core of the fond is criminal cases of the 1920s–1980s [from search snippet]. A file may contain enclosures that exist nowhere else: a **passport** and personal letters seized at arrest — a source of addresses and family ties (N. Lipes, isrageo.com, 2019).
- **Filtration files on FamilySearch**: the collection "Ukraine, Soviet Population Filtration Records, 1943–1991" — about 230 thousand records for the Poltava, Kyiv, Khmelnytskyi and Kirovohrad oblasts, searchable [from search snippet: lostrussianfamily; unverified].
- **National Data Bank of the Repressed** (reabit.org.ua) and "Ukrainian Martyrology of the 20th Century" (LRF).
- **Book of Memory of Ukraine** (memory-book.ua) — the killed and missing in the Second World War [from search snippet: FamilySearch].
- **Filtration files of repatriates of Kyiv oblast** (opisi.dako.gov.ua/camps/filtr) — more than 115 thousand people, with scans (LRF).
- **Babi Yar** — the name database of the Memorial Center (babynyar.org): about 29,700 names with ages, addresses, relatives, professions [from search snippet].
- **"Book of Sorrow of Ukraine"** (*Knyha skorboty Ukrainy*) — civilian victims of the occupation. For Khmelnytskyi oblast — 4 volumes; online only Krasyliv (vol. I) and Starokostiantyniv (vol. II) were found on KehilaLinks; the other districts were not found online. Some volumes for other oblasts are on archive.org [from search snippet].
- **The Holodomor** (the Great Famine): memorialholodomors.org.ua (LRF).

## 6. Belarus

- **Book of Memory of the Republic of Belarus** (memory.mil.by) — the killed in the Second World War (LRF).
- **Partisans of Belarus** (partizany.by) — about 400 thousand (LRF). USHMM has an index "Jewish Partisans in Belarus 1941–1944" (8,451 people; full name with patronymic, year of birth, place of residence, brigade, detachment, date of death) [from search snippet].
- **JewishGen Belarus Database** [from search snippet].

## 7. Moldova and Bessarabia

- **"Moldavian Jewish Victims of Soviet Oppression 1941–1951"** (JewishGen) [checked]: 4,750 records. Fields: surname, given name, father's name, year and place of birth, address, relation to the head of the family, date of deportation, **train number**, reference of the court case.
- **Deportation from Moldova, 13–16 June 1941** (JewishGen) [from search snippet]; the National Archives Agency of Moldova has put declassified name lists of 1941 and 1949 online as PDFs [from search snippet].

## 8. The Baltic states

- **Lithuania**: JewishGen "Evacuation, Exile and Return" [checked] — questionnaires of those returning from Russia after the First World War (14,383), evacuees of 1941, those repressed and deported in 1940–1941 (2,606); the register of the Genocide and Resistance Research Centre of Lithuania (lietuviaisibire.lt) [checked: LRT].
- **Latvia, Estonia**: online databases of the repressed were not checked.

## 9. The Holocaust

- **Yad Vashem, the central names database** (collections.yadvashem.org): about 5 million names; the basis is **Pages of Testimony** (questionnaires from relatives and acquaintances). Most valuable are **the name and address of the person who submitted the page** — a relative (alive at the time of submission) through whom the whole branch can be found. Search in three tabs: victims, **submitters**, others. Detailed techniques — [Indexed databases](../../catalog/indexed-databases.md).
- **"The Untold Stories"** (Yad Vashem) — sites of mass murder of Jews in the occupied territory of the USSR, more than 2,700 sites [from search snippet].
- **USHMM** — search modes Exact, Fuzzy and D-M Soundex.
- **The databases do not coincide**: Yad Vashem, Arolsen, USHMM and the Ancestry Holocaust collections have different holdings — check them all. Search "as it sounds", not the exact spelling (advice of the Hebrew-language forum Tapuz "שורשים משפחתיים" ["family roots"], 2026) [checked]. The Ancestry Holocaust collections are reportedly open free of charge [unverified].
- **If there is no Page of Testimony: the acts of the ChGK.** Name lists of victims collected by the Extraordinary State Commission (ChGK) in 1944–1945 by district and village: the originals are in GARF (State Archive of the Russian Federation) fond 7021; USHMM microfilm RG-22.002M with an index of 1,456 places [checked: ushmm.org]. The handwriting is difficult, the lists are in Russian.
- **Date and place of death by place of residence.** Find the article on the shtetl in the encyclopedias: "The Holocaust in the Territory of the USSR" (*Kholokost na territorii SSSR*; ed. I. Altman, 2009; places where at least 100 Jews were killed or there were ghettos) and the works of A. Kruglov ("Encyclopedia of the Holocaust", Kyiv, 2000) [from search snippet]. Dates of the "actions" matched with the family's last known address are not yet proof of death, but they are a precise question for Yad Vashem and the ChGK acts.
- **The Romanian zone ("Transnistria")** — the south of Podolia and Bessarabia: a different kind of fate (camps and ghettos, not the shootings of the first months); testimonies were collected, for example, by B. Zabarko [from search snippet].
- **Arolsen Archives**, **EHRI** (a portal of archival inventories on the Holocaust across Europe).
- "(Un)Known Holocaust" (*(Ne)Vidomyi Holokost*) is a documentary film (2021), not a database.

## 10. If the databases have nothing

- Check **all** spellings and **the place of birth without a surname**.
- Check **neighbouring databases**: what is not in "Pamyat naroda" is sometimes in OBD, and vice versa; the siege books are in several volumes.
- Check **the surroundings**: people of the same household, neighbours at the same address, fellow servicemen.
- **Russia:** documents with personal and family secrets are closed for 75 years (before that they are issued to the person himself, after his death to heirs); ZAGS record books are kept by the ZAGS offices for 100 years. **Ukraine:** the archives of the repressive bodies are open under law no. 316-VIII of 09.04.2015; access may be restricted by the repressed person himself, and by relatives only for certain sensitive information, for no longer than 25 years — see [Requests and letters](20-requests-letters.md).

> **How to ask AI**
>
> - "Here are the details of a person: [full name, year of birth, place]. Make a list of databases (military, evacuation, repression, Holocaust) in which to look for him, and all name forms to search: Russian, Jewish, distorted." *Check yourself:* each record — against the scan of the card or report, not the search-result line. (The AI may need to read Russian- and Ukrainian-language sources.)
> - "The family lived in [shtetl] in 1941. What is known about the fate of the Jews of this place: dates of actions, ghetto, evacuation? Give sources (Holocaust encyclopedias, ChGK acts)." *Check yourself:* this is not yet proof of the death of a specific person, but a precise question to the documents.
> - "Here is an evacuation card [scan]. Write out everyone who was evacuated together, and what from the card can be used to look for their ZAGS records." *Check yourself:* the place of birth on the card may lead to a shtetl, not a city.
