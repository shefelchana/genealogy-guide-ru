# War, evacuation, repression, the siege, the Holocaust: Soviet sources

What this section covers: how to find documents about a person who fought, was killed or went missing, was evacuated, was repressed, died in the siege of Leningrad or was killed in the Holocaust. Databases by region, which fields they have, and how to search when the surname is distorted.

The markers in square brackets ([checked], [from search snippet], [unverified] and others) are explained in the [Glossary](../0-start/06-glossary.md). (LRF) marks a figure from the consolidated Lost Russian Family list (lostrussianfamily.wpcomstaging.com/free-databases/).

⚠️ Many Russian government sites on this list do not open from foreign addresses. This is a network restriction, not a sign that the site is closed (see [Access, captchas, pace](19-access-captchas-pace.md)).

Most of the databases below are Russian- or Ukrainian-language sites; search in Cyrillic. Don't read Russian? See [For English speakers](../0-start/08-for-english-speakers.md).

## 1. How to search: general rules

All the guides I have read repeat these rules, and they have been confirmed in practice:

- **Search in Cyrillic.** FamilySearch writes that the Russian spelling gives noticeably more results than a transliteration [checked: FamilySearch blog on Second World War military records].
- **Try all variants of the full name** — scribes distorted names. In one study, a relative recorded as "Sheffel" (arrested in 1937) was found only on the second attempt: the first time only "Shefel" had been searched.
- **Enter the beginning of the surname** where the database allows it: on martyrology.org.ua, "Shev" finds Shevchenko, Shevchuk and so on.
- **Use the patronymic** (among Jews often Russified) and **the year of birth ±2**.
- **The main filter is the place of birth.** It helps to identify a person across different spellings of the surname.
- **Cross-check a record in several databases.**
- **Printed memorial books reprint distortions**: a surname in a book may be distorted so badly that "there is no such surname". Check against the primary record in OBD (the Unified Database "Memorial", ОБД «Мемориал») or "Pamyat naroda" ("People's Memory", «Память народа»). The volunteer project "Imya voina" ("Warrior's Name", «Имя воина») drew attention to this in 2026.

A real example. On "Pamyat naroda" and in OBD "Memorial" one man turned up in five documents: called up at 16, regiment, captivity and liberation, a wound. The surname and patronymic are spelled differently in them; he was identified by his year and place of birth.

Another example. In the memorial book "Blockade" (Книга памяти «Блокада») a grandmother's parents were found — both died in 1942, at one address. The mother's surname was distorted beyond recognition and stood in a different volume. Distorted surnames in the siege books are common.

## 2. Nadia Lipes's advice

Nadia (Sara-Beila) Lipes is a Ukrainian historian and genealogist who has indexed more than a million archival records of the Russian Empire (more in [Genealogists and authors](../../reading/genealogists-and-authors.md)). Her advice (from an article by V. Pletinsky, isrageo.com, 10.04.2019) [checked]:
- state the goal of the search clearly in advance, so as not to be disappointed later;
- **Soviet documents are organised in the same way in different countries**; only the difficulty of getting them differs;
- a DNA test is only an auxiliary tool;
- with pre-revolutionary documents it is better to work through local genealogists; it can take months.

The main book on the topic of this section is **N. Lipes, "Soviet Documents. What, Where, Why"** (*Sovetskie dokumenty. Chto, gde, zachem*; Odessa: Astroprint, 2018, series "Genealogy for Dummies"). It describes all kinds of Soviet documents — from ZAGS (civil registry) to the files of the repressed — with photocopies of real documents. No separate articles by Lipes specifically on OBD, "Pamyat naroda", evacuation cards and the SBU (Security Service of Ukraine) archives were found on open pages.

## 3. All-Union databases and Russia

### War

| Database | What is there | How to search |
|---|---|---|
| **"Pamyat naroda"** (pamyat-naroda.ru) | awards, **the 1985 jubilee card index (only here)**, loss reports, **hospital registers** (they have a "relatives' address" column — more informative than loss reports), documents of prisoners of war | the main filter is place of birth, combined with first name and patronymic; in "grouping" mode exact matches come first, then fuzzy ones. Captcha on automated requests — a human passes it |
| **OBD "Memorial"** (obd-memorial.ru) | killed and missing in action, documents of TsAMO (Central Archive of the Russian Ministry of Defence). Fields: full name, date or year of birth, date and place of call-up, last place of service, rank, reason for leaving the ranks | the search is **exact** by surname — each spelling is a separate query; truncated words are not searched |
| **"Podvig naroda"** ("The People's Feat", podvignaroda.ru) | awards, scans of award recommendation sheets; now part of "Pamyat naroda" | — |
| **"Doroga pamyati"** ("Road of Memory") | photos of front-line soldiers; there are photos from personal files that are not in other databases [from search snippet] | — |
| **"Bessmertny polk"** ("Immortal Regiment", moypolk.ru) | stories of front-line soldiers told by relatives | ordinary search by surname |
| **1914.svrt.ru**, loss card index 1914–1918 (gwar.mil.ru) | the First World War; the loss card index is a source for the guberniya, uezd and volost of a soldier's birth | gwar.mil.ru did not open from abroad |
| **Soviet prisoners of war** — database of the Dokumentationsstelle Dresden (en.stsg.de/cms/dokstelle/search-online-archives) | about 900,000 records [from search snippet]; fields: surname, first name, father's name, date of birth | free; interface also in Russian [checked] |
| **Arolsen Archives** (collections.arolsen-archives.org) | concentration camps, forced labourers, displaced persons; more than 40 million documents in the digital archive [checked: arolsen-archives.org, 2026-10-07; an old press release says about 30 million documents and 17.5 million people] | a request can be submitted in Russian, free of charge [checked: press release] |
| Medals "For the Defence of Moscow" (vov.mos.ru), "For the Defence of Leningrad" (medal.spbarchives.ru) | (LRF) | — |

### Evacuation

- **Yad Vashem**: the central database contains data on more than half of the roughly 1.5 million evacuated Jews [from search snippet]. In one study, evacuation cards proved the patronymics of two family members where there were no metrical records.
- **An evacuation card is also a list of relatives.** Look for those who were evacuated together afterwards in the ZAGS books: the births of children of **all** the men of the family. The place of birth named by an evacuee may lead to a shtetl rather than a city (N. Lipes, nadialipes.info).
- **Tashkent, JewishGen database** (jewishgen.org/databases/holocaust/0136_uzbek.html) [checked]: 151,966 cards of Jewish evacuees of 1941–1942 from the Central State Archive of Uzbekistan. Fields: surname, first name, **patronymic**, year of birth, sex, where evacuated from, district of evacuation. The **scan of the card** opens by its number. The cards themselves also have occupation, place of work, children under 16. Limitation: only those who arrived in Tashkent before February 1942.
- **USHMM survivors and victims database** (ushmm.org/online/hsv): the same evacuation cards, free and without logging in. ⚠️ The pages announce that the site will stop working in autumn 2026 — save what you need in advance.
- **Regional databases**: Chelyabinsk oblast (archive74.ru), Kirov (reCAPTCHA on the form), Tomsk, the Altai Republic, Komi (LRF).
- **Regional archives**: in one case the evacuation of a child from Leningrad was found in the register "Movement of Children" ("Движение детей") of a regional archive (through the EAIS, the unified archive information system).

Full-text search is switched off. Strict throttling: after three quick queries there is a pause |

## 4. Leningrad: the siege and repression

- **"Blockade. 1941–1944. Leningrad. Book of Memory"** — 35 volumes, 631,063 records; the electronic version is on the site of the "Returned Names" centre of the Russian National Library (visz.nlr.ru/blockade), searchable by surname and by the text of the entry [from search snippet].
- **The consolidated "Book of Memory of Besieged Leningrad"** (blockade.spb.ru) combines TsAMO, the siege books, evacuation and regional archives; no captcha. It covers only the Leningrad circle — it is **not** a universal replacement for "Pamyat naroda". Scans of files lie as separate numbered files: adjacent images of the same file can be opened by changing the number, so you can read the whole file.
- **"Leningrad Martyrology 1937–1938"** — volumes and a consolidated name index on visz.nlr.ru [from search snippet].
- **Evacuation from Leningrad 1941–1943** (evacuation.spbarchives.ru, Central State Archive of St Petersburg) — about 620,800 cards; search by full name, year of birth, year of evacuation [from search snippet].
- **Piskaryovskoe Cemetery** (pmemorial.ru) — ⚠️ as of September 2026 the site no longer has a search database.

## 5. Ukraine

- **martyrology.org.ua** (National Museum of the History of Ukraine in the Second World War) [checked]: 697,836 records for 12 oblasts, including Vinnytsia, Khmelnytskyi, Zhytomyr, Kyiv and Chernivtsi. Fields: full name, year of birth, oblast and district, rank, unit, year and reason of leaving the ranks, burial place. Search by the beginning of the surname.
- **"Archive of Ukrainians' Memory"** (*Arkhiv pamiati ukraintsiv*, archive.memory.org.ua) [checked]: Red Army soldiers from Ukraine in German captivity (so far Vinnytsia oblast); full name in Cyrillic and Latin, date and place of birth, camps, photos.
- **Archive of National Memory (the State Archive of the Ukrainian Institute of National Remembrance, ГДА УІНП)**, hdauinp.org.ua [checked]: free, accessible from abroad; video lessons on searching for the repressed; free guides "Practical guide to access to archives. The right to truth" and "KGB archives for media" (both in Ukrainian).
- **The SBU sectoral state archive** — since 2015 documents of the Soviet period have been open; the core of the fond is criminal cases of the 1920s–1980s [from search snippet]. A case file can contain enclosures found nowhere else: a **passport** and personal letters seized at arrest — a source of addresses and family ties (N. Lipes, isrageo.com, 2019).
- **Filtration files on FamilySearch**: the collection "Ukraine, Soviet Population Filtration Records, 1943–1991" — about 230,000 records for Poltava, Kyiv, Khmelnytskyi and Kirovohrad oblasts, searchable [from search snippet: lostrussianfamily; unverified].
- **National databank of the repressed** (reabit.org.ua) and "Ukrainian Martyrology of the 20th Century" ("Український мартиролог ХХ ст.") (LRF).
- **Book of Memory of Ukraine** (memory-book.ua) — those killed and missing in the Second World War [from search snippet: FamilySearch]. ⚠️ The home page has casino spam links inserted into it (08.10.2026) — click only through the database menu.
- **Filtration files of repatriates of Kyiv oblast** (opisi.dako.gov.ua/camps/filtr) — more than 115,000 people, with scans (LRF).
- **Babi Yar** — the name database of the Memorial Centre (babynyar.org): about 29,700 names with ages, addresses, relatives, occupations [from search snippet].
- **"Book of Sorrow of Ukraine"** (*Knyha skorboty Ukrainy*) — civilian victims of the occupation. For Khmelnytskyi oblast there are 4 volumes; online only Krasyliv (vol. I) and Starokostiantyniv (vol. II) turned up, on KehilaLinks; the other districts were not found online. Some volumes for other oblasts are on archive.org [from search snippet].
- **Holodomor** (the Great Famine): memorialholodomors.org.ua (LRF).

## 6. Belarus

- **Book of Memory of the Republic of Belarus** (memory.mil.by) — those killed in the Second World War (LRF).
- **Partisans of Belarus** (partizany.by) — about 400,000 (LRF). USHMM has an index "Jewish Partisans in Belarus 1941–1944" (8,451 people; full name with patronymic, year of birth, place of residence, brigade, detachment, date of death) [from search snippet].
- **JewishGen Belarus Database** [from search snippet].

## 7. Moldova and Bessarabia

- **"Moldavian Jewish Victims of Soviet Oppression 1941–1951"** (JewishGen) [checked]: 4,750 records. Fields: surname, first name, father's name, year and place of birth, address, relation to the head of the family, date of deportation, **train number**, reference of the court case.
- **Deportation from Moldova on 13–16 June 1941** (JewishGen) [from search snippet]; the National Archives Agency of Moldova has put declassified name lists of 1941 and 1949 online as PDFs [from search snippet].

## 8. The Baltic states

- **Lithuania**: JewishGen "Evacuation, Exile and Return" [checked] — questionnaires of people returning from Russia after the First World War (14,383), those evacuated in 1941, the repressed and deported in 1940–1941 (2,606); the register of the Genocide and Resistance Research Centre of Lithuania (lietuviaisibire.lt) [checked: LRT].
- **Latvia, Estonia**: online databases of the repressed were not checked.

## 9. The Holocaust

- **Yad Vashem, central database of names** (collections.yadvashem.org): about 5 million names; the core is the **Pages of Testimony** (forms filled in by relatives and acquaintances). Most valuable is the **name and address of the person who submitted the page** — a relative alive at the time of submission, through whom a whole branch can be found. Search in all three tabs: victims, **submitters**, others. Detailed techniques are in [Indexed databases](../../catalog/indexed-databases.md).
- **"The Untold Stories"** (Yad Vashem) — places of mass murder of Jews in the occupied territory of the USSR, more than 2,700 places [from search snippet].
- **USHMM** — search modes Exact, Fuzzy and D-M Soundex.
- **The databases do not coincide**: Yad Vashem, Arolsen, USHMM and the Ancestry Holocaust collections have different holdings — check them all. Search for "how it sounds", not the exact spelling (advice from the Hebrew-language forum Tapuz "שורשים משפחתיים", "Family Roots", 2026) [checked]. The Ancestry Holocaust collections are reported to be free to access [unverified].
- **If there is no Page of Testimony: the ChGK acts.** Name lists of victims compiled by the Extraordinary State Commission (ЧГК) in 1944–1945 by district and village: the originals are GARF (State Archive of the Russian Federation) fond 7021; USHMM microfilm RG-22.002M with an index of 1,456 places [checked: ushmm.org]. The handwriting is hard and the lists are in Russian.
- **Date and place of death by place of residence.** Find the article about the shtetl in the encyclopedias: "The Holocaust on the Territory of the USSR" (*Kholokost na territorii SSSR*, ed. I. Altman, 2009; places where at least 100 Jews were killed or there were ghettos) and the works of A. Kruglov ("Encyclopedia of the Holocaust", Kyiv, 2000) [from search snippet]. Dates of "actions", matched against the family's last known address, are not yet proof of death, but they make a precise question for Yad Vashem and the ChGK acts.
- **The Romanian zone ("Transnistria")** — the south of Podolia and Bessarabia: a different kind of fate (camps and ghettos, rather than shootings in the first months); testimonies have been collected, for example, by B. Zabarko [from search snippet].
- **Arolsen Archives**, **EHRI** (a portal of archival finding aids on the Holocaust across Europe).
- "(Un)Known Holocaust" ("(Не)Відомий Голокост") is a documentary film (2021), not a database.

## 10. If it is not in the databases

- Check **all** spellings and **the place of birth without a surname**.
- Check **neighbouring databases**: what is not in "Pamyat naroda" is sometimes in OBD, and the other way round; the siege books are spread over several volumes.
- Check **the surroundings**: fellow villagers, neighbours at the same address, comrades in the unit.
- **Russia:** documents containing personal and family secrets are closed for 75 years (before that they are issued to the person himself, after his death to his heirs); ZAGS registers are kept in ZAGS offices for 100 years. **Ukraine:** the archives of the repressive bodies are open under law No. 316-VIII of 09.04.2015; the repressed person himself may restrict access, and relatives may restrict access to individual sensitive information, for no longer than 25 years — see [Requests and letters](20-requests-letters.md).

> **How to ask AI**
>
> *For a plain chat, add: "If you can't open a source, say so; mark fonds, villages and dates you name from memory as [from memory, verify]. Answer in English, and give names, places and archive titles in the original script in parentheses." ([why](../1-ai-search/02-prompt-library.md)). The note "(browsing agent)" is for an AI that opens websites itself; the other prompts suit any chat.*
>
> - "Here is the data on a person: [full name, year of birth, place]. Draw up a list of databases (military, evacuation, repression, Holocaust) in which to look for him, and all the forms of the name for searching: Russian, Jewish, distorted." *Check yourself:* every record against the scan of the card or report, not against a line in the search results.
> - "The family lived in [shtetl] in 1941. In which sources can I learn the fate of the Jews of this place — dates of the actions, ghetto, evacuation (Holocaust encyclopedias, ChGK acts, Yad Vashem databases)? Give dates and figures only with a reference to a source, otherwise mark them [from memory, verify]." *Check yourself:* open the sources; this is not yet proof of the death of a specific person, but a precise question to the documents.
>
> *Bad → good:* "Find everything about my grandfather in the war" (asks for "everything", no name, year or place) → "[Full name], born [year], called up from [place]: in which databases should I look for loss reports, and under which forms of the name?"
