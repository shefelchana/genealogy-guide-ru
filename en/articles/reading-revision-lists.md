# How to read a Russian Empire revision list (1795–1858): a step-by-step guide

Revision lists (*revizskie skazki*, ревизские сказки) let descendants of Jewish families from the Russian Empire go back from 1858 to 1795, one generation at a time. This guide shows how to read the columns, how to chain households from one revision to the one before, and which traps make people reject the right family or accept the wrong one.

---

## What a revision list is

Revisions were censuses of the tax-paying population, taken for taxes and conscription. People were listed by place of registration, not residence: a *meshchanin* (мещанин, townsman) of such-and-such shtetl, a merchant of such-and-such guild.

| Revision | Year | What matters for Jewish research |
|---|---|---|
| 1st–4th | 1719–1782 | mainly the Orthodox and peasant population; few Jews, since most communities entered the empire with the partitions of Poland (1772–1795) |
| 5th | **1795** | for the Jews of Right-Bank Ukraine, the earliest; surnames often absent; may be bilingual, Russian-Polish; completeness varies |
| 6th | 1811 | **men only**: a missing wife or daughter means nothing |
| 7th | **1816** | men and women again; for many communities the first with the whole family; almost everyone has a surname |
| 8th | 1834 | complete |
| 9th | **1850** | complete |
| 10th | **1858** | the last; the family lists (*posemeinye spiski*, посемейные списки) of the 1870s refer back to it |

Before 1795, for the Jewish communities of the Right Bank, there are almost only community record books (*pinkasim*) and *kahal* tax registers, with very few survivals.

## Step 1. Read the printed header before you copy a single age

A revision list is a table with a printed header, filled in by hand. A typical header:

> «По послѣдней ревизiи состояло и послѣ оной прибыло — Лѣта» ‖ «Изъ того числа выбыло» ‖ «Нынѣ налицо»

That is: "according to the last revision there were, and since then arrived — age" ‖ "of that number, departed" ‖ "now present".

The first age column almost always refers to the **previous** revision, not the current one. Reading the wrong column shifts a birth year by the interval between revisions, 16 years between 1834 and 1850, and the right family gets rejected as "the wrong age".

A real case: an age of "13" was taken as belonging to 1850, giving a birth year of 1837, and the version was dropped. In fact the 13 belonged to 1834, the person was born about 1821, and everything agreed within a year.

So read the header word by word, and **always write an age together with its year**: "13 (in 1834)", never just "13". The letters ѣ, ъ, і and ѳ belong to the pre-reform spelling removed in 1918; keep them in your transcription as written.

## Step 2. Use the bridge column, and never skip a rung

The column «по последней ревизии» ("at the last revision") gives the age of the same person in the previous revision. It is the bridge between generations. Go down strictly in sequence:

1. Find the household in 1858.
2. Copy each person's age "at the last revision".
3. Look for the same household in 1850 at those ages.
4. Repeat from 1850 to 1834, then to 1816.

End every find with a **bridge line**: "age at the previous revision is NN, so look in the revision of [year] at age NN".

Also copy the column «из того числа выбыло — когда именно» ("of that number departed — when exactly"): «умеръ въ 18.. году» ("died in 18.."), «отданъ въ рекруты» ("given up as a recruit"), «перечисленъ въ …» ("transferred to …"). The year of death of a head of household often dates a whole generation. Look closely: «бѣжалъ» ("fled") can look like «умеръ» ("died").

**Check the rung by arithmetic.** The current age minus the "last revision" age should equal the interval between revisions: 8 from 1850 to 1858, 16 from 1834 to 1850. In one 1811 file the difference was exactly 5 in every row: the previous rung was a census of 1806, not the revision of 1795.

**A ready-made bridge in 1858.** Lists of 1858 often carry two family numbers, «№ 9 ревизiи» and «№ 10 ревизiи» ("No. in the 9th revision", "No. in the 10th"), pointing straight to the household in 1850.

## Step 3. Family numbers do not carry over

The official instruction (*nastavlenie*) told clerks to write families «по нынешнему разделению семей, а не по тому, как они при последней ревизии разделены были» ("according to the present division of families, and not as they were divided at the last revision"). Households split, families left, and the numbering was new each time. A number is valid only inside its own revision; link households by **given name, patronymic, age and household composition**.

Two checks help:

- **Order of seniority of the children** (a technique of Chaim Freedman): it should match between revisions. If not, look for a second wife or a second family under the same number.
- **One number is not always one family.** A "foster child" (*priyomysh*, приёмыш) of 49 beside a head of 32 is an adult attached to the household with his own family.

The exception: Bessarabian lists of 1848–1859 have a column for the **former family number**, a direct bridge.

## Step 4. Before the 1820s, search by patronymic, not surname

Jews of the empire were required to take surnames by the law of **1804**, but it was carried out over decades. In 1795 and 1811 a man is often just "Duwid Leybowicz". In one 1811 census for a Podolian shtetl, three full brothers appear under three different bynames (anonymised in this guide as Feigel, Gross and Kravets). A surname search here gives false negatives, and a missing surname proves nothing.

Search instead for **given name + patronymic + age + community**, or for a family, **father + sons' names + ages**. The patronymic is the anchor: "Shmuel Nusyovich" had a father called Nus, so look for a Nus of suitable age in the same community in the previous revision. Surnames can be double, «Нудельманъ онъ же Капланъ» ("Nudelman, he is also Kaplan"): search under both.

## Step 5. Double given names are one name

Хаимъ-Іосъ, Иегуда-Лейбъ, Аврумъ-Ицко (Khaim-Yos, Yehuda-Leib, Avrum-Itsko) are one name in two parts, and a clerk could write either half. Search under both. In «Хаимъ Іосъ Мендельовичъ», "Іосъ" is part of the name; the patronymic is Mendelovich.

A father's double name can give his children different patronymics, and names were sometimes changed during life. So different patronymics among one father's children do not refute kinship; age, household and registration decide. Watch for **uncle and nephew with the same name and close ages**, both named after the same dead grandfather: check by father and household.

## Step 6. Merchants, meshchane and separate lists

Estate (*soslovie*) is a status, not an origin: the guild certificate was bought each year, and one family can be *meshchane* in 1850 and merchants in 1875. Inventories often title files «ревізькі казки купців і міщан» ("revision lists of merchants and townsmen").

A shtetl can have **two estate lists**, for example guild artisans (*tsekhovye*, цеховые) and *meshchane*, and the numbering in the second **starts again from No. 1**. Always note which list "household No. 26" comes from, and check both.

## Step 7. Duplicates and the clerks' own corrections

**Whole lists are sometimes bound into one file twice**, by different clerks. A matching list number on the title page is not proof: in one case the same number headed a different list. Compare the first households. A true duplicate lets you check a disputed reading against a second hand.

**Clerks corrected the previous revision themselves.** Search for «ошибкою» ("by mistake"), «ошибочно» ("in error") and «а настоящее» ("but the real one is"), as in «по ревизiи 1795 года написанъ ошибкою Маiорковъ, а настоящее его имя…» ("in the revision of 1795 he was written by mistake as Mayorkov, but his real name is…"). Such lines show that early patronymics were sometimes wrong, so link generations by age and household, not patronymic alone.

A son missing from his father's household is not a contradiction either. If he was transferred to another community before the revision, he appears there as «прибылый» ("arrived").

## Where to find the scans, and how to judge them

**Wikimedia Commons** holds whole files from Ukrainian archives, including revision lists, metrical books, censuses and typescript fond inventories of DAKhmO (Khmelnytskyi oblast), DAViO (Vinnytsia), DAZhO (Zhytomyr), DAKO (Kyiv) and others. There is no name index: Commons gives access, not search.

- **Download the PDF whole.** Previews are over-compressed and lose faded ink.
- **Judge quality first:** file size ÷ pages. 2.5–3 MB per page reads easily; 0.9–1.6 MB with cropping; 0.25–0.5 MB only by cropping single columns.
- **Check the spread.** Open four pages in a row and note the household numbers. If a PDF page is a whole spread, reading "every other page" loses half the households. One volume read at every fourth page gave 25% coverage and a baseless "not there".

**Ukrainian Wikisource**, namespace «Архів:» ("Archive:"), lists files with links to Commons. A listing is a **sample, not an inventory** (one covers 308 files out of 9,288): use it to find files, never to conclude one does not exist. For that, use the paper inventory (Commons files like "ДАХмО фонд N опис N.pdf") or the archive's register of inventories. The "Duck Inspector" (Качиний Інспектор, inspector.duckarchive.com) shows whether a file is online; an empty answer does not mean there is no scan.

**FamilySearch** has microfilms from Moldovan and Ukrainian archives, including revisions of Izmail 1824–1854 and Akkerman 1835 and 1859. Registration is free; many films open only in FamilySearch centres. One film can hold several books, so check a known control record.

If a scan is damaged, look for **another copy**: revision lists were made in several, kept by the treasury chamber (usually the digitised one), the city duma or town hall, and the district treasury.

## What "not found" means

"The record does not exist" and "I did not find it" are different statements. A "not found" carries weight only if you state four things:

1. **the source and its exact limits**: which file, which pages;
2. **the spellings and forms searched**, including patronymic variants;
3. **the method**: exact, phonetic, page by page;
4. **whether you read an index or the file itself.**

Then check the structure: men only in 1811, both estate lists, every column of the spread. A household missing because part of a town is not digitised is "not digitised", not "not there".

## A worked example (fictional)

Invented, using the guide's fictional families.

**1858, *meshchane*, household No. 112.** Head: Хаимъ-Іосъ Мендельовъ Розенбергъ (Khaim-Yos, son of Mendel, Rozenberg): at the last revision **38**, now **46**. Difference 8, so the rung is 1850. Sons Leib (12 → 20) and Nukhim (6 → 14).

Bridge line: *age at the 9th revision is 38, so look in 1850 at age 38, with sons of about 12 and 6.*

**1850, *meshchane*, household No. 58** (a new number, as expected). It opens with the father, Мендель Лейбовъ Розенбергъ (Mendel, son of Leib): at the last revision 40, departed «умеръ въ 1847 году» ("died in 1847"). The son is written as «Іосъ Мендельовъ», one half of his double name: now 38, at the last revision 22, sons 12 and 6 in the same order. 38 − 22 = 16 confirms the rung. Had we read 22 as his age in 1850, we would have put his birth 16 years too late.

**1834.** Nothing among the *meshchane*; the family is in the **guild artisans' list**, household No. 12, numbered from 1 again. Mendel 40, at the last revision 22: 18 years, the interval from 1816, the next rung.

**Below 1816**, surnames thin out. For 1795 search not "Rozenberg" but "a Leib with an infant son Mendel in this community". That Mendel's grandson is also Leib hints at naming after the dead: a hypothesis, not proof.

## Checklist

- [ ] Header read; I know which column is "at the last revision"
- [ ] Every age written with its year
- [ ] A bridge line for every find; no rung skipped
- [ ] Column difference checked against the interval between revisions
- [ ] Departed column copied, with year and reason
- [ ] Households linked by name, patronymic, age and composition, never by number
- [ ] Children's order of seniority compared
- [ ] Both halves of double names searched; before the 1820s, patronymic + age + community
- [ ] Both estate lists checked; each number noted with its list
- [ ] File searched for «ошибкою», «ошибочно», «а настоящее»; duplicates compared by first households
- [ ] PDF downloaded whole; MB per page noted; spread checked on four pages
- [ ] Every "not found" states source and limits, spellings, method, index or file

## Doing this with an AI agent

AI models read the printed part of a form well, handwritten names and places worse, and they tend to "see" the names you tell them to expect. Work in two passes: first give the document type, year and place but **not** the expected names, on a high-resolution crop of the column; then compare the reading with your hypotheses.

The rule: **every claim needs a verbatim quote and an archive reference** (archive, fond, inventory, file, page or folio). Without them a reading is a hint, not a find, and only what you have checked by eye against the scan goes into your tree.

- "This is a revision list of [place], [year], in Russian with pre-1918 spelling. First read the printed header and tell me which column is 'at the last revision' and which is 'now present'. Then copy household [number] verbatim in the original spelling: names, both ages, the departed column. Mark anything unreadable as (?)."
- "Here is household [number] from [year] and household [number] from [earlier year]. For each person, subtract the 'last revision' age from the current age and say whether it matches the interval between revisions. Compare the children's order of seniority. Quote each line you rely on with its page."
- "I searched file [reference], pages [A–B], for [forms]. Write this as a negative conclusion stating the source and limits, the forms, the method, and whether I read an index or the file. Claim nothing more."
